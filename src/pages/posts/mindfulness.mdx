#### Context Setting

> _“This is the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation, for the passing away of discomfort and distress, for the achievement of the method, for the personal realization of Nibbāna, that is, the **four establishments of mindfulness** (cattāro satipaṭṭhānā).”_

— [SN 47.1 ¶1](/sn47.1#1)

> _“The mind, brahmin, takes recourse in **mindfulness** (sati).”_

> _“And sir Gotama, what is it that mindfulness (sati) takes recourse in?”_

> _“**Mindfulness** (sati), brahmin, takes recourse in liberation.”_

— [SN 48.42 ¶6 - ¶8](/sn48.42#6-8)

> _“And if one speaking rightly, were to speak of ‘a heap of the wholesome,’ it is the **four establishments of mindfulness** (cattāro satipaṭṭhāne) that one, speaking rightly, would call ‘a heap of the wholesome.’”_

— [SN 47.5 ¶4](/sn47.5#4)

> _“For one with wrong effort, **wrong mindfulness** (micchāsati) arises; for one with wrong mindfulness, **wrong collectedness** (micchāsamādhi) arises; for one with wrong collectedness, wrong knowledge arises; for one with wrong knowledge, false liberation arises._

> _For one with right effort, **right mindfulness** (sammāsati) arises; for one with right mindfulness, **right collectedness** (sammāsamādhi) arises; for one with right collectedness, right knowledge arises; for one with right knowledge, true liberation arises.”_

— [AN 10.103 ¶1 - ¶2](/an10.103#1-2)

#### Definitional

> _“And where is the **faculty of mindfulness** (satindriyaṁ) to be seen? In the four establishments of mindfulness—here the faculty of mindfulness is to be seen.”_

— [SN 48.8 ¶4](/sn48.8#4)

> _“And what is the **faculty of mindfulness** (satindriyaṁ)? Here, bhikkhus, a disciple of the Noble Ones is **mindful** (satimā), possessing supreme **mindfulness and skillfulness** (satinepakkena), **remembering** (sarita) and **recollecting** (anussarita) what was done and said long ago. This is called the faculty of mindfulness.”_

— [SN 48.9 ¶4](/sn48.9#4)

> _“And what is the faculty of mindfulness? Here, bhikkhus, a disciple of the Noble Ones is mindful, possessing supreme mindfulness and skillfulness, remembering and recollecting what was done and said long ago. He dwells observing the **body** (kāya) in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with **excellent recollection** (satimā), having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. He dwells observing **felt experience** (vedanā) in and of itself ... **mind** (cittā) in and of itself ... **mental qualities** (dhammā) in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with **excellent recollection** (satimā), having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. This is called the faculty of mindfulness.”_

— [SN 48.10 ¶4](/sn48.10#4)

> _“And what is the faculty of mindfulness? Whatever **mindfulness** (satiṁ) one acquires by undertaking the four establishments of mindfulness—this is called the faculty of mindfulness.”_

— [SN 48.11 ¶4](/sn48.11#4)

> _“For, venerable sir, it is to be expected that a disciple of the Noble Ones who has faith and has aroused energy will be **mindful** (satimā), possessing supreme **mindfulness and skillfulness** (satinepakkena), **remembering** (sarita) and **recollecting** (anussarita) what was done and said long ago. That mindfulness of his, venerable sir, is his faculty of mindfulness.”_

— [SN 48.50 ¶5](/sn48.50#5)

#### Cultivation

> _“Therefore, bhikkhu, purify the very starting point of wholesome qualities. And what is the starting point of wholesome qualities? Virtue that is well purified, and view that is straight. When, bhikkhu, your virtue is well purified and your view is straight, then, relying on virtue, being established in virtue, you should cultivate the **four establishments of mindfulness** (cattāro satipaṭṭhāne) in a threefold way._

> _What four?_

> _1.) Here, bhikkhu, dwell observing the body in and of itself internally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world; or dwell observing the body in and of itself externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world; or dwell observing the body in and of itself both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world._

> _2.) Dwell observing the felt experience in and of itself internally, ․․․ or dwell observing the felt experience in and of itself externally, ․․․ or dwell observing the felt experience in and of itself both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world._

> _3.) Dwell observing the mind in and of itself internally, ․․․ or dwell observing the mind in and of itself externally, ․․․ or dwell observing the mind in and of itself both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world._

> _4.) Dwell observing the mental qualities in and of themselves internally, ․․․ or dwell observing the mental qualities in and of themselves externally, ․․․ or dwell observing the mental qualities in and of themselves both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world._

> _When, bhikkhu, relying on virtue and being established in virtue, you cultivate these four establishments of mindfulness thus in a threefold way, then, bhikkhu, whether night or day comes, only growth in wholesome qualities is to be expected for you, not decline.”_

— [SN 47.3 ¶5 - ¶11](/sn47.3#5-11)

##### Directed & Undirected Cultivation

A directed cultivation is observing sluggishness of mind,

> _“So it is, Ānanda! So it is, Ānanda! For whoever, Ānanda, whether a bhikkhu or a bhikkhunī, dwells with their mind well established in the four establishments of mindfulness, it is to be expected of them: ‘They will perceive successive stages of noble distinction.’_

> _What four? Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. While he is dwelling observing the body in and of itself, based on the body there arises a fever in the body, or sluggishness of mind, or the mind is scattered externally. That bhikkhu should then direct his mind toward some inspiring theme. When he directs the mind to some inspiring theme, joy arises. When one is joyful, uplifting joy arises. In one whose mind is filled with uplifting joy, the body becomes tranquil. One tranquil in body dwells in contentment. The mind of one who dwells in contentment becomes collected. He reflects thus: ‘The purpose for which I directed my mind has been accomplished. Let me now withdraw it.’ He withdraws it and does not reflect or examine. He discerns: ‘Free from reflection and examination, internally mindful, there is contentment.’_

> _Furthermore, Ānanda, a bhikkhu dwells observing the felt experience in and of itself ... dwells observing the mind in and of itself ... dwells observing the mental qualities in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. While he is dwelling observing mental qualities in and of themselves, based on mental qualities there arises a fever in the body, or sluggishness of mind, or the mind is scattered externally. That bhikkhu should then direct his mind toward some inspiring theme. When he directs the mind to some inspiring theme, joy arises. When one is joyful, uplifting joy arises. In one whose mind is filled with uplifting joy, the body becomes tranquil. One tranquil in body dwells in contentment. The mind of one who dwells in contentment becomes collected. He reflects thus: ‘The purpose for which I directed my mind has been accomplished. Let me now withdraw it.’ He withdraws it and does not reflect or examine. He discerns: ‘Free from reflection and examination, internally mindful, there is contentment.’ In this way, Ānanda, there is directed cultivation._

> _And how, Ānanda, is there undirected cultivation? Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the body in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the felt experience in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the mind in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the mental qualities in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ In this way, Ānanda, there is undirected cultivation._

— [SN 47.10 ¶6 - ¶9](/sn47.10#6-9)
