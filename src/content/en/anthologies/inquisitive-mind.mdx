This is a site-curated anthology gathered from the discourses of the Pāli Canon whose teaching turns on **inquisitiveness** — asking, testing, and investigating. It is not a published book, but a reading path of graduated progress: it begins with the questions no one can opt out of — aging, illness, death, and loss — and then turns to how to question at all, which questions to set aside, how to investigate experience and the mind, how the training unfolds step by step, and finally quenching itself: the ending of greed, aversion, and delusion, which each person can check in their own mind.

The English translations to the discourses are provided by WordsOfTheBuddha.org project and based on the Pali texts of the connected discourses from the Mahāsaṅgīti Tipiṭaka Buddhavasse 2500 Pali Tipiṭaka in Roman script edition as made available by SuttaCentral.net.

### 1. The Questions We Avoid

1. [On Raṭṭhapāla - MN 82](/mn82) — a young man of means questions his way to ordination, then tells the king the four summaries of the Dhamma
2. [Unobtainable States - AN 5.48](/an5.48) — aging, illness, death, and loss: how the learned disciple meets what no one can prevent
3. [Kosala - AN 5.49](/an5.49) — a king grieves his queen; the Buddha contrasts self-torment with the practice that brings peace
4. [Aging And Death - SN 3.3](/sn3.3) — King Pasenadi asks whether, for one who is born, there is anything other than aging and death; not for the rich, and not even for arahants
5. [Simile of the Mountain - SN 3.25](/sn3.25) — old age and death roll in from the four quarters; no army, wealth, or spell turns them back
6. [Delicate - AN 3.39](/an3.39) — the young Bodhisatta turns the disgust he sees in others at the aged, the sick, and the dead back onto himself, and the intoxications with youth, health, and life fall away
7. [Subjects for Frequent Reflection - AN 5.57](/an5.57) — five subjects everyone shares, and one responsibility that is one's own
8. [Illness - AN 4.157](/an4.157) — rare even for a moment: freedom from mental illness, and how to train for it
9. [Ageing - Snp 4.6](/snp4.6) — all we call "mine" must be lost at death — the verse on not clinging
10. [Piṅgiya’s Questions - Snp 5.16](/snp5.16) — old, his eyes and ears failing, he asks not to perish confused along the way
11. [Kappa’s Questions - Snp 5.10](/snp5.10) — for those standing in the flood of aging and death, he asks for an island

### 2. The Spirit of Inquiry

1. [The Noble Quest - MN 26](/mn26) — the Buddha recounts his own search, the model inquirer at work
2. [Don't Go by Authority Alone - AN 3.65](/an3.65) — the Kālāmas are told to test the teaching against their own experience
3. [Hemaka’s Questions - Snp 5.8](/snp5.8) — he sets aside the "so it was, so it will be" of earlier teachers as hearsay that only feeds speculation, and asks for the ending of craving
4. [Trained in Questioning - AN 2.47](/an2.47) — two assemblies: one trained in empty talk, which memorizes poetry and never asks what it means; one trained in questioning, which learns the deep discourses and examines them — "How is this? What is the meaning of this?" — until the hidden is revealed and doubt is dispelled
5. [Superficially - AN 3.5](/an3.5) — the fool and the wise differ in how they ask a question, how they answer one, and whether they approve a well-reasoned reply
6. [Asking Questions - AN 5.165](/an5.165) — five reasons anyone asks a question, and the reason Sāriputta asks his
7. [Investigating the Teacher - MN 95](/mn95) — how to examine a teacher step by step, without blind faith or blind contempt
8. [Examining - MN 47](/mn47) — the Buddha himself lays out the method for investigating him: observe, question, and realize
9. [Answering a Question - AN 4.42](/an4.42) — four ways a question is met: a categorical answer, an analysis, a counter-question, or setting it aside
10. [Basis of Conversation - AN 3.67](/an3.67) — how a person handles questions shows whether they are fit to talk with; the purpose of talk is liberation of mind through non-clinging
11. [Person of Integrity - AN 4.73](/an4.73) — the qualities that distinguish a person of integrity from one lacking it
12. [Traits - AN 4.192](/an4.192) — a person's true character is known only over time, to a wise and attentive observer
13. [Fundamental Questions - AN 4.186](/an4.186) — a bhikkhu asks about the world, the mind, and wisdom, and receives a direct analysis

### 10. The Fruit of Inquiry: Quenching

1. What quenching is
    1. [A Question On Nibbāna - SN 38.1](/sn38.1) — asked "What is Nibbāna?", Sāriputta answers: the wearing away of passion, aversion, and delusion; the way to it is the Noble Eightfold Path
    2. [Questions on Awakening - SN 38.2](/sn38.2) — asked "What is arahantship?", he gives the same answer
    3. [The Unconditioned - SN 43.12](/sn43.12) — the unconditioned too is defined by what has ended: passion, aversion, and delusion
2. How the three arise and how they end
    1. [Who Follows Another Religion - AN 3.68](/an3.68) — passion arises from superficial attention to the beautiful, aversion from superficial attention to the aversive, and delusion from superficial attention itself; radical attention ends each
    2. [Root of the Unwholesome - AN 3.69](/an3.69) — with what is born of greed, aversion, and delusion cut off at the root, one attains Nibbāna in this very life
3. Greed and aversion stilled in seclusion
    1. [The Lesser Discourse on the Mass of Suffering - MN 14](/mn14) — Mahānāma asks why greed, aversion, and delusion still invade his mind; seeing the drawback in sensual pleasures is not enough until one reaches the joy that is apart from them
    2. [First Jhāna (Second) - AN 6.74](/an6.74) — thoughts and perceptions of sensual desire, ill will, and harm are what must be given up to enter the first jhāna
4. Radical attention carried through to the end of delusion
    1. [Overcoming Of All The Taints - MN 2](/mn2) — "Was I in the past? What am I?" is superficial attention and breeds views; radical attention to the four truths abandons the three fetters of stream-entry
    2. [Trainee (First) - Iti 16](/iti16) — for one in training, no inner factor is as helpful as radical attention
    3. [Virtuous - SN 22.122](/sn22.122) — "Which things should a virtuous bhikkhu radically attend to?" The same attention to the five aggregates leads to stream-entry, and on through each stage to arahantship
    4. [The Man from the Town of Aṭṭhaka - MN 52](/mn52) — asked for one thing that frees the mind, Ānanda shows each jhāna seen as conditioned and intentionally constructed, and therefore impermanent and subject to ending
    5. [Jhāna - AN 9.36](/an9.36) — from within jhāna, the mind turns away from all five aggregates toward the deathless: the stilling of all intentional constructs
5. Checking it for oneself
    1. [Directly Visible with Upavāṇa - SN 35.70](/sn35.70) — the Dhamma is directly visible when one knows "there is passion in me" and "there is no passion in me"
    2. [The Bhikkhus of Kosambi - MN 48](/mn48) — seven knowledges a disciple examines in himself, among them "do I personally gain quenching?", by which stream-entry is confirmed
    3. [Peril - AN 10.92](/an10.92) — with the five perilous animosities subsided and dependent co-arising radically attended to, a disciple may declare stream-entry of themselves
    4. [With Sāḷha - AN 3.66](/an3.66) — not by tradition but through "What do you think?", greed, aversion, and delusion are seen to be harmful; with them gone, one lives quenched and cool in this very life
6. [Directly Visible Dhamma - AN 9.46](/an9.46) — the Dhamma seen for oneself: provisionally in the attainments, definitively in cessation
7. [Directly Visible Nibbāna - AN 9.47](/an9.47) — Nibbāna as something to be verified, not merely believed
8. [Causes for Liberation - AN 5.26](/an5.26) — five ways inquiry matures into release — from hearing to reciting to reflecting
9. [With Hatthaka - AN 3.35](/an3.35) — how can the Buddha sleep at ease in the open? The serene ease of the unburdened mind
