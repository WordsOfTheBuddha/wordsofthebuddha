“I ask the sage of abundant wisdom,”
(said |Cunda::::Chunda| the smith)
“The Buddha, |Lord of Dhamma::master of truth, sovereign of teaching [dhammassāmī]|, free from craving,
best of men, foremost of guides, please tell me this:
how many |ascetics::contemplatives, renunciants [samaṇa]| are there in the world?”

“There are four kinds of ascetics; there is no fifth,
(|Cunda::::Chunda|,” said the Blessed One)
“Being asked in person, let me explain them to you:
the Victor of the Path, the Teacher of the Path,
the one who lives on the path, and the spoiler of the path.”

“Whom do the Buddhas call the Victor
(of the Path?” said |Cunda::::Chunda| the smith)
“How is one unequalled as a Teacher of the Path?
Being asked, tell me about the one who lives
on the path, and explain the spoiler of the path.”

“One who has |crossed over perplexity::overcome doubt and hesitation [tiṇṇakathaṅkatha]|, with dart
removed, delighting in |Nibbāna::extinguishment, unbinding [nibbāna]|, free from greed,
a guide for the world with its gods:
such a one the Buddhas call the Victor of the Path.

Having known the ultimate as the ultimate,
whoever here explains and analyzes the Dhamma,
the sage who has cut off doubt, |unruffled::unperturbed, unmoved [aneja]|,
is the second bhikkhu, the Teacher of the Path.

The one on the well-taught trail of the Dhamma
who lives on the path, |restrained::self-controlled [saññata]| and mindful,
resorting to blameless ways of conduct,
is the third bhikkhu, the one said to live on the path.

Putting on the outward cloak of virtuous persons,
|intrusive::lit. jumping forward [pakkhandī]|, a |corrupter of families::a monk who ingratiates himself inappropriately into households, compromising their pure faith [kuladūsaka]|, |brash::impudent [pagabbha]|,
deceitful, unrestrained, mere |chaff::empty husk, worthless pretender [palāpa]|,
wandering about in counterfeit disguise: he is the spoiler of the path.

When these have been |penetrated::comprehended; lit. pierced back [paṭivijjhi]| by a layperson,
a learned, wise disciple of the Noble Ones—
knowing that: ‘They are not all alike,’
seeing this, their faith does not diminish.
For how could one equate the corrupt with the
uncorrupt, or place the impure on par with the pure?”
