Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was on a walking tour in the country of the Anguttarāpans together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and eventually he arrived at a town of the Anguttarāpans named |Āpaṇa::name of a town in Bengal; lit. market [āpaṇa]|. The matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya heard: “It seems the ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from the Sakyan clan, while wandering on a walking tour in the country of the Anguttarāpans together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, has arrived at Āpaṇa. Now a good report about that sir Gotama has circulated thus: ‘The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One. He declares this world with its |deities::gods [devas]|, |Māras::demons, tempters, beings of delusion; lit. causing death [mārake]|, |Brahmas::Gods; celestial beings residing in the Brahmā realms, often considered to be highly refined and long-lived deities. [brahmā]|, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, kings and commoners, which he has himself realized with direct knowledge. He teaches the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| that is beneficial in the beginning, beneficial in the middle, and beneficial in the end, meaningful and well-phrased, and he reveals a |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| that is entirely perfect and pure.’ It is auspicious to see such arahants.”

Then the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One. After having engaged in courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. While the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya was seated to one side, the Blessed One gave him a talk on the Dhamma, instructing, encouraging, inspiring, and gladdening him. Then the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya, having been instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened by the Blessed One’s talk on the Dhamma, said to the Blessed One: “May sir Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus accept tomorrow’s meal from me.” When this was said, the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya: “The Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large, Keṇiya—twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus—and you have deep confidence in the brahmins.”

A second time, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya said to the Blessed One: “Even though, sir Gotama, the Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large—twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus—and I have deep confidence in the brahmins, still may sir Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus accept tomorrow’s meal from me.” A second time, the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya: “The Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large, Keṇiya—twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus—and you have deep confidence in the brahmins.”

A third time, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya said to the Blessed One: “Even though, sir Gotama, the Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large—twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus—and I have deep confidence in the brahmins, still may sir Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus accept tomorrow’s meal from me.” The Blessed One consented in silence. Then the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya, having received the Blessed One’s consent, rose from his seat and went to his own hermitage. Having arrived, he addressed his friends and colleagues, relatives and kin: “Listen to me, sirs, friends and colleagues, relatives and kin! I have invited the ascetic Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus for tomorrow’s meal. Please assist me with the physical work.” “Yes, sir,” the friends and colleagues, relatives and kin replied to the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya, and some dug out fireplaces, some chopped firewood, some washed dishes, some set up water jars, and some prepared seats. Meanwhile, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya prepared the circular pavilion himself.

Now at that time the brahmin Sela was residing at Āpaṇa. He was one who had mastered the |three Vedas::the primary sacred scriptures of the Vedic religion: Ṛgveda, Sāmaveda, and Yajurveda [tiṇṇaṁ vedānaṁ]|, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth. He was a reciter of the Vedas, a grammarian and linguistic scholar, fully versed in |natural philosophy::science of disputation; lit. extension of the world [lokāyata]| and the |auspicious marks of a great man::characteristics of an illustrious being [mahāpurisalakkhaṇa]|, and he was teaching the hymns to three hundred young brahmins.

Now at that time the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya had deep confidence in the brahmin Sela. Then the brahmin Sela, surrounded by three hundred young brahmins, while walking and wandering about for exercise, approached the hermitage of the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya. The brahmin Sela saw some digging out fireplaces in the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya’s hermitage ... and some preparing seats, while the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya was preparing the circular pavilion himself. Seeing this, he said to the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya: “Is there to be a marriage of a son or a marriage of a daughter for Master Keṇiya? Or has a great sacrifice been arranged? Or has King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha been invited for tomorrow’s meal together with his army?”

“There is to be no marriage of a son or daughter for me, Master Sela, nor has King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha been invited for tomorrow’s meal together with his army. But a great sacrifice has indeed been arranged for me. There is the ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from the Sakyan clan, who while wandering on a walking tour in the country of the Anguttarāpans together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, has arrived at Āpaṇa. Now a good report about that sir Gotama has circulated thus: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ He has been invited by me for tomorrow’s meal together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.” “Did you say ‘Buddha,’ Master Keṇiya?” “I said ‘Buddha,’ Master Sela.” “Did you say ‘Buddha,’ Master Keṇiya?” “I said ‘Buddha,’ Master Sela.”

Then it occurred to the brahmin Sela: “Even this sound is rare in the world, that is, ‘Buddha.’ The thirty-two marks of a great man have been handed down in our hymns, and for a great man endowed with them, there are only two possible courses, no other. If he lives the household life, he becomes a |wheel-turning monarch::a just and virtuous ruler whose domain extends over the entire known world [rājā cakkavattī]|, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, a conqueror of the four quarters, who has established stability in the realm, endowed with the seven treasures. He has these seven treasures, namely: the wheel treasure, the elephant treasure, the horse treasure, the jewel treasure, the woman treasure, the householder treasure, and the counselor treasure as the seventh. Moreover, he has more than a thousand sons who are valiant, heroic, able to crush the armies of their enemies. Having conquered this earth bounded by the ocean without rod or sword, solely through the Dhamma, he rules over it. But if he goes forth from the household life into homelessness, he becomes an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, who draws back the veil from the world. Where, Master Keṇiya, is that sir Gotama, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One, dwelling at present?”

When this was said, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya extended his right arm and said to the brahmin Sela: “Over there, Master Sela, at that line of dark blue forest.” Then the brahmin Sela, together with the three hundred young brahmins, went to where the Blessed One was. Then the brahmin Sela instructed those young brahmins: “Come quietly, sirs, placing footstep after footstep. For those Blessed Ones are difficult to approach, like solitary lions. And when, sirs, I am in conversation with the ascetic Gotama, do not interrupt my speech; wait, sirs, until my speech is finished.”

Then the brahmin Sela approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One. After having engaged in courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the brahmin Sela scrutinized the thirty-two marks of a great man on the Blessed One’s body. The brahmin Sela saw the thirty-two marks of a great man on the Blessed One’s body for the most part, except for two. He had doubt and uncertainty, and was undecided and unsure about two marks of a great man: whether the private parts were enclosed in a sheath, and the largeness of the tongue.

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “This brahmin Sela sees the thirty-two marks of a great man on me for the most part, except for two. He has doubt and uncertainty, and is undecided and unsure about two marks of a great man: whether the private parts are enclosed in a sheath, and the largeness of the tongue.” Then the Blessed One performed such an act of psychic power that the brahmin Sela saw the Blessed One’s private parts enclosed in a sheath. Then the Blessed One extended his tongue, repeatedly touched and stroked both his ear-holes, repeatedly touched and stroked both his nostrils, and completely covered his entire forehead with his tongue.

Then it occurred to the brahmin Sela: “The ascetic Gotama is indeed endowed with the thirty-two marks of a great man in full, not incompletely. But I still do not know whether he is an Awakened One or not. Yet I have heard this from elderly, senior brahmins, teachers of teachers, as they spoke: ‘Those who are Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones, reveal themselves when their praise is spoken.’ What if I were to extol the ascetic Gotama in his presence with suitable verses?” Then the brahmin Sela extolled the Blessed One in his presence with suitable verses:

“Your body is complete and radiant,
well-formed and lovely to behold;
you are golden-complexioned, Blessed One,
with dazzling white teeth and full of vigor.

For whatever features belong
to a man of noble birth,
all those marks of a great man
are found upon your body.

With clear eyes and handsome face,
tall, upright, and majestic;
in the midst of the Saṅgha of ascetics,
you shine like the radiant sun.

A bhikkhu pleasant to behold,
with skin like finest gold:
with such supreme complexion,
what need have you for the ascetic life?

You are worthy of being a king,
a |wheel-turning monarch::a just and virtuous ruler whose domain extends over the entire known world [rājā cakkavattī]|, leader of charioteers;
a conqueror of the four quarters,
the sovereign ruler of the |Jambusaṇḍa::a reference to the Indian subcontinent, lit. land of the black plum aka jambu trees [jambusaṇḍa]|.

Let aristocrats and wealthy kings
be vassals following your rule;
reign as king of kings, ruler of men,
rule the realm, Gotama!”

“I am a king, Sela,”
(said the Blessed One)
“the |unsurpassed::highest, incomparable [anuttara]| King of the Dhamma;
by the Dhamma I turn the wheel,
the wheel that cannot be turned back.”

“You claim to be awakened,”
(said the brahmin Sela)
“the unsurpassed King of the Dhamma;
‘By the Dhamma I turn the wheel’—
so you say, Gotama.

Who then is your general,
the disciple who follows after the Teacher?
Who keeps rolling along after you
the wheel of the Dhamma you have set in motion?”

“By me the wheel was set in motion,
(Sela,” said the Blessed One)
“the unsurpassed wheel of the Dhamma;
Sāriputta keeps it rolling along,
following in the footsteps of the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|.

What should be |directly known::completely known, realized [abhiññāya]| has been known,
what should be cultivated has been cultivated,
what should be given up has been given up by me:
therefore, brahmin, I am an Awakened One.

Dispel your doubt in me,
be resolved, brahmin!
The sight of the fully Awakened Ones
is difficult to obtain again and again.

Of those whose appearance in the world
is difficult to obtain again and again,
I am that fully Awakened One, brahmin,
the unsurpassed surgeon.

Become like Brahmā, incomparable,
crusher of Māra’s army;
having subdued all adversaries,
I rejoice, having nothing to fear from anywhere.”

“Listen to this, gentlemen,
to what the |clear-eyed one::who can see, gifted with sight [cakkhumant]| says;
the surgeon, the great hero,
roars like a lion in the forest.

Become like Brahmā, incomparable,
crusher of Māra’s army;
who, having seen him, would not be inspired,
even one of low birth?

Whoever wishes, let him follow me;
whoever does not wish, let him go;
here I shall go forth
in the presence of the one of excellent wisdom.”

“If this pleases you, sir,
in the teaching of the perfectly Awakened One,
we too will go forth
in the presence of the one of excellent wisdom.”

“These three hundred brahmins
entreat you with raised palms:
‘May we live the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]|
in your presence, Blessed One.’”

“The spiritual life is well proclaimed,
(Sela,” said the Blessed One)
“visible here and now, |immediate::(comm) giving immediate fruit; not related to time [akālika]|;
where the going forth is not in vain
for one who trains |with diligence::quality of doing one’s work or duty well, with alertness, carefulness and care [appamatta]|.”

Then the brahmin Sela and his assembly received the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence, they received full ordination. Then, after the passage of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya had various kinds of excellent food prepared in his own hermitage, and announced the time to the Blessed One: “It is time, sir Gotama, the meal is ready.” Then the Blessed One, having dressed early in the morning, taking his alms bowl and outer robe, went to the hermitage of the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya. Having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.

Then the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya, with his own hands, served and satisfied the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha with various kinds of excellent food. Then the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya took a low seat and sat down to one side after the Blessed One had finished his meal and put away his bowl. While the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya was seated to one side, the Blessed One expressed his appreciation with these verses:

“The fire sacrifice is the foremost of offerings;
the |Sāvitrī::the Vedic verse Sāvitrī [sāvitti]| is the foremost of poetic meters;
the king is the foremost of humans;
the ocean is the foremost of rivers.

The moon is the foremost of constellations;
the sun is the foremost of shining lights;
for those desiring for merit,
the Saṅgha is truly the foremost.”

Then the Blessed One, having offered these verses of appreciation to the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya, rose from his seat and departed. Thereafter, the venerable Sela together with his assembly, dwelling alone, secluded, diligent, resolute, and with continuous effort, soon realized with direct knowledge and attained in that very life the unsurpassed culmination of the spiritual life, for the sake of which sons of good families rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness. And the venerable Sela together with his assembly became arahants.

Then the venerable Sela together with his assembly approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he arranged his robe over one shoulder, raised his joined palms towards the Blessed One, and addressed the Blessed One in verse:

“Eight days ago from today, O clear-eyed one,
we went to you for refuge;
in these seven nights, Blessed One,
we have been tamed within your dispensation.

You are the Buddha, you are the Teacher,
you are the sage who has conquered Māra;
having cut off the underlying tendencies,
having crossed over, you bring these people across.

You have overcome all attachments,
your taints are completely shattered;
you are a lion free from clinging,
with fear and dread abandoned.

These three hundred bhikkhus
stand with palms joined in reverence;
extend your feet, hero,
let these noble giants bow to the Teacher!”
