title: Dhaniya sutta - Dhaniya
description: Verses depicting a poetic duel between the wealthy cowherd Dhaniya and the Buddha. While Dhaniya boasts of his worldly security, family, and thriving herd, the Buddha counters with declarations of profound spiritual freedom.
qualities: contentment, quenching, attachment, free from attachment, sorrow
slug: snp1.2
character:
  - Dhaniya the Cowherd
  - Māra the Evil One
priority: 1.2
---

“I have cooked my rice, I have milked the cows,”
(said the cowherd Dhaniya)
“living with my family on the banks of the |Mahī::name of a river; one of the five great rivers of ancient India [mahī]|;
my hut is roofed, the fire is lit:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“I am free from anger, I am rid of |barrenness::coldness, hard-heartedness [khila]|,”
(said the Blessed One)
“passing a single night on the banks of the Mahī;
my hut uncovered, and my fire |quenched::liberated from mental defilements [nibbuta]|:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“There are no gadflies or mosquitoes here,”
(said the cowherd Dhaniya)
“the cows roam in meadows lush with grass;
they can endure the rain that comes:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“I have put together a well-made raft,”
(said the Blessed One)
“[with it] I have crossed, gone beyond, escaped the flood.
I have no more need for a raft:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“My wife is |obedient::dutiful; lit. who listens [assava]| and not |restless::not covetous or distracted by desires [alola]|,”
(said the cowherd Dhaniya)
“living together with me long, she is |agreeable::pleasant, likable [manāpa]|,
and I do not hear any evil about her:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“My mind is obedient and |liberated::freed [vimutta]|,”
(said the Blessed One)
“it has been long |fostered::fortified; lit. caused to be all around [paribhāvita]| and |well-tamed::well trained, well controlled [sudanta]|;
and no evil is found in me:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“I am living off my own earnings,”
(said the cowherd Dhaniya)
“and my children are with me and healthy;
I do not hear any evil about them:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“I am not a hired worker of anyone,”
(said the Blessed One)
“I wander in all the world by what I have earned.
I have no need at all for wages:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“I have mature calves and suckling calves,”
(said the cowherd Dhaniya)
“I have pregnant cows and breeding cows;
there is also a bull, the lord of the herd, here:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“I have no mature calves, no suckling calves,”
(said the Blessed One)
“I have no pregnant cows, no breeding cows;
there is no bull, the lord of the herd, here:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“The stakes are driven in, unshakable,”
(said the cowherd Dhaniya)
“the ropes of muñja grass are new and well-made;
not even the suckling calves can break them:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

“Like a bull cutting through its bonds,”
(said the Blessed One)
“like a bull elephant bursting a rotten vine;
I will never again enter a womb:
So if you wish, rain down, O sky!”

Filling the lowlands and the high ground,
the great rain cloud immediately poured down.
Hearing the sky raining,
Dhaniya said this:

“Our gains are truly not small,
that we have seen the Blessed One!
We go to you for refuge, O |Clear-eyed One::who can see, gifted with sight [cakkhumant]|;
be our teacher, O Great Sage!

“My wife and I will be obedient,
we will lead the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| under the Accomplished One.
Going beyond birth and death,
we will make an end of suffering.”

“One with children delights because of children,”
(said Māra the Evil One)
“one with cows likewise delights because of cows;
for acquisitions are a person's delight,
one without acquisitions does not delight.”

“One with children sorrows because of children,”
(said the Blessed One)
“one with cows likewise sorrows because of cows;
a person sorrows because of acquisitions,
without acquisitions, one does not sorrow.”
