title: Muni sutta - Sage
description: The Buddha describes the true sage as one who abandons intimacy and home to wander in peace. Unshaken by the world, the sage is compared to a swift swan, whose flight a worldly householder, like a peacock, cannot match.
qualities: wisdom, giving up, dispassion, solitude, spiritual life, non-harm, Arahant
theme: wisdom, inspiration

From intimacy, peril has arisen,
from home, there arises dust.
Without a home, free from intimacy:
this is indeed the vision of the sage.

He who, having uprooted what’s grown,
would not plant anew or nurture what is arising;
they call him a solitary, wandering sage;
that great seer has seen the state of peace.

Having comprehended the bases and destroyed the seed,
he would not nurture it with affection;
that sage, a seer of the final end of birth,
having abandoned |speculation::conjecture, logic, reasoning [takka]|, is beyond |measurement::calculation, enumeration; mental constructs that form the basis for self-identity [saṅkhā]|.

Having known all |abodes::houses; lit. entering down [nivesanāni]|,
not attracted to even a single one of them;
that sage, free from greed and without desire,
does not strive, for he |has crossed over::has reached the far shore, epithet of an Arahant [pāragata]|.

Overcoming all, knowing all, of profound wisdom,
|untainted::unstained, unsullied [anūpalitta]| by any phenomena at all,
abandoning all, liberated in the |wearing away of craving::depletion of desire, extinction of longing [taṇhakkhaya]|—
he is one the wise know as a sage.

With the strength of wisdom, with virtue and observances,
|collected::composed, settled [samāhita]|, delighting in jhāna, |mindful::possessing active presence of mind and lucid retention [satīmant]|,
freed from |bonds::ties, clinging, attachment, holding on [saṅga]|, open-hearted, without taints—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

The sage wandering alone, diligent,
unshaken by blame and praise;
like a lion, unstartled by sounds,
like the wind, uncaught in a net,
like a lotus, unsullied by water,
a leader of others, by others unled—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

One who remains firm like a pillar at a ford,
when others speak praise and blame;
that one, |free from passion::without lust [vītarāga]|, with well composed faculties—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

Who is steadfast in himself, straight as a shuttle,
who is disgusted by |injurious::harmful, bad, or evil. Encompasses the deceptively alluring that is ultimately detrimental or ruinous [pāpaka]| actions,
who investigates the uneven and the even—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

Who is self-restrained and does no evil,
whether young or middle-aged, the sage is restrained;
unprovokable, he provokes no one—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

Whether from the top, the middle, or the remainder,
whatever food an alms-collector may receive,
he does not think to praise it or disparage it—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

The wandering sage, abstaining from sexual intimacy,
who in his youth is bound to nothing;
abstaining from intoxication and negligence, fully freed—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

Having known the world, a seer of the highest truth,
an impartial one who has crossed the flood and the ocean,
whose knots are severed, independent, without taints—
he, too, is one the wise know as a sage.

Unequal are the two, their ways of life far apart:
the householder supporting a wife, and the virtuous one free from ‘mine’;
the householder is unrestrained in harming other beings,
while the sage, ever restrained, protects living beings.

Just as the crested, blue-necked peacock
never matches the flight of the swan;
so the householder cannot equal the bhikkhu,
the sage, secluded, meditating in the forest.
