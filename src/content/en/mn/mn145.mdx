Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Then, in the late afternoon, having emerged from |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, the venerable Puṇṇa approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, the venerable Puṇṇa said to the Blessed One: “It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One were to exhort me with a brief exhortation, so that, having heard the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| from the Blessed One, I might dwell alone, secluded, |with diligence::quality of doing one’s work or duty well, with alertness, carefulness and care [appamatta]|, |continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, and |resoluteness::applying oneself [pahitatta]|.”

“Then listen to this, Puṇṇa, and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Puṇṇa replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“There are, Puṇṇa, |forms::visible objects such as beautiful sights, faces, expressions, art, ornaments, possessions, status symbols, admired appearances, enticing scenery, or objects of desire and attachment [rūpe]| cognizable by the eye that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and |enticing::arousing, tantalizing [rajanīya]|. If a bhikkhu delights in them, welcomes them, and |remains holding to them::remains fixated on them [ajjhosāya + tiṭṭhati]|, then for that one who delights, welcomes, and clings to them, |delight::pleasure, enjoyment, relish [nandī]| arises. ‘With the arising of delight, Puṇṇa, there is the arising of |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|,’ I say.

There are, Puṇṇa, |sounds::auditory experiences such as speech, music, praise, blame, ambient noise, verbal expression, or melodic tones—any sound that can stir emotion, craving for praise, aesthetic delight, or attachment to communication and identity [saddā]| cognizable by the ear … |odors::smells and fragrances experienced through the nose, including perfumes, flowers, food aromas, earth after rain, incense, or even stench—any olfactory experience that may evoke craving, aversion, nostalgia, comfort, or sensual pleasure [gandhā]| cognizable by the nose … |tastes::flavors experienced through the tongue such as sweetness, bitterness, sourness, saltiness, spiciness, richness, or subtle tastes like umami or astringency—any gustatory experience that can become an object of craving, indulgence, comfort, or sensory delight [rasā]| cognizable by the tongue … |tangible objects::tangible contact such as the feel of skin, warmth, softness, intimacy, physical affection, or sensations like massage, breath, water, air, pressure—anything felt through the body that can become an object of desire, arousal, comfort, or emotional attachment [phoṭṭhabba]| cognizable by the body … |mental objects::thoughts, ideas, memories, intentions, perceptions, concepts, beliefs, mental images, or fabrications arising from past experiences and future projections [dhammā]| cognizable by the mind that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing. If a bhikkhu delights in them, welcomes them, and remains holding to them, then for that one who delights, welcomes, and clings to them, delight arises. ‘With the arising of delight, Puṇṇa, there is the arising of suffering,’ I say.

And there are, Puṇṇa, forms cognizable by the eye that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing. If a bhikkhu does not delight in them, does not welcome them, and does not remain holding to them, then for that one who does not delight, does not welcome, and does not cling to them, delight ceases. ‘With the cessation of delight, Puṇṇa, there is the cessation of suffering,’ I say.

And there are, Puṇṇa, sounds cognizable by the ear … odors cognizable by the nose … tastes cognizable by the tongue … tangible objects cognizable by the body … mental objects cognizable by the mind that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing. If a bhikkhu does not delight in them, does not welcome them, and does not remain holding to them, then for that one who does not delight, does not welcome, and does not cling to them, delight ceases. ‘With the cessation of delight, Puṇṇa, there is the cessation of suffering,’ I say.

Now that you have been exhorted by me with this brief exhortation, Puṇṇa, in which country will you dwell?”

“Venerable sir, now that I have been exhorted by the Blessed One with this brief exhortation, there is a country named |Sunāparanta::a western border region of ancient India (often identified with Aparānta on the western coast) [sunāparanta]|; I will dwell there.”

“The people of Sunāparanta are |fierce::violent, hot-tempered, aggressive [caṇḍa]|, Puṇṇa; the people of Sunāparanta are |rough::harsh, rude, cruel [pharusa]|, Puṇṇa. If the people of Sunāparanta |abuse::insult, revile, scold [akkosati]| and |berate::rail at, disparage, verbally attack [paribhāsati]| you, Puṇṇa, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta abuse and berate me, then I will think thus: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are |good::kind, gracious, decent [bhaddaka]|, truly good are these people of Sunāparanta, in that they do not strike me with their hands.’ Thus will it be for me in this case, Blessed One; thus will it be for me in this case, Accomplished One.”

“But if the people of Sunāparanta do strike you with their hands, Puṇṇa, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta strike me with their hands, then I will think thus: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are good, truly good are these people of Sunāparanta, in that they do not strike me with a |clod::lump of earth, stone [leḍḍu]|.’ Thus will it be for me in this case, Blessed One; thus will it be for me in this case, Accomplished One.”

“But if the people of Sunāparanta do strike you with a clod, Puṇṇa, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta strike me with a clod, then I will think thus: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are good, truly good are these people of Sunāparanta, in that they do not strike me with a |stick::rod, club, staff [daṇḍa]|.’ Thus will it be for me in this case, Blessed One; thus will it be for me in this case, Accomplished One.”

“But if the people of Sunāparanta do strike you with a stick, Puṇṇa, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta strike me with a stick, then I will think thus: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are good, truly good are these people of Sunāparanta, in that they do not strike me with a |weapon::knife, blade, sword [sattha]|.’ Thus will it be for me in this case, Blessed One; thus will it be for me in this case, Accomplished One.”

“But if the people of Sunāparanta do strike you with a weapon, Puṇṇa, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta strike me with a weapon, then I will think thus: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are good, truly good are these people of Sunāparanta, in that they do not deprive me of life with a sharp weapon.’ Thus will it be for me in this case, Blessed One; thus will it be for me in this case, Accomplished One.”

“But if the people of Sunāparanta do deprive you of life with a sharp weapon, Puṇṇa, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta deprive me of life with a sharp weapon, then I will think thus: ‘There are disciples of the Blessed One who, distressed, ashamed, and disgusted with the body and with life, seek an |assassin::hit man; lit. weapon carrier [satthahāraka]|. And I have obtained this assassin without even seeking one!’ Thus will it be for me in this case, Blessed One; thus will it be for me in this case, Accomplished One.”

“Good, good, Puṇṇa! Endowed with such |self-control and calmness::restraint and peacefulness, taming and pacification [damūpasama]|, Puṇṇa, you will be able to dwell in the country of Sunāparanta. Now, Puṇṇa, you may go at your own convenience.”

Then the venerable Puṇṇa, delighting and rejoicing in the Blessed One’s words, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right, put his lodging in order, took his bowl and robe, and set out to wander toward the country of Sunāparanta. Wandering by stages, he eventually arrived at the country of Sunāparanta. There, the venerable Puṇṇa stayed in the country of Sunāparanta. Then, within that very rains retreat, the venerable Puṇṇa established about five hundred male lay disciples in the practice; within that very rains retreat, he established about five hundred female lay disciples in the practice; and within that very rains retreat, he realized for himself the |threefold knowledge::remembrance of past lives, knowledge of the passing away and rebirth of beings according to their kamma, and knowledge of the wearing away of the taints [tisso vijjā]|. Then, at a later time, the venerable Puṇṇa attained |final Nibbāna::complete cooling, full quenching, total emancipation, dying one’s final death [parinibbāyati]|.

Then several bhikkhus went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, they sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, those bhikkhus said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, the clansman named Puṇṇa, whom the Blessed One briefly advised, has passed away. What is his destination? What is his future rebirth?”

“The clansman Puṇṇa|, bhikkhus,::::| was |wise::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled [paṇḍita]|. He practiced |in accordance with the teaching::in line with the training guidelines of the Buddha’s teachings that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhammassānudhamma]| and did not pester me with the |technical points of the Dhamma::reasons or basis of the teaching [dhammādhikaraṇa]|. |Bhikkhus,::::| the clansman Puṇṇa has attained final Nibbāna.”

The Blessed One said this. The bhikkhus were delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words.
