Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling among the Kosalans at |Naḷakapāna::name of a village; lit. drinking reed; straw [naḷakapāna]| in the Palāsa Grove.

Now at that time several very well-known clansmen had gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness dedicated to the Blessed One—the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Bhaddiya, the venerable Kimila, the venerable Bhagu, the venerable Koṇḍañña, the venerable Revata, the venerable Ānanda, and other very well-known clansmen.

#### Delight in the Spiritual Life

Now at that time the Blessed One was sitting in the open, surrounded by the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. Then the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus regarding those clansmen: “|Bhikkhus,::::| those clansmen who have gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness dedicated to me—I hope those bhikkhus are finding delight in the spiritual life?” When this was said, those bhikkhus remained silent.

For a second time the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus regarding those clansmen: “|Bhikkhus,::::| those clansmen who have gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness dedicated to me—I hope those bhikkhus are finding delight in the spiritual life?” For a second time those bhikkhus remained silent. For a third time the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus regarding those clansmen: “|Bhikkhus,::::| those clansmen who have gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness dedicated to me—I hope those bhikkhus are finding delight in the spiritual life?” For a third time those bhikkhus remained silent.

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Why don’t I question those clansmen themselves?” Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Anuruddha: “I hope, Anuruddha and friends, that you are finding delight in the spiritual life?”

“Indeed, venerable sir, we are finding delight in the spiritual life.”

“Good, good, Anuruddha and friends. It is appropriate for you clansmen who have gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness that you should find delight in the spiritual life. Anuruddha and friends, endowed with the blessing of youth, in the prime of life, with pitch-black hair, you could have enjoyed sensual pleasures; yet, endowed with the blessing of youth, in the prime of life, with pitch-black hair, you have gone forth from the household life into homelessness. Furthermore, Anuruddha and friends, you did not go forth from the household life into homelessness coerced by kings, nor coerced by bandits, nor burdened by debt, nor burdened by fear, nor deprived of a livelihood. But rather you went forth thinking: ‘I am swamped by birth, aging, and death, by sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair; I am swamped by |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|, overwhelmed by suffering. Perhaps an end to this entire mass of suffering might be found!’ Is it not true, Anuruddha and friends, that you went forth from the household life into homelessness out of such faith?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

#### Uplifting Joy and the Hindrances

“But, Anuruddha and friends, when a clansman has thus gone forth, what should he do? If, Anuruddha and friends, he does not attain the |uplifting joy and pleasure::delight and ease; sometimes experienced as ecstasy, intense exhilaration or rapture [pītisukha]| that are secluded from sensual pleasures and secluded from unwholesome states, or something more peaceful than that, then |craving::greediness, wanting, yearning [abhijjhā]| |occupies::obsesses, controls [pariyādāya]| his mind and remains; ill will occupies his mind and remains; dullness and drowsiness occupies his mind and remain; restlessness and worry occupy his mind and remain; doubt occupies his mind and remains; |dissatisfaction::dislike, discontent, aversion, boredom [arati]| occupies his mind and remains; |laziness::sloth, tiredness [tandī]| occupies his mind and remains. This happens if he does not attain the uplifting joy and pleasure that are secluded from sensual pleasures and secluded from unwholesome states, or something more peaceful than that.

If, Anuruddha and friends, he attains the uplifting joy and pleasure that are secluded from sensual pleasures and secluded from unwholesome states, or something more peaceful than that, then craving does not occupy his mind and remain; ill will does not occupy his mind and remain; dullness and drowsiness do not occupy his mind and remain; restlessness and worry do not occupy his mind and remain; doubt does not occupy his mind and remain; dissatisfaction does not occupy his mind and remain; laziness does not occupy his mind and remain. This happens if he attains the uplifting joy and pleasure that are secluded from sensual pleasures and secluded from unwholesome states, or something more peaceful than that.

What do you think of me, Anuruddha and friends? Do you think: ‘The taints that are |defiling::corrupting, tainting [saṅkilesika]|, leading to renewed existence, troublesome, ripening in suffering, leading to future birth, aging, and death, have not been abandoned by the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|. That is why the Tathāgata, after reflection, uses some things, endures some things, avoids some things, and dispels some things’?”

“We do not think of the Blessed One in this way, venerable sir: ‘The taints that are defiling, leading to renewed existence, troublesome, ripening in suffering, leading to future birth, aging, and death, have not been abandoned by the Tathāgata. That is why the Tathāgata, after reflection, uses some things, endures some things, avoids some things, and dispels some things.’ We think of the Blessed One in this way, venerable sir: ‘The taints that are defiling, leading to renewed existence, troublesome, ripening in suffering, leading to future birth, aging, and death, have been abandoned by the Tathāgata. That is why the Tathāgata, after reflection, uses some things, endures some things, avoids some things, and dispels some things.’”

“Good, good, Anuruddha and friends. For the Tathāgata, Anuruddha and friends, the taints that are defiling, leading to renewed existence, troublesome, ripening in suffering, leading to future birth, aging, and death, have been abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and made incapable of arising in the future. Just as a palm tree with its crown cut off is incapable of further growth, so too, Anuruddha and friends, for the Tathāgata the taints that are defiling, leading to renewed existence, troublesome, ripening in suffering, leading to future birth, aging, and death, have been abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and made incapable of arising in the future. That is why the Tathāgata, after reflection, uses some things, endures some things, avoids some things, and dispels some things.

#### The Benefit of Revealing the Rebirth of Disciples

What do you think, Anuruddha and friends? Seeing what benefit does the Tathāgata declare the rebirth of disciples who have passed away and died, saying: ‘This one was reborn there; that one was reborn there’?”

“Venerable sir, our teachings are rooted in the Blessed One, guided by the Blessed One, and have the Blessed One as their refuge. It would be good if the Blessed One himself would clarify the meaning of this statement. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will remember it.”

“Anuruddha and friends, the Tathāgata does not declare the rebirth of disciples who have passed away and died, saying, ‘This one was reborn there; that one was reborn there,’ for the purpose of deceiving people, nor for the purpose of winning favor with people, nor for the sake of |acquisitions::gain, money, profit, possessions [lābhā]|, |respect::honor, accolade, reverence [sakkāra]|, and |popularity::fame, praise [siloka]|, nor with the thought ‘Let people know me thus.’ But, Anuruddha and friends, there are clansmen with faith who experience great inspiration and are full of joy. Having heard this, they apply their minds on that goal. That will lead to their welfare and happiness for a long time.

Here, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu hears: ‘The bhikkhu named so-and-so has died; he was declared by the Blessed One to have been established in final knowledge.’ And that venerable one was either seen by him personally or heard of by report: ‘That venerable one was of such virtue, he was of such character, he was of such wisdom, he dwelled like this, he was liberated in such a way.’ Recollecting his faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, he applies his mind on that goal. In this way, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu dwells in comfort.

Here, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu hears: ‘The bhikkhu named so-and-so has died; he was declared by the Blessed One to have been reborn spontaneously with the destruction of the five lower fetters, and will attain Nibbāna there, not liable to return from that world.’ And that venerable one was either seen by him personally or heard of by report: ‘That venerable one was of such virtue, he was of such character ... he was of such wisdom ... he dwelled like this ... he was liberated in such a way.’ Recollecting his faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, he applies his mind on that goal. In this way, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu dwells in comfort.

Here, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu hears: ‘The bhikkhu named so-and-so has died; he was declared by the Blessed One that with the destruction of the three fetters and the weakening of greed, hatred, and delusion, he is a once-returner; coming back to this world only once more, he will make an end of suffering.’ And that venerable one was either seen by him personally or heard of by report: ‘That venerable one was of such virtue, he was of such character ... he was of such wisdom ... he dwelled like this ... he was liberated in such a way.’ Recollecting his faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, he applies his mind on that goal. In this way, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu dwells in comfort.

Here, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu hears: ‘The bhikkhu named so-and-so has died; he was declared by the Blessed One that with the destruction of the three fetters, he is a stream-enterer, not liable to states of suffering, fixed in destiny, with full awakening as his destination.’ And that venerable one was either seen by him personally or heard of by report: ‘That venerable one was of such virtue, he was of such character ... he was of such wisdom ... he dwelled like this ... he was liberated in such a way.’ Recollecting his faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, he applies his mind on that goal. In this way, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhu dwells in comfort.

Here, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhunī hears: ‘The bhikkhunī named so-and-so has died; she was declared by the Blessed One to have been established in final knowledge.’ And that sister was either seen by her personally or heard of by report: ‘That sister was of such virtue, she was of such character, she was of such wisdom, she dwelled like this, she was liberated in such a way.’ Recollecting her faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, she applies her mind on that goal. In this way, Anuruddha and friends, a bhikkhunī dwells in comfort.

Here, Anuruddha and friends, a female lay follower hears: ‘The female lay follower named so-and-so has died; she was declared by the Blessed One that with the destruction of the three fetters, she is a stream-enterer, not liable to states of suffering, fixed in destiny, with full awakening as her destination.’ And that sister was either seen by her personally or heard of by report: ‘That sister was of such virtue, she was of such character, she was of such wisdom, she dwelled like this, she was liberated in such a way.’ Recollecting her faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, she applies her mind on that goal. Thus, Anuruddha and friends, the Tathāgata does not declare the rebirth of disciples who have passed away and died, saying, ‘This one was reborn there; that one was reborn there,’ for the purpose of deceiving people, nor for the purpose of winning favor with people, nor for the sake of acquisitions, respect, and popularity, nor with the thought, ‘Let people know me thus.’ But, Anuruddha and friends, there are clansmen with faith who experience great inspiration and are full of joy. The Blessed One said this. The venerable Anuruddha was delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words.
