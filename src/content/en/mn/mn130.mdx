Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park. There, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus.”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

#### The Simile of the Two Houses

“Just as if there were two houses with doors, and a man with good eyesight standing between them could see people coming into and going out of the houses, walking around and roaming inside;

in the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| with the |divine eye::the faculty of clairvoyance, the ability to see beyond the ordinary human range [dibbacakkhu]|, purified and surpassing human vision, I see beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in fortunate and unfortunate destinations—and I understand how beings fare according to their kamma: ‘These beings, who engaged in good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct, who did not revile the Noble Ones, held right view, and undertook actions under the influence of right view—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen in a good destination, a heavenly world. Or these beings, who engaged in good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct, who did not revile the Noble Ones, held right view, and undertook actions under the influence of right view—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen among humans. These beings, who engaged in bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct, who reviled the Noble Ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions under the influence of wrong view—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen in the realm of ghosts. Or these beings, who engaged in bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct, who reviled the Noble Ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions under the influence of wrong view—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen in the animal realm. Or these beings, who engaged in bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct, who reviled the Noble Ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions under the influence of wrong view—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell.’

|Bhikkhus,::::| then the hell wardens seize that person by both arms and present them to |King Yama::lord of the underworld, who acts as a moral examiner and questions the recently deceased about their failure to heed the divine messengers—aging, illness, and death. He reminds beings that they alone are responsible for their past deeds [yama]|, saying: ‘This person, your majesty, did not respect his mother, did not respect his father, did not respect ascetics, did not respect the |Brahmans::a title used for an Arahant, an awakened being [brahmañña]|, and did not respect the elders in his family. May your majesty impose due punishment on him.’

#### The Five Divine Messengers

1.) Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama cross-questions, closely examines, and admonishes that man about the first divine messenger: ‘Good man, did you not see the first divine messenger that appeared among human beings?’

He replies: ‘No, venerable sir, I did not see.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did you never see among human beings a tender young infant, lying on their back, lying in their own urine and excrement?’

And he replies: ‘Yes, venerable sir, I have seen.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you, an intelligent and mature person—I too am subject to birth, I am not exempt from birth. Let me now do good by body, speech, and mind?’

And he replies: ‘I was unable, venerable sir. I was negligent, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, due to negligence, you did not do good by body, speech, or mind. Surely, they will treat you in a way that fits your negligence. That bad kamma of yours was not done by your mother or father, nor by your brother or sister, nor by your friends and companions, nor by your relatives and family members, nor by ascetics and brahmins, nor by the deities. But rather, you were the one who did that bad kamma, and you yourself will have to experience its result.’

2.) Then, |bhikkhus,::::| after having cross-questioned, closely examined, and admonished him regarding the first divine messenger, King Yama cross-questions, closely examines, and admonishes him about the second divine messenger, saying: ‘Good man, did you not see the second divine messenger that appeared among human beings?’

He replies: ‘No, venerable sir, I did not see.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did you never see among human beings a woman or a man, frail, bent like a |rafter::roof bracket [gopānasivaṅka]|, hunched over, leaning on a staff for support, shaking, walking with difficulty, youth gone, with broken teeth, with grey and scanty hair or bald, with wrinkled skin and limbs marked by aging spots?’

And he replies: ‘Yes, venerable sir, I have seen.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you, an intelligent and mature person—I too am subject to aging, I am not exempt from growing old. Let me now do good by body, speech, and mind?’

And he replies: ‘I was unable, venerable sir. I was negligent, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, due to negligence, you did not do good by body, speech, or mind. Surely, they will treat you in a way that fits your negligence. That bad kamma of yours was not done by your mother or father, nor by your brother or sister, nor by your friends and companions, nor by your relatives and family members, nor by ascetics and brahmins, nor by the deities. But rather, you were the one who did that bad kamma, and you yourself will have to experience its result.’

3.) Then, |bhikkhus,::::| after having cross-questioned, closely examined, and admonished him regarding the second divine messenger, King Yama cross-questions, closely examines, and admonishes him about the third divine messenger, saying: ‘Good man, did you not see the third divine messenger that appeared among human beings?’

And he replies: ‘No, venerable sir, I did not see.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did you never see among human beings a woman or a man, sick, afflicted, gravely ill, lying in their own urine and excrement, unable to rise on their own, being lifted by others, and being laid down by others?’

And he replies: ‘Yes, venerable sir, I have seen.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you, an intelligent and mature person—I too am subject to illness, I am not exempt from illness. Let me now do good by body, speech, and mind?’

And he replies: ‘I was unable, venerable sir. I was negligent, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, due to negligence, you did not do good by body, speech, or mind. Surely, they will treat you in a way that fits your negligence. That bad kamma of yours was not done by your mother or father, nor by your brother or sister, nor by your friends and companions, nor by your relatives and family members, nor by ascetics and brahmins, nor by the deities. But rather, you were the one who did that bad kamma, and you yourself will have to experience its result.’

4.) Then, |bhikkhus,::::| after having cross-questioned, closely examined, and admonished him regarding the third divine messenger, King Yama cross-questions, closely examines, and admonishes him about the fourth divine messenger, saying: ‘Good man, did you not see the fourth divine messenger that appeared among human beings?’

And he replies: ‘No, venerable sir, I did not see.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did you never see among human beings kings who, having caught a thief, a criminal, subject him to various punishments—beating him with whips, beating him with canes, beating him with short sticks; cutting off hands, cutting off feet, cutting off both hands and feet; cutting off ears, cutting off nose, cutting off both ears and nose; subjecting him to the ‘sour pot’, the ‘bald shave’, ‘Rāhu’s mouth’, the ‘fiery wreath’, the ‘blazing hand’, the ‘grass twist’, the ‘bark dress’, the ‘black antelope’, the ‘meat hook’, the ‘silver coins’, ‘caustic peeling’, the ‘spinning bar’, the ‘straw chair’; dousing him with hot oil, having him devoured by dogs, impaling him alive on stakes, and cutting off his head with a sword?’

And he replies: ‘Yes, venerable sir, I have seen.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you, an intelligent and mature person—Those who commit bad deeds are subjected to such various punishments in this very life, how much more so in the next life? Let me now do good by body, speech, and mind?’

And he replies: ‘I was unable, venerable sir. I was negligent, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, due to negligence, you did not do good by body, speech, or mind. Surely, they will treat you in a way that fits your negligence. That bad kamma of yours was not done by your mother or father, nor by your brother or sister, nor by your friends and companions, nor by your relatives and family members, nor by ascetics and brahmins, nor by the deities. But rather, you were the one who did that bad kamma, and you yourself will have to experience its result.’

5.) Then, |bhikkhus,::::| after having cross-questioned, closely examined, and admonished him regarding the fourth divine messenger, King Yama cross-questions, closely examines, and admonishes him about the fifth divine messenger, saying: ‘Good man, did you not see the fifth divine messenger that appeared among human beings?’

And he replies: ‘No, venerable sir, I did not see.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did you never see among human beings a woman or a man who had been dead for one day, for two days, or for three days—bloated, discolored, and festering?’

And he replies: ‘Yes, venerable sir, I have seen.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you, an intelligent and mature person—I too am subject to death, I am not exempt from death. Let me now do good by body, speech, and mind?’

And he replies: ‘I was unable, venerable sir. I was negligent, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, due to negligence, you did not do good by body, speech, or mind. Surely, they will treat you in a way that fits your negligence. That bad kamma of yours was not done by your mother or father, nor by your brother or sister, nor by your friends and companions, nor by your relatives and family members, nor by ascetics and brahmins, nor by the deities. But rather, you were the one who did that bad kamma, and you yourself will have to experience its result.’

When|, bhikkhus,::::| King Yama has cross-questioned, closely examined, and admonished him regarding the fifth divine messenger, he falls silent.

#### The Torments of Hell

Then the wardens of hell torture him with the fivefold transfixing. They drive a red-hot iron stake through one hand and another red-hot iron stake through the other hand; they drive a red-hot iron stake through one foot and another red-hot iron stake through the other foot; they drive a red-hot iron stake through the middle of his chest. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell lay him down and hack him apart with machetes. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell seize him, suspend him upside down, and hack him apart with axes ...

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell harness him to a chariot and drive him back and forth across a blazing, fully ignited, and glowing ground ...

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell force him to ascend and descend a massive mountain of burning, fully ignited, and blazing embers ...

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell seize him, suspend him upside down, and plunge him into a boiling copper cauldron, blazing, fully ignited, and glowing. There, as foam bubbles up, he is boiled and suffers, rising up once, sinking down once, and moving sideways once. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

#### The Great Hell and the Neighboring Hells

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell cast him into the great hell. Now, |bhikkhus,::::| as to that great hell—

It is rectangular, with four doors,
and is divided into separate compartments;
it is surrounded by iron fortification,
and shut in with an iron roof.

Its ground is made of iron,
blazing and infused with fire.
It extends for a hundred |yojanas::yojana is a unit of distance used in ancient India, ranging from 3.5 to 15 km [yojana]| in all directions,
which it ever covers pervasively.

Now, |bhikkhus,::::| in that great hell, flames shoot up from the eastern wall and crash against the western wall; flames shoot up from the western wall and crash against the eastern wall; flames shoot up from the northern wall and crash against the southern wall; flames shoot up from the southern wall and crash against the northern wall; flames shoot up from below and crash against the top; flames shoot down from above and crash against the bottom. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

There comes a time|, bhikkhus,::::| once in a while, after the passage of a long period, when the eastern door of that great hell is opened. He runs toward it with swift speed. As he runs with swift speed, his outer skin burns, his inner skin burns, his flesh burns, his sinews burn, and even his bones smoke; when he is lifted out, it becomes just the same again. But when|, bhikkhus,::::| he has almost reached it, that door is slammed shut. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

There comes a time|, bhikkhus,::::| once in a while, after the passage of a long period, when the western door of that great hell is opened ... when the northern door is opened ... when the southern door is opened. He runs toward it with swift speed. As he runs with swift speed, his outer skin burns, his inner skin burns, his flesh burns, his sinews burn, and even his bones smoke; when he is lifted out, it becomes just the same again. But when|, bhikkhus,::::| he has almost reached it, that door is slammed shut. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

There comes a time|, bhikkhus,::::| once in a while, after the passage of a long period, when the eastern door of that great hell is opened. He runs toward it with swift speed. As he runs with swift speed, his outer skin burns, his inner skin burns, his flesh burns, his sinews burn, and even his bones smoke; when he is lifted out, it becomes just the same again. And he escapes through that door.

Immediately adjacent to that great hell|, bhikkhus,::::| is the vast Excrement Hell. He falls into it. In that Excrement Hell, needle-mouthed creatures pierce his outer skin; having pierced the outer skin, they pierce the inner skin; having pierced the inner skin, they pierce the flesh; having pierced the flesh, they pierce the sinews; having pierced the sinews, they pierce the bones; having pierced the bones, they devour the bone marrow. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Immediately adjacent to that Excrement Hell|, bhikkhus,::::| is the vast Hot Ashes Hell. He falls into it. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Immediately adjacent to that Hot Ashes Hell|, bhikkhus,::::| is the vast Silk-Cotton Forest, soaring a yojana high, with sixteen-finger-length thorns, blazing, fully ignited, and glowing. There they force him to climb up and climb down. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Immediately adjacent to that Silk-Cotton Forest|, bhikkhus,::::| is the vast Sword-Leaf Forest. He enters it. The leaves stirred by the wind fall and cut off his hands, cut off his feet, cut off both his hands and feet; cut off his ears, cut off his nose, cut off both his ears and nose. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Immediately adjacent to that Sword-Leaf Forest|, bhikkhus,::::| is the vast Caustic Water River. He falls into it. There he is swept downstream, swept upstream, and swept both downstream and upstream. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell pull him out with a fishhook, place him on dry ground, and say to him: ‘Good man, what do you desire?’

He replies: ‘I am hungry, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell force open his mouth with a red-hot iron spike, blazing, fully ignited, and glowing, and throw into his mouth a red-hot copper ball, blazing, fully ignited, and glowing. It burns his lips, burns his mouth, burns his throat, burns his chest, and taking along his intestines and mesentery, passes out from below. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell say to him: ‘Good man, what do you desire?’

He replies: ‘I am thirsty, venerable sir.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell force open his mouth with a red-hot iron spike, blazing, fully ignited, and glowing, and pour into his mouth molten bronze, blazing, fully ignited, and glowing. It burns his lips, burns his mouth, burns his throat, burns his chest, and taking along his intestines and mesentery, passes out from below. There he experiences painful, agonizing, and piercing feelings, yet he does not die so long as that bad kamma has not been exhausted.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the wardens of hell cast him back into the great hell.

#### The Reflection of King Yama

|Bhikkhus,::::| once in the past, this thought occurred to King Yama: ‘Those who commit evil deeds in the world are subjected to various forms of punishment. Oh, if only I could obtain human birth, and if a |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One were to arise in the world! Then I would associate closely with that Blessed One, and he would teach me the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, and I would come to understand his Dhamma.’

|Bhikkhus,::::| I do not say this having heard it from another ascetic or brahmin. But rather, what I have known for myself, seen for myself, and realized for myself, that is what I declare.”

That is what the Blessed One said. Having said this, the Fortunate One, the Teacher, further said this:

“Though warned by the divine messengers,
those people who remain negligent;
they |sorrow::grieve [socati]| for a long time,
having fared on to a lower realm.

But those who, when warned by the divine messengers,
are virtuous and noble here;
who do not become negligent,
in regard to the noble Dhamma—

Who, seeing the danger in |clinging::grasping, acquiring, appropriating, taking possession, identifying; fuel for fire; lit. taking near [upādāna]|,
which leads to birth and death;
are liberated by non-clinging,
bringing an end to birth and death.

Those who have attained security are at ease,
|completely calmed in this very life::perfectly extinguished here and now [diṭṭhadhammābhinibbuta]|;
having overcome all |hostility::hatred, ill will, animosity, enmity [vera]| and fear,
they have transcended all |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|.”
