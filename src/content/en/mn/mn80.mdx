Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

#### The Supreme Beauty

Then the wanderer Vekhanasa approached the Blessed One; having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One. After the exchange of a courteous and polite conversation, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, the wanderer Vekhanasa uttered this inspired utterance in the presence of the Blessed One: “This is the supreme beauty! This is the supreme beauty!”

“Why, |Kaccāna::family name; lit. descendant of Kati [kaccāna]|, do you say: ‘This is the supreme beauty! This is the supreme beauty!’? What, Kaccāna, is that supreme beauty?”

“Sir Gotama, that beauty than which there is no other beauty more excellent or more sublime is the supreme beauty.”

“And what, Kaccāna, is that beauty than which there is no other beauty more excellent or more sublime?”

“Sir Gotama, that beauty than which there is no other beauty more excellent or more sublime is the supreme beauty.”

“You could go on like this for a long time, Kaccāna: you say, ‘Sir Gotama, that beauty than which there is no other beauty more excellent or more sublime is the supreme beauty,’ yet you do not specify what that beauty is.

Suppose, Kaccāna, a man were to say: ‘I desire and long for the |most beautiful woman of the country::the finest woman of the land [janapadakalyāṇī]|.’

They might say to him: ‘Good man, that most beautiful woman of the country whom you desire and long for: do you know whether she is an aristocrat, a brahmin, a merchant, or a worker?’

Being questioned thus, he would say: ‘No.’

They might say to him: ‘Good man, that most beautiful woman of the country whom you desire and long for: do you know her name, or her clan, or whether she is tall, short, or of medium height, or whether she is dark, brown, or golden-skinned, or which village, town, or city she lives in?’

Being questioned thus, he would say: ‘No.’

They might say to him: ‘Good man, do you desire and long for someone whom you do not know and do not see?’

Being questioned thus, he would say: ‘Yes.’

What do you think, Kaccāna: this being the case, does not that man’s statement turn out to be ungrounded?”

“Certainly, sir Gotama, this being the case, that man’s statement turns out to be ungrounded.”

“In the same way, Kaccāna, you say: ‘Sir Gotama, that beauty than which there is no other beauty more excellent or more sublime is the supreme beauty,’ yet you do not specify what that beauty is.”

#### The Berly Gem and the Graded Radiance

“Just as, sir Gotama, a beautiful beryl gem of purest water, eight-faceted, well cut, clear and transparent, luminous, and of excellent workmanship, laid on a crimson blanket, shines, glows, and radiates: of such beauty is the self, healthy after death.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna: which of these two kinds of beauty is more excellent and more sublime: that beautiful beryl gem of purest water, eight-faceted, well cut, clear and transparent, luminous, and of excellent workmanship, laid on a crimson blanket, that shines, glows, and radiates, or a firefly in the dark of night?”

“This firefly in the dark of night, sir Gotama: of these two kinds of beauty, this is more excellent and more sublime.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna: which of these two kinds of beauty is more excellent and more sublime: a firefly in the dark of night, or an oil lamp in the dark of night?”

“This oil lamp in the dark of night, sir Gotama: of these two kinds of beauty, this is more excellent and more sublime.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna: which of these two kinds of beauty is more excellent and more sublime: an oil lamp in the dark of night, or a great bonfire in the dark of night?”

“This great bonfire in the dark of night, sir Gotama: of these two kinds of beauty, this is more excellent and more sublime.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna: which of these two kinds of beauty is more excellent and more sublime: a great bonfire in the dark of night, or the morning star at the break of dawn in a clear, cloudless sky?”

“This morning star at the break of dawn in a clear, cloudless sky, sir Gotama: of these two kinds of beauty, this is more excellent and more sublime.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna: which of these two kinds of beauty is more excellent and more sublime: the morning star at the break of dawn in a clear, cloudless sky, or the moon around midnight on the fifteenth-day Uposatha in a clear, cloudless sky?”

“This moon around midnight on the fifteenth-day Uposatha in a clear, cloudless sky, sir Gotama: of these two kinds of beauty, this is more excellent and more sublime.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna: which of these two kinds of beauty is more excellent and more sublime: the moon around midnight on the fifteenth-day Uposatha in a clear, cloudless sky, or the sun around midday in autumn, in the last month of the rainy season, in a clear, cloudless sky?”

“This sun around midday in autumn, in the last month of the rainy season, in a clear, cloudless sky, sir Gotama: of these two kinds of beauty, this is more excellent and more sublime.”

“Beyond this, Kaccāna, there are many deities, far more numerous than these, on whom the radiance of this sun and moon makes no impression, yet I know them. Even so, I do not say: ‘There is no other beauty more excellent or more sublime than this beauty.’ Yet you, Kaccāna, say of a beauty inferior and viler than that of a firefly: ‘This is the supreme beauty,’ and you do not specify what that beauty is.

#### The Five Cords of Sensual Pleasure

There are these five cords of sensual pleasure, Kaccāna. What five? |Forms::visible objects such as beautiful sights, faces, expressions, art, ornaments, possessions, status symbols, admired appearances, enticing scenery, or objects of desire and attachment [rūpe]| cognizable by the eye that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and |enticing::arousing, tantalizing [rajanīya]|; |sounds::auditory experiences such as speech, music, praise, blame, ambient noise, verbal expression, or melodic tones—any sound that can stir emotion, craving for praise, aesthetic delight, or attachment to communication and identity [saddā]| cognizable by the ear <collapse>that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing;</collapse> |odors::smells and fragrances experienced through the nose, including perfumes, flowers, food aromas, earth after rain, incense, or even stench—any olfactory experience that may evoke craving, aversion, nostalgia, comfort, or sensual pleasure [gandhā]| cognizable by the nose <collapse>that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing;</collapse> |tastes::flavors experienced through the tongue such as sweetness, bitterness, sourness, saltiness, spiciness, richness, or subtle tastes like umami or astringency—any gustatory experience that can become an object of craving, indulgence, comfort, or sensory delight [rasā]| cognizable by the tongue <collapse>that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing;</collapse> |tangible objects::tangible contact such as the feel of skin, warmth, softness, intimacy, physical affection, or sensations like massage, breath, water, air, pressure—anything felt through the body that can become an object of desire, arousal, comfort, or emotional attachment [phoṭṭhabba]| cognizable by the body that are desirable, lovely, agreeable, pleasing, sensual, and enticing. These, Kaccāna, are the five cords of sensual pleasure.

Whatever |happiness::contentment, ease, pleasant abiding [sukha]| and |pleasure::a positive state of mind; agreeable feeling born of mental contact, mental happiness [somanassa]| arises dependent on the five cords of sensual pleasure, Kaccāna, is called sensual pleasure. Thus sensual pleasure arises through sensual objects, but there is a pleasure higher than sensual pleasure, and that is declared to be the supreme pleasure among them.”

When this was said, the wanderer Vekhanasa said to the Blessed One: “It is wonderful, sir Gotama, it is marvelous, sir Gotama! How well this was said by sir Gotama: ‘Thus sensual pleasure arises through sensual objects, but there is a pleasure higher than sensual pleasure, and that is declared to be the supreme pleasure among them.’ Indeed, sir Gotama, thus sensual pleasure arises through sensual objects, but there is a pleasure higher than sensual pleasure, and that is declared to be the supreme pleasure among them.”

“It is difficult for you to understand this, Kaccāna, when you hold another view, approve of another teaching, practice another discipline, and follow another teacher: what sensual objects are, or what sensual pleasure is, or what the pleasure higher than sensual pleasure is. Those bhikkhus, Kaccāna, who are |Arahants::fully awakened ones, free from all mental defilements; worthy of offerings and veneration; also an epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, |whose taints have worn away::who is awakened through the complete exhaustion of the taints of sensuality, becoming, and ignorance; an epithet for an Arahant [khīṇāsava]|, who have fulfilled the spiritual life, who have done what had to be done, having put down the burden, having achieved the highest goal, |having exhausted the fetter of existence::who has worn away the bonds of continued conditional existence, i.e. the karmically conditioned mode of being that leads to future rebirth [parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojana]|, and |who are freed through complete comprehension::one who is liberated by perfect understanding, epithet of an Arahant [sammadaññāvimutta]|: they would understand this: what sensual objects are, or what sensual pleasure is, or what the pleasure higher than sensual pleasure is.”

When this was said, the wanderer Vekhanasa, angry and displeased, deriding the Blessed One, disparaging the Blessed One, spoke directly against the Blessed One, thinking, ‘The ascetic Gotama will be defeated!’ he said to the Blessed One: “In this very way, there are some ascetics and brahmins here who, not knowing the past nor seeing the future, nevertheless claim: ‘We clearly know: “Birth is ended, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no further for this state of being.”’ Their statement turns out to be ridiculous, mere words, empty and hollow.”

“Those ascetics and brahmins, Kaccāna, who not knowing the past nor seeing the future, claim: ‘We clearly know: “Birth is ended, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no further for this state of being,”’ are legitimately refuted by that very fact. Nevertheless, Kaccāna, let the past be, let the future be. Let an intelligent person come: honest, sincere, and upright. I shall instruct him, I shall teach the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|. Practicing as instructed, before long he will know for himself, see for himself: ‘So it is in this way that there is right release from the bond, that is, the bond of |ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]|.’ Suppose, Kaccāna, there were a young, helpless infant lying on his back, tied with five bonds: strings around both hands, both feet, and the neck as the fifth. With his growth and the maturation of his faculties, those bonds would be removed. He would know, ‘I am released,’ and there would be no more bond.

“In the same way, Kaccāna, let an intelligent person come: honest, sincere, and upright. I shall instruct him, I shall teach the Dhamma. Practicing as instructed, before long he will know for himself, see for himself: ‘So it is in this way that there is right release from the bond, that is, the bond of ignorance.’”

When this was said, the wanderer Vekhanasa said to the Blessed One: “Magnificent, sir Gotama! <collapse>Magnificent, sir Gotama! Just as if one were to set upright what had been overturned, or to reveal what was hidden, or to point out the way to one who had gone astray, or to hold an oil lamp in the dark so that those with eyes might see forms: in the same way has the Dhamma been made clear in many ways by sir Gotama. I go to the Blessed One for refuge, and to the Dhamma, and to the Sangha of monks.</collapse> May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forth for life.”
