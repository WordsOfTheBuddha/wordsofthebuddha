Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Uruññā::name of a town in Kosala [uruññā]|, in the deer park at |Kaṇṇakatthala::name of a park in Ujuññā [kaṇṇakatthala]|.

At that time King Pasenadi of Kosala had arrived at Uruññā on some business. Then King Pasenadi of Kosala addressed a certain man: “Come, good man, go to the Blessed One. When you have approached him, bow with your head at the Blessed One’s feet on my behalf, and ask whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably, saying: ‘Venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala bows with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and asks whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably.’ And say this: ‘Today, venerable sir, after his meal, |having eaten his breakfast::after taking the morning meal [bhuttapātarāsa]|, King Pasenadi of Kosala will come to see the Blessed One.’”

“Yes, lord,” that man replied to King Pasenadi of Kosala. He approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, that man said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala bows with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and asks whether you are well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably. And he says this: ‘Today, venerable sir, after his meal, having eaten his breakfast, King Pasenadi of Kosala will come to see the Blessed One.’”

The sisters |Somā::name of a laywoman, sister of Pasenadi's queen [somā]| and |Sakulā::name of a laywoman, sister of Pasenadi's queen [sakulā]| heard: “Today, it seems, after his meal, having eaten his breakfast, King Pasenadi of Kosala will go to see the Blessed One.” Then the sisters Somā and Sakulā approached King Pasenadi of Kosala while his meal was being served and said to him: “Then, great king, bow with your head at the Blessed One’s feet on our behalf too, and ask whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably, saying: ‘Venerable sir, the sisters Somā and Sakulā bow with their heads at the Blessed One’s feet, and ask whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably.’”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala, after his meal, having eaten his breakfast, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, the sisters Somā and Sakulā bow with their heads at the Blessed One’s feet, and ask whether you are well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably.”

“Great king, could the sisters Somā and Sakulā not find another messenger?”

“Venerable sir, the sisters Somā and Sakulā heard: ‘Today, it seems, after his meal, having eaten his breakfast, King Pasenadi of Kosala will go to see the Blessed One.’ Then, venerable sir, the sisters Somā and Sakulā approached me while my meal was being served and said to me: ‘Then, great king, bow with your head at the Blessed One’s feet on our behalf too, and ask whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably—the sisters Somā and Sakulā bow with their heads at the Blessed One’s feet, and ask whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably.’”

“May the sisters Somā and Sakulā be happy, great king.”

#### Omniscience

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the Blessed One: “I have heard this, venerable sir: ‘The ascetic Gotama says this: “There is no ascetic or brahmin who is |omniscient::all-knowing, with complete understanding [sabbaññū]| and |all-seeing::who perceives everything [sabbadassāvī]|, who can claim to have complete |knowledge and vision::understanding and insight; (comm) gaining knowledge and vision via divine eye [ñāṇadassana]|; such a thing is impossible.”’ Venerable sir, those who say this: ‘The ascetic Gotama says this: “There is no ascetic or brahmin who is omniscient and all-seeing, who can claim to have complete knowledge and vision; such a thing is impossible”’—are they stating what has been said by the Blessed One, and are not misrepresenting the Blessed One with what is untrue? Do they explain in accordance with the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, and does no reasonable consequence of their statement afford grounds for criticism?”

“Great king, those who say: ‘The ascetic Gotama says: “There is no ascetic or brahmin who is omniscient and all-seeing, who can claim to have complete knowledge and vision; such a thing is impossible”’—they do not speak what has been said by me, but misrepresent me with what is false and untrue.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala addressed |General::leader of the army [senāpati]| |Viṭaṭūbha::name of king Pasenadi's general [viṭaṭūbha]|: “General, who introduced this topic of conversation into the royal court?”

“Great king, it was the brahmin |Sañjaya::name of a Brahman; lit. victory [sañjaya]| of the Ākāsa clan.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala addressed a certain man: “Come, good man, summon the brahmin Sañjaya of the Ākāsa clan in my name: ‘Sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala summons you.’”

“Yes, lord,” that man replied to King Pasenadi of Kosala. He approached the brahmin Sañjaya of the Ākāsa clan and said to him: “Sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala summons you.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the Blessed One: “Could it be, venerable sir, that the Blessed One spoke in reference to something else, but that person understood it differently? In what way does the Blessed One recall making that statement?”

“Great king, I recall making the statement in this way: ‘There is no ascetic or brahmin who will know all, see all at once; such a thing is impossible.’”

“The Blessed One speaks with reason, venerable sir; the Blessed One speaks with good reason: ‘There is no ascetic or brahmin who will know all, see all at once; such a thing is impossible.’

“There are these four castes, venerable sir: nobles, brahmins, merchants, and workers. Between these four castes, venerable sir, is there any distinction, any difference?”

“There are these four castes, great king: nobles, brahmins, merchants, and workers. Of these four castes, great king, two castes are declared to be foremost—nobles and brahmins—in terms of paying homage, rising up in respect, saluting with joined palms, and proper courtesies.”

“I am not asking the Blessed One about the present life, venerable sir; I am asking the Blessed One about what concerns the next life. There are these four castes, venerable sir: nobles, brahmins, merchants, and workers. Between these four castes, is there any distinction, any difference?”

#### The Five Factors of Striving

“Great king, there are these five factors of striving. What five?

1.) Here, great king, a bhikkhu has faith; he has conviction in the awakening of the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, acknowledging: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’

2.) He is without affliction and rarely ill, endowed with a balanced digestion that is neither too cold nor too hot, but moderate and suitable for striving.

3.) He is neither deceitful nor hypocritical, revealing himself as he truly is to the Teacher or to wise spiritual companions.

4.) He dwells with energy aroused for abandoning |unwholesome qualities::unskillful actions, bad habits [akusaladhammā]| and acquiring |wholesome qualities::skillful actions, good habits [kusaladhammā]|; he is steadfast, firm in his effort, and does not shirk responsibility with regard to wholesome qualities.

5.) He is wise, endowed with |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]| that discerns the |arising and passing away::appearance and disappearance, formation and dissolution [udayatthagāmī]|, which is noble and penetrative, |leading to the complete end of suffering::leading to extinction of stress, leading to gradual and complete wearing away of discontentment [dukkhakkhayagāmī]|. These, great king, are the five factors of striving.

If people of these four castes—nobles, brahmins, merchants, and workers—were endowed with these five factors of striving, it would lead to their welfare and happiness for a long time.”

“There are these four castes, venerable sir: nobles, brahmins, merchants, and workers. If they were endowed with these five factors of striving, would there be any distinction, any difference among them?”

“In that case, great king, I declare a difference in striving. Just as, great king, there might be two tamable elephants, horses, or oxen that are well tamed and well trained, and two tamable elephants, horses, or oxen that are untamed and untrained. What do you think, great king?  Would those two tameable elephants, horses, or oxen that are well tamed and well trained, being tamed, be fit for the work of the tamed and reach the stage of the tamed?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“But would those two tamable elephants, horses, or oxen that are untamed and untrained, being untamed, be fit for the work of the tamed and reach the stage of the tamed, just like those two that are well tamed and well trained?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“In the same way, great king, that which is to be attained by one who has faith, is rarely ill, sincere, honest, |energetic::with energy aroused, with initiative [āraddhavīriya]|, and |wise::discerning, percipient; lit. having the wisdom quality [paññavant]|—that one who lacks faith, is often ill, deceitful, hypocritical, |lazy::procrastinating, inactive person, indolent [kusīta]|, and |undiscerning::without wisdom; lit. bad understanding [duppañña]| would attain it: such a thing is impossible.”

“The Blessed One speaks with reason, venerable sir; the Blessed One speaks with good reason. There are these four castes, venerable sir: nobles, brahmins, merchants, and workers. If they were endowed with these five factors of striving and they exerted themselves rightly, would there be any distinction, any difference among them?”

“In that case, great king, I declare no difference whatsoever, that is, between |liberation::release, deliverance, freedom, emancipation [vimutti]| and liberation. Suppose, great king, a person took dry teak wood and kindled a fire, making heat appear. And another person took dry sal wood and kindled a fire, making heat appear. And another person took dry mango wood and kindled a fire, making heat appear. And another person took dry fig wood and kindled a fire, making heat appear. What do you think, great king? Would there be any difference between these fires produced from different kinds of wood, in flame from flame, color from color, or radiance from radiance?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“In the same way, great king, when that radiance is churned out by energy and produced by striving, I declare no difference whatsoever, that is, between liberation and liberation.”

#### Deities Who Return and Who Do Not

“The Blessed One speaks with reason, venerable sir; the Blessed One speaks with good reason. But, venerable sir, do deities exist?”

“Why do you ask, great king: ‘But, venerable sir, do deities exist?’”

“Venerable sir, whether those deities return to this state of existence or do not return to this state of existence?”

“Those deities who are subject to affliction, great king, return to this state of existence; those deities who are free from affliction do not return to this state of existence.”

When this was said, General Viṭaṭūbha said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, can those deities who are subject to affliction and return to this state of existence displace or banish those deities who are free from affliction and do not return to this state of existence from their place?”

Then the venerable Ānanda thought: “This General Viṭaṭūbha is the son of King Pasenadi of Kosala; I am a son of the Blessed One. Now is the time for son to confer with son.” Then the venerable Ānanda addressed General Viṭaṭūbha: “Then, General, I will ask you a question in return about this matter. Answer it as you see fit. What do you think, General? As far as the realm of King Pasenadi of Kosala extends, where King Pasenadi of Kosala exercises sovereign rule, can King Pasenadi of Kosala displace or banish from that place any ascetic or brahmin, whether virtuous or unvirtuous, whether practicing the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| or not practicing the spiritual life?”

“Sir, as far as the realm of King Pasenadi of Kosala extends, where King Pasenadi exercises sovereign rule, King Pasenadi can displace or banish from that place an ascetic or brahmin, whether virtuous or unvirtuous, whether practicing the spiritual life or not practicing the spiritual life.”

“What do you think, General? As for the territory that is outside the extent of the realm of King Pasenadi of Kosala, where King Pasenadi does not exercise sovereign rule, can King Pasenadi displace or banish from that territory an ascetic or brahmin, whether virtuous or unvirtuous, whether practicing the spiritual life or not practicing the spiritual life?”

“Sir, as for the territory that is outside the extent of the realm of King Pasenadi of Kosala, where King Pasenadi does not exercise sovereign rule, King Pasenadi cannot displace or banish from that territory an ascetic or brahmin, whether virtuous or unvirtuous, whether practicing the spiritual life or not practicing the spiritual life.”

“What do you think, General? Have you heard of the gods of the Thirty-Three?”

“Yes, sir. I have heard of the gods of the Thirty-Three, and King Pasenadi of Kosala has heard of them too.”

“What do you think, General? Can King Pasenadi of Kosala displace or banish the gods of the Thirty-Three from their place?”

“Sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala cannot even see the gods of the Thirty-Three, so how could he displace or banish them from their place?”

“In the same way, General, those deities who are subject to affliction and return to this state of existence cannot even see those deities who are free from affliction and do not return to this state of existence, so how could they displace or banish them from their place?”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, what is the name of this bhikkhu?”

“His name is Ānanda, great king.”

#### Brahmās

“Joy (Ānanda) he is indeed! Joyful in appearance he is indeed! Venerable Ānanda speaks with reason, venerable sir; venerable Ānanda speaks with good reason. But, venerable sir, do |Brahmās::Gods; celestial beings residing in the Brahmā realms, often considered to be highly refined and long-lived deities. [brahmā]| exist?”

“Why do you ask, great king: ‘But, venerable sir, do Brahmās exist?’”

“Venerable sir, whether those Brahmās return to this state of existence or do not return to this state of existence?”

“Those Brahmās who are subject to affliction, great king, return to this state of existence; those Brahmās who are free from affliction do not return to this state of existence.”

Then a certain man said to King Pasenadi of Kosala: “Great king, the brahmin Sañjaya of the Ākāsa clan has arrived.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the brahmin Sañjaya of the Ākāsa clan: “Brahmin, who introduced this topic of conversation into the royal court?”

“Great king, it was General Viṭaṭūbha.”

But General Viṭaṭūbha said: “Great king, it was the brahmin Sañjaya of the Ākāsa clan.”

Then a certain man said to King Pasenadi of Kosala: “Great king, it is time to go.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, we asked the Blessed One about omniscience, and the Blessed One answered about omniscience; we approve of that and accept it, and we are pleased with it. We asked the Blessed One about the purification of the four castes, and the Blessed One answered about the purification of the four castes; we approve of that and accept it, and we are pleased with it. We asked the Blessed One about higher deities, and the Blessed One answered about higher deities; we approve of that and accept it, and we are pleased with it. We asked the Blessed One about higher Brahmās, and the Blessed One answered about higher Brahmās; we approve of that and accept it, and we are pleased with it. Whatever we asked the Blessed One, the Blessed One answered; we approve of that and accept it, and we are pleased with it. And now, venerable sir, we must depart; we are busy and have much to do.”

“You may proceed, Great King, whenever you think it is time.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala, having delighted in and approved of the Blessed One’s words, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him with his right side toward him, and departed.
