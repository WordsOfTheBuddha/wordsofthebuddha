Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Now on that occasion, about five hundred brahmins from various regions were residing in Sāvatthi on some business or other. Then those brahmins thought: “This ascetic Gotama declares purification for all four castes. Who can debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion?”

Now at that time, a young brahmin named Assalāyana was living in Sāvatthi: young, shaven-headed, sixteen years old, a master of the |three Vedas::the primary sacred scriptures of the Vedic religion: Ṛgveda, Sāmaveda, and Yajurveda [tiṇṇaṁ vedānaṁ]|, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, with historical narratives as the fifth; a reciter of the Vedas, a grammarian and linguistic scholar, fully versed in |natural philosophy::science of disputation; lit. extension of the world [lokāyata]| and the |auspicious marks of a great man::characteristics of an illustrious being [mahāpurisalakkhaṇa]|.

Then those brahmins thought: “This young brahmin Assalāyana is living in Sāvatthi: young, shaven-headed, sixteen years old, a master of the three Vedas<collapse>, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, with historical narratives as the fifth; a reciter of the Vedas, a grammarian and linguistic scholar, fully versed in natural philosophy and the auspicious marks of a great man.</collapse> He is capable of debating with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion.”

Then those brahmins went to the young brahmin Assalāyana and said to him: “Sir Assalāyana, this ascetic Gotama declares purification for all four castes. Let sir Assalāyana go and debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion.”

When this was said, the young brahmin Assalāyana said to those brahmins: “Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is a speaker of the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, and speakers of the Dhamma are difficult to debate with. I cannot debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion.”

A second time, those brahmins said to the young brahmin Assalāyana: “Sir Assalāyana, this ascetic Gotama declares purification for all four castes. Let sir Assalāyana go and debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion. For sir Assalāyana has lived the wandering life.” A second time, the young brahmin Assalāyana said to those brahmins: “Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is a speaker of the Dhamma, and speakers of the Dhamma are difficult to debate with. I cannot debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion.”

A third time, those brahmins said to the young brahmin Assalāyana: “Sir Assalāyana, this ascetic Gotama declares purification for all four castes. Let sir Assalāyana go and debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion. For sir Assalāyana has lived the wandering life. Do not, Master Assalāyana, be defeated without putting up a fight.”

When this was said, the young brahmin Assalāyana said to those brahmins: “Surely, sirs, I cannot prevail upon you. The ascetic Gotama is indeed a speaker of the Dhamma, and speakers of the Dhamma are difficult to debate with. I cannot debate with the ascetic Gotama on this assertion. Even so, at your word, sirs, I will go.”

Then the young brahmin Assalāyana, together with a large assembly of brahmins, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the young brahmin Assalāyana said to the Blessed One:

“Sir Gotama, the brahmins say this: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of |Brahmā::God, the first deity to be born at the beginning of a new cosmic cycle and whose lifespan lasts for the entire cycle [brahmā]|, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā.’ What does sir Gotama say about this?”

#### Brahmin Women Still Give Birth

“Yet, Assalāyana, the brahmin women are seen menstruating, pregnant, giving birth, and breast-feeding. Although they are born from a brahmin woman’s womb, they still say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā.’”

“Even though sir Gotama says this, still the brahmins think: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’”

#### Castes Reverse in the Borderlands

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Have you heard that in |Yona::Bactria; part of the Greek empire; modern day Afghanistan [yona]|, |Kamboja::an ancient kingdom in the area that is now northeastern Afghanistan and northern Pakistan [kamboja]|, and other border regions, there are only two castes: master and slave; and that having been a master, one becomes a slave, and having been a slave, one becomes a master?”

“Yes, sir, I have heard that in Yona, Kamboja, and other border regions, there are only two castes: master and slave; and that having been a master, one becomes a slave, and having been a slave, one becomes a master.”

“Here, Assalāyana, what strength do the brahmins have, what basis for confidence, when they say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā’?”

“Even though sir Gotama says this, still the brahmins think: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’”

#### Wrong Conduct Leads to Ruin in Every Caste

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Would only an aristocrat who kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, speaks falsely, speaks divisively, speaks harshly, chatters frivolously, is full of longing, has a mind of ill will, and holds wrong view, with the breakup of the body, after death, be reborn in a |state of loss::state of misery [apāya]|, a |bad destination::state of misery [duggati]|, a |realm of downfall::realm of misery [vinipāta]|, in |hell::a place of intense suffering, lit. no good fortune [niraya]|, but not a brahmin? Would a merchant, or a worker who kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, speaks falsely, speaks divisively, speaks harshly, chatters frivolously, is full of longing, has a mind of ill will, and holds wrong view, with the breakup of the body, after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a realm of downfall, in hell, but not a brahmin?”

“No, sir Gotama. For an aristocrat too, sir Gotama, who kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, speaks falsely, speaks divisively, speaks harshly, chatters frivolously, is full of longing, has a mind of ill will, and holds wrong view, with the breakup of the body, after death, would be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a realm of downfall, in hell. A brahmin too, sir Gotama... a merchant too, sir Gotama... a worker too, sir Gotama... indeed, anyone of all four castes who kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, speaks falsely, speaks divisively, speaks harshly, chatters frivolously, is full of longing, has a mind of ill will, and holds wrong view, with the breakup of the body, after death, would be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a realm of downfall, in hell.”

“Here, Assalāyana, what strength do the brahmins have, what basis for confidence, when they say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā’?”

“Even though sir Gotama says this, still the brahmins think: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’”

#### Virtue Leads to Heaven in Every Caste

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Would only a brahmin who abstains from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given, abstains from sexual misconduct, abstains from false speech, abstains from divisive speech, abstains from harsh speech, abstains from frivolous chatter, is |without intense craving::un-covetous, free of greed [anabhijjhālu]|, has |a benevolent mind::who have a non-malicious mind, free from ill will [abyāpannacittā]|, and holds |right view::correct view, seeing things as they are [sammādiṭṭhi]|, with the breakup of the body, after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a |heavenly world::a blissful realm, celestial world [sagga]|, but not an aristocrat, not a merchant, not a worker?”

“No, sir Gotama. For an aristocrat too, sir Gotama, who abstains from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given, abstains from sexual misconduct, abstains from false speech, abstains from divisive speech, abstains from harsh speech, abstains from frivolous chatter, is without intense craving, has a benevolent mind, and holds right view, with the breakup of the body, after death, would be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world. A brahmin too, sir Gotama... a merchant too, sir Gotama... a worker too, sir Gotama... indeed, anyone of all four castes who abstains from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given, abstains from sexual misconduct, abstains from false speech, abstains from divisive speech, abstains from harsh speech, abstains from frivolous chatter, is without intense craving, has a benevolent mind, and holds right view, with the breakup of the body, after death, would be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“Here, Assalāyana, what strength do the brahmins have, what basis for confidence, when they say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā’?”

“Even though sir Gotama says this, still the brahmins think: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’”

#### All Castes Can Develop Loving-Kindness

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Is only a brahmin capable of developing in this region a mind of loving-kindness, free of enmity and ill will, but not an aristocrat, not a merchant, not a worker?”

“No, sir Gotama. For an aristocrat too, sir Gotama, is capable of developing in this region a mind of loving-kindness, free of enmity and ill will; a brahmin too, sir Gotama... a merchant too, sir Gotama... a worker too, sir Gotama... indeed, anyone of all four castes is capable of developing in this region a mind of loving-kindness, free of enmity and ill will.”

“Here, Assalāyana, what strength do the brahmins have, what basis for confidence, when they say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā’?”

“Even though sir Gotama says this, still the brahmins think: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’”

#### All Castes Can Bathe Away Grime

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Is only a brahmin capable of taking soap and bath powder, going to a river, and washing away dirt and grime, but not an aristocrat, not a merchant, not a worker?”

“No, sir Gotama. For an aristocrat too, sir Gotama, is capable of taking soap and bath powder, going to a river, and washing away dirt and grime; a brahmin too, sir Gotama... a merchant too, sir Gotama... a worker too, sir Gotama... indeed, anyone of all four castes is capable of taking soap and bath powder, going to a river, and washing away dirt and grime.”

“Here, Assalāyana, what strength do the brahmins have, what basis for confidence, when they say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā’?”

#### The Simile of the Fire Sticks

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Suppose an anointed aristocratic king were to assemble a hundred men of varied births: ‘Come, sirs, let those born of an aristocratic clan, a brahmin clan, or a royal clan take an upper fire-stick made of teak, sāla, pine, sandalwood, or wild cherry, and kindle a fire, producing heat. And come, sirs, let those born of an outcast clan, a hunter clan, a reed-worker clan, a cartwright clan, or a scavenger clan take an upper fire-stick made from a dog’s feeding trough, a pig’s feeding trough, a dyer’s tub, or castor-wood, and kindle a fire, producing heat.’

What do you think, Assalāyana? Would only the fire kindled and heat produced by those born of an aristocratic clan, a brahmin clan, or a royal clan taking an upper fire-stick of teak, sāla, pine, sandalwood, or wild cherry have flames, color, and radiance, so that it could be used for the purposes of fire; while the fire kindled and heat produced by those born of an outcast clan, a hunter clan, a reed-worker clan, a cartwright clan, or a scavenger clan taking an upper fire-stick made from a dog’s feeding trough, a pig’s feeding trough, a dyer’s tub, or castor-wood have no flames, no color, and no radiance, so that it could not be used for the purposes of fire?”

“No, sir Gotama. For the fire kindled and heat produced by those born of an aristocratic clan, a brahmin clan, or a royal clan taking an upper fire-stick of teak, sāla, pine, sandalwood, or wild cherry would have flames, color, and radiance, and could be used for the purposes of fire; and the fire kindled and heat produced by those born of an outcast clan, a hunter clan, a reed-worker clan, a cartwright clan, or a scavenger clan taking an upper fire-stick made from a dog’s feeding trough, a pig’s feeding trough, a dyer’s tub, or castor-wood would also have flames, color, and radiance, and could be used for the purposes of fire. For every fire, sir Gotama, has flames, color, and radiance, and any fire can be used for the purposes of fire.”

“Here, Assalāyana, what strength do the brahmins have, what basis for confidence, when they say: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā’?”

“Even though sir Gotama says this, still the brahmins think: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’”

#### Mixed Descent and the Mule

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Suppose an aristocratic youth were to cohabit with a young brahmin woman, and through their cohabitation a son were born. Would that son born of an aristocratic youth and a young brahmin woman resemble both the mother and the father, and could he be called both an aristocrat and a brahmin?”

“That son born of an aristocratic youth and a young brahmin woman, sir Gotama, would resemble both the mother and the father, and he could be called both an aristocrat and a brahmin.”

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Suppose a brahmin youth were to cohabit with a young aristocratic woman, and through their cohabitation a son were born. Would that son born of a brahmin youth and a young aristocratic woman resemble both the mother and the father, and could he be called both an aristocrat and a brahmin?”

“That son born of a brahmin youth and a young aristocratic woman, sir Gotama, would resemble both the mother and the father, and he could be called both an aristocrat and a brahmin.”

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Suppose a mare were mated with a donkey, and through their mating a foal were born. Would that foal born of a mare and a donkey resemble both the mother and the father, and could it be called both a horse and a donkey?”

“No, sir Gotama, it is a crossbreed, a mule. Here, sir Gotama, I see a difference; but in the other cases, I see no difference.”

#### Virtue Outweighs Vedic Initiation

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Suppose there were two young brahmin brothers born of the same mother: one a student of the Vedas, initiated, and one not a student of the Vedas, uninitiated. Which one would the brahmins feed first at a funeral feast, an offering of rice boiled in a pot, a sacrifice, or a feast for guests?”

“The young brahmin who is a student of the Vedas, initiated, him the brahmins would feed first at a funeral feast, an offering of rice boiled in a pot, a sacrifice, or a feast for guests. For what, sir Gotama, given to one who is not a student of the Vedas, uninitiated, would be of great fruit?”

“What do you think, Assalāyana? Suppose there were two young brahmin brothers born of the same mother: one a student of the Vedas, initiated, but unprincipled and of a bad character; and one not a student of the Vedas, uninitiated, but virtuous and of good character. Which one would the brahmins feed first at a funeral feast, an offering of rice boiled in a pot, a sacrifice, or a feast for guests?”

“The young brahmin who is not a student of the Vedas, uninitiated, but virtuous and of good character, him the brahmins would feed first at a funeral feast, an offering of rice boiled in a pot, a sacrifice, or a feast for guests. For what, sir Gotama, given to one who is unprincipled and of a bad character, would be of great fruit?”

“Earlier, Assalāyana, you took your stand on birth; having left birth, you took your stand on Vedic hymns; having left Vedic hymns, you took your stand on ascetic discipline; having left ascetic discipline, you have come right back to the purification of the four castes that I declare.” When this was said, the young brahmin Assalāyana sat silent, dejected, with shoulders drooping and head down, reflecting, unable to respond.

Then the Blessed One, knowing that the young brahmin Assalāyana was silent, dejected, with shoulders drooping and head down, reflecting, and unable to respond, said this to the young brahmin Assalāyana:

### The Story of Seer Asita Devala

“Once in the past, Assalāyana, when seven brahmin seers were dwelling in leaf huts in a wilderness region, such a |harmful::injurious, bad, or evil. Encompasses the deceptively alluring that is ultimately detrimental or ruinous [pāpaka]| |misconception::theory, doctrine [diṭṭhigata]| arose in them: ‘Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.’

The seer Asita Devala heard, Assalāyana: ‘It is said that in seven brahmin seers dwelling in leaf huts in a wilderness region, such a harmful misconception has arisen: “Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior<collapse>; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā,</collapse> heirs of Brahmā.”’ Then, Assalāyana, the seer Asita Devala trimmed his hair and beard, dressed in crimson garments, put on scarlet footwear, took a staff made of gold, and appeared in the courtyard of the seven brahmin seers. Then, walking up and down in the courtyard of the seven brahmin seers, the seer Asita Devala said: ‘Well now, where have these master brahmin seers gone? Well now, where have these master brahmin seers gone?’

Then, Assalāyana, the seven brahmin seers thought: ‘Who is this fellow walking up and down in our courtyard like an outcast clown, saying: “Well now, where have these master brahmin seers gone? Well now, where have these master brahmin seers gone?” Let us curse him!’

Then, Assalāyana, the seven brahmin seers cursed the seer Asita Devala: ‘Become ashes, wretch! Become ashes, wretch!’ But the more the seven brahmin seers cursed the seer Asita Devala, the more handsome, attractive, and graceful the seer Asita Devala became.

Then, Assalāyana, the seven brahmin seers thought: ‘Our asceticism is in vain, our spiritual life is fruitless! For in the past, whomever we cursed saying, “Become ashes, wretch! Become ashes, wretch!” turned into ashes. But the more we curse this fellow, the more handsome, attractive, and graceful he becomes.’

‘Your asceticism is not in vain, sirs, nor is your spiritual life fruitless. Come now, sirs, abandon the resentment of mind you hold toward me.’

‘We abandon the resentment of mind we held toward you. But who is your honor?’

‘Have you heard, sirs, of the seer Asita Devala?’

‘Yes, sir.’

‘I am he, sirs.’ Then, Assalāyana, the seven brahmin seers approached to pay homage to the seer Asita Devala.

Then, Assalāyana, the seer Asita Devala said to the seven brahmin seers: ‘I have heard, sirs, that in seven brahmin seers dwelling in leaf huts in a wilderness region, such a harmful misconception has arisen: “Only brahmins are the highest caste, other castes are inferior; only brahmins are the fair caste, other castes are dark; only brahmins are purified, not non-brahmins; brahmins are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā.”’

‘That is so, sir.’

‘Do you know, sirs, whether the mother who gave you birth consorted only with a brahmin and not with a non-brahmin?’

‘No, sir.’

‘Do you know, sirs, whether the mother’s mother of the mother who gave you birth, back through seven generations of mothers and grandmothers, consorted only with a brahmin and not with a non-brahmin?’

‘No, sir.’

‘Do you know, sirs, whether the father who begot you consorted only with a brahmin woman and not with a non-brahmin woman?’

‘No, sir.’

‘Do you know, sirs, whether the father’s father of the father who begot you, back through seven generations of fathers and grandfathers, consorted only with a brahmin woman and not with a non-brahmin woman?’

‘No, sir.’

‘Do you know, sirs, how the descent of an embryo occurs?’

‘We know, sir, how the descent of an embryo occurs. Here, the mother and father come together, the mother is in season, and the being to be reborn is present; with the conjunction of these three, the descent of an embryo occurs.’

‘Do you know for certain, sirs, whether that being to be reborn was an aristocrat, a brahmin, a merchant, or a worker?’

‘We do not know for certain, sir, whether that being to be reborn was an aristocrat, a brahmin, a merchant, or a worker.’

‘Such being the case, sirs, do you know who you are?’

‘Such being the case, sir, we do not know who we are.’

“Even those seven brahmin seers, Assalāyana, when cross-examined, pressed, and questioned by the seer Asita Devala regarding their own doctrine of birth, could not hold their ground. How then could you now, when cross-examined, pressed, and questioned by me regarding your own doctrine of birth, hold your ground, when compared to them, you together with your teachers do not even amount to a ladleful?”

When this was said, the young brahmin Assalāyana said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! <collapse>Just as if one might set upright what had been overturned, reveal what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way, sir Gotama, the Dhamma has been explained by you in many ways. I go for refuge to venerable Gotama, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.</collapse> May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forth for life.”
