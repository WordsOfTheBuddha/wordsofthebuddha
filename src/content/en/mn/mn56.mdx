Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Nāḷandā::name of a town near Rājagaha [nāḷandā]|, in Pāvārika’s Mango Grove.

Now at that time, |Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta::the Jain leader Mahāvīra; lit. the knotless one, son of the Nāṭa clan [nigaṇṭha nāṭaputta]| was residing at Nāḷandā together with a large assembly of Nigaṇṭhas. Then, having walked for alms in Nāḷandā and having returned from his alms round after the meal, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi went to Pāvārika’s Mango Grove, to the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One.

After having engaged in courteous and polite conversation, he stood to one side. While the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi was standing to one side, the Blessed One said to him: “There are seats, Tapassi, if you wish to sit down.”

### Bodily Rod and Mental Action

When this was said, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi, having taken a lower seat, sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Blessed One said to the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi: “How many kinds of |kamma::action, deed [kamma]|, Tapassi, does Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declare for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma?”

“Friend Gotama, it is not the custom of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta to declare ‘kamma, kamma’; rather, friend Gotama, it is the custom of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta to declare ‘|rod::punishment, violence, instrument of harm [daṇḍa]|, rod.’”

“Then how many kinds of rod, Tapassi, does Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declare for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma?”

“Friend Gotama, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declares three kinds of rod for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, namely: the bodily rod, the verbal rod, and the mental rod.”

“Then, Tapassi, is the bodily rod one thing, the verbal rod another, and the mental rod another?”

“The bodily rod is one thing, friend Gotama, the verbal rod is another, and the mental rod is another.”

“Of these three rods, Tapassi, thus analyzed and thus distinguished, which rod does Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declare to be the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma: whether the bodily rod, the verbal rod, or the mental rod?”

“Of these three rods, friend Gotama, thus analyzed and thus distinguished, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declares the bodily rod to be the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.”

“Do you say the bodily rod, Tapassi?”

“I say the bodily rod, friend Gotama.”

“Do you say the bodily rod, Tapassi?”

“I say the bodily rod, friend Gotama.”

“Do you say the bodily rod, Tapassi?”

“I say the bodily rod, friend Gotama.”

Thus the Blessed One made the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi maintain his statement up to the third time.

When this was said, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi said to the Blessed One: “And you, friend Gotama, how many kinds of rod do you declare for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma?”

“Tapassi, it is not the custom of the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| to declare ‘rod, rod’; rather, Tapassi, it is the custom of the Tathāgata to declare ‘kamma, kamma.’”

“And you, friend Gotama, how many kinds of kamma do you declare for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma?”

“Tapassi, I declare three kinds of kamma for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, namely: bodily action, verbal action, and mental action.”

“Then, friend Gotama, is bodily action one thing, verbal action another, and mental action another?”

“Bodily action is one thing, Tapassi, verbal action is another, and mental action is another.”

“Of these three kinds of actions, friend Gotama, thus analyzed and thus distinguished, which action do you declare to be the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma: whether bodily action, verbal action, or mental action?”

“Of these three kinds of action, Tapassi, thus analyzed and thus distinguished, I declare mental action to be the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much bodily action, not so much verbal action.”

“Do you say mental action, friend Gotama?”

“I say mental action, Tapassi.”

“Do you say mental action, friend Gotama?”

“I say mental action, Tapassi.”

“Do you say mental action, friend Gotama?”

“I say mental action, Tapassi.”

Thus the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi made the Blessed One maintain his statement up to the third time, after which he rose from his seat and went to where Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta was staying.

### Upāli Offers to Dispute

Now at that time, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta was sitting with a large assembly of laypeople from Bālaka headed by Upāli. Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta saw the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi coming from afar; seeing him, he said to the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi: “Now where are you coming from in the middle of the day, Tapassi?”

“Just now, venerable sir, I am coming from the presence of the ascetic Gotama.”

“Did you have some conversation with the ascetic Gotama, Tapassi?”

“I did have some conversation with the ascetic Gotama, venerable sir.”

“And how did your conversation with the ascetic Gotama go, Tapassi?” Then the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi reported to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta the entire conversation he had had with the Blessed One. When this was said, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta said to the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi: “Good, good, Tapassi! Just as it should be with a learned disciple, one who truly understands the Teacher’s instruction, so too has the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi answered the ascetic Gotama. For what does the |paltry::insignificant, wretched; lit. corpse-like [chava]| mental rod amount to in comparison with this |gross::coarse, substantial [oḷārika]| bodily rod? Rather, the bodily rod is the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.”

When this was said, the householder Upāli said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “Good, good, venerable Dīghatapassi! Just as it should be with a learned disciple, one who truly understands the Teacher’s instruction, so too has the venerable Tapassi answered the ascetic Gotama. For what does the paltry mental rod amount to in comparison with this gross bodily rod? Rather, the bodily rod is the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.

Now, venerable sir, I shall go and |dispute::disprove, refute [āropeti]| the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama on the basis of this statement. If the ascetic Gotama maintains before me what the venerable Tapassi made him maintain, then just as a strong man might seize a long-fleeced ram by the fleece and drag it to, drag it fro, and drag it round about, so too in debate I will drag the ascetic Gotama to, drag him fro, and drag him round about! Just as a strong brewer’s worker might toss a large brewer’s mat into a deep pool of water, seize it by the corners, and drag it to, drag it fro, and drag it round about, so too in debate I will drag the ascetic Gotama to, drag him fro, and drag him round about! Just as a strong brewer’s mixer might seize a strainer by the corners and shake it down, shake it out, and thump it about, so too in debate I will shake the ascetic Gotama down, shake him out, and thump him about! Just as a sixty-year-old bull elephant might plunge into a deep lotus pond and play the game called hemp-washing, so too I will, as it were, play the game of hemp-washing with the ascetic Gotama! Now, venerable sir, I shall go and dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama on the basis of this statement.”

“Go, householder, dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama on the basis of this statement. For either I, householder, should dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama, or the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi, or you.”

When this was said, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “Venerable sir, it does not please me that the householder Upāli should dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama. For the ascetic Gotama, venerable sir, is a magician; he knows a |mind-changing spell::converting magic, alluring spell [āvaṭṭanī māyā]| by which he entices the disciples of other sects.”

“It is impossible, Tapassi, it is inconceivable that the householder Upāli could become a disciple of the ascetic Gotama. But it is possible that the ascetic Gotama might become a disciple of the householder Upāli. Go, householder, dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama on the basis of this statement. For either I, householder, should dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama, or the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi, or you.”

For a second time ... and a third time, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “Venerable sir, it does not please me that the householder Upāli should dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama. For the ascetic Gotama, venerable sir, is a magician; he knows a mind-changing spell by which he entices the disciples of other sects.”

“It is impossible, Tapassi, it is inconceivable that the householder Upāli could become a disciple of the ascetic Gotama. But it is possible that the ascetic Gotama might become a disciple of the householder Upāli. Go, householder, dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama on the basis of this statement. For either I, householder, should dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama, or the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi, or you.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the householder Upāli replied to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta. Then he rose from his seat, and after paying homage to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, keeping him on his right, he went to Pāvārika’s Mango Grove, to the Blessed One. Having drawn near and paid homage to the Blessed One, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the householder Upāli said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, did the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi come here?”

“The Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi came here, householder.”

“Did you have some conversation with the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi, venerable sir?”

“I did have some conversation with the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi, householder.”

“And how did your conversation with the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi go, venerable sir?”

Then the Blessed One reported to the householder Upāli the entire conversation that had taken place with the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi.

When this was said, the householder Upāli said to the Blessed One: “Good, good, venerable Tapassi! Just as it should be with a learned disciple, one who truly understands the Teacher’s instruction, so too has the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi answered the Blessed One. For what does the paltry mental rod amount to in comparison with this gross bodily rod? Rather, the bodily rod is the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.”

### Standing on Truth

“If, householder, you are willing to discuss |on the basis of truth::taking your stand on truth [sacce patiṭṭhāya]|, we might have some conversation about this.”

“I will discuss on the basis of truth, venerable sir; let us have some conversation about this.”

#### The Gravely Ill Nigaṇṭha

“What do you think, householder? Suppose there were a Nigaṇṭha here who was |afflicted::sick [ābādhika]|, suffering, and |gravely ill::seriously sick [bāḷhagilāna]| and he might refuse cold water and use only hot water.^[1]^ Not getting cold water, he might pass away. Now where, householder, does Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declare his rebirth to be?”

“Venerable sir, there are deities called ‘mind-bound’; he is reborn there.” “Why is that?” “Because, venerable sir, he passes away bound by the mind.”

“Reflect carefully, householder, reflect carefully on how you answer! What you said after does not agree with what you said before, nor does what you said before agree with what you said after. Yet you uttered this statement, householder: ‘I will discuss on the basis of truth, venerable sir; let us have some conversation about this.’”

“Even though the Blessed One speaks thus, venerable sir, still the bodily rod is the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.”

#### The Fourfold Restraint

“What do you think, householder? Suppose there were a Nigaṇṭha here who is restrained by the |fourfold restraint::the fourfold code of restraint practiced by Jain ascetics [cātuyāmasaṁvara]|—curbed by all restraints, endowed with all restraints, cleansed by all restraints, and suffused with all restraints. Yet while going forward and returning, he brings about the destruction of many tiny living creatures. What result, householder, does Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declare for him?”

“Venerable sir, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta declares what is |unintentional::unwilled, inadvertent [asañcetanika]| to be not greatly blameworthy.”

“But if one intends it, householder?”

“Then it is greatly blameworthy, venerable sir.”

“And under what, householder, does Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta classify |intention::volition, will [cetanā]|?”

“Under the mental rod, venerable sir.”

“Reflect carefully, householder, reflect carefully on how you answer! What you said after does not agree with what you said before, nor does what you said before agree with what you said after. Yet you uttered this statement, householder: ‘I will discuss on the basis of truth, venerable sir; let us have some conversation about this.’”

“Even though the Blessed One speaks thus, venerable sir, still the bodily rod is the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.”

#### A Single Act of Mental Corruption

“What do you think, householder? Is this Nāḷandā rich and prosperous, populous, and crowded with people?”

“Yes, venerable sir, this Nāḷandā is rich and prosperous, populous, and crowded with people.”

“What do you think, householder? Suppose a man were to come here with a drawn sword, and he were to say: ‘In a single moment, in a single instant, I will reduce all the living beings in this Nāḷandā to a single mass of flesh, a single heap of flesh!’ What do you think, householder, is that man able in a single moment, in a single instant, to reduce all the living beings in this Nāḷandā to a single mass of flesh, a single heap of flesh?”

“Even ten men, venerable sir, even twenty men, venerable sir, even thirty men, venerable sir, even forty men, venerable sir, even fifty men, venerable sir, are not able in a single moment, in a single instant, to reduce all the living beings in this Nāḷandā to a single mass of flesh, a single heap of flesh. What does a single paltry man amount to?”

“What do you think, householder? Suppose an ascetic or brahmin with psychic power, who has achieved mastery of the mind, were to come here, and he were to say: ‘I will reduce this Nāḷandā to ashes with a single |act of mental corruption::thought of hate, malevolent act of mind [manopadosa]|!’ What do you think, householder, is that ascetic or brahmin with psychic power, who has achieved mastery of the mind, able to reduce this Nāḷandā to ashes with a single act of mental corruption?”

“Even ten Nāḷandās, venerable sir, even twenty Nāḷandās, even thirty Nāḷandās, even forty Nāḷandās, even fifty Nāḷandās—that ascetic or brahmin with psychic power, who has achieved mastery of the mind, is able to reduce them to ashes with a single act of mental corruption. What does a single paltry Nāḷandā amount to?”

“Reflect carefully, householder, reflect carefully on how you answer! What you said after does not agree with what you said before, nor does what you said before agree with what you said after. Yet you uttered this statement, householder: ‘I will discuss on the basis of truth, venerable sir; let us have some conversation about this.’”

“Even though the Blessed One speaks thus, venerable sir, still the bodily rod is the most blameworthy for the doing of bad kamma, for the performance of bad kamma, not so much the verbal rod, not so much the mental rod.”

#### The Four Forests

“What do you think, householder? Have you heard of how the Daṇḍaka forest, the Kāliṅga forest, the Mejjha forest, and the Mātaṅga forest became forests, became wilderness?”

“Yes, venerable sir, I have heard of how the Daṇḍaka forest, the Kāliṅga forest, the Mejjha forest, and the Mātaṅga forest became forests, became wilderness.”

“What do you think, householder? As you have heard, by what means did the Daṇḍaka forest, the Kāliṅga forest, the Mejjha forest, and the Mātaṅga forest become forests, become wilderness?”

“I have heard this, venerable sir: by an act of mental corruption on the part of the seers, the Daṇḍaka forest, the Kāliṅga forest, the Mejjha forest, and the Mātaṅga forest became forests, became wilderness.”

“Reflect carefully, householder, reflect carefully on how you answer! What you said after does not agree with what you said before, nor does what you said before agree with what you said after. Yet you uttered this statement, householder: ‘I will discuss on the basis of truth, venerable sir; let us have some conversation about this.’”

### Going for Refuge

“Venerable sir, I was already pleased and satisfied with the Blessed One’s very first simile. Nevertheless, wishing to hear the Blessed One’s varied and eloquent solutions to the questions, I thought I would oppose the Blessed One in this way.

Excellent, venerable sir! Excellent, venerable sir! Just as if one were to set upright what had been overturned, reveal what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms—even so, the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| has been explained by the Blessed One in many ways. I go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of bhikkhus. May the Blessed One remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forward for life.”

“Make a |thorough investigation::careful consideration, acting after inquiry [anuviccakāra]|, householder! A thorough investigation is good for well-known people like yourself.”

“Venerable sir, by this too I am even more pleased and satisfied with the Blessed One, because of what the Blessed One said to me: ‘Make a thorough investigation, householder! A thorough investigation is good for well-known people like yourself.’ For if the wanderers of other sects had gained me as a disciple, venerable sir, they would have paraded a banner all around Nāḷandā, saying: ‘The householder Upāli has become our disciple!’ Yet the Blessed One says to me: ‘Make a thorough investigation, householder! A thorough investigation is good for well-known people like yourself.’

For a second time, venerable sir, I go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May the Blessed One remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forward for life.”

“For a long time, householder, your family has been a |well-spring::public watering place, source of constant support [opānabhūta]| for the Nigaṇṭhas, so you should consider giving |alms food::::almsfood| to them when they approach.”

“Venerable sir, by this too I am even more pleased and satisfied with the Blessed One, because of what the Blessed One said to me: ‘For a long time, householder, your family has been a well-spring for the Nigaṇṭhas, so you should consider giving |alms food::::almsfood| to them when they approach.’ I have heard this, venerable sir: ‘The ascetic Gotama says this: “A |gift::offering, donation, charity [dāna]| should only be given to me, not to others; a gift should only be given to my disciples, not to the disciples of others; only what is given to me bears great fruit, not what is given to others; only what is given to my disciples bears great fruit, not what is given to the disciples of others.”’ Yet the Blessed One encourages me to give even to the Nigaṇṭhas. Even so, venerable sir, we will know the proper time for that.

For a third time, venerable sir, I go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May the Blessed One remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forward for life.”

Then the Blessed One gradually gave the householder Upāli a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that the mind of the householder Upāli was ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the |stainless::dustless, pure, untainted [viraja]|, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in the householder Upāli: “Whatever is subject to arising, is subject to cessation.” Then the householder Upāli—having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction—said to the Blessed One:

“And now, venerable sir, we must depart; we are busy and have much to do.”

“You may go, householder, at your convenience.”

### The Closed Door

Then the householder Upāli, having delighted and rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words, rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right, approached his own residence. Having approached, he addressed the gatekeeper: “From today onward, good gatekeeper, I close my door to Nigaṇṭhas and Nigaṇṭhīs, and open my door to the Blessed One’s bhikkhus, bhikkhunīs, male lay disciples, and female lay disciples. If any Nigaṇṭha comes, you should say to him: ‘Wait, venerable sir, do not enter. From today onward, the householder Upāli has become a disciple of the ascetic Gotama. His door is closed to Nigaṇṭhas and Nigaṇṭhīs, and open to the Blessed One’s bhikkhus, bhikkhunīs, male lay disciples, and female lay disciples. If you need |alms food::::almsfood|, venerable sir, wait right here; they will bring it to you right here.’”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the gatekeeper replied to the householder Upāli.

The Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi heard: “The householder Upāli, it seems, has become a disciple of the ascetic Gotama.” Then the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi went to where Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta was staying. After approaching, he said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “I have heard this, venerable sir: the householder Upāli, it seems, has become a disciple of the ascetic Gotama.”

“It is impossible, Tapassi, it is inconceivable that the householder Upāli could become a disciple of the ascetic Gotama. But it is possible that the ascetic Gotama might become a disciple of the householder Upāli.”

For a second time, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi ... and a third time, the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “I have heard this, venerable sir ...

Then the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi approached the residence of the householder Upāli. The gatekeeper saw the Nigaṇṭha Dīghatapassi coming from afar. “Well then, good gatekeeper, go to the householder Upāli; having approached, say this to the householder Upāli: ‘Venerable sir, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta is standing outside the gatehouse together with a large assembly of Nigaṇṭhas; he wishes to see you.’”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the gatekeeper replied to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, and he went to the householder Upāli. Having approached, he said to the householder Upāli: “Venerable sir, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta is standing outside the gatehouse together with a large assembly of Nigaṇṭhas; he wishes to see you.” “Well then, good gatekeeper, prepare seats in the hall of the middle gate.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the gatekeeper replied to the householder Upāli. Having prepared seats in the hall of the middle gate, he went to the householder Upāli; having approached, he said to the householder Upāli: “Venerable sir, seats are prepared in the hall of the middle gate. You may come at your convenience.”

Then the householder Upāli went to the hall of the middle gate. Having approached, he himself sat down on the seat there that was the highest, the best, the foremost, and the finest, and addressed the gatekeeper: “Well then, good gatekeeper, go to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta; having approached, say this to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: ‘Venerable sir, the householder Upāli says: “Enter, venerable sir, if you wish.”’”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the gatekeeper replied to the householder Upāli, and he went to where Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta was staying. After approaching, he said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “Venerable sir, the householder Upāli says: ‘Enter, venerable sir, if you wish.’”

Then Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, together with the large assembly of Nigaṇṭhas, went to the hall of the middle gate. Previously, whenever the householder Upāli saw Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta coming from afar, he would go out from there to meet him, wipe off with his upper robe whichever seat there was the highest, the best, the foremost, and the finest, take him around, and have him sit down; but now, having seated himself on whichever seat there was the highest, the best, the foremost, and the finest, he said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “There are seats, venerable sir, if you wish to sit down.”

### The Baby Monkey

When this was said, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta said to the householder Upāli: “You are mad, householder, you are a fool, householder! You went saying, ‘Venerable sir, I will go and dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama,’ and you have come back caught in the vast net of his doctrine! Just as if, householder, a man who went to castrate were to come back with his own testicles torn out, or just as if, householder, a man who went to put out eyes were to come back with his own eyes torn out; so too, householder, you went saying, ‘Venerable sir, I will go and dispute the doctrine of the ascetic Gotama,’ and you have come back caught in the vast net of his doctrine! You have been enticed, householder, by the ascetic Gotama with his mind-changing spell!”

“Auspicious, venerable sir, is that mind-changing spell! Lovely, venerable sir, is that mind-changing spell! If my beloved relatives and blood-kin, venerable sir, were to be enticed by this mind-changing spell, it would lead to the welfare and happiness of my beloved relatives and blood-kin for a long time. If all the noblemen, venerable sir, were to be enticed by this mind-changing spell, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of all the noblemen for a long time. If all the brahmins, venerable sir ... the merchants ... the workers were to be enticed by this mind-changing spell, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of all the workers for a long time. And if the world with its |deities::gods [devas]|, |Māras::demons, tempters, beings of delusion; lit. causing death [mārake]|, and |Brahmas::Gods; celestial beings residing in the Brahmā realms, often considered to be highly refined and long-lived deities. [brahmā]|, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its kings and commoners, venerable sir, were to be enticed by this mind-changing spell, it would lead to the welfare and happiness of the world with its deities, Māras, and Brahmas, of this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its kings and commoners for a long time. Well then, venerable sir, I shall give you a simile, for there are some wise persons who understand the meaning of a statement through a simile.

Once in the past, venerable sir, a certain old, aged, and elderly brahmin had a young brahmin girl as his wife, who was pregnant and about to give birth. Then, venerable sir, that young brahmin woman said to that brahmin: ‘Go, brahmin, buy a baby monkey from the market and bring it back so that it will be a playmate for my boy.’

When this was said, that brahmin said to that young brahmin woman: ‘Wait first, dear, until you give birth. If you give birth to a boy, dear, I will buy a male baby monkey for you from the market and bring it back to be a playmate for your boy. But if you give birth to a girl, dear, I will buy a female baby monkey for you from the market and bring it back to be a playmate for your girl.’

For a second time, venerable sir, that young brahmin woman ... and a third time, venerable sir, that young brahmin woman said to that brahmin: ‘Go, brahmin, buy a baby monkey from the market and bring it back so that it will be a playmate for my boy.’

Then, venerable sir, that brahmin, infatuated with and bound in heart to that young brahmin woman, bought a male baby monkey from the market, brought it back, and said to that young brahmin woman: ‘Here, dear, I have bought this male baby monkey from the market and brought it back for you to be a playmate for your boy.’

When this was said, venerable sir, that young brahmin woman said to that brahmin: ‘Go, brahmin, take this baby monkey and approach Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son; having approached, say this to Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son: “Good Rattapāṇi, I want this baby monkey dyed with the dye called yellow ointment, pounded and re-pounded, and smoothed on both sides.”’

Then, venerable sir, that brahmin, infatuated with and bound in heart to that young brahmin woman, took that baby monkey and approached Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son; having approached, he said to Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son: ‘Good Rattapāṇi, I want this baby monkey dyed with the dye called yellow ointment, pounded and re-pounded, and smoothed on both sides.’

When this was said, venerable sir, Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son said to that brahmin: ‘Venerable sir, this baby monkey of yours can endure a dyeing, but it cannot endure a pounding or a smoothing.’

In the same way, venerable sir, the doctrine of the foolish Nigaṇṭhas can endure a dyeing—delighting fools, not the wise—but it cannot endure scrutiny or being smoothed out.

Then, venerable sir, on a later occasion that brahmin, taking a new pair of garments, approached Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son; having approached, he said to Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son: ‘Good Rattapāṇi, I want this new pair of garments dyed with the dye called yellow ointment, pounded and re-pounded, and smoothed on both sides.’

When this was said, venerable sir, Rattapāṇi the dyer’s son said to that brahmin: ‘Venerable sir, this new pair of garments of yours can endure a dyeing, and it can endure both a pounding and a smoothing.’

In the same way, venerable sir, the doctrine of that Blessed One, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One, can endure a dyeing—delighting the wise, not fools—and it can endure both scrutiny and being smoothed out.”

### Whose Disciple Am I

“The assembly along with the king, householder, knows thus: ‘The householder Upāli is a disciple of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta.’ Whose disciple should we consider you to be, householder?”

When this was said, the householder Upāli rose from his seat, draped his outer robe over one shoulder, raised his joined palms towards the Blessed One, and said to Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: “Well then, venerable sir, listen to whose disciple I am:

Of the wise one, freed from delusion,
whose |barrenness::mental rigidity, stubbornness [khīla]| is shattered, victor of victory;
of the |trouble-free::distress-free, serene [anīgha]| one, with a well-balanced mind,
mature in virtue, of excellent wisdom;

who has vomited up the |world’s bait::material pleasures and gains of the world [lokāmisa]|, joyful;
the human who has completed the ascetic’s task,

Of the |chief bull::preeminent leader, noble bull [nisabha]|, the immeasurable one,

Of the |great being::nāga, tusker elephant, spiritual giant [nāga]|, dwelling in remote lodgings,

“And when, householder, were these praises of the ascetic Gotama’s qualities composed by you?”

“Suppose, venerable sir, there were a great heap of many different flowers, and a skilled garland-maker or his apprentice were to weave them into a variegated garland; in the same way, venerable sir, that Blessed One has many praiseworthy qualities, many hundreds of praiseworthy qualities. For who, venerable sir, would not praise one who is worthy of praise?”

Then, unable to bear this honor paid to the Blessed One, hot blood gushed forth from the mouth of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta right there on the spot.
