“Homage to the Blessed One, the |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, the perfectly Awakened One!

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One!

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One!”

Now at that time a young brahmin named |Saṅgārava::name of a young brahmin; lit. dignified [saṅgārava]| was living at Cañcalikappa. The young brahmin Saṅgārava heard the brahmin woman Dhanañjānī speaking these words. Having heard her, he said to the brahmin woman Dhanañjānī: “This brahmin woman Dhanañjānī is |degraded::fallen, disgraced [avabhūta]|, this brahmin woman Dhanañjānī is |ruined::overcome, defeated [parabhāta]|—for while brahmins versed in the three Vedas are present, she would speak the praises of that bald-headed ascetic!”

“Dear good sir, you do not know that Blessed One’s virtue and wisdom. If you, dear good sir, knew that Blessed One’s virtue and wisdom, you would not think that Blessed One should be abused or reviled.”

“Then, madam, when the ascetic Gotama arrives at Cañcalikappa, let me know.”

“Yes, good sir,” the brahmin woman Dhanañjānī replied to the young brahmin Saṅgārava.

Then the Blessed One, wandering by stages among the Kosalans, arrived at Cañcalikappa. There the Blessed One dwelled at Cañcalikappa in the mango grove of the |Todeyya brahmins::brahmin lineage of Todeyya [todeyyānaṁ brāhmaṇānaṁ]|.

#### Distinction Among Those Who Claim Direct Knowledge

“Sir Gotama, there are some ascetics and brahmins who, having reached the perfection and culmination of |direct knowledge::experiential understanding [abhiññā]| in this very life, profess the |fundamentals of the spiritual life::the foundational holy life [ādibrahmacariya]|. Among those ascetics and brahmins who, having reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life, profess the fundamentals of the spiritual life, which of them is sir Gotama?”

“|Bhāradvāja::clan name of the young brahmin Saṅgārava [bhāradvāja]|, I declare that there is a distinction even among those who, having reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life, profess the fundamentals of the spiritual life. There are, Bhāradvāja, some ascetics and brahmins who are |traditionalists::those reliant on oral tradition [anussavikā]|. Relying on |oral tradition::what has been heard, hearsay, lineage tradition [anussava]|, they claim to have reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life, professing the fundamentals of the spiritual life—such as the brahmins versed in the three Vedas. But there are, Bhāradvāja, some ascetics and brahmins who, through mere faith alone, claim to have reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life, professing the fundamentals of the spiritual life—such as the |logicians and reasoners::speculative thinkers [takkī vīmaṁsī]|. And there are, Bhāradvāja, some ascetics and brahmins who, regarding teachings unheard before, having directly known the Dhamma for themselves, claim to have reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life, professing the fundamentals of the spiritual life. Among those ascetics and brahmins who, regarding teachings unheard before, having directly known the Dhamma for themselves, claim to have reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life and profess the fundamentals of the spiritual life, I am one. And this, Bhāradvāja, is to be understood by this exposition, as to how I am one of those ascetics and brahmins who, regarding teachings unheard before, having directly known the Dhamma for themselves, claim to have reached the perfection and culmination of direct knowledge in this very life and profess the fundamentals of the spiritual life.

#### The Noble Quest

Here, Bhāradvāja, before my |full awakening::perfect understanding, enlightenment [sambodha]|, while I was still an unawakened |Bodhisatta::Buddha before his awakening, aspirant Buddha [bodhisatta]|, it occurred to me: ‘Household life is |confined::crowded, cramped [sambādha]|, a dusty path; life gone forth is wide open. While dwelling in a house, it is not easy to live the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| completely perfect and pure as a polished shell. Suppose I shave off my hair and beard, put on the ochre robes, and go forth from the household life into homelessness.’ Then, Bhāradvāja, at a later time, while I was still young, with black hair, endowed with the blessing of youth, and in the prime of life, although my mother and father wished otherwise and wept with tearful faces, I shaved off my hair and beard, put on the ochre robes, and went forth from the household life into homelessness.

#### Base of Nothingness

Thus, having gone forth, in search of what is |wholesome::healthy, beneficial, useful [kusala]|, seeking the unsurpassed state of sublime peace, I approached |Āḷāra Kālāma::name of the Buddha’s first teacher; lit. curved [āḷāra + kālāma]|. Having approached him, I said to Āḷāra Kālāma, ‘Friend Kālāma, I wish to lead the spiritual life in this teaching and training.’

When this was said, Bhāradvāja, Āḷāra Kālāma said to me, ‘The venerable one may stay here. This teaching is such that a wise person can soon enter upon and abide in it, realizing for themselves through direct knowledge their own teacher’s doctrine.’

Then, Bhāradvāja, before long, I quickly learned that teaching. And in so doing, merely by repetition, recitation, and verbal expression, I claimed both knowledge and seniority, saying ‘I know, I see,’ along with others who claimed the same.

Then, Bhāradvāja, it occurred to me: ‘Āḷāra Kālāma does not proclaim that he abides in this teaching simply based on faith alone, but he claims to have directly realized it by himself. Surely, Āḷāra Kālāma abides knowing and seeing this teaching.’

So, Bhāradvāja, I went to Āḷāra Kālāma, and having approached him, I asked, ‘To what extent, friend Kālāma, do you proclaim that you have directly realized and abide in this teaching?’ When this was said, Bhāradvāja, Āḷāra Kālāma made known the |attainment of the base of nothingness::experience of the dimension of nothingness, a meditative absorption where one transcends all perception of material form, focusing instead on the experience of ‘nothingness’ [ākiñcaññāyatana]|.

Then, Bhāradvāja, it occurred to me: ‘It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has |faith::confidence, conviction, trust [saddha]|; I too have faith. It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has |energy::willpower, determination [vīriya]|; I too have energy. It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has |mindfulness::active presence of mind and lucid retention, recollection, keeping in mind [sati]|; I too have mindfulness. It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has |collectedness::stability of mind, stillness of mind, mental composure [samādhi]|; I too have collectedness. It is not only Āḷāra Kālāma who has |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [pañña]|; I too have wisdom. Then, Bhāradvāja, I went to Āḷāra Kālāma, and having approached him, I asked, ‘Friend Kālāma, is it to this extent that you declare that you enter upon and abide in this Dhamma by realizing it for yourself with direct knowledge?’

‘Friend, this is indeed the extent to which I declare that I enter upon and abide in this Dhamma by realizing it for myself with direct knowledge.’

‘Friend, I too have entered upon and abided in this Dhamma by realizing it for myself with direct knowledge to this extent.’

‘Friend, it is our good fortune, truly our great fortune, that we have such a companion in the spiritual life as the venerable one. The Dhamma that I declare by realizing it for myself with direct knowledge, you abide in by realizing it for yourself with direct knowledge. The Dhamma that you abide in by realizing it for yourself with direct knowledge, I declare by realizing it for myself with direct knowledge. So the Dhamma I know, you also know; the Dhamma you know, I also know. Thus, as I am, so are you; as you are, so am I. Come, friend, let us lead this community together.’

Thus, Bhāradvāja, Āḷāra Kālāma, being my teacher, placed me, his student, as an equal to himself and honored me with great reverence.

Then, Bhāradvāja, it occurred to me: ‘This Dhamma does not lead to |disenchantment::de-illusionment, disinterest, dispassion [nibbidā]|, to the |fading of desire::dispassion, detachment [virāga]|, to |cessation::ending, termination [nirodha]|, to |tranquility::calmness, serenity, stillness, peace [upasama]|, to |direct knowledge::experiential understanding [abhiññāya]|, to full awakening, to |Nibbāna::complete cooling, letting go of everything, deathless, freedom from calamity, the non-disintegrating; lit. blowing away [nibbāna]|, but only to reappearance in the base of nothingness.’ So, Bhāradvāja, without becoming attached to that Dhamma, I grew disenchanted with it and departed.

#### Base of Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception

Then, Bhāradvāja, still in search of what is wholesome, seeking the unsurpassed state of sublime peace, I gradually walked through |Magadha::an ancient kingdom in northeastern India and the setting for much of the Buddha’s teachings after his full awakening [magadha]| until I arrived at |Uruvelā::a region near the Nerañjarā river where the Buddha engaged in intensive meditation before attaining full awakening [uruvelā]|, near |Senānigama::a nearby village at Uruvelā, where the Buddha often went for alms. #### Three Similes on Lighting a Fire

Now, Bhāradvāja, these three similes occurred to me spontaneously, never heard before. “No, sir Gotama. And why is that? Because, sir Gotama, it is a wet sappy piece of wood, and furthermore it is lying in water. Eventually the man would reap only weariness and disappointment.”

#### The Four Jhānas

Then, Bhāradvāja, it occurred to me: ‘Why am I afraid of that pleasure which is a pleasure apart from sensual pleasures and apart from unwholesome mental states?’ Then, Bhāradvāja, it occurred to me: ‘I am not afraid of that pleasure since it has nothing to do with sensual pleasures and unwholesome mental states.’

Then, Bhāradvāja, it occurred to me: ‘It is not easy to attain that pleasure with a body so extremely emaciated. Suppose I were to take some solid food, such as rice and porridge.’ So, Bhāradvāja, I took solid food, rice and porridge.

Now at that time, Bhāradvāja, the |group of five bhikkhus::the Buddha’s first five companions [pañcavaggiyā bhikkhū]| were attending to me, thinking: ‘When the ascetic Gotama attains the Dhamma, he will inform us.’ But when I took solid food, rice and porridge, those five bhikkhus, losing interest, abandoned me, saying: ‘The ascetic Gotama has become indulgent, he has |strayed from the exertion::given up striving [padhāna + vibbhanta]|, and he has reverted to luxury.’

Thus, with my mind collected, purified, clarified, blemish-free, free from impurities, flexible, suitable for use, stable, and attained to imperturbability, I directed my mind toward the knowledge of recollecting past lives. #### Do Gods Exist?

When this was said, the young brahmin Saṅgārava said to the Blessed One: “Sir Gotama’s striving was steadfast indeed; sir Gotama’s striving was worthy of a true person indeed, as befits an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One. But, sir Gotama, do gods exist?”

“Bhāradvāja, it is known to me |according to cause::lit. from the place [ṭhānaso]| that there are |higher gods::gods of a higher order; gods known by higher knowledge [adhidevā]|.”

“Why is it, sir Gotama, that when asked ‘Do gods exist?’, you say: ‘Bhāradvāja, it is known to me according to cause that there are higher gods’? Surely, sir Gotama, if that is so, isn’t that empty and false?”

“Bhāradvāja, when asked ‘Do gods exist?’, whether one says ‘Gods exist’ or one says ‘It is known to me according to cause that there are higher gods,’ a wise person should come to the definite conclusion here, namely: ‘Gods exist.’”

“Why then did sir Gotama not explain this to me from the very beginning?”

“Bhāradvāja, it is widely agreed in the world that gods exist.”

When this was said, the young brahmin Saṅgārava said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! Just as if one might set upright what had been overturned, |reveal::uncover [vivarati]| what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way, sir Gotama, the Dhamma has been |explained::illustrated [pakāsita]| by you in many ways. I go for refuge to venerable Gotama, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who, from this day forward, has gone to refuge for life.”
