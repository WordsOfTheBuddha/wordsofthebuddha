“Friends, when the Blessed One rose from his seat and entered his dwelling after giving a summary in brief without explaining the meaning in detail:

‘Let not a person revive the past,
<collapse>nor place hope upon the future;

Whatever phenomena are presently arisen,

This, friends, is how I understand in detail the meaning of that brief summary declared by the Blessed One, which was not explained in full—

##### Past

And how, friends, does one revive the past? Thinking, ‘My eye was like this in the past, and |forms::visible objects [rūpā]| were like this,’ one’s |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]| becomes |bound by desire and passion::tied up with desire and lust, fettered by craving and passion [chandarāgappaṭibaddha]| for that. Because one’s consciousness is bound by desire and passion, one |delights in::relishes, takes pleasure in, welcomes [abhinandati]| that. Delighting in that, one revives the past.

Thinking, ‘My ear was like this in the past, and |sounds::auditory objects [saddā]| were like this’ … ‘My nose was like this in the past, and |odors::olfactory objects, smells [gandhā]| were like this’ … ‘My tongue was like this in the past, and |tastes::gustatory objects, flavors [rasā]| were like this’ … ‘My body was like this in the past, and |tangible objects::tactile sensations [phoṭṭhabbā]| were like this’ … ‘My mind was like this in the past, and |mental objects::thoughts, ideas, mental phenomena [dhammā]| were like this,’ one’s consciousness becomes bound by desire and passion for that. Because one’s consciousness is bound by desire and passion, one delights in that. Delighting in that, one revives the past. This, friends, is how one revives the past.

And how, friends, does one not revive the past? Thinking, ‘My eye was like this in the past, and forms were like this,’ one’s consciousness does not become bound by desire and passion for that. Because one’s consciousness is not bound by desire and passion, one does not delight in that. Not delighting in that, one does not revive the past.

Thinking, ‘My ear was like this in the past, and sounds were like this’ … ‘My nose was like this in the past, and odors were like this’ … ‘My tongue was like this in the past, and tastes were like this’ … ‘My body was like this in the past, and tangible objects were like this’ … ‘My mind was like this in the past, and mental objects were like this,’ one’s consciousness does not become bound by desire and passion for that. Because one’s consciousness is not bound by desire and passion, one does not delight in that. Not delighting in that, one does not revive the past. This, friends, is how one does not revive the past.

##### Future

And how, friends, does one place hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘May my eye be like this in the future, and forms be like this,’ one |sets one’s mind::directs one's heart, resolves, aspires [cittaṁ paṇidahati]| on obtaining what has not yet been obtained. Because of that mental aspiration, one delights in that. Delighting in that, one places hope upon the future. Thinking, ‘May my ear be like this in the future, and sounds be like this’ … ‘May my nose be like this in the future, and odors be like this’ … ‘May my tongue be like this in the future, and tastes be like this’ … ‘May my body be like this in the future, and tangible objects be like this’ … ‘May my mind be like this in the future, and mental objects be like this,’ one sets one’s mind on obtaining what has not yet been obtained. Because of that mental aspiration, one delights in that. Delighting in that, one places hope upon the future. This, friends, is how one places hope upon the future.

And how, friends, does one not place hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘May my eye be like this in the future, and forms be like this,’ one does not set one’s mind on obtaining what has not yet been obtained. Because of the absence of that mental aspiration, one does not delight in that. Not delighting in that, one does not place hope upon the future. Thinking, ‘May my ear be like this in the future, and sounds be like this’ … ‘May my nose be like this in the future, and odors be like this’ … ‘May my tongue be like this in the future, and tastes be like this’ … ‘May my body be like this in the future, and tangible objects be like this’ … ‘May my mind be like this in the future, and mental objects be like this,’ one does not set one’s mind on obtaining what has not yet been obtained. Because of the absence of that mental aspiration, one does not delight in that. Not delighting in that, one does not place hope upon the future. This, friends, is how one does not place hope upon the future.

##### Presently Arisen Phenomena

And how, friends, |is one swept away by::is carried away by, is drawn along by [saṁhīrati]| presently arisen |phenomena::characteristics, thoughts, mental states, mental qualities [dhammā]|? Both the eye and forms, friends, are presently arisen. If one’s consciousness becomes bound by desire and passion for what is presently arisen there, then because one’s consciousness is bound by desire and passion, one delights in that. Delighting in that, one is swept away by presently arisen phenomena. Both the ear and sounds, friends … Both the nose and odors, friends … Both the tongue and tastes, friends … Both the body and tangible objects, friends … Both the mind and mental objects, friends, are presently arisen. If one’s consciousness becomes bound by desire and passion for what is presently arisen there, then because one’s consciousness is bound by desire and passion, one delights in that. Delighting in that, one is swept away by presently arisen phenomena. In this way, friends, one is swept away by presently arisen phenomena.

And how, friends, |is one not swept away by::is not carried away by, is not drawn along by [na + saṁhīrati]| presently arisen phenomena? Both the eye and forms, friends, are presently arisen. If one’s consciousness does not become bound by desire and passion for what is presently arisen there, then because one’s consciousness is not bound by desire and passion, one does not delight in that. Not delighting in that, one is not swept away by presently arisen phenomena. Both the ear and sounds, friends … Both the nose and odors, friends … Both the tongue and tastes, friends … Both the body and tangible objects, friends … Both the mind and mental objects, friends, are presently arisen. If one’s consciousness does not become bound by desire and passion for what is presently arisen there, then because one’s consciousness is not bound by desire and passion, one does not delight in that. Not delighting in that, one is not swept away by presently arisen phenomena. In this way, friends, one is not swept away by presently arisen phenomena.
