Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was wandering on a walking tour in the Kosalan country with a large |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of |bhikkhus,::::| and eventually he arrived at a Kosalan brahmin village named |Sālā::name of a Brahman village in Kosala [sālā]|.

The brahmins and householders of Sālā heard:

“The ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from a Sakyan clan, while wandering on a walking tour in the Kosalan country with a large Saṅgha of |bhikkhus,::::| has arrived at Sālā. Now a good report of venerable Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One. He declares this world with its |deities::gods [devas]|, |Māras::demons, tempters, beings of delusion; lit. causing death [mārake]|, |Brahmas::Gods; celestial beings residing in the Brahmā realms, often considered to be highly refined and long-lived deities. [brahmā]|, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, kings and commoners, which he has himself realized with direct knowledge. He teaches the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| that is beneficial in the beginning, beneficial in the middle, and beneficial in the end, meaningful and well-phrased, and he reveals a |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| that is entirely perfect and pure.’ It is auspicious to see such arahants.”

Then the brahmins and householders of Sālā approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, some paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One, and after exchanging courteous and polite conversation, sat down to one side; some raised their joined palms towards the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some stated their names and clan affiliation in the presence of the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some kept silent and sat down to one side. Once they were seated, the Blessed One said to the brahmins and householders of Sālā:

“Householders, is there any agreeable teacher in whom you have acquired grounded faith?”

“No, venerable sir, there is no agreeable teacher in whom we have acquired grounded faith.”

### The Incontrovertible Teaching

“Householders, since you have not found an agreeable teacher, you should undertake and practice this |incontrovertible teaching::an unambiguous, reliable teaching that does not fail or mislead [apaṇṇakadhamma]|. For when the incontrovertible teaching is properly accepted and undertaken, it will lead to your welfare and happiness for a long time. And what, householders, is the incontrovertible teaching?

#### No Other World

“There are, householders, some ascetics and brahmins who have such a doctrine, such a view: ‘There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; no fruit or result of good and bad actions; no this world, no other world; no mother, no father; no beings who are reborn spontaneously; no ascetics and brahmins in the world who are rightly practicing or have rightly practiced, and who, having realized for themselves by |direct knowledge::experiential understanding [abhiññāya]|, declare this world and the other world.’

Now, householders, there are certain ascetics and brahmins whose doctrine is directly opposed to those ascetics and brahmins. They speak thus: ‘There is what is given, what is offered, what is sacrificed; there is fruit and result of good and bad actions; there is this world, there is another world; there is mother, there is father; there are beings who are reborn spontaneously; there are ascetics and brahmins in the world who are rightly practicing and have rightly practiced, and who, having realized for themselves by direct knowledge, declare this world and the other world.’

What do you think, householders: do not these ascetics and brahmins hold doctrines directly opposed to one another?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Here, householders, regarding those ascetics and brahmins who have such a doctrine, such a view: ‘There is nothing given, nothing offered... who, having realized for themselves by direct knowledge, declare this world and the other world’—this is to be expected of them: that avoiding these three wholesome qualities, namely, good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, and good mental conduct, they will undertake and engage in these three unwholesome qualities, namely, bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, and mental misconduct. Why is that? Because those good ascetics and brahmins do not see the |drawback::disadvantage, unsatisfactoriness, inadequacy, danger [ādīnava]|, degradation, and |defilement::corruption, impurity [saṅkilesa]| in unwholesome qualities, nor do they see the benefit of renunciation and the cleansing power in wholesome qualities.

Since there is indeed another world, one who holds the view ‘there is no other world’ has |wrong view::view not aligned with reality [micchādiṭṭhi]|. Since there is indeed another world, one who intends ‘there is no other world’ has |wrong intention::unwholesome intentions of ill will, cruelty, or sensuality [micchāsaṅkappa]|. Since there is indeed another world, one who asserts ‘there is no other world’ has |wrong speech::false, divisive, harsh, or idle speech [micchāvācā]|. Since there is indeed another world, one who says ‘there is no other world’ is opposed to those arahants who know the other world. Since there is indeed another world, one who convinces another ‘there is no other world’ convinces him to accept an untrue Dhamma. And because he convinces another to accept an untrue Dhamma, he exalts himself and disparages others. Thus any principled behavior he formerly had is abandoned and unprincipled behavior is established—along with wrong view, wrong intention, wrong speech, opposition to the Noble Ones, convincing another to accept an untrue Dhamma, exaltation of oneself, and disparagement of others. Thus many harmful, unwholesome states arise with wrong view as condition.

Regarding this, householders, a sensible person reflects thus: ‘If there is no other world, then on the breakup of the body this good individual will make himself safe. But if there is another world, then on the breakup of the body, after death, he will be reborn in a |state of loss::state of misery [apāya]|, in a bad destination, in a |realm of downfall::realm of misery [vinipāta]|, in hell. Even granting that there is no other world, and that the word of those good ascetics and brahmins is true, still here in the present life this good individual is criticized by the wise: “An unprincipled person of wrong view, a |nihilist::one who adheres to the theory of ethical inefficacy; lit. there is nothing doctrine [natthikavāda]|.” But if there is indeed another world, then this good individual loses on both sides: he is criticized by the wise in the present life, and on the breakup of the body, after death, he will be reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell. Thus this incontrovertible teaching has been poorly undertaken by him; it extends to only one side and leaves behind the wholesome position.’

“Now, householders, regarding those ascetics and brahmins who have such a doctrine, such a view: ‘There is what is given... who, having realized for themselves by direct knowledge, declare this world and the other world’—this is to be expected of them: that avoiding these three unwholesome qualities, namely, bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, and mental misconduct, they will undertake and engage in these three wholesome qualities, namely, good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, and good mental conduct. Why is that? Because those good ascetics and brahmins see the drawback, degradation, and defilement in unwholesome qualities, and they see the benefit of renunciation and the cleansing power in wholesome qualities.

Since there is indeed another world, one who holds the view ‘there is another world’ has |right view::understanding in line with the four noble truths and the reality of action and its results [sammādiṭṭhi]|. Since there is indeed another world, one who intends ‘there is another world’ has |right intention::thoughts of renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness [sammāsaṅkappa]|. Since there is indeed another world, one who asserts ‘there is another world’ has |right speech::truthful, harmonizing, gentle, and meaningful speech [sammāvācā]|. Since there is indeed another world, one who says ‘there is another world’ is not opposed to those arahants who know the other world. Since there is indeed another world, one who convinces another ‘there is another world’ convinces him to accept the true Dhamma. And because he convinces another to accept the true Dhamma, he neither exalts himself nor disparages others. Thus any unprincipled behavior he formerly had is abandoned and principled behavior is established—along with right view, right intention, right speech, non-opposition to the Noble Ones, convincing another to accept the true Dhamma, not exalting oneself, and not disparaging others. Thus many wholesome states arise with right view as condition.

Regarding this, householders, a sensible person reflects thus: ‘If there is another world, then on the breakup of the body, after death, this good individual will be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world. Even granting that there is no other world, and that the word of those good ascetics and brahmins is true, still here in the present life this good individual is praised by the wise: “A principled person of right view, one who affirms moral efficacy.” But if there is indeed another world, then this good individual wins on both sides: he is praised by the wise in the present life, and on the breakup of the body, after death, he will be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world. Thus this incontrovertible teaching has been well undertaken by him; it extends to both sides and leaves behind the unwholesome position.’

#### No Cause and No Condition

“There are, householders, some ascetics and brahmins who have such a doctrine, such a view: ‘There is no cause, no condition for the defilement of beings; beings are defiled without cause and without condition. There is no cause, no condition for the purification of beings; beings are purified without cause and without condition. There is no power, no energy, no human steadfastness, no human prowess. All beings, all living things, all creatures, all souls are without control, without power, without energy; molded by destiny, chance, and nature, they experience pleasure and pain through the six classes of birth.’

Now, householders, there are certain ascetics and brahmins whose doctrine is directly opposed to those ascetics and brahmins. They speak thus: ‘There is a cause, there is a condition for the defilement of beings; beings are defiled with cause and with condition. There is a cause, there is a condition for the purification of beings; beings are purified with cause and with condition. There is power, there is energy, there is human steadfastness, there is human prowess. It is not that all beings, all living things, all creatures, all souls are without control, without power, without energy, molded by destiny, chance, and nature, experiencing pleasure and pain through the six classes of birth.’

#### No Formless Realms

“There are, householders, some ascetics and brahmins who have such a doctrine, such a view: ‘There are no formless realms whatsoever.’

Now, householders, there are certain ascetics and brahmins whose doctrine is directly opposed to those ascetics and brahmins. They speak thus: ‘There are indeed formless realms.’

What do you think, householders: do not these ascetics and brahmins hold doctrines directly opposed to one another?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Here, householders, a sensible person reflects thus: ‘As to those good ascetics and brahmins who say: “There are no formless realms whatsoever,” that is unseen by me. And as to those good ascetics and brahmins who say: “There are indeed formless realms,” that is unknown to me. If, without knowing or seeing, I were to take one side and dogmatically declare: “Only this is true, anything else is wrong,” that would not be proper for me. If the word of those good ascetics and brahmins who say: “There are no formless realms whatsoever” is true, then it is possible that my rebirth will be among those gods who possess form, made of mind. If the word of those good ascetics and brahmins who say: “There are indeed formless realms” is true, then it is possible that my rebirth will be among those formless gods, made of perception. But it is seen that on account of form there occurs the taking up of sticks and weapons, quarrels, disputes, arguments, accusations, slander, and lies. None of this exists in the completely formless.’ Reflecting thus, he practices for disenchantment with forms, for their fading away, and for their cessation.

The view of those good ascetics and brahmins who say: “There is no cessation of existence whatsoever” is close to passion, close to bondage, close to delight, close to clinging, close to grasping. But the view of those good ascetics and brahmins who say: “There is indeed the cessation of existence” is close to dispassion, close to non-bondage, close to non-delight, close to non-clinging, close to non-grasping.’ Reflecting thus, he practices for disenchantment with states of existence, for their fading away, and for their cessation.

### Four Kinds of Persons

“Householders, there are these four kinds of persons found existing in the world. What four?

Here, householders, a certain person torments himself, being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself.

Here, householders, a certain person torments others, being devoted to the practice of torturing others.

Here, householders, a certain person torments himself and torments others, being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself and others.

Here, householders, a certain person neither torments himself nor torments others, not being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself or others;

without tormenting oneself or others, in this very life he is |satiated::free from craving, desireless [nicchāta]|, |quenched::liberated from mental defilements [nibbuta]|, |become cool::calmed, liberated [sītibhūta]|, |experiencing ease::feeling comfort [sukhappaṭisaṃvedī]|, and abides with a purified mind like the |Brahmā::God, the first deity to be born at the beginning of a new cosmic cycle and whose lifespan lasts for the entire cycle [brahmā]|.

#### One Who Torments Himself

And what, householders, is a person who torments himself, being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself? Here, householders, a certain person becomes a naked ascetic, rejects social norms, licks his hands clean<collapse>, does not respond when called, does not stop when asked, does not accept food brought or food specially made, and does not consent to an invitation for a meal. He receives nothing from a pot, from a bowl, inside a doorway, near a stick, near a club, from two eating together, from a pregnant woman, from a nursing woman, from a woman in the midst of men, from where food is advertised to be distributed, from where a dog is waiting, or from where flies are buzzing. He does not eat fish or meat, does not drink alcohol, fermented liquor, or sour gruel. He keeps to one house, to one morsel; he keeps to two houses, to two morsels ... he keeps to seven houses, to seven morsels. He sustains himself on one saucerful, or two saucerfuls ... up to seven saucerfuls a day. He takes food once a day, once every two days ... up to once every seven days; thus even up to once every fortnight, he dwells pursuing the practice of taking food at stated intervals. He subsists on raw greens, or on wild millet, or on wild red rice, or on bark parings, or on water plants and slime, or on rice husk powder, or on rice water and pot scum, or on ground sesame, or on grass, or on cow dung. He sustains himself on forest roots and fruits, and eats only naturally occurring fruit. He wears garments made of hemp, hemp-mixed cloth, shrouds, refuse rags, tree bark, antelope hides, strips of antelope hide, woven kusa grass, bark fiber, strips of wood, human hair blankets, horsehair blankets, or owl feathers. He plucks out his hair and beard, devoted to the practice of plucking them out. He remains continuously standing, rejecting seats. He practices squatting as an ascetic discipline, remaining devoted to the squatting posture. He sleeps on a bed of thorns. He bathes three times daily, engaging in ritual bathing.</collapse> Thus, in various ways, he dwells devoted to the practice of torturing and mortifying the body. This is called, householders, a person who torments himself, being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself.

#### One Who Torments Others

And what, householders, is a person who torments others, being devoted to the practice of torturing others? Here, householders, a person is a butcher of sheep, a butcher of pigs<collapse>, a fowler, a deer hunter, a savage huntsman, a fisherman, a thief, an executioner, a slaughterer of cattle, a jailer</collapse>, or anyone else who engages in cruel trades. This is called, householders, a person who torments others, being devoted to the practice of torturing others.

#### One Who Torments Himself and Others

And what, householders, is a person who torments himself and torments others, being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself and others? Here, householders, a person is an anointed noble king or a wealthy high-status brahmin. <collapse>Having built a new sacrificial hall east of the city, shaved his hair and beard, put on a rough hide, and anointed his body with ghee and oil, scratching his back with a deer horn, he enters the new sacrificial hall together with his chief queen and his royal brahmin chaplain. There he sleeps on the bare ground smeared with dung. The king lives on the milk from one teat of a cow that has a calf of like color, the chief queen lives on the second teat, the royal chaplain on the third, the fourth is poured as an offering into the fire, and the calf lives on what remains. He says: ‘Let this many bulls be slaughtered for the sacrifice! Let this many bullocks be slaughtered for the sacrifice! Let this many heifers be slaughtered for the sacrifice! Let this many goats be slaughtered for the sacrifice! Let this many rams be slaughtered for the sacrifice! Let this many horses be slaughtered for the sacrifice! Let this many trees be felled for the sacrificial posts! Let this much grass be mowed for the sacrificial ground!’ And his bondservants, messengers, and workers carry out the preparations</collapse> weeping with tear-stained faces, driven by the fear of punishment and beatings. This is called, householders, a person who torments himself and torments others, being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself and others.

#### One Who Neither Torments Himself Nor Others

And what, householders, is a person who neither torments himself nor torments others, not being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself or others, who without tormenting oneself or others, in this very life is satiated, quenched, become cool, experiencing ease, and abides with a purified mind like the Brahmā?

Here, householders, a |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One arises in the world... Having abandoned these five hindrances, imperfections of the mind that weaken wisdom, entirely secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome mental states, he enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by thought and examination, born of seclusion, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure. Further, with the settling of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second jhāna, which is characterized by internal tranquility and unification of mind, free from thought and examination, born of collectedness, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure. <collapse>Further, with the fading of desire for uplifting joy, he dwells equanimous, mindful and clearly aware, experiencing pleasure with the body;</collapse> he enters and dwells in the third jhāna<collapse>, which the Noble Ones describe as, ‘one who dwells equanimous, mindful, and at ease.’ With the abandoning of pleasure and pain, and with the prior settling down of mental pleasure and displeasure,</collapse> he enters and dwells in the fourth jhāna<collapse>, which is characterized by purification of mindfulness through equanimity, experiencing a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.</collapse>

Thus, with the mind collected, purified, clarified, blemish-free, free from impurities, flexible, suitable for use, stable, and attained to imperturbability, he directs the mind toward the knowledge of recollecting past lives. He recollects his manifold past lives, that is, one birth, two births<collapse>, three births, four, five, ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many |aeon::lifespan of a world system, a vast cosmic time span [kappa]|s of cosmic contraction, many aeons of cosmic expansion, many aeons of cosmic contraction and expansion: “There I was so named, of such a clan, with such an appearance, such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my life-span; passing away from there, I was reborn elsewhere; there too I was so named, of such a clan, with such an appearance, such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my life-span; passing away from there, I was reborn here.”</collapse> Thus he recollects his manifold past lives with their aspects and particulars.

Thus, with the mind collected, purified, clarified, blemish-free, free from impurities, flexible, suitable for use, stable, and attained to imperturbability, he directs the mind toward the knowledge of the passing away and reappearing of beings. With the |divine eye::the faculty of clairvoyance, the ability to see beyond the ordinary human range [dibbacakkhu]|, which is purified and surpassing human vision, he sees beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in fortunate and unfortunate destinations<collapse>—and he understands how beings fare |according to their kamma::in line with their actions [yathākammūpaga]|: “These beings, who engaged in bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct, who reviled the Noble Ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions under the influence of |wrong view::a distorted perception, an untrue view, a false belief [micchādiṭṭhi]|—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, even in hell. But these beings, who engaged in good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct, who did not revile the Noble Ones, held right view, and undertook actions under the influence of |right view::view that is in line with the Dhamma - teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [sammādiṭṭhi]|—upon the breaking up of the body, after death, have arisen in a good destination, a heavenly world.”</collapse> Thus with the divine eye, which is purified and surpassing human vision, he sees beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in fortunate and unfortunate destinations—and he understands how beings fare according to their kamma.

Thus, with the mind collected, purified, clarified, blemish-free, free from impurities, malleable, flexible, suitable for use, stable, and attained to imperturbability, he directs the mind toward the knowledge of the ending of the |taints::outflows, discharges; (comm) mental defilements [āsavā]|. He understands as it actually is, ‘This is |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|,’<collapse> he understands as it actually is, ‘This is the |arising of suffering::source of stress, appearance of discomfort [dukkhasamudaya]|,’ he understands as it actually is, ‘This is the |cessation of suffering::end of suffering, cessation of distress [dukkhanirodha]|,’ he understands as it actually is, ‘This is the |way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering::i.e. the Noble Eightfold Path consisting of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right collectedness [dukkhanirodhagāmī]|.’ He understands as it actually is, ‘These are the taints,’ he understands as it actually is, ‘This is the arising of taints,’ he understands as it actually is, ‘This is the end of taints,’</collapse> he understands as it actually is, ‘This is the way of practice leading to the end of taints.’ Knowing and seeing thus, his mind is liberated from the taint of sensual desire, the taint of becoming, and the taint of |ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]|. In liberation, there arises the knowledge: ‘Liberated.’

He knows clearly: ‘Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.’

This is called, householders, a person who neither torments himself nor torments others, not being devoted to the practice of torturing oneself or others—who, without tormenting oneself or others, in this very life is satiated, quenched, become cool, experiencing ease, and abides with a purified mind like the Brahmā.”

When this was said, the brahmins and householders of Sālā said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! Just as if one were to set upright what had been overturned, or reveal what was hidden, or point the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the darkness so that those with eyesight could see forms—even so has the Dhamma been made clear in many ways by sir Gotama. We go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha. May sir Gotama remember us as lay followers who, from this day forward, have gone for refuge for life.”
