Then, at the end of those seven days, the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]| emerged from that |perfect peace of mind::stability of mind, stillness of mind [samādhi]| and went from the foot of the Rājāyatana tree to the goatherd’s banyan. There the Blessed One stayed at the foot of the goatherd’s banyan.

Then, while the Blessed One was alone in |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, this reflection arose in his mind: “The |Dhamma::the ultimate truth that the Buddha’s teachings point to [dhamma]| I have attained is |profound::hard to fathom [gambhīra]|, hard to perceive, difficult to comprehend, |tranquil::peaceful, calm [santa]|, sublime, |beyond the realm of conjecture::outside the realm of thought, beyond logic [atakkāvacara]|, subtle, to be experienced by the wise. But this generation delights in attachment, is devoted to attachment, and is pleased with attachment. For a generation that delights in attachment, is devoted to attachment and is pleased with attachment, this fact is difficult to see, that is, the |general law of conditionality::actuality of dependence [idappaccayatā]| and |dependent co-arising::the process of arising together from a cause, chain of causation, dependent origination [paṭiccasamuppāda]|. And this fact too is very difficult to see, that is, |the stilling of all intentional constructs::calming of all intentions, volitions, mental activities. This is sometimes rendered as stilling of all formations [sabbasaṅkhārasamatha]|, the |relinquishing of all acquisitions::letting go of all attachments, releasing of all ‘I’, ‘me’, ‘mine’ making [sabbūpadhipaṭinissagga]|, |wearing away of craving::depletion of craving, wanting, yearning, passion [taṇhākkhaya]|, fading of desire, cessation, Nibbāna. If I were to teach the Dhamma and others did not understand me, that would be wearying for me, that would be troublesome for me.”

Then these verses, never heard before, spontaneously occurred to the Blessed One:

“With difficulty I have attained this,
there is no need to now teach;
For those |overwhelmed by craving and aversion::afflicted with lust and hatred, occupied by passion and blame [rāgadosapareta]|,
this Dhamma is not easily understood —
Against the [worldly] flow, refined,
profound, hard to perceive, and subtle;
Those |delighting in passion::infatuated with lust [rāgaratta]| will not see,
veiled as they are by the |mountain of ignorance::heap of darkness [tamokhandha]|.”

As the Blessed One reflected thus, his mind inclined toward being unconcerned, not toward teaching the Dhamma. Then Brahmā |Sahampati::name of the Brahmā who requested the Buddha to teach [sahampati]|, perceiving with his own mind the chain of thought in the Blessed One’s mind, thought: “The world is lost, truly, the world is utterly lost, in that the mind of the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One, inclines toward being unconcerned and not toward teaching the Dhamma.”

Then, just as easily as a strong man might extend his flexed arm or flex his extended arm, Brahmā Sahampati disappeared from the Brahmā world and appeared in front of the Blessed One.

Then Brahmā Sahampati arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, and raised his hands in reverence toward the Blessed One, and said: “Venerable sir, may the Blessed One teach the Dhamma, may the Accomplished One teach the Dhamma. There are beings with little dust in their eyes who are suffering loss from not hearing the Dhamma. There will be those who understand the Dhamma.”

Brahmā Sahampati said this. Having said this, he added further:

“In Magadha, there has appeared in times past
an impure teaching, devised by the defiled;
open the door to the |deathless::deathless state, epithet of Nibbāna [amata]|!
Let them hear the Dhamma that the Unblemished One has discovered.
Just as one who stands on a mountain peak
can see below the people all around;
so too, O wise one, bearer of the Dhamma,
ascend the palace of wisdom, all-seeing one;
let the Sorrowless One survey the world
engulfed in sorrow, overcome by birth and old age.
Arise, hero! Victor in battle,
leader of the caravan, debtless one;
wander in the world, Blessed One, teach the Dhamma,
there will be those who will understand.”

When this was said, the Blessed One said to Brahmā Sahampati: “It occurred to me too, Brahmā: ‘The Dhamma I have attained is profound, hard to perceive, difficult to comprehend, tranquil, sublime, beyond the realm of conjecture, subtle, to be experienced by the wise. But this generation delights in attachment, is devoted to attachment, and is pleased with attachment. For a generation that delights in attachment, is devoted to attachment and is pleased with attachment, this fact is difficult to see, that is, the general law of conditionality and dependent co-arising. And this fact too is very difficult to see, that is, the stilling of all intentional constructs, the relinquishing of all acquisitions, the wearing away of craving, fading of desire, cessation, Nibbāna. If I were to teach the Dhamma and others did not understand me, that would be wearying for me, that would be troublesome for me.’ And these verses, never heard before, spontaneously occurred to me, Brahmā: ‘With difficulty I have attained this,
there is no need to now teach;
For those overwhelmed by craving and aversion,
this Dhamma is not easily understood —
Against the [worldly] flow, refined,
profound, hard to perceive, and subtle;
Those delighting in passion will not see,
veiled as they are by the mountain of ignorance.’
As I reflected thus, Brahmā, my mind inclined toward being unconcerned, not toward teaching the Dhamma.”

A second time Brahmā Sahampati made the same request and spoke the same verses, and a second time the Blessed One gave the same reply. A third time Brahmā Sahampati said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may the Blessed One teach the Dhamma. May the Accomplished One teach the Dhamma. There are beings with little dust in their eyes who are suffering loss from not hearing the Dhamma. There will be those who understand the Dhamma.” And a third time he spoke the same verses.

Then the Blessed One, knowing Brahmā’s request and out of compassion for beings, surveyed the world with the |Buddha’s eye::eye of complete understanding [buddhacakkhu]|.

Surveying the world with the Buddha’s eye, the Blessed One saw beings with little dust in their eyes and with much dust in their eyes, with sharp faculties and with dull faculties, with good qualities and with bad qualities, easy to instruct and difficult to instruct, some who dwelt seeing the problems and the dangers of the next world, and others who dwelt without seeing the problems and the dangers of the next world. Just as in a pond of blue or red or white lotuses, some lotuses that are born and grown in the water thrive immersed in the water without rising out of it, and some other lotuses that are born and grow in the water rest on the water’s surface, and some other lotuses that are born and grow in the water rise out of the water and stand clear, unsoiled by the water; so too, surveying the world with the Buddha’s eye, the Blessed One saw beings with little dust in their eyes and with much dust in their eyes, with sharp faculties and with dull faculties, with good qualities and with bad qualities, easy to instruct and difficult to instruct, some who dwelt seeing the problems and the dangers of the next world, and others who dwelt without seeing the problems and the dangers of the next world.

Having seen this, he replied to Brahmā Sahampati in verse:

“The doors to the deathless are open,
let those with ears now show their faith;
|perceiving as troublesome::thinking it burdensome [vihiṁsasaññī]|, I did not speak, O Brahma!
the subtle and refined Dhamma to humankind.”

Then Brahmā Sahampati, thinking, “The Blessed One has consented to my request that he teach the Dhamma,” paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him keeping him on the right, and disappeared right there.
