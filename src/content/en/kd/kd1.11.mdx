Then the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]|, having stayed at |Vārāṇasī::the city of Benares [bārāṇasī]| as long as he wished, set out on tour toward |Uruvelā::name of a town in Magadha, lit. broad banks [uruvelā]|. Then the Blessed One left the road, entered a certain forest grove, and sat at the foot of a certain tree. At that time about thirty friends of the good group, with their wives, were amusing themselves in that forest grove. One of them had no wife, and a courtesan had been brought for him. Then, while they were heedless and amusing themselves, that courtesan took the goods and ran away.

Then those friends, doing their friend a service and searching for that woman, were wandering about that forest grove and saw the Blessed One sitting at the foot of a certain tree. Seeing him, they approached the Blessed One and said: “Venerable sir, has the Blessed One seen a woman?” “Young men, what have you to do with a woman?” “Here, venerable sir, we are about thirty friends of the good group, with our wives, and we were amusing ourselves in this forest grove. One of us had no wife, and a courtesan had been brought for him. Then, venerable sir, while we were heedless and amusing ourselves, that courtesan took the goods and ran away. We friends, venerable sir, doing our friend a service and searching for that woman, are wandering about this forest grove.”

“What do you think, young men? Which is better for you: that you search for the woman, or that you search for yourselves?” “This is better for us, venerable sir: that we search for ourselves.” “Then, young men, sit down. I will teach you the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|.” “Yes, venerable sir,” those friends of the good group replied. They paid homage to the Blessed One and sat to one side.

The Blessed One gradually gave them a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that their minds were ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the |stainless::dustless, pure, untainted [viraja]|, immaculate |Dhamma eye::vision of the truth, insight into stream-entry [dhammacakkhu]| arose in them: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

They, having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation [brahmacariya]| for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones.
