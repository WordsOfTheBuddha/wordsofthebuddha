Then the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]|, having stayed at Gayā’s Head as long as he wished, set out on tour toward |Rājagaha::the capital of Magadha [rājagaha]| with a great |Saṅgha::the community of disciples practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of bhikkhus, a thousand bhikkhus, all of them former matted-hair ascetics. Then the Blessed One, wandering by stages, arrived at Rājagaha. There the Blessed One stayed at Rājagaha, in the Sapling Grove, at the Suppatiṭṭha shrine.

King Seniya |Bimbisāra::king of Magadha [bimbisāra]| of Magadha heard: “The ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan, gone forth from a Sakyan family, has arrived at Rājagaha and is staying at Rājagaha, in the Sapling Grove, at the Suppatiṭṭha shrine. And a good report has spread about the Blessed One Gotama: ‘The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One. He makes known this world with its gods, its |Māras::embodiment of the forces that keep beings trapped in the cycle of rebirth [māra]|, and its Brahmās, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, its gods and humans, having realized it for himself by direct knowledge. He teaches the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, and he proclaims the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation [brahmacariya]| with its meaning and its phrasing, complete and pure. It is good to see such arahants.’”

Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, accompanied by a hundred and twenty thousand Magadhan brahmin householders, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat to one side. Of those hundred and twenty thousand Magadhan brahmin householders, some paid homage to the Blessed One and sat to one side; some exchanged greetings with the Blessed One, and when they had finished their courteous and polite talk, sat to one side; some saluted the Blessed One with joined palms and sat to one side; some announced their name and clan in the Blessed One’s presence and sat to one side; and some sat to one side in silence.

Then it occurred to those hundred and twenty thousand Magadhan brahmin householders: “Does the great ascetic live the spiritual life under Uruvelakassapa, or does Uruvelakassapa live the spiritual life under the great ascetic?” Then the Blessed One, knowing with his own mind the reflection in their minds, addressed the venerable Uruvelakassapa in verse:
“What did you see, dweller at Uruvelā,
you who are lean from the vow, that you abandoned the fire?
I ask you this matter, Kassapa:
how was your fire-sacrifice abandoned?”
“Forms and sounds, and also tastes,
the women they desire, and the sacrifices they praise —
having known this as a stain among acquisitions,
I therefore take no delight in sacrifice or offering.”

“Your mind did not delight just there,” said the Blessed One,
“in forms, in sounds, and in tastes.
Then in what, in the world of gods and humans,
does your mind delight, Kassapa? Tell me this.”
“Having seen the state that is peaceful, without acquisitions,
nothing, unattached to sensual existence,
that does not become otherwise and is not to be known by another —
therefore I take no delight in sacrifice or offering.”

Then the venerable Uruvelakassapa rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, the Blessed One is my teacher. I am a disciple. Venerable sir, the Blessed One is my teacher. I am a disciple.” Then it occurred to those hundred and twenty thousand Magadhan brahmin householders: “Uruvelakassapa lives the spiritual life under the great ascetic.”

Then the Blessed One, knowing with his own mind the reflection in the minds of those hundred and twenty thousand Magadhan brahmin householders, gradually gave a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that their minds were ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path.

Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the |stainless::dustless, pure, untainted [viraja]|, immaculate |Dhamma eye::vision of the truth, insight into stream-entry [dhammacakkhu]| arose in a hundred and ten thousand Magadhan brahmin householders, with Bimbisāra at their head: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.” Ten thousand declared themselves lay disciples.

Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, when I was a boy I had five hopes, and they are now fulfilled. When I was a boy, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘If only I were consecrated in kingship.’ This, venerable sir, was my first hope, and it is now fulfilled. ‘And may an arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, enter my realm.’ This, venerable sir, was my second hope, and it is now fulfilled.

“‘And may I attend upon that Blessed One.’ This, venerable sir, was my third hope, and it is now fulfilled. ‘And may that Blessed One teach me the Dhamma.’ This, venerable sir, was my fourth hope, and it is now fulfilled. ‘And may I understand that Blessed One’s Dhamma.’ This, venerable sir, was my fifth hope, and it is now fulfilled. When I was a boy, venerable sir, I had these five hopes, and they are now fulfilled.

“Excellent, venerable sir! Excellent, venerable sir! Just as if one were to set upright what had been overturned, reveal what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms—even so, the Dhamma has been explained by the Blessed One in many ways. I go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May the Blessed One remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forward for life. And, venerable sir, may the Blessed One accept tomorrow’s meal, together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.” The Blessed One consented by remaining silent.

Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, knowing that the Blessed One had consented, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him keeping him on the right, and left. Then, at the end of that night, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha had excellent food, hard and soft, prepared and announced the time to the Blessed One: “It is time, venerable sir. The meal is ready.” Then, in the morning, the Blessed One dressed, took his bowl and robe, and entered Rājagaha with a great Saṅgha of bhikkhus, a thousand bhikkhus, all of them former matted-hair ascetics.

At that time |Sakka::king of the gods of the Tāvatiṁsa heaven [sakka]|, lord of the gods, having created the appearance of a young man, walked in front of the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha, singing these verses:
“Tamed, with the tamed, the former matted-hair ascetics,
freed, with the freed —
gold-colored,
the Blessed One entered Rājagaha.
Released, with the released, the former matted-hair ascetics,
freed, with the freed —
gold-colored,
the Blessed One entered Rājagaha.
Crossed over, with those who have crossed over, the former matted-hair ascetics,
freed, with the freed —
gold-colored,
the Blessed One entered Rājagaha.
Peaceful, with the peaceful, the former matted-hair ascetics,
freed, with the freed —
gold-colored,
the Blessed One entered Rājagaha.
Ten-dwelling, ten-powered,
knower of the ten qualities, endowed with ten —
he, with a following of a thousand,
the Blessed One entered Rājagaha.”

People, seeing Sakka, lord of the gods, said: “This young man is handsome indeed. This young man is lovely to see indeed. This young man is inspiring indeed. Whose young man is this?” When this was said, Sakka, lord of the gods, addressed those people in verse:
“He who is steadfast, tamed in every way,
pure, without a counterpart,
the arahant, the well-gone in the world —
I am his attendant.”

Then the Blessed One approached the home of King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha and sat on a prepared seat together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, with his own hands, served and satisfied the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha with excellent food, hard and soft. When the Blessed One had finished his meal and taken his hand from the bowl, the king sat to one side.

Then, while King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha was sitting to one side, it occurred to him: “Where might the Blessed One stay? It should be neither too far from a village nor too near, accessible for coming and going, approachable by people who want to come, not crowded by day, quiet at night and without noise, free of people, a place where humans can withdraw, suitable for seclusion.”

Then it occurred to King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha: “This Bamboo Grove park of ours is neither too far from a village nor too near, accessible for coming and going, approachable by people who want to come, not crowded by day, quiet at night and without noise, free of people, a place where humans can withdraw, suitable for seclusion. Suppose I were to give the Bamboo Grove park to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha.”

Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha took a golden pitcher and offered it to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I give this Bamboo Grove park to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha.” The Blessed One accepted the park. Then the Blessed One instructed, encouraged, roused, and gladdened King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha with a talk on the Dhamma, rose from his seat, and left. Then the Blessed One, with a talk on the Dhamma concerning this occasion and this incident, addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus, I allow a park.”
