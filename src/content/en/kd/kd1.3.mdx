Then, at the end of those seven days, the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]| emerged from that |perfect peace of mind::stability of mind, stillness of mind [samādhi]| and went from the foot of the goatherd’s banyan to the Mucalinda tree. There he sat at the foot of the Mucalinda tree for seven days in one posture, experiencing the ease of liberation.

At that time a great unseasonal rain cloud arose—a weeklong deluge marked by cold winds and bad weather. Then the serpent king |Mucalinda::name of a nāga king [mucalinda]| came forth from his abode, encircled the Blessed One’s body seven times with his coils, and stood with his great hood spread over the Blessed One’s head, thinking: “May the Blessed One not be cold. May the Blessed One not be hot. May the Blessed One not come into contact with gadflies, mosquitoes, wind, sun, or creeping things.”

Then, at the end of those seven days, the serpent king Mucalinda knew that the sky was clear and free of clouds. He uncoiled himself from the Blessed One’s body, withdrew his own form, and created the appearance of a young man. He stood in front of the Blessed One with his hands joined in reverence, honoring the Blessed One. Then, understanding the significance of this, the Blessed One at that time expressed this inspired utterance:

“Pleasant is |seclusion::solitude, detachment [viveka]| for the content one,
who has heard the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| and who sees;
|harmlessness::benevolence, kindness [abyāpajja]| is happiness in the world—
restraint towards living beings.
Pleasant is |dispassion::fading of desire, absence of lust [virāga]| towards the world,
the surpassing of |sensual desire::sensual pleasure [kāma]|;
but for he who has given up the |conceit ‘I am’::egotism, self-conceit, self comparison [asmimāna]|,
this is the highest happiness.”
