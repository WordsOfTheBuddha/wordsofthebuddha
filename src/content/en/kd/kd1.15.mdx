At that time the wanderer |Sañcaya::a wanderer at Rājagaha, teacher of Sāriputta and Moggallāna [sañcaya]| was living at |Rājagaha::the capital of Magadha [rājagaha]| with a great company of wanderers, two hundred and fifty wanderers. At that time |Sāriputta::one of the Buddha’s two chief disciples [sāriputta]| and |Moggallāna::one of the Buddha’s two chief disciples [moggallāna]| were living the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation [brahmacariya]| under the wanderer Sañcaya. They had made a pact: “Whoever reaches the |deathless::deathless state, epithet of Nibbāna [amata]| first is to tell the other.”

Then, in the morning, the venerable |Assaji::one of the group of five, newly gone forth [assaji]| dressed, took his bowl and robe, and entered Rājagaha for alms, pleasing in going forward and going back, in looking ahead and looking aside, in bending and stretching, with eyes cast down, accomplished in deportment. The wanderer Sāriputta saw the venerable Assaji walking for alms in Rājagaha, pleasing in going forward and going back, in looking ahead and looking aside, in bending and stretching, with eyes cast down, accomplished in deportment. Seeing him, it occurred to Sāriputta: “Those in the world who are |arahants::worthy ones, fully awakened beings [arahant]|, or who have entered the path to arahantship — this bhikkhu is one of them. Suppose I were to approach this bhikkhu and ask: ‘For whose sake have you gone forth, friend? Who is your teacher? Whose |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| do you approve of?’”

Then it occurred to the wanderer Sāriputta: “This is not the time to question this bhikkhu. He has entered among the houses and is walking for alms. Suppose I were to follow behind this bhikkhu, a path recognized by those who seek.” Then the venerable Assaji, having walked for alms in Rājagaha and taken his almsfood, returned. Then the wanderer Sāriputta approached the venerable Assaji, exchanged greetings with him, and when they had finished their courteous and polite talk, stood to one side. Standing to one side, the wanderer Sāriputta said to the venerable Assaji: “Friend, your faculties are clear. Your complexion is pure and bright. For whose sake have you gone forth, friend? Who is your teacher? Whose Dhamma do you approve of?”

“There is, friend, the great ascetic, a Sakyan, gone forth from a Sakyan family. I have gone forth for the sake of that |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]|. He is my teacher, and it is his Dhamma that I approve of.” “What does your teacher say, venerable sir? What does he teach?” “I, friend, am new, not long gone forth, lately come to this Dhamma and discipline. I am not able to teach the Dhamma at length. But I can tell you the meaning in brief.” Then the wanderer Sāriputta said to the venerable Assaji: “Let it be so, friend. Speak little or much, but tell me the meaning. Meaning is what I need. What will you do with a lot of phrasing?”

Then the venerable Assaji spoke this exposition of the Dhamma to the wanderer Sāriputta:
“Whatever phenomena arise from a cause,
their cause the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| has told,
and also their cessation.
Thus teaches the great ascetic.”
Then, when the wanderer Sāriputta had heard this exposition of the Dhamma, the |stainless::dustless, pure, untainted [viraja]|, immaculate |Dhamma eye::vision of the truth, insight into stream-entry [dhammacakkhu]| arose in him: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”
“This indeed is the Dhamma, even if only this far.
We have reached the sorrowless state,
unseen, passed over
by many tens of thousands of eons.”

Then the wanderer Sāriputta approached the wanderer Moggallāna. The wanderer Moggallāna saw the wanderer Sāriputta coming in the distance. Seeing him, he said to the wanderer Sāriputta: “Friend, your faculties are clear. Your complexion is pure and bright. Have you reached the deathless, friend?” “Yes, friend, I have reached the deathless.” “And how, friend, did you reach the deathless?”

“Here, friend, I saw the bhikkhu Assaji walking for alms in Rājagaha, pleasing in going forward and going back, in looking ahead and looking aside, in bending and stretching, with eyes cast down, accomplished in deportment. Seeing him, it occurred to me: ‘Those in the world who are arahants, or who have entered the path to arahantship — this bhikkhu is one of them. Suppose I were to approach this bhikkhu and ask: “For whose sake have you gone forth, friend? Who is your teacher? Whose Dhamma do you approve of?”’ Then, friend, it occurred to me: ‘This is not the time to question this bhikkhu. He has entered among the houses and is walking for alms. Suppose I were to follow behind this bhikkhu, a path recognized by those who seek.’ Then, friend, the bhikkhu Assaji, having walked for alms in Rājagaha and taken his almsfood, returned. Then I, friend, approached the bhikkhu Assaji, exchanged greetings with him, and when we had finished our courteous and polite talk, I stood to one side. Standing to one side, I said to the bhikkhu Assaji: ‘Friend, your faculties are clear. Your complexion is pure and bright. For whose sake have you gone forth, friend? Who is your teacher? Whose Dhamma do you approve of?’ ‘There is, friend, the great ascetic, a Sakyan, gone forth from a Sakyan family. I have gone forth for the sake of that Blessed One. He is my teacher, and it is his Dhamma that I approve of.’ ‘What does your teacher say, venerable sir? What does he teach?’ ‘I, friend, am new, not long gone forth, lately come to this Dhamma and discipline. I am not able to teach the Dhamma at length. But I can tell you the meaning in brief.’ Then I, friend, said to the bhikkhu Assaji: ‘Let it be so, friend. Speak little or much, but tell me the meaning. Meaning is what I need. What will you do with a lot of phrasing?’ Then, friend, the bhikkhu Assaji spoke this exposition of the Dhamma: ‘Whatever phenomena arise from a cause, their cause the Tathāgata has told, and also their cessation. Thus teaches the great ascetic.’” Then, when the wanderer Moggallāna had heard this exposition of the Dhamma, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in him: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.” “This indeed is the Dhamma, even if only this far. We have reached the sorrowless state, unseen, passed over by many tens of thousands of eons.”

Then the wanderer Moggallāna said to the wanderer Sāriputta: “Friend, let us go to the Blessed One. He is our teacher.” “Friend, these two hundred and fifty wanderers are living here looking to us and depending on us. Let us ask them first. They will do as they think.” Then Sāriputta and Moggallāna approached those wanderers and said: “Friends, let us go to the Blessed One. He is our teacher.” “We are living here looking to you and depending on you. If you will live the spiritual life under the great ascetic, all of us will live the spiritual life under the great ascetic.”

Then Sāriputta and Moggallāna approached the wanderer Sañcaya and said: “Friend, let us go to the Blessed One. He is our teacher.” “Enough, friends. Do not go. All three of us will lead this company.” A second time they said the same thing, and a third time Sāriputta and Moggallāna said to the wanderer Sañcaya: “Friend, let us go to the Blessed One. He is our teacher.” “Enough, friends. Do not go. All three of us will lead this company.”

Then Sāriputta and Moggallāna took those two hundred and fifty wanderers and approached the Bamboo Grove. And hot blood rose from the mouth of the wanderer Sañcaya right there. The Blessed One saw Sāriputta and Moggallāna coming in the distance. Seeing them, he addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus, these two companions are coming, Kolita and Upatissa. This will be my pair of disciples, the chief, the auspicious pair.”
In the deep field of knowledge,
unsurpassed, in the wearing away of acquisitions,
freed, they reached the Bamboo Grove,
and then the Teacher declared of them:
“These two companions
are coming, Kolita and Upatissa.
This will be my pair of disciples,
the chief, the auspicious pair.”

Then Sāriputta and Moggallāna approached the Blessed One, placed their heads at the Blessed One’s feet, and said: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones.
