Then the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]|, wandering by stages, arrived at |Uruvelā::name of a town in Magadha, lit. broad banks [uruvelā]|. At that time three matted-hair ascetics were living at Uruvelā: |Uruvelakassapa::the matted-hair ascetic who led five hundred [uruvelakassapa]|, |Nadīkassapa::his brother, who led three hundred [nadīkassapa]|, and |Gayākassapa::his brother, who led two hundred [gayākassapa]|. Of them, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa was the leader, guide, chief, foremost, and head of five hundred matted-hair ascetics. The matted-hair ascetic Nadīkassapa was the leader, guide, chief, foremost, and head of three hundred. The matted-hair ascetic Gayākassapa was the leader, guide, chief, foremost, and head of two hundred.

Then the Blessed One approached the hermitage of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa and said to him: “Kassapa, if it is not troublesome for you, may I stay one night in the fire hall?” “It is not troublesome for me, great ascetic. But a fierce serpent king is in there, powerful, a venomous snake, terribly venomous. May he not harm you.” A second time the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “Kassapa, if it is not troublesome for you, may I stay one night in the fire hall?” “It is not troublesome for me, great ascetic. But a fierce serpent king is in there, powerful, a venomous snake, terribly venomous. May he not harm you.” A third time the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “Kassapa, if it is not troublesome for you, may I stay one night in the fire hall?” “It is not troublesome for me, great ascetic. But a fierce serpent king is in there, powerful, a venomous snake, terribly venomous. May he not harm you.” “Perhaps he will not harm me. Come, Kassapa, allow the fire hall.” “Stay, great ascetic, as you like.”

Then the Blessed One entered the fire hall, spread a grass mat, and sat down, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect, and establishing |mindfulness::attentive observing of the present, wakefulness, presence of mind [sati]| in front of him. The serpent saw that the Blessed One had entered. Seeing him, he was dejected and blew smoke. Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Suppose I were to overcome this serpent’s fire with fire, without harming his skin, his hide, his flesh, his sinews, his bones, or his bone-marrow.”

Then the Blessed One performed such a feat of psychic power and blew smoke. Then the serpent, unable to bear the rivalry, blazed. The Blessed One too, having attained the fire element, blazed. With both of them ablaze, the fire hall was as if on fire, burning and ablaze. Then those matted-hair ascetics surrounded the fire hall and said: “The great ascetic is handsome indeed, and he is being harmed by the serpent.”

Then, at the end of that night, the Blessed One, having overcome that serpent’s fire with fire without harming his skin, his hide, his flesh, his sinews, his bones, or his bone-marrow, placed him in his bowl and showed him to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “This, Kassapa, is your serpent. His fire has been overcome by fire.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that he overcomes with fire the fire of a fierce serpent king, powerful, a venomous snake, terribly venomous. But he is not an |arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being [arahant]| as I am.”

Then, at the end of that night, the serpent’s flames were destroyed, while the flames of the powerful one remained, flames of many colors. Blue, and red, crimson, yellow, and the color of crystal — on the body of the Radiant One there were flames of many colors. Having placed the serpent in the bowl, he showed him to the brahmin: “This, Kassapa, is your serpent. His fire has been overcome by fire.” Then the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, pleased by this wonder of psychic power, said to the Blessed One: “Stay right here, great ascetic. I will provide you with a regular meal.” This was the first wonder.

Then the Blessed One stayed in a certain forest grove not far from the hermitage of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa. Then, when the night was far advanced, the |Four Great Kings::the four divine kings who guard the quarters [cattāro mahārājāno]|, of excellent appearance, lighting up the whole forest grove, approached the Blessed One, paid homage, and stood at the four directions like great masses of fire.

Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and said: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready. Who were they, great ascetic, who, when the night was far advanced, of excellent appearance, lighting up the whole forest grove, approached you, paid homage, and stood at the four directions like great masses of fire?” “They, Kassapa, were the Four Great Kings, who approached me to hear the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even the Four Great Kings approach him to hear the Dhamma. But he is not an arahant as I am.” Then the Blessed One, having eaten the meal of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, stayed in that same forest grove. This was the second wonder.

Then, when the night was far advanced, |Sakka::king of the gods of the Tāvatiṁsa heaven [sakka]|, lord of the gods, of excellent appearance, lighting up the whole forest grove, approached the Blessed One, paid homage, and stood to one side like a great mass of fire, more excellent and more refined than the previous splendor.

Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and said: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready. Who was he, great ascetic, who, when the night was far advanced, of excellent appearance, lighting up the whole forest grove, approached you, paid homage, and stood to one side like a great mass of fire, more excellent and more refined than the previous splendor?” “He, Kassapa, was Sakka, lord of the gods, who approached me to hear the Dhamma.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even Sakka, lord of the gods, approaches him to hear the Dhamma. But he is not an arahant as I am.” Then the Blessed One, having eaten the meal of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, stayed in that same forest grove. This was the third wonder.

Then, when the night was far advanced, Brahmā |Sahampati::name of the Brahmā who requested the Buddha to teach [sahampati]|, of excellent appearance, lighting up the whole forest grove, approached the Blessed One, paid homage, and stood to one side like a great mass of fire, more excellent and more refined than the previous splendor.

Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and said: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready. Who was he, great ascetic, who, when the night was far advanced, of excellent appearance, lighting up the whole forest grove, approached you, paid homage, and stood to one side like a great mass of fire, more excellent and more refined than the previous splendor?” “He, Kassapa, was Brahmā Sahampati, who approached me to hear the Dhamma.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even Brahmā Sahampati approaches him to hear the Dhamma. But he is not an arahant as I am.” Then the Blessed One, having eaten the meal of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, stayed in that same forest grove. This was the fourth wonder.

At that time a great sacrifice was at hand for the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, and all of Aṅga and Magadha wanted to come, bringing abundant food, hard and soft. Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “Now my great sacrifice is at hand, and all of Aṅga and Magadha will come, bringing abundant food, hard and soft. If the great ascetic performs a wonder of psychic power before the great crowd, the great ascetic’s gains and honor will increase, and my gains and honor will decline. If only the great ascetic would not come tomorrow.”

Then the Blessed One, knowing with his own mind the reflection in the mind of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, went to Uttarakuru, brought almsfood from there, ate it at Lake Anotatta, and spent the day right there. Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and said: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready. Why, great ascetic, did you not come yesterday? We remembered you, thinking: ‘Why does the great ascetic not come?’ A share of the food, hard and soft, was set aside for you.”

“Did it not occur to you, Kassapa: ‘Now my great sacrifice is at hand, and all of Aṅga and Magadha will come, bringing abundant food, hard and soft. If the great ascetic performs a wonder of psychic power before the great crowd, the great ascetic’s gains and honor will increase, and my gains and honor will decline. If only the great ascetic would not come tomorrow’?

“So I, Kassapa, knowing with my own mind the reflection in your mind, went to Uttarakuru, brought almsfood from there, ate it at Lake Anotatta, and spent the day right there.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that he knows even a mind with his mind. But he is not an arahant as I am.” Then the Blessed One, having eaten the meal of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, stayed in that same forest grove. This was the fifth wonder.

At that time a rag-robe had come to the Blessed One. Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Where should I wash this rag-robe?” Then Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the reflection in the Blessed One’s mind, dug a pond with his hand and said to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One wash the rag-robe.” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “On what should I rub the rag-robe?” Then Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the reflection in the Blessed One’s mind, placed a large stone nearby: “Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One rub the rag-robe.”

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Holding on to what should I come up out of the water?” Then a deity living in a |kakudha tree::a tree with a crooked trunk [kakudha]| , knowing with his own mind the reflection in the Blessed One’s mind, bent down a branch: “Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One hold on and come up.” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “On what should I lay out the rag-robe?” Then Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the reflection in the Blessed One’s mind, placed a large stone nearby: “Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One lay out the rag-robe.”

Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and said: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready. How is it, great ascetic, that this pond was not here before, and now this pond is here? These stones were not placed here before. By whom were these stones placed here? This kakudha tree’s branch was not bent down before, and now the branch is bent down.”

“Here, Kassapa, a rag-robe had come to me. Then, Kassapa, it occurred to me: ‘Where should I wash this rag-robe?’ Then, Kassapa, Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the reflection in my mind, dug a pond with his hand and said to me: ‘Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One wash the rag-robe.’ This pond, Kassapa, was dug by a non-human’s hand. Then, Kassapa, it occurred to me: ‘On what should I rub the rag-robe?’ Then, Kassapa, Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the reflection in my mind, placed a large stone nearby: ‘Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One rub the rag-robe.’ This stone, Kassapa, was placed here by a non-human.

“Then, Kassapa, it occurred to me: ‘Holding on to what should I come up out of the water?’ Then, Kassapa, a deity living in the kakudha tree, knowing with his own mind the reflection in my mind, bent down a branch: ‘Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One hold on and come up.’ So this kakudha tree had a branch brought within reach. Then, Kassapa, it occurred to me: ‘On what should I lay out the rag-robe?’ Then, Kassapa, Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the reflection in my mind, placed a large stone nearby: ‘Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One lay out the rag-robe.’ This stone, Kassapa, was placed here by a non-human.”

Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even Sakka, lord of the gods, performs a service for him. But he is not an arahant as I am.” Then the Blessed One, having eaten the meal of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, stayed in that same forest grove.

Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and announced the time: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready.” “You go, Kassapa. I am coming.” Having sent the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa on ahead, he took a fruit from the rose-apple tree by which the Rose-apple Land is known, arrived first, and sat in the fire hall.

The matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa saw the Blessed One sitting in the fire hall. Seeing him, he said to the Blessed One: “By which road did you come, great ascetic? I left before you, and yet you arrived first and are sitting in the fire hall.”

“Here, Kassapa, having sent you on ahead, I took a fruit from the rose-apple tree by which the Rose-apple Land is known, arrived first, and am sitting in the fire hall. This rose-apple fruit, Kassapa, is excellent in color, excellent in scent, and excellent in taste. If you wish, eat it.” “Enough, great ascetic. You deserve it. You eat it.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that, having sent me on ahead, he takes a fruit from the rose-apple tree by which the Rose-apple Land is known, arrives first, and sits in the fire hall. But he is not an arahant as I am.” Then the Blessed One, having eaten the meal of the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa, stayed in that same forest grove.

Then, at the end of that night, the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached the Blessed One and announced the time: “It is time, great ascetic. The meal is ready.” “You go, Kassapa. I am coming.” Having sent him on ahead, he went, in the same way, to a mango tree not far from that rose-apple, then to an emblic myrobalan, then to a yellow myrobalan, and then to the |Tāvatiṁsa::the heaven of the Thirty-three [tāvatiṁsa]| heaven, took a flower of the coral tree, arrived first, and sat in the fire hall. The matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa saw the Blessed One sitting in the fire hall. Seeing him, he said: “By which road did you come, great ascetic? I left before you, and yet you arrived first and are sitting in the fire hall.”

“Here, Kassapa, having sent you on ahead, I went to the Tāvatiṁsa heaven, took a flower of the coral tree, arrived first, and am sitting in the fire hall. This coral-tree flower, Kassapa, is excellent in color and excellent in scent. If you wish, take it.” “Enough, great ascetic. You deserve it. At that time those matted-hair ascetics, wanting to tend the fire, were unable to split the logs. Then it occurred to those matted-hair ascetics: “Surely this is the great ascetic’s psychic power, that we are unable to split the logs.” Then the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “Kassapa, let the logs split.” “Let them split, great ascetic.” At once five hundred logs split. Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even the logs split. But he is not an arahant as I am.”

At that time those matted-hair ascetics, wanting to tend the fire, were unable to kindle the fires. Then it occurred to them: “Surely this is the great ascetic’s psychic power, that we are unable to kindle the fires.” Then the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “Kassapa, let the fires kindle.” “Let them kindle, great ascetic.” At once five hundred fires kindled. Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even the fires kindle. But he is not an arahant as I am.”

At that time those matted-hair ascetics, having tended the fire, were unable to put the fires out. Then it occurred to them: “Surely this is the great ascetic’s psychic power, that we are unable to put the fires out.” Then the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “Kassapa, let the fires go out.” “Let them go out, great ascetic.” At once five hundred fires went out. Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even the fires go out. But he is not an arahant as I am.”

At that time, on cold winter nights, in the ‘between the eights,’ at a time of snowfall, those matted-hair ascetics were plunging into the river Nerañjarā, emerging, and plunging and emerging. Then the Blessed One created about five hundred braziers, where those matted-hair ascetics came up and warmed themselves. Then it occurred to those matted-hair ascetics: “Surely this is the great ascetic’s psychic power, that these braziers have been created.” Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that he creates even so many braziers. But he is not an arahant as I am.”

At that time a great unseasonal rain cloud rained, and a great flood arose. The place where the Blessed One was staying was not covered by the water. Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Suppose I were to push the water back all around and walk in the middle on ground covered with dust.” Then the Blessed One pushed the water back all around and walked in the middle on ground covered with dust. Then the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa thought, “I hope the great ascetic has not been swept away by the water,” and went by boat, with a number of matted-hair ascetics, to the place where the Blessed One was staying. The matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa saw the Blessed One, who had pushed the water back all around and was walking in the middle on ground covered with dust. Seeing him, he said to the Blessed One: “Is this you, great ascetic?” “It is I, Kassapa.” And the Blessed One rose into the air and stood in the boat. Then it occurred to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, in that even the water does not sweep him away. But he is not an arahant as I am.”

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “For a long time this |misguided man::vain person [moghapurisa]| will keep thinking, ‘The great ascetic is powerful and mighty, but he is not an arahant as I am.’ Suppose I were to stir this matted-hair ascetic.” Then the Blessed One said to the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa: “You are not an arahant, Kassapa, nor have you entered the path to arahantship. You do not even have the practice by which you could be an arahant or one who has entered the path to arahantship.” Then the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa placed his head at the Blessed One’s feet and said: “Venerable sir, may I receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May I receive full ordination?”

“You, Kassapa, are the leader, guide, chief, foremost, and head of five hundred matted-hair ascetics. Ask them first. They will do as they think.” Then the matted-hair ascetic Uruvelakassapa approached those matted-hair ascetics and said: “Sirs, I wish to live the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation [brahmacariya]| under the great ascetic. Do as you think.” “Sir, we have long been pleased with the great ascetic. If you will live the spiritual life under the great ascetic, all of us will live the spiritual life under the great ascetic.”

Then those matted-hair ascetics, having thrown their hair, their matted locks, their carrying-poles, and their fire-worship implements into the water, approached the Blessed One, placed their heads at the Blessed One’s feet, and said: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones.

The matted-hair ascetic Nadīkassapa saw the hair, the matted locks, the carrying-poles, and the fire-worship implements being carried along in the water. By the Blessed One’s resolve, five hundred logs did not split, and then they did; the fires did not kindle, and then they did; they did not go out, and then they did; and five hundred braziers were created. In this way there were three and a half thousand wonders.
