Then, at the end of those seven days, the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]| emerged from that |perfect peace of mind::stability of mind, stillness of mind [samādhi]| and went from the foot of the Mucalinda tree to the |Rājāyatana::a tree near Uruvelā, the royal tree [rājāyatana]|. There he sat at the foot of the Rājāyatana tree for seven days in one posture, experiencing the ease of liberation.

At that time the merchants |Tapussa::name of a merchant from Ukkala [tapussa]| and |Bhallika::name of a merchant from Ukkala [bhallika]|, from |Ukkala::a country, identified with present-day Orissa [ukkala]|, were traveling along that road. Then a deity who was a blood relative of the merchants Tapussa and Bhallika said to them: “Good sirs, this Blessed One is staying at the foot of the Rājāyatana tree, having just attained full awakening. Go and serve that Blessed One with barley meal and a honey ball. That will be for your welfare and happiness for a long time.”

Then the merchants Tapussa and Bhallika took barley meal and a honey ball and approached the Blessed One. Having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and stood to one side. Standing to one side, they said: “Venerable sir, may the Blessed One accept our barley meal and honey ball, so that it may be for our welfare and happiness for a long time.”

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “|Tathāgatas::ones who have arrived at the truth, epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| do not accept food in their hands. In what should I accept the barley meal and the honey ball?” Then the |Four Great Kings::the four divine kings who guard the quarters [cattāro mahārājāno]|, knowing with their own minds the reflection in the Blessed One’s mind, presented the Blessed One with four stone bowls from the four directions: “Here, venerable sir, may the Blessed One accept the barley meal and the honey ball.” The Blessed One accepted the barley meal and the honey ball in a new stone bowl, and having accepted it, he ate.

Then the merchants Tapussa and Bhallika, knowing that the Blessed One had finished his meal and taken his hand from the bowl, placed their heads at the Blessed One’s feet and said: “We, venerable sir, go for refuge to the Blessed One and to the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|. May the Blessed One remember us as lay followers who have gone for refuge from this day forward for life.” And they were the first lay disciples in the world, with the twofold formula.
