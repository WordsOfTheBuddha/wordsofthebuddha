Then it occurred to the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]|: “To whom should I first teach the |Dhamma::the ultimate truth that the Buddha’s teachings point to [dhamma]|? Who will quickly understand this Dhamma?” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Āḷāra Kālāma is wise, learned, intelligent, and has for a long time had little dust in his eyes. Suppose I were to teach the Dhamma first to Āḷāra Kālāma. He would quickly understand this Dhamma.”

Then an invisible deity informed the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, Āḷāra Kālāma passed away seven days ago.” And knowledge and vision arose in the Blessed One too: “Āḷāra Kālāma passed away seven days ago.” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Āḷāra Kālāma has incurred a great loss. If he had heard this Dhamma, he would have understood it quickly.”

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “To whom should I first teach the Dhamma? Who will quickly understand this Dhamma?” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Uddaka Rāmaputta is wise, learned, intelligent, and has for a long time had little dust in his eyes. Suppose I were to teach the Dhamma first to Uddaka Rāmaputta. He would quickly understand this Dhamma.”

Then an invisible deity informed the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, Uddaka Rāmaputta passed away yesterday evening.” And knowledge and vision arose in the Blessed One too: “Uddaka Rāmaputta passed away yesterday evening.” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Uddaka Rāmaputta has incurred a great loss. If he had heard this Dhamma, he would have understood it quickly.”

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “To whom should I first teach the Dhamma? Who will quickly understand this Dhamma?” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “The group of five bhikkhus who attended upon me when I was resolutely striving.

Suppose I were to teach the Dhamma first to the group of five bhikkhus.” Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “Where are the group of five bhikkhus living now?” Then, with the |divine eye::the faculty of clairvoyance, the ability to see beyond the ordinary human range [dibbacakkhu]|, purified and surpassing human vision, the Blessed One saw the group of five bhikkhus living in |Varanasi::name of a city; modern day Varanasi [bārāṇasī]|, in the Deer Park at |Isipatana::name of a forest near Varanasi [isipatana]|. Then, after dwelling at |Uruvelā::name of a town in Magadha, lit. broad banks [uruvelā]| as long as he wished, the Blessed One set out on a journey to Varanasi.

|Upaka::an Ājīvaka wanderer who met the Buddha on the road to Varanasi [upaka]| the |Ājīvaka::A contemporary movement during the lifetime of the Buddha that professed that all events unfold by fixed cosmic destiny and actions have no consequences. [ājīvaka]| saw the Blessed One walking along the road between |Gayā::name of a village [gayā]| and the Bodhi Tree. Seeing him, he said: “Friend, your faculties are clear, your complexion is pure and bright. Who is your teacher, friend? For whom have you gone forth? Whose Dhamma do you follow?”

When this was said, the Blessed One addressed Upaka the Ājīvaka in verse:
“Victorious over everything, all I have known,
yet I remain |untainted::unstained, unsullied [anūpalitta]| by all things conquered and known.
Having abandoned all, liberated through the |complete ending of craving::gradual wearing away of craving, depletion of desire, extinction of longing [taṇhakkhaya]|,
thus directly knowing by myself—whom should I call as my teacher?
I have no teacher,
and one like me does not exist;
in the world with its |deities::gods [devas]|,
there is none equal to me.
I am |the Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]| in the world;
I am the unsurpassed teacher;
I alone am the perfectly Awakened One;
I am cooled and quenched.
To set the wheel of Dhamma in motion,
I go to the city of |Kāsī::modern day Varanasi [kāsī]|.
In a world that has become blind,
I will beat the drum of the |deathless::deathless state, epithet of Nibbāna [amata]|.”

“As you claim, friend, you are the worthy |universal conqueror::infinite victor [anantajina]|.”
“Conquerors like me,
are those who have reached the end of defilements;
harmful qualities have been vanquished by me,
and so, Upaka, I am a conqueror.”
When this was said, Upaka the Ājīvaka replied, “Perhaps so, friend,” shook his head, took a side road, and departed.

Then, wandering by stages, the Blessed One arrived at Varanasi, in the Deer Park at Isipatana, where the group of five bhikkhus were staying. The group of five bhikkhus saw the Blessed One coming from afar. Seeing him, they agreed among themselves: “Friends, here comes the ascetic Gotama, who lives excessively, who has strayed from his striving, and reverted to |excess::self-indulgence, hedonism; lit. abundant state [bāhulla]|. He should not be greeted, nor stood up for, nor should his bowl and robe be taken. However, a seat should be set out. If he wishes, he may sit.”

But as the Blessed One approached, the group of five bhikkhus were unable to stand by their agreement. One received his bowl and robe, one prepared a seat, one set out water for the feet, one set out a footstool, and one set out a foot-scraping stone. The Blessed One sat on the prepared seat and washed his feet. However, they still addressed him by name and as “friend.”

When this was said, the Blessed One said this to the group of five bhikkhus: “Do not address the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| by name and as ‘friend.’ The Tathāgata is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One. Listen carefully, bhikkhus, the deathless has been attained. I shall instruct you. I shall teach you the Dhamma. Practicing as instructed, you will before long enter and abide in that unsurpassed goal of the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]|, realizing it for yourselves with direct knowledge here and now, the very goal for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness.”

When this was said, the group of five bhikkhus said this to the Blessed One: “By that conduct, friend Gotama, by that practice, by that |performance of difficult tasks::practice of austerities, gruelling discipline [dukkarakārikā]|, you did not achieve any superhuman state, any distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of a noble person. So how will you now, living excessively, having strayed from striving, and having reverted to excess, achieve any superhuman state, any distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of a noble person?”

When this was said, the Blessed One said this to the group of five bhikkhus: “The Tathāgata does not live excessively, has not strayed from striving, and has not reverted to excess. The Tathāgata is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One. Listen carefully, bhikkhus, the deathless has been attained. I shall instruct you. I shall teach you the Dhamma. Practicing as instructed, you will before long enter and abide in that unsurpassed goal of the spiritual life, realizing it for yourselves with direct knowledge here and now, the very goal for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness.”

A second time the group of five bhikkhus said the same thing, and a second time the Blessed One replied in the same way. A third time the group of five bhikkhus said this to the Blessed One: “By that conduct, friend Gotama, by that practice, by that performance of difficult tasks, you did not achieve any superhuman state, any distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of a noble person. So how will you now, living excessively, having strayed from striving, and having reverted to excess, achieve any superhuman state, any distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of a noble person?”

When this was said, the Blessed One asked the group of five bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus, have you ever known me to speak like this before?” “No, venerable sir.” “The Tathāgata is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One. Listen carefully, bhikkhus, the deathless has been attained. I shall instruct you. I shall teach you the Dhamma. Practicing as instructed, you will before long enter and abide in that unsurpassed goal of the spiritual life, realizing it for yourselves with direct knowledge here and now, the very goal for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness.” The Blessed One was able to convince the group of five bhikkhus. Then the group of five bhikkhus listened to the Blessed One, gave ear, and set their minds on understanding.

Then the venerable Aññā Koṇḍañña, having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may I receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May I receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhu. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was that venerable one’s full ordination.

Then the Blessed One instructed and advised the remaining bhikkhus with a talk on the Dhamma. And while they were being instructed and advised by the Blessed One with a talk on the Dhamma, the |stainless::dustless, pure, untainted [viraja]|, immaculate |Dhamma eye::vision of the truth, insight into stream-entry [dhammacakkhu]| arose in the venerable Vappa and the venerable Bhaddiya: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

They, having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones.

Then the Blessed One, living on the meal that was brought, instructed and advised the remaining bhikkhus with a talk on the Dhamma. Whatever the three bhikkhus brought back from their alms round, the group of six lived on that.

Then, while they were being instructed and advised by the Blessed One with a talk on the Dhamma, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in the venerable Mahānāma and the venerable Assaji: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

They, having seen the Dhamma and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones.

While it was being spoken, the minds of the group of five bhikkhus were liberated from the taints through non-clinging.</p>

At that time there were six arahants in the world.
