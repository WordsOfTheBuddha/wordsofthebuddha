Then, at the end of those seven days, the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]| emerged from that |perfect peace of mind::stability of mind, stillness of mind [samādhi]| and went from the foot of the Bodhi tree to the goatherd’s banyan. There he sat at the foot of the goatherd’s banyan for seven days in one posture, experiencing the ease of liberation.

Then a certain brahmin of the |Huṁhuṅka clan::humphy, huffy, one who snorts to express indignation or derision [huṁhuṅka + jātika]| approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One. After the exchange of a courteous and polite conversation, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, that brahmin said this to the Blessed One: “To what extent, sir Gotama, is one a |sage::Brāhmaṇa, a title used for an Arahant, an awakened being [brāhmaṇa]|, and what are the qualities that make one a sage?”

Then, understanding the significance of this, the Blessed One at that time expressed this inspired utterance:

“That sage who has barred out |harmful::injurious, bad, or evil. Encompasses the deceptively alluring that is ultimately detrimental or ruinous [pāpaka]| qualities,
who is |free from haughtiness::not huffy; lit. not humph humph [nihuṁhuṅka]|, stainless, and self-controlled;
|who has complete knowledge::epithet of an arahant; lit. gone to the end of the Vedas [vedantagū]| and lived the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]|,
rightly could that person speak the word of a sage;
for whom there is no |swelling of pride::arrogance, conceit, self-importance [ussada]| anywhere in the world.”
