#### Yasa

At that time in |Vārāṇasī::the city of Benares [bārāṇasī]| there was a young man of family named |Yasa::a merchant’s son of Vārāṇasī [yasa]|, a merchant’s son, delicate. He had three mansions: one for the winter, one for the summer, and one for the rainy season. During the four months in the rains mansion, entertained by female musicians, he did not come down from the mansion.

Then, while the young man Yasa was supplied and endowed with the five strands of sensual pleasure and was being entertained, sleep came over him early, and sleep came over the attendants too, and an oil lamp burned all night. Then the young man Yasa woke early and saw his own attendants sleeping — one with a lute under her arm, one with a drum at her throat, one with a small drum under her arm, one with hair disheveled, one drooling, and others muttering — like a charnel ground come within reach. Seeing this, the drawback became evident to him, and his mind settled in |disenchantment::dissatisfaction, disillusionment, loss of interest [nibbidā]|. Then the young man Yasa uttered this inspired utterance: “It is afflicted indeed, sir! It is oppressed indeed, sir!”

Then the young man Yasa put on his golden slippers and went to the door of the house. Non-human beings opened the door, thinking: “Let no one make an obstacle for the young man Yasa’s going forth from home into homelessness.” Then the young man Yasa went to the city gate. Non-human beings opened the door, thinking: “Let no one make an obstacle for the young man Yasa’s going forth from home into homelessness.” Then the young man Yasa went to the Deer Park at |Isipatana::the seers’ resort, near Vārāṇasī [isipatana]|.

At that time the |Blessed One::Sublime One, Fortunate One, epithet of the Buddha [bhagavā]|, having risen in the last watch of the night, was walking in the open. The Blessed One saw the young man Yasa coming in the distance. Seeing him, he came down from the walking path and sat on a prepared seat. Then the young man Yasa, not far from the Blessed One, uttered this inspired utterance: “It is afflicted indeed, sir! It is oppressed indeed, sir!” Then the Blessed One said to the young man Yasa: “This, Yasa, is unafflicted. This is unoppressed. Come, Yasa, sit. I will teach you the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|.”

Then the young man Yasa, thinking, “This, it seems, is unafflicted; this is unoppressed,” joyful and glad, stepped down from his golden slippers, approached the Blessed One, paid homage, and sat to one side. While the young man Yasa was sitting to one side, the Blessed One gradually gave a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation.

When the Blessed One knew that the young man Yasa’s mind was ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the |stainless::dustless, pure, untainted [viraja]|, immaculate |Dhamma eye::vision of the truth, insight into stream-entry [dhammacakkhu]| arose in the young man Yasa: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

Then the young man Yasa’s mother, having gone up into the mansion and not seeing the young man Yasa, approached the merchant householder and said: “Householder, your son Yasa is not to be seen.” Then the merchant householder sent messengers on horseback to the four directions and himself went to the Deer Park at Isipatana. The merchant householder saw the place where the golden slippers had been set down, and seeing that, he followed in that direction.

The Blessed One saw the merchant householder coming in the distance. Seeing him, it occurred to the Blessed One: “Suppose I were to perform such a feat of psychic power that the merchant householder, sitting here, would not see the young man Yasa sitting here.” Then the Blessed One performed such a feat of psychic power.

Then the merchant householder approached the Blessed One and said: “Venerable sir, has the Blessed One seen the young man Yasa?” “Then, householder, sit down. Perhaps, sitting here, you will see the young man Yasa sitting here.” Then the merchant householder, thinking, “Sitting right here, it seems, I will see the young man Yasa sitting here,” joyful and glad, paid homage to the Blessed One and sat to one side.

While the merchant householder was sitting to one side, the Blessed One gradually gave a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that the merchant householder’s mind was ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in the merchant householder: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.” Then the merchant householder, having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, venerable sir! Excellent, venerable sir! Just as if one were to set upright what had been overturned, reveal what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms—even so, the Dhamma has been explained by the Blessed One in many ways. I go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the |Saṅgha::the community of disciples practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of bhikkhus. May the Blessed One remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forward for life.” And he was the first lay disciple in the world, with the threefold formula.

Then, while the Dhamma was being taught to the young man Yasa’s father, the young man Yasa, reviewing the ground as he had seen it and understood it, was liberated in mind from the taints through non-clinging. Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “While the Dhamma was being taught to the young man Yasa’s father, the young man Yasa, reviewing the ground as he had seen it and understood it, was liberated in mind from the taints through non-clinging. The young man Yasa is incapable of returning to the lower life and enjoying sensual pleasures as he did before, when he was a layman. Suppose I were to still that feat of psychic power.” Then the Blessed One stilled that feat of psychic power.

The merchant householder saw the young man Yasa sitting there. Seeing him, he said to the young man Yasa: “Dear Yasa, your mother is overcome by grief and lamentation. |Give your mother her life::restore her from her grief [mātuyā jīvitaṁ deti]|.”

Then the young man Yasa looked up at the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One said to the merchant householder: “What do you think, householder? One for whom the Dhamma has been seen and understood with a |trainee’s::one who has entered the path and is not yet an arahant [sekha]| knowledge and a trainee’s vision, as it has been by you — could he, when his mind has been liberated from the taints through non-clinging as he reviews the ground as he has seen it and understood it, return to the lower life and enjoy sensual pleasures as he did before, when he was a layman?” “No, venerable sir.” “Householder, the Dhamma has been seen and understood by the young man Yasa with a trainee’s knowledge and a trainee’s vision, as it has been by you, and his mind has been liberated from the taints through non-clinging as he reviews the ground as he has seen it and understood it. The young man Yasa is incapable of returning to the lower life and enjoying sensual pleasures as he did before, when he was a layman.”

“It is a gain for the young man Yasa, venerable sir, a good gain for the young man Yasa, that his mind has been liberated from the taints through non-clinging. May the Blessed One accept today’s meal from me, with the young man Yasa as his attendant.” The Blessed One consented by remaining silent. Then the merchant householder, knowing that the Blessed One had consented, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him keeping him on the right, and left.

Then, not long after the merchant householder had left, the young man Yasa said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may I receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May I receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhu. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation [brahmacariya]| for the complete ending of suffering.” That was that venerable one’s full ordination. At that time there were seven arahants in the world.

#### The first female lay disciples

Then, in the morning, the Blessed One dressed, took his bowl and robe, and with the venerable Yasa as his attendant approached the merchant householder’s home. Having approached, he sat on a prepared seat. Then the venerable Yasa’s mother and his former wife approached the Blessed One, paid homage, and sat to one side.

The Blessed One gradually gave them a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that their minds were ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in them: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

They, having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, venerable sir! Excellent, venerable sir! We go for refuge to the Blessed One, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May the Blessed One remember us as female lay followers who have gone for refuge from this day forward for life.” And they were the first female lay disciples in the world, with the threefold formula.

Then the venerable Yasa’s mother, father, and former wife served the Blessed One and the venerable Yasa with their own hands with excellent food, hard and soft, and satisfied them. When the Blessed One had finished his meal and taken his hand from the bowl, they sat to one side. Then the Blessed One instructed, encouraged, roused, and gladdened the venerable Yasa’s mother, father, and former wife with a talk on the Dhamma, rose from his seat, and left.

#### Four friends

The venerable Yasa’s four lay friends, sons of the leading and next-to-leading families in Vārāṇasī — |Vimala::one of Yasa’s four friends [vimala]|, |Subāhu::one of Yasa’s four friends [subāhu]|, |Puṇṇaji::one of Yasa’s four friends [puṇṇaji]|, and |Gavampati::one of Yasa’s four friends [gavampati]| — heard: “The young man Yasa, it seems, has shaved off his hair and beard, put on the dyed robes, and gone forth from home into homelessness.” Having heard this, it occurred to them: “That is no lesser Dhamma and discipline, and no lesser going forth, in which the young man Yasa has shaved off his hair and beard, put on the dyed robes, and gone forth from home into homelessness.”

They approached the venerable Yasa, paid homage, and stood to one side. Then the venerable Yasa took those four lay friends and approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat to one side. Sitting to one side, the venerable Yasa said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, these are my four lay friends, sons of the leading and next-to-leading families in Vārāṇasī: Vimala, Subāhu, Puṇṇaji, and Gavampati. May the Blessed One instruct and advise them.”

The Blessed One gradually gave them a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that their minds were ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in them: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

They, having seen the Dhamma and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones. Then the Blessed One instructed and advised those bhikkhus with a talk on the Dhamma. While they were being instructed and advised by the Blessed One with a talk on the Dhamma, their minds were liberated from the taints through non-clinging. At that time there were eleven arahants in the world.

#### Fifty friends

About fifty lay friends of the venerable Yasa, sons of successive leading families in the countryside, heard: “The young man Yasa, it seems, has shaved off his hair and beard, put on the dyed robes, and gone forth from home into homelessness.” Having heard this, it occurred to them: “That is no lesser Dhamma and discipline, and no lesser going forth, in which the young man Yasa has shaved off his hair and beard, put on the dyed robes, and gone forth from home into homelessness.”

They approached the venerable Yasa, paid homage, and stood to one side. Then the venerable Yasa took those fifty or so lay friends and approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat to one side. Sitting to one side, the venerable Yasa said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, these are about fifty lay friends of mine, sons of successive leading families in the countryside. May the Blessed One instruct and advise them.”

The Blessed One gradually gave them a discourse, first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens; he explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures, and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that their minds were ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would properly absorb dye, so too, in that very seat, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in them: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

They, having seen the Dhamma and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may we receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May we receive full ordination?” The Blessed One said: “Come, bhikkhus. The Dhamma is well proclaimed. Live the spiritual life for the complete ending of suffering.” That was the full ordination of those venerable ones. Then the Blessed One instructed and advised those bhikkhus with a talk on the Dhamma. While they were being instructed and advised by the Blessed One with a talk on the Dhamma, their minds were liberated from the taints through non-clinging. At that time there were sixty-one arahants in the world.
