#### 1. The Brahmins and Householders of Campā

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was on a walking tour, wandering in the |Aṅga::name of an ancient kingdom in eastern India, bordering Magadha [aṅga]| country with a large |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of five hundred bhikkhus, and eventually he arrived at |Campā::name of a town in Aṅga [campā]|. There, the Blessed One stayed at Campā on the bank of the |Gaggara::name of a lotus pond near Campā; lit. gurgling [gaggarā]| lotus pond.

Now at that time, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa was ruling over Campā, a crown property abounding in living beings, rich in grasslands, woodlands, waterways, and grain, a royal endowment, an irrevocable grant given to him by King Seniya Bimbisāra of |Magadha::An ancient kingdom in northeastern India (present day Bihar) and the setting for much of the Buddha’s teachings after his full awakening [magadha]|.

The brahmin householders of Campā heard:

“The ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from a Sakyan clan, while wandering in the Aṅga country with a large Saṅgha of five hundred bhikkhus, has arrived at Campā and is staying on the bank of the Gaggara lotus pond. Now a good report of venerable Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One. He declares this world with its |deities::gods [devas]|, |Māras::demons, tempters, beings of delusion; lit. causing death [mārake]|, |Brahmas::Gods; celestial beings residing in the Brahmā realms, often considered to be highly refined and long-lived deities. [brahmā]|, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, kings and commoners, which he has himself realized with |direct knowledge::experiential realization [abhiññā]|. He teaches the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| that is beneficial in the beginning, beneficial in the middle, and beneficial in the end, meaningful and well-phrased, and he reveals a |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| that is entirely perfect and pure.’ It is auspicious to see such arahants.” Then the brahmin householders of Campā, having left Campā, moved in groups, gathered together, and approached the Gaggara lotus pond.

Now on that occasion, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa was resting in the upper story of his mansion, taking his midday rest. Then Soṇadaṇḍa saw the brahmin householders of Campā leaving Campā in groups, gathered together, and heading toward the Gaggara lotus pond. When he saw them, he asked his steward: “Good steward, why are the brahmin householders of Campā leaving Campā in groups, gathered together, and heading toward the Gaggara lotus pond?”

“Sir, there is the ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from a Sakyan clan, who while wandering in the Aṅga country with a large Saṅgha of five hundred bhikkhus, has arrived at Campā and is staying on the bank of the Gaggara lotus pond. Now a good report of venerable Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ They are going to see sir Gotama.”

“Then, dear steward, go to the brahmin householders of Campā. Having drawn near, tell them: ‘Sirs, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa says this: “Please wait, sirs. The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa will also go to visit the ascetic Gotama.”’”

“Yes, sir,” the steward replied to the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa. Having agreed, he approached the brahmin householders of Campā. Having drawn near, he said: “Sirs, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa says this: ‘Please wait, sirs. The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa will also go to visit the ascetic Gotama.’”

#### 2. The Praises of Soṇadaṇḍa

Now on that occasion, five hundred brahmins from various kingdoms were residing in Campā for some business or other. These brahmins heard the news: “It is said that the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is going to visit the ascetic Gotama.” Then those brahmins went to the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa. Having drawn near, they said: “Sir Soṇadaṇḍa, is it true that you are going to visit the ascetic Gotama?”

“Yes, sirs, it occurred to me: ‘I too will go to visit the ascetic Gotama.’”

“Sir, do not go to see the ascetic Gotama. It is not proper, sir Soṇadaṇḍa, for you to go to see the ascetic Gotama. If sir Soṇadaṇḍa goes to see the ascetic Gotama, sir Soṇadaṇḍa’s reputation will decline, and the ascetic Gotama’s reputation will increase. And because sir Soṇadaṇḍa’s reputation will decline and the ascetic Gotama’s reputation will increase—on account of this reason, sir Soṇadaṇḍa, it is not proper for you to go to see the ascetic Gotama; rather, it is proper for the ascetic Gotama to come to see sir Soṇadaṇḍa.

For you, sir, are well-born on both sides, on your mother’s side and your father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing your lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth. And because sir Soṇadaṇḍa is well-born on both sides, from his mother’s side and father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in matters of birth—on account of this distinction, sir Soṇadaṇḍa, it is not proper for you to go to visit the ascetic Gotama; rather, it is proper for the ascetic Gotama to come to visit you.

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is wealthy, possessing great riches, with many possessions…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is one who has mastered the |three Vedas::the primary sacred scriptures of the Vedic religion: Ṛgveda, Sāmaveda, and Yajurveda [tiṇṇaṁ vedānaṁ]|, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth. He is a reciter of the Vedas, a grammarian and linguistic scholar, fully versed in |natural philosophy::science of disputation; lit. extension of the world [lokāyata]| and the |auspicious marks of a great man::characteristics of an illustrious being [mahāpurisalakkhaṇa]|…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is a good speaker with a good delivery, speaking words that are courteous, distinct, free from hesitation, and expressive in conveying the meaning…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa teaches the teachers of many, and instructs three hundred young brahmins in the recitation of the sacred hymns. Furthermore, many young brahmins come from various directions and various lands to sir Soṇadaṇḍa, seeking the hymns and wishing to study the hymns under him…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is aged, grown old, elderly, advanced in years, having reached the last stage of life, while the ascetic Gotama is youthful, a youth who has newly gone forth…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha…

Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by the brahmin Pokkharasāti…

For sir Soṇadaṇḍa rules over Campā, a crown property abounding in living beings, rich in grasslands, woodlands, waterways, and grain, a royal endowment, an irrevocable grant given to him by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha. And because sir Soṇadaṇḍa rules over Campā, a crown property abounding in living beings, rich in grasslands, woodlands, waterways, and grain, a royal endowment, an irrevocable grant given to him by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha—on account of this distinction, sir Soṇadaṇḍa, it is not proper for you to go to visit the ascetic Gotama; rather, it is proper for the ascetic Gotama to come to visit you.”

#### 3. The Praises of the Buddha

When this was said, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa told those brahmins:

“Now, sirs, listen to me as well: It is proper for us alone to visit sir Gotama, and it is not proper for sir Gotama to come to visit us. Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is well-born on both sides, from his mother’s side and father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in matters of birth. And because the ascetic Gotama is well-born on both sides, from his mother’s side and father’s side, of completely pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in matters of birth—on account of this distinction, it is not proper for sir Gotama to come to visit us; rather, it is proper for us to go to visit sir Gotama.

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth, leaving behind a great community of relatives…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth giving up much gold and treasure stored in the earth and in lofts…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth from the household life into homelessness while still young, with black hair, endowed with the blessing of youth, and in the prime of life…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama shaved off his hair and beard, put on the ochre robes, and went forth from the household life into homelessness though his mother and father wished otherwise and wept with tearful faces…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is virtuous, possessing noble ethics, skilled in ethical conduct, and possessing wholesome ethical conduct…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is a good speaker with a good delivery, speaking words that are courteous, distinct, free from hesitation, and expressive in conveying the meaning…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is a teacher of many teachers…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama has removed the passion for sensual pleasures and is without fickleness…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama holds the doctrine of the moral efficacy of actions, holds that actions bear consequences; he does not seek any harm for the brahmin people…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth from a noble family, from one of the unbroken families of noblemen…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth from a rich family, from a family of great wealth and great possessions…

Sirs, people come from other kingdoms, from across the country to question the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, many thousands of deities have gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, a good report of sir Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One’…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama possesses thirty-two auspicious marks of a great man…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama welcomes visitors, is friendly, congenial, cheerful, open-faced, and the first to speak…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by the four assemblies…

Sirs, many gods and humans are deeply devoted to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, in whatever village or town the ascetic Gotama stays, non-human beings do not harass the humans in that village or town…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama has a community, a following, is a teacher of a group, and is acknowledged as the foremost of the various religious leaders. And while the reputation of some ascetics and brahmins is acquired haphazardly, the ascetic Gotama’s reputation was not acquired like that; rather, the ascetic Gotama’s reputation was acquired through his unsurpassed accomplishment in true knowledge and conduct…

Sirs, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, together with his children, wives, retinue, and ministers, has gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, King Pasenadi of Kosala, together with his children, wives, retinue, and ministers, has gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, the brahmin Pokkharasāti, together with his children, wives, retinue, and ministers, has gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by King Pasenadi of Kosala…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by the brahmin Pokkharasāti…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama has arrived at Campā and is staying on the bank of the Gaggara lotus pond. Now, whatever ascetics or brahmins come to our territory are our guests. And guests should be honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by us. Since the ascetic Gotama has arrived at Campā and is staying on the bank of the Gaggara lotus pond, the ascetic Gotama is our guest. And a guest should be honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by us. On account of this distinction, it is not proper for sir Gotama to come to visit us; rather, it is proper for us to go to visit sir Gotama. This much, sirs, is the praise of sir Gotama that I have learned, but the praise of sir Gotama is not limited to this; indeed, sir Gotama has immeasurable qualities.”

When this was said, those brahmins said to the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa: “As sir Soṇadaṇḍa describes the praises of the ascetic Gotama, even if sir Gotama were living a hundred leagues away, it would be worthwhile for a faithful son of a good family to go to see him, even carrying provisions in a knapsack.”

“Then, sirs, let us all go to visit the ascetic Gotama.”

#### 4. Soṇadaṇḍa’s Apprehension

Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa, together with a large group of brahmins, approached the Gaggara lotus pond.

Then, while the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa was passing through the woods, the following line of thought arose in his mind: “If I were to ask the ascetic Gotama a question, and if the ascetic Gotama were to say to me: ‘Brahmin, this question should not be asked in this way; this question should be asked in that way,’ then this assembly might despise me, thinking: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is foolish and incompetent; he could not ask the ascetic Gotama a question properly.’ And whoever this assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation.

“If, on the other hand, the ascetic Gotama were to ask me a question, and I were not to satisfy his mind with my answer, and if the ascetic Gotama were to say to me: ‘Brahmin, this question should not be answered in this way; this question should be answered in that way,’ then this assembly might despise me, thinking: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is foolish and incompetent; he could not satisfy the mind of the ascetic Gotama with his answer to the question.’ And whoever this assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation.

“And if, having come so close, I were to turn back without seeing the ascetic Gotama, this assembly might despise me, thinking: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is foolish and incompetent, stiff with conceit, and afraid; he does not dare to go and see the ascetic Gotama. For how could he turn back without seeing the ascetic Gotama after coming so close?’ And whoever this assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation.”

Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa approached the Blessed One; having drawn near, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Some of the brahmin householders of Campā also paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One, and after exchanging courteous and polite conversation, sat down to one side; some raised their joined palms toward the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some announced their name and clan in the presence of the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some kept silent and sat down to one side.

There too, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa sat dwelling repeatedly on this very thought: “If I were to ask the ascetic Gotama a question, and if the ascetic Gotama were to say to me: ‘Brahmin, this question should not be asked in this way; this question should be asked in that way,’ then this assembly might despise me, thinking: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is foolish and incompetent; he could not ask the ascetic Gotama a question properly.’ And whoever this assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation. If, on the other hand, the ascetic Gotama were to ask me a question, and I were not to satisfy his mind with my answer, and if the ascetic Gotama were to say to me: ‘Brahmin, this question should not be answered in this way; this question should be answered in that way,’ then this assembly might despise me, thinking: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is foolish and incompetent; he could not satisfy the mind of the ascetic Gotama with his answer to the question.’ And whoever this assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation. Oh, if only the ascetic Gotama would ask me a question regarding my own field as a teacher of the three Vedas! Surely I could satisfy his mind with my answer!”

#### 5. Defining a Brahmin

Then the Blessed One, knowing with his own mind the thought in the mind of the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa, thought: “This brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is |distressed in::afflicted by, suffers anguish from; lit. is hurt by [vihaññati]| his own mind. Why don’t I ask the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa a question regarding his own field as a teacher of the three Vedas?”

Then the Blessed One said to the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa: “Brahmin, endowed with how many factors do the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech?”

Then it occurred to the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa: “What I wished for, longed for, intended, and hoped for: ‘Oh, if only the ascetic Gotama would ask me a question regarding my own field as a teacher of the three Vedas! Surely I could satisfy his mind with my answer!’—that is precisely what the ascetic Gotama asks me about! Surely I will satisfy his mind with my answer!”

Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa, straightening his body and looking around at the assembly, said to the Blessed One: “Sir Gotama, endowed with five factors the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech. Which five? Here, sir Gotama, a brahmin is well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth. He is a reciter, an expert in the hymns, one who has mastered the three Vedas, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth; a linguistic scholar, a grammarian, fully versed in natural philosophy and the auspicious marks of a great man. He is handsome, pleasing to look at, graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence. He is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue. And he is wise and |discerning::who has good judgement [medhāvī]|, the first or second among those holding the |sacrificial ladle::sacred spoon used in a traditional Indian fire ceremony (Yajna or Havan) [suja]|. Endowed with these five factors, sir Gotama, the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech.”

“Among these five factors, brahmin, could one factor be set aside, and a brahmin be defined by the remaining four factors, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech?”

“It could, sir Gotama. For among these five factors, sir Gotama, we can set aside appearance. What will appearance do? For when, sir Gotama, a brahmin is well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth; when he is a reciter, an expert in the hymns, one who has mastered the three Vedas, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth, a linguistic scholar, a grammarian, fully versed in natural philosophy and the auspicious marks of a great man; when he is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue; and when he is wise and discerning, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle—endowed with these four factors, sir Gotama, the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech.”

“Among these four factors, brahmin, could one factor be set aside, and a brahmin be defined by the remaining three factors, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech?”

“It could, sir Gotama. For among these four factors, sir Gotama, we can set aside the hymns. What will the hymns do? For when, sir Gotama, a brahmin is well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth; when he is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue; and when he is wise and discerning, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle—endowed with these three factors, sir Gotama, the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech.”

“Among these three factors, brahmin, could one factor be set aside, and a brahmin be defined by the remaining two factors, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech?”

“It could, sir Gotama. For among these three factors, sir Gotama, we can set aside birth. What will birth do? For when, sir Gotama, a brahmin is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue; and when he is wise and discerning, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle—endowed with these two factors, sir Gotama, the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech.”

When this was said, those brahmins said to the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa: “Sir Soṇadaṇḍa, do not say so! Sir Soṇadaṇḍa, do not say so! Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is disparaging appearance, disparaging the hymns, disparaging birth; sir Soṇadaṇḍa is outright capitulating to the ascetic Gotama’s doctrine!”

Then the Blessed One said to those brahmins: “Brahmins, if you think: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa has little learning, poor delivery, and |undiscerning::without wisdom; lit. bad understanding [duppañña]|, and the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is not competent to debate this statement with the ascetic Gotama,’ then let the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa step aside, and you debate this statement with me. But if you think: ‘The brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is very learned, has good delivery, and is wise, and the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa is competent to debate this statement with the ascetic Gotama,’ then you stand aside, and let the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa debate with me.”

When this was said, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa said to the Blessed One: “Let sir Gotama wait; let sir Gotama be silent. I myself will give them a legitimate reply.” Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa said to those brahmins: “Sirs, do not speak thus; do not speak thus: ‘Sir Soṇadaṇḍa is disparaging appearance, disparaging the hymns, disparaging birth; sir Soṇadaṇḍa is outright capitulating to the ascetic Gotama’s doctrine.’ Sirs, I am not disparaging appearance, the hymns, or birth.”

Now on that occasion, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa’s nephew, a young brahmin named Aṅgaka, was sitting in that assembly. Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa said to those brahmins: “Do you sirs see this young brahmin Aṅgaka, my nephew?”

“Yes, sir.”

“Sirs, the young brahmin Aṅgaka is handsome, pleasing to look at, graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence; there is none in this assembly his equal in appearance except the ascetic Gotama. The young brahmin Aṅgaka is a reciter, an expert in the hymns, one who has mastered the three Vedas, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth, a linguistic scholar, a grammarian, fully versed in natural philosophy and the auspicious marks of a great man. I am his teacher in the hymns. The young brahmin Aṅgaka is well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth. I know his parents. But if the young brahmin Aṅgaka were to kill living beings, take what is not given, commit adultery, speak falsehood, and consume intoxicants, what would appearance do for him then, sirs? What would the hymns do? What would birth do? But when, sirs, a brahmin is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue; and when he is wise and discerning, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle—endowed with these two factors, sirs, the brahmins define a brahmin, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech.”

#### 6. The Discussion of Virtue and Wisdom

“Among these two factors, brahmin, could one factor be set aside, and a brahmin be defined by the remaining one factor alone, so that one declaring ‘I am a brahmin’ speaks accurately and does not fall into false speech?”

“No, sir Gotama. For |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]| is purified by |virtue::moral conduct, ethical behavior [sīla]|, and virtue is purified by wisdom. Where there is virtue, there is wisdom; where there is wisdom, there is virtue. For the virtuous one there is wisdom; for the wise one there is virtue. And the combination of virtue and wisdom is declared to be the highest thing in the world. Just as, sir Gotama, one hand washes the other hand, or one foot washes the other foot; even so, sir Gotama, wisdom is purified by virtue, and virtue is purified by wisdom. Where there is virtue, there is wisdom; where there is wisdom, there is virtue. For the virtuous one there is wisdom; for the wise one there is virtue. And the combination of virtue and wisdom is declared to be the highest thing in the world.”

“So it is, brahmin! So it is, brahmin! For wisdom is purified by virtue, and virtue is purified by wisdom. Where there is virtue, there is wisdom; where there is wisdom, there is virtue. For the virtuous one there is wisdom; for the wise one there is virtue. And the combination of virtue and wisdom is declared to be the highest thing in the world. Just as, brahmin, one hand washes the other hand, or one foot washes the other foot; even so, brahmin, wisdom is purified by virtue, and virtue is purified by wisdom. Where there is virtue, there is wisdom; where there is wisdom, there is virtue. For the virtuous one there is wisdom; for the wise one there is virtue. And the combination of virtue and wisdom is declared to be the highest thing in the world.

“But what, brahmin, is that virtue? And what is that wisdom?”

“Our understanding of this matter goes only so far, sir Gotama. It would be good if sir Gotama himself would clarify the meaning of this statement.”

“Well then, brahmin, listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Very well, sir,” the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Here, brahmin, a |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| arises in the world, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One … In this way, brahmin, a bhikkhu is |accomplished in virtue::of excellent morality; accomplished in ethical conduct [sīlasampanna]|. This, brahmin, is that virtue … He enters and dwells in the first |jhāna::meditative absorption [jhāna]| … the second jhāna … the third jhāna … the fourth jhāna … He directs and inclines his mind toward |knowledge and vision::insight into reality, direct experiential knowing [ñāṇadassana]| … This too pertains to his wisdom … He understands: ‘… there is no more coming to any state of existence.’ This too pertains to his wisdom. This, brahmin, is that wisdom.”

#### 7. Soṇadaṇḍa Declares Himself a Lay Follower

When this was said, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! Just as if one might set upright what had been overturned, |reveal::uncover [vivarati]| what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way, sir Gotama, the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| has been |explained::illustrated [pakāsita]| by you in many ways. I go for refuge to venerable Gotama, to the Dhamma, and to the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of bhikkhus. May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who, from this day forward, has gone for refuge for life. And may sir Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus accept tomorrow’s meal from me.” The Blessed One consented in silence.

Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa, having received the Blessed One’s consent, rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right, he departed. Then, after the passage of that night, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa had various kinds of excellent food prepared in his own residence and had the time announced to the Blessed One: “It is time, sir Gotama, the meal is ready.” Then the Blessed One, having dressed early in the morning, taking his alms bowl and outer robe, went to the residence of the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. Having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat. Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa personally served and satisfied the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha with various kinds of excellent food.

Then the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa took a low seat and sat down to one side after the Blessed One had finished his meal and put away his bowl. Seated to one side, the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa said to the Blessed One: “If, sir Gotama, when sitting in an assembly, I were to rise from my seat to pay homage to sir Gotama, that assembly might despise me. And whoever that assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation. If, sir Gotama, when sitting in an assembly, I should raise my joined palms, let sir Gotama consider that as my rising from my seat. If, sir Gotama, when sitting in an assembly, I should take off my turban, let sir Gotama consider that as my bowing with my head. If, sir Gotama, when riding in a carriage, I were to dismount from the carriage to pay homage to sir Gotama, that assembly might despise me. And whoever that assembly despises, their reputation declines. And whoever’s reputation declines, their wealth also declines, for our wealth is acquired through reputation. If, sir Gotama, when riding in a carriage, I should raise my driving goad, let sir Gotama consider that as my dismounting from the carriage. If, sir Gotama, when riding in a carriage, I should lower my sunshade, let sir Gotama consider that as my bowing with my head.”

Then the Blessed One, having instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened the brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa with a talk on the Dhamma, rose from his seat and departed.
