#### 1. The Brahmins and Householders of Khāṇumata

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was on a walking tour, wandering in the country of |Magadha::An ancient kingdom in northeastern India (present day Bihar) and the setting for much of the Buddha’s teachings after his full awakening [magadha]| with a large |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of five hundred bhikkhus, and eventually he arrived at a Magadhan brahmin village named |Khāṇumata::A village in Magadha where the brahmin Kūṭadanta lived [khāṇumata]|. There the Blessed One stayed at |Ambalaṭṭhikā::A royal park or pavilion in Magadha [ambalaṭṭhikā]|.

Now at that time, the brahmin Kūṭadanta was ruling over Khāṇumata, a crown property abounding in living beings, rich in grasslands, woodlands, waterways, and grain, a royal endowment, an irrevocable grant given to him by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha. And at that time the brahmin Kūṭadanta had prepared a great sacrifice. Seven hundred bulls, seven hundred bullocks, seven hundred heifers, seven hundred goats, and seven hundred rams were tied to posts for the sacrifice.

The brahmin householders of Khāṇumata heard:

“It seems the ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from a Sakyan clan, while wandering in the Magadhan country with a large Saṅgha of five hundred bhikkhus, has arrived at Khāṇumata and is staying at Ambalaṭṭhikā. Now a good report of sir Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One. He declares this world with its |deities::gods [devas]|, |Māras::demons, tempters, beings of delusion; lit. causing death [mārake]|, |Brahmas::Gods; celestial beings residing in the Brahmā realms, often considered to be highly refined and long-lived deities. [brahmā]|, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, kings and commoners, which he has himself realized with |direct knowledge::experiential realization [abhiññā]|. He teaches the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| that is beneficial in the beginning, beneficial in the middle, and beneficial in the end, meaningful and well-phrased, and he reveals a |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| that is entirely perfect and pure.’ It is auspicious to see such arahants.”

Then the brahmin householders of Khāṇumata left Khāṇumata in groups, gathered together, and headed toward Ambalaṭṭhikā.

Now on that occasion, the brahmin Kūṭadanta was resting in the upper story of his mansion, taking his midday rest. Then Kūṭadanta saw the brahmin householders of Khāṇumata leaving Khāṇumata in groups, gathered together, and heading toward Ambalaṭṭhikā. When he saw them, he asked his steward: “Good steward, why are the brahmin householders of Khāṇumata leaving Khāṇumata in groups, gathered together, and heading toward Ambalaṭṭhikā?”

“Sir, there is the ascetic Gotama, the Sakyan son who went forth from a Sakyan clan, who while wandering in the Magadhan country with a large Saṅgha of five hundred bhikkhus, has arrived at Khāṇumata and is staying at Ambalaṭṭhikā. Now a good report of sir Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ They are going to see sir Gotama.”

Then it occurred to the brahmin Kūṭadanta: “I have heard: ‘The ascetic Gotama knows the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories.’ Now I do not know the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories. But I want to perform a great sacrifice. Why don't I approach the ascetic Gotama and ask him about the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories?”

Then the brahmin Kūṭadanta addressed his steward: “In that case, dear steward, go to the brahmin householders of Khāṇumata. Having drawn near, tell them: ‘Sirs, the brahmin Kūṭadanta says this: “Please wait, sirs. The brahmin Kūṭadanta will also go to see the ascetic Gotama.”’”

“Yes, sir,” the steward replied to the brahmin Kūṭadanta. Having agreed, he approached the brahmin householders of Khāṇumata. Having drawn near, he said: “Sirs, the brahmin Kūṭadanta says this: ‘Please wait, sirs. The brahmin Kūṭadanta will also go to see the ascetic Gotama.’”

#### 2. The Praises of Kūṭadanta

Now at that time, several hundred brahmins were residing in Khāṇumata, thinking: “We will participate in the brahmin Kūṭadanta’s great sacrifice.” Those brahmins heard: “It is said that the brahmin Kūṭadanta is going to see the ascetic Gotama.” Then those brahmins approached the brahmin Kūṭadanta and said to him:

“Sir Kūṭadanta, is it true that you are going to see the ascetic Gotama?”

“Yes, sirs. I am going to see the ascetic Gotama.”

“Sir Kūṭadanta, do not go to see the ascetic Gotama. It is not proper, sir Kūṭadanta, for you to go to see the ascetic Gotama. If sir Kūṭadanta goes to see the ascetic Gotama, sir Kūṭadanta’s reputation will decline, and the ascetic Gotama’s reputation will increase. And because sir Kūṭadanta’s reputation will decline and the ascetic Gotama’s reputation will increase—on account of this reason, sir Kūṭadanta, it is not proper for you to go to see the ascetic Gotama; rather, it is proper for the ascetic Gotama to come to see sir Kūṭadanta.

For you, sir, are well-born on both sides, on your mother’s side and your father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing your lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth. And because sir Kūṭadanta is well-born on both sides, from his mother’s side and father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in matters of birth—on account of this distinction, sir Kūṭadanta, it is not proper for you to go to visit the ascetic Gotama; rather, it is proper for the ascetic Gotama to come to visit you.

Sir Kūṭadanta is wealthy, possessing great riches, with many possessions, having abundant gold and silver, and plentiful wealth and property…

Sir Kūṭadanta is one who has mastered the |three Vedas::the primary sacred scriptures of the Vedic religion: Ṛgveda, Sāmaveda, and Yajurveda [tiṇṇaṁ vedānaṁ]|, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth. He is a reciter of the Vedas, a grammarian and linguistic scholar, fully versed in |natural philosophy::science of disputation; lit. extension of the world [lokāyata]| and the |auspicious marks of a great man::characteristics of an illustrious being [mahāpurisalakkhaṇa]|…

Sir Kūṭadanta is handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence…

Sir Kūṭadanta is virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue…

Sir Kūṭadanta is a good speaker with a good delivery, speaking words that are courteous, distinct, free from hesitation, and expressive in conveying the meaning…

Sir Kūṭadanta teaches the teachers of many, and instructs three hundred young brahmins in the recitation of the sacred hymns. Furthermore, many young brahmins come from various directions and various lands to sir Kūṭadanta, seeking the hymns and wishing to study the hymns under him…

Sir Kūṭadanta is aged, grown old, elderly, advanced in years, having reached the last stage of life, while the ascetic Gotama is youthful, a youth who has newly gone forth…

Sir Kūṭadanta is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha…

Sir Kūṭadanta is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by the brahmin Pokkharasāti…

For sir Kūṭadanta rules over Khāṇumata, a crown property abounding in living beings, rich in grasslands, woodlands, waterways, and grain, a royal endowment, an irrevocable grant given to him by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha. And because sir Kūṭadanta rules over Khāṇumata, a crown property abounding in living beings, rich in grasslands, woodlands, waterways, and grain, a royal endowment, an irrevocable grant given to him by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha—on account of this distinction, sir Kūṭadanta, it is not proper for you to go to visit the ascetic Gotama; rather, it is proper for the ascetic Gotama to come to visit you.”

#### 3. The Praises of the Buddha

When this was said, the brahmin Kūṭadanta told those brahmins:

“Now, sirs, listen to me as well as to why it is proper for us alone to visit sir Gotama, and not proper for sir Gotama to come to visit us. Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is well-born on both sides, from his mother’s side and father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in matters of birth. And because the ascetic Gotama is well-born on both sides, from his mother’s side and father’s side, of completely pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in matters of birth—on account of this distinction, it is not proper for sir Gotama to come to visit us; rather, it is proper for us to go to visit sir Gotama.

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth, leaving behind a great community of relatives…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth giving up much gold and treasure stored in the earth and in lofts…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth from the household life into homelessness while still young, with black hair, endowed with the blessing of youth, and in the prime of life…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama shaved off his hair and beard, put on the ochre robes, and went forth from the household life into homelessness though his mother and father wished otherwise and wept with tearful faces…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is virtuous, possessing noble ethics, skilled in ethical conduct, and possessing wholesome ethical conduct…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is a good speaker with a good delivery, speaking words that are courteous, distinct, free from hesitation, and expressive in conveying the meaning…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is a teacher of many teachers…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama has removed the passion for sensual pleasures and is without fickleness…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama holds the doctrine of the moral efficacy of actions, holds that actions bear consequences; he does not seek any harm for the brahmin people…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth from a noble family, from one of the unbroken families of noblemen…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama went forth from a rich family, from a family of great wealth and great possessions…

Sirs, people come from other kingdoms, from across the country to question the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, many thousands of deities have gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, a good report of sir Gotama has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One’…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama possesses thirty-two auspicious marks of a great man…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama welcomes visitors, is friendly, congenial, cheerful, open-faced, and the first to speak…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by the four assemblies…

Sirs, many gods and humans are deeply devoted to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, in whatever village or town the ascetic Gotama stays, non-human beings do not harass the humans in that village or town…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama has a community, a following, is a teacher of a group, and is acknowledged as the foremost of the various religious leaders. And while the reputation of some ascetics and brahmins is acquired haphazardly, the ascetic Gotama’s reputation was not acquired like that; rather, the ascetic Gotama’s reputation was acquired through his unsurpassed accomplishment in true knowledge and conduct…

Sirs, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, together with his children, wives, retinue, and ministers, has gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, King Pasenadi of Kosala, together with his children, wives, retinue, and ministers, has gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, the brahmin Pokkharasāti, together with his children, wives, retinue, and ministers, has gone for refuge for life to the ascetic Gotama…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by King Pasenadi of Kosala…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama is honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by the brahmin Pokkharasāti…

Sirs, the ascetic Gotama has arrived at Khāṇumata and is staying at Ambalaṭṭhikā. Now, whatever ascetics or brahmins come to our territory are our guests. And guests should be honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by us. Since the ascetic Gotama has arrived at Khāṇumata and is staying at Ambalaṭṭhikā, the ascetic Gotama is our guest. And a guest should be honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed by us. On account of this distinction, it is not proper for sir Gotama to come to visit us; rather, it is proper for us to go to visit sir Gotama. This much, sirs, is the praise of sir Gotama that I have learned, but the praise of sir Gotama is not limited to this; indeed, honorable Gotama has immeasurable qualities.”

When this was said, those brahmins said to the brahmin Kūṭadanta: “As sir Kūṭadanta describes the praises of the ascetic Gotama, even if sir Gotama were living a hundred leagues away, it would be worthwhile for a faithful son of a good family to go to see him, even carrying provisions in a knapsack.”

“In that case, sirs, let us all go to see the ascetic Gotama.”

#### 4. The Story of King Mahāvijita’s Sacrifice

Then the brahmin Kūṭadanta, together with a large group of brahmins, approached Ambalaṭṭhikā where the Blessed One was staying. Having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. And some of the brahmin householders of Khāṇumata, having paid homage to the Blessed One, sat down to one side; some exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One, and after exchanging courteous and polite conversation, sat down to one side; some raised their joined hands in reverent salutation toward the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some announced their name and clan and sat down to one side; and some sat down to one side in silence.

Seated to one side, the brahmin Kūṭadanta said to the Blessed One: “Sir Gotama, I have heard: ‘The ascetic Gotama knows the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories.’ Now I do not know the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, but I wish to perform a great sacrifice. It would be good if sir Gotama would teach me the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories.”

“In that case, brahmin, listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, sir,” the brahmin Kūṭadanta replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this: “In the past, brahmin, there was a king named Mahāvijita. He was rich, with great wealth and great possessions, having abundant gold and silver, plentiful wealth and property, ample money and grain, with full treasuries and storehouses. Then, brahmin, while King Mahāvijita was alone in |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, the following |chain of thought::thought process; lit. thinking around [parivitakka]| arose in his mind: ‘I have acquired abundant human wealth; I have conquered and govern a vast territory on the earth. Why don't I perform a great sacrifice that will lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time?’

Then, brahmin, King Mahāvijita summoned his high priest, a brahmin, and said to him: ‘Here, brahmin, while I was alone in seclusion, the following chain of thought arose in my mind: “I have acquired abundant human wealth; I have conquered and govern a vast territory on the earth. Why don't I perform a great sacrifice that will lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time?” I wish, brahmin, to perform a great sacrifice. Instruct me, sir, in what will be for my welfare and happiness for a long time.’

When this was said, brahmin, the high priest said to King Mahāvijita: ‘Your Majesty’s realm is troubled by bandits and oppressed: raids on villages are seen, raids on towns are seen, raids on cities are seen, and ambushes along highways are seen. If Your Majesty were to levy taxes in a realm so troubled and oppressed, you would be acting wrongly.

Perhaps Your Majesty might think: “I will eradicate this plague of bandits by execution, imprisonment, confiscation, censure, or banishment.” But that is not how this plague of bandits can be properly eliminated. For those who survive will later ravage Your Majesty’s realm.

However, by adopting this plan, that plague of bandits will be properly eliminated. To those in Your Majesty’s realm who are dedicated to farming and cattle breeding, let Your Majesty provide seed and fodder. To those in Your Majesty’s realm who are dedicated to commerce, let Your Majesty provide capital. To those in Your Majesty’s realm who are dedicated to government service, let Your Majesty provide rations and wages. Then those people, occupied with their own work, will not harass the realm; Your Majesty’s revenue will be great; the country will be safe, peaceful, and unmolested; and people, happy and rejoicing, dancing with their children in their arms, will live with open doors, I imagine.’

‘Very well, sir,’ said King Mahāvijita, consenting to the high priest. To those in his realm who were dedicated to farming and cattle breeding, King Mahāvijita provided seed and fodder. To those dedicated to commerce, King Mahāvijita provided capital. To those dedicated to government service, King Mahāvijita provided rations and wages. And those people, occupied with their own work, did not harass the realm; the king’s revenue became great; the country became safe, peaceful, and unmolested; and people, happy and rejoicing, dancing with their children in their arms, lived with open doors, as it were.

Then, brahmin, King Mahāvijita summoned his high priest and said to him: ‘The plague of bandits has been eliminated; thanks to your plan, my revenue is great; the country is safe, peaceful, and unmolested; and people, happy and rejoicing, dancing with their children in their arms, live with open doors, as it were. I wish, brahmin, to perform a great sacrifice. Instruct me, sir, in what will be for my welfare and happiness for a long time.’

##### 4.1. The Four Accessories

‘In that case, let Your Majesty invite the noble vassals in your realm, both of town and countryside, saying: “I wish, sirs, to perform a great sacrifice; may you grant your approval, so that it may lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time.” Let Your Majesty invite the royal counselors and ministers, both of town and countryside… the influential brahmins, both of town and countryside… the wealthy householders, both of town and countryside, saying: “I wish, sirs, to perform a great sacrifice; may you grant your approval, so that it may lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time.”’

‘Very well, sir,’ said King Mahāvijita, consenting to the high priest. He invited the noble vassals in his realm, both of town and countryside, saying: ‘I wish, sirs, to perform a great sacrifice; may you grant your approval, so that it may lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time.’ They replied: ‘Let Your Majesty perform the sacrifice! It is time for the sacrifice, Great King!’ He invited the counselors and ministers, both of town and countryside… the influential brahmins, both of town and countryside… the wealthy householders, both of town and countryside, saying: ‘I wish, sirs, to perform a great sacrifice; may you grant your approval, so that it may lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time.’ They replied: ‘Let Your Majesty perform the sacrifice! It is time for the sacrifice, Great King!’ These four parties who gave their consent became accessories to that sacrifice.

##### 4.2. The Eight Accessories

King Mahāvijita was endowed with eight qualities—

he was well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth;

he was handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, resembling Brahmā in appearance, radiant like Brahmā, magnificent to behold, and remarkable in presence;

he was rich, with great wealth and great possessions, having abundant gold and silver, plentiful wealth and property, ample money and grain, with full treasuries and storehouses;

he was powerful, commanding a fourfold army that was loyal and obedient, so that he seemed to crush his enemies with his splendor;

he was faithful, a generous giver, a benefactor, keeping an open door, a public fountain for ascetics, brahmins, the needy, travelers, mendicants, and beggars, performing deeds of merit;

he was learned in various traditions, understanding the meaning of various sayings: ‘This is the meaning of that statement; this is the meaning of that statement’; and

he was wise, competent, intelligent, able to think through matters of the past, future, and present.

King Mahāvijita was endowed with these eight qualities. These eight qualities also became accessories to that sacrifice.

##### 4.3. Four More Accessories

The high priest, a brahmin, was endowed with four qualities—

he was well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth;

he was one who had mastered the three Vedas, together with their vocabularies, liturgy, phonology, and etymology, and with historical narratives as the fifth; a reciter of the Vedas, a grammarian and linguistic scholar, fully versed in natural philosophy and the auspicious marks of a great man;

he was virtuous, cultivated in ethics, possessing well-developed virtue; and

he was wise, competent, intelligent, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle.

The high priest was endowed with these four qualities. These four qualities also became accessories to that sacrifice.

##### 4.4. The Three Modes

Then, brahmin, the high priest explained the three modes to King Mahāvijita before the sacrifice: ‘Lest Your Majesty, wishing to perform the great sacrifice, should feel any regret: “A great portion of my wealth will be lost,” Your Majesty should harbor no such regret. Lest Your Majesty, while performing the great sacrifice, should feel any regret: “A great portion of my wealth is being lost,” Your Majesty should harbor no such regret. Lest Your Majesty, having performed the great sacrifice, should feel any regret: “A great portion of my wealth has been lost,” Your Majesty should harbor no such regret.’

These are the three modes, brahmin, that the high priest taught to King Mahāvijita before the sacrifice.

##### 4.5. The Ten Respects

Then, brahmin, before the sacrifice, the high priest dispelled King Mahāvijita’s regrets concerning the recipients of the gifts in ten respects.

‘There will come to Your Majesty’s sacrifice both those who take life and those who refrain from taking life. As for those who take life, that is their own affair. But concerning those who refrain from taking life, let Your Majesty make the offering, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within.

There will come to Your Majesty’s sacrifice both those who take what is not given and those who refrain from taking what is not given… both those who engage in sexual misconduct and those who refrain from sexual misconduct… both those who speak falsely and those who refrain from speaking falsely… both those who speak divisively and those who refrain from speaking divisively… both those who speak harshly and those who refrain from speaking harshly… both those who chatter frivolously and those who refrain from chattering frivolously… both those who harbor intense craving and those who do not harbor intense craving… both those with ill will in their hearts and those without ill will in their hearts… both those of wrong view and those of right view. As for those of wrong view, that is their own affair. But concerning those of right view, let Your Majesty make the offering, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within.’

In these ten respects, brahmin, the high priest dispelled King Mahāvijita’s regrets concerning the recipients before the sacrifice.

##### 4.6. The Sixteen Respects

Then, brahmin, as King Mahāvijita was performing the great sacrifice, the high priest instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened his mind in sixteen respects.

‘Someone might say of Your Majesty as you perform the great sacrifice: “King Mahāvijita performs a great sacrifice, but he has not invited the noble vassals in his realm, both of town and countryside; yet the king performs such a sacrifice!” But no one could rightly say this of Your Majesty, for Your Majesty has invited the noble vassals in your realm, both of town and countryside. Knowing this, let Your Majesty sacrifice, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within.

Someone might say of Your Majesty as you perform the great sacrifice: “King Mahāvijita performs a great sacrifice, but he has not invited the counselors and ministers, both of town and countryside… the influential brahmins, both of town and countryside… the wealthy householders, both of town and countryside; yet the king performs such a sacrifice!” But no one could rightly say this of Your Majesty, for Your Majesty has invited the wealthy householders, both of town and countryside. Knowing this, let Your Majesty sacrifice, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within.

Someone might say of Your Majesty as you perform the great sacrifice: “King Mahāvijita performs a great sacrifice, but he is not well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth; yet the king performs such a sacrifice!” But no one could rightly say this of Your Majesty, for Your Majesty is well-born on both sides, on your mother’s side and your father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing your lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth. Knowing this, let Your Majesty sacrifice, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within. Someone might say of Your Majesty as you perform the great sacrifice: “King Mahāvijita performs a great sacrifice, but he is not handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful… he is not rich, with great wealth and great possessions… he is not powerful, commanding a fourfold army… he is not faithful, a generous giver, a benefactor… he is not learned in various traditions… he does not understand the meaning of various sayings… he is not wise, competent, intelligent, able to think through matters of the past, future, and present; yet the king performs such a sacrifice!” But no one could rightly say this of Your Majesty, for Your Majesty is wise, competent, intelligent, able to think through matters of the past, future, and present. Knowing this, let Your Majesty sacrifice, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within.

Someone might say of Your Majesty as you perform the great sacrifice: “King Mahāvijita performs a great sacrifice, but his high priest is not well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth; yet the king performs such a sacrifice!” But no one could rightly say this of Your Majesty, for Your Majesty’s high priest is well-born on both sides, on his mother’s side and his father’s side, of pure lineage, tracing his lineage back through seven generations of forefathers, unassailable and irreproachable in respect of birth. Knowing this, let Your Majesty sacrifice, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within. Someone might say of Your Majesty as you perform the great sacrifice: “King Mahāvijita performs a great sacrifice, but his high priest is not one who has mastered the three Vedas… he is not virtuous, cultivated in ethics… he is not wise, competent, intelligent, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle; yet the king performs such a sacrifice!” But no one could rightly say this of Your Majesty, for Your Majesty’s high priest is wise, competent, intelligent, the first or second among those holding the sacrificial ladle. Knowing this, let Your Majesty sacrifice, let Your Majesty give, let Your Majesty rejoice, and let Your Majesty gladden your mind within.’

In these sixteen respects, brahmin, the high priest instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened King Mahāvijita’s mind as he was performing the great sacrifice.

In that sacrifice, brahmin, no oxen were killed, no goats or sheep were killed, no chickens or pigs were killed, no diverse creatures were put to slaughter, no trees were cut down for sacrificial posts, and no kusa grass was mown to strew upon the ground. And whatever bondservants, messengers, or laborers there were, they did not carry out their duties compelled by rods, terrified by fear, weeping with tearful faces. Rather, those who wished to work worked, and those who did not wish to work did not work; they did what they wished to do, and did not do what they did not wish to do. With ghee, oil, butter, curd, honey, and molasses alone was that sacrifice brought to completion.

Then, brahmin, the noble vassals in the realm, both of town and countryside, the counselors and ministers, both of town and countryside, the influential brahmins, both of town and countryside, and the wealthy householders, both of town and countryside, took abundant wealth and, approaching King Mahāvijita, said: ‘This abundant wealth, Your Majesty, has been brought especially for Your Majesty; may Your Majesty accept it.’

‘Enough, sirs! I already have abundant wealth, acquired through righteous taxation. Let this be yours, and take even more from here!’

Refused by the king, they stepped aside to one side and reflected: ‘It is not proper for us to take this wealth back to our own homes. King Mahāvijita is performing a great sacrifice; let us join in following his sacrifice.’

Then, brahmin, to the east of the sacrificial enclosure, the noble vassals of town and countryside instituted their gifts. To the south of the sacrificial enclosure, the counselors and ministers of town and countryside instituted their gifts. To the west of the sacrificial enclosure, the influential brahmins of town and countryside instituted their gifts. To the north of the sacrificial enclosure, the wealthy householders of town and countryside instituted their gifts.

And in those sacrifices too, brahmin, no oxen were killed, no goats or sheep were killed, no chickens or pigs were killed, no diverse creatures were put to slaughter, no trees were cut down for sacrificial posts, and no kusa grass was mown to strew upon the ground. And whatever bondservants, messengers, or laborers they had, they did not carry out their duties compelled by rods, terrified by fear, weeping with tearful faces. Rather, those who wished to work worked, and those who did not wish to work did not work; they did what they wished to do, and did not do what they did not wish to do. With ghee, oil, butter, curd, honey, and molasses alone were those sacrifices brought to completion.

Thus there were the four consenting parties, King Mahāvijita was endowed with eight qualities, the high priest was endowed with four qualities, and there were the three modes. This, brahmin, is called the threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories.”

When this was said, those brahmins raised a loud clamor and uproar: “Oh, what a sacrifice! Oh, what a splendid sacrifice!”

The brahmin Kūṭadanta, however, sat in complete silence. Then those brahmins said to the brahmin Kūṭadanta: “Why does sir Kūṭadanta not applaud what was well spoken by the ascetic Gotama as well spoken?”

“I do not refrain from applauding what was well spoken by the ascetic Gotama as well spoken, sirs. The head of anyone who did not applaud what was well spoken by the ascetic Gotama as well spoken would surely split open!

Still, sirs, it occurs to me: The ascetic Gotama does not say: ‘Thus have I heard,’ or ‘So it must have been.’ Rather, the ascetic Gotama speaks as if saying: ‘Thus it was then, like this it was then.’

Therefore, sirs, it occurs to me: ‘Surely at that time the ascetic Gotama was either King Mahāvijita, the master of the sacrifice, or the high priest who officiated at that sacrifice.’

Does sir Gotama recall having performed or officiated at such a sacrifice, and that with the breakup of the body, after death, he was reborn in a good destination, in a |heavenly world::a blissful realm, celestial world [sagga]|?”

“I do recall, brahmin, having performed or officiated at such a sacrifice, and that with the breakup of the body, after death, I was reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world. At that time I was the high priest who officiated at that sacrifice.”

#### 5. Sacrifices Yielding Greater Fruit

“Is there, sir Gotama, any other sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“There is, brahmin, another sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“What is that sacrifice, sir Gotama, less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“Brahmin, regular gifts and offerings according to family tradition given to virtuous renunciants—this sacrifice, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why the regular gifts and offerings according to family tradition given to virtuous renunciants are less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, yet yield greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“Brahmin, those who are arahants or who have entered the path to arahantship do not attend a sacrifice of that sort. Why is that? Because there beating with sticks and seizing by the throat are seen. That is why those who are arahants or who have entered the path to arahantship do not attend a sacrifice of that sort.

But as for regular gifts and offerings according to family tradition given to virtuous renunciants, those who are arahants or who have entered the path to arahantship do attend such a sacrifice. Why is that? Because there no beating with sticks or seizing by the throat is seen. That is why those who are arahants or who have entered the path to arahantship attend a sacrifice of that sort.

This, brahmin, is the cause and condition why the regular gifts and offerings according to family tradition given to virtuous renunciants are less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, yet yield greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“Is there, sir Gotama, any other sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories and this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“There is, brahmin, another sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories and this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“What is that sacrifice, sir Gotama, less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories and this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“When one builds a |monastery::dwelling where one is free to move around [vihāra + cātuddisa]| dedicated to the Saṅgha of the four quarters—this sacrifice, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories and this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“Is there, sir Gotama, any other sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, and this gift of a monastery, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“There is, brahmin, another sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, and this gift of a monastery, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“What is that sacrifice, sir Gotama, less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, and this gift of a monastery, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“When, with a confident mind, one goes for refuge to the Buddha, goes for refuge to the Dhamma, and goes for refuge to the Saṅgha—this sacrifice, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, and this gift of a monastery, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“Is there, sir Gotama, any other sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, and these refuges, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“There is, brahmin, another sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, and these refuges, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“What is that sacrifice, sir Gotama, less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, and these refuges, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“When, with a confident mind, one undertakes the training rules: abstinence from taking life, abstinence from taking what is not given, abstinence from sexual misconduct, abstinence from false speech, and abstinence from |consuming intoxicants::consuming wine, spirits, or drugs and getting intoxicated [surāmerayamajja]| that are the basis for |negligence::heedlessness, carelessness [pamāda]|—this sacrifice, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, and these refuges, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“Is there, sir Gotama, any other sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, these refuges, and these training rules, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“There is, brahmin, another sacrifice less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, these refuges, and these training rules, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit.”

“What is that sacrifice, sir Gotama, less troublesome and less fraught with effort than this threefold sacrifice with its sixteen accessories, this regular gift and offering according to family tradition, this gift of a monastery, these refuges, and these training rules, yet yielding greater fruit and greater benefit?”

“Here, brahmin, a |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One arises in the world… In this way, brahmin, a bhikkhu is |accomplished in virtue::of excellent morality; accomplished in ethical conduct [sīlasampanna]|… enters and dwells in the first |jhāna::meditative absorption [jhāna]|. This sacrifice, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than the previous sacrifices, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit…

He enters and dwells in the second jhāna… the third jhāna… the fourth jhāna. This sacrifice too, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than the previous sacrifices, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit…

He directs and inclines his mind toward |knowledge and vision::insight into reality, direct experiential knowing [ñāṇadassana]|… This sacrifice too, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than the previous sacrifices, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit…

He knows clearly: ‘There is no more coming to any state of existence.’ This sacrifice too, brahmin, is less troublesome and less fraught with effort than the previous sacrifices, yet yields greater fruit and greater benefit. And, brahmin, there is no other sacrifice higher or more sublime than this accomplishment of sacrifice.”

#### 6. Kūṭadanta Declares Himself a Lay Follower

When this was said, the brahmin Kūṭadanta said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! Just as if one might set upright what had been overturned, |reveal::uncover [vivarati]| what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way, sir Gotama, the Dhamma has been |explained::illustrated [pakāsita]| by you in many ways. I go for refuge to sir Gotama, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forth for life.

I release, sir Gotama, these seven hundred bulls, seven hundred bullocks, seven hundred heifers, seven hundred goats, and seven hundred rams; I grant them life! Let them eat green grass, let them drink cool water, and let the cool wind blow upon them!”

#### 7. The Realization of the Fruit of Stream-Entry

Then the Blessed One gave the brahmin Kūṭadanta a progressive discourse, namely: first on giving, then on virtue, and then on the heavens. He explained the dangers, degradation, and defilement of sensual pleasures and the benefit of renunciation. When the Blessed One knew that the brahmin Kūṭadanta’s mind was ready, receptive, free of hindrances, uplifted, and confident, he then expounded the distinctive teaching of the Buddhas: suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path. Just as a clean cloth with no dark spots would perfectly absorb dye, so too, as the brahmin Kūṭadanta was sitting there, the stainless, immaculate Dhamma eye arose in him: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.”

Then the brahmin Kūṭadanta—having seen the Dhamma, attained the Dhamma, understood the Dhamma, deeply penetrated the Dhamma, having crossed beyond doubt, with no more uncertainty, confident and self-assured, and become independent of others in the Teacher’s instruction—said to the Blessed One: “May sir Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus accept tomorrow’s meal from me.” The Blessed One consented in silence.

Then the brahmin Kūṭadanta, having received the Blessed One’s consent, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, and keeping him on his right, departed. Then, after the passage of that night, the brahmin Kūṭadanta had various kinds of excellent food prepared in his own sacrificial enclosure, and announced the time to the Blessed One: “It is time, sir Gotama; the meal is ready.”

Then the Blessed One, having dressed early in the morning, taking his alms bowl and outer robe, went to the sacrificial enclosure of the brahmin Kūṭadanta together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. Having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat.

Then the brahmin Kūṭadanta personally served and satisfied the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha with various kinds of excellent food. Then the brahmin Kūṭadanta took a low seat and sat down to one side after the Blessed One had finished his meal and put away his bowl. While the brahmin Kūṭadanta was seated to one side, the Blessed One instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened him with a talk on the Dhamma, rose from his seat, and departed.
