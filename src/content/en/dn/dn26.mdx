#### 1. An Island and Refuge Unto Oneself

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in the Magadhan country at Mātulā. There, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus.”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said:

“Dwell |with yourselves as your own island::independent, self-reliant; lit. own island [attadīpa]|, |bhikkhus,::::| with yourselves as your own refuge, with no other refuge; dwell with the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| as your island, with the Dhamma as your refuge, with no other refuge. And how, |bhikkhus,::::| does a bhikkhu dwell as an island unto himself, with himself as his refuge, with no other refuge, with the Dhamma as his island, with the Dhamma as his refuge, with no other refuge? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application [ātāpī]|, clearly aware and mindful, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; he dwells observing |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself ... the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| in and of itself ... |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and mindful, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu dwells as an island unto himself, with himself as his refuge, with no other refuge, with the Dhamma as his island, with the Dhamma as his refuge, with no other refuge.

Wander in your own resort, |bhikkhus,::::| your own ancestral domain. Māra will not gain access to those who wander in their own resort, in their own ancestral domain; Māra will not get a hold on them. It is because of undertaking wholesome qualities, |bhikkhus,::::| that this merit grows in this way.

#### 2. King Daḷhanemi, the Wheel-Turning Monarch

Once in the past, |bhikkhus,::::| there was a king named |Daḷhanemi::name of a mythological king; lit. with a strong rim [daḷhanemi]|, a |wheel-turning monarch::a just and virtuous ruler whose domain extends over the entire known world [rājā cakkavattī]|, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, a conqueror of the four quarters, who had established stability in the realm, endowed with the seven treasures. He had these seven treasures, namely: the |wheel treasure::jewelled wheel; wheel treasure; one of the seven treasures of a wheel-turning monarch [cakkaratana]|, the elephant treasure, the horse treasure, the jewel treasure, the woman treasure, the householder treasure, and the counselor treasure as the seventh. Moreover, he had more than a thousand sons who were valiant, heroic, able to crush the armies of their enemies. Having conquered this earth bounded by the ocean without rod or sword, solely through the Dhamma, he ruled over it.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| after many years, many centuries, many millennia had passed, King Daḷhanemi addressed a certain man: ‘When, my good man, you see that the celestial wheel treasure has slipped down, shifted from its place, then report it to me.’

‘Yes, your majesty,’ that man replied to King Daḷhanemi.

After many years, many centuries, many millennia had passed, that man saw that the celestial wheel treasure had slipped down, shifted from its place. Seeing this, he approached King Daḷhanemi; having approached, he said to King Daḷhanemi: ‘Sire, you should know that your celestial wheel treasure has slipped down, shifted from its place.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Daḷhanemi summoned the eldest prince and said: ‘Dear prince, it is said that my celestial wheel treasure has slipped down, shifted from its place. And I have heard: “When the celestial wheel treasure of a wheel-turning monarch slips down, shifts from its place, that king does not have long to live.” Human pleasures have been enjoyed by me; now it is time for me to seek celestial pleasures. Come, dear prince, take charge of this earth bounded by the ocean. As for me, having shaved off my hair and beard and donned the ochre robes, I will go forth from the household life into homelessness.’

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| King Daḷhanemi, having properly instructed the eldest prince in royal governance, shaved off his hair and beard, donned the ochre robes, and went forth from the household life into homelessness. And one week after the royal sage had gone forth, the celestial wheel treasure disappeared.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain man approached the anointed noble king; having approached, he said to the anointed noble king: ‘Sire, you should know that the celestial wheel treasure has disappeared.’ At the disappearance of the celestial wheel treasure, the anointed noble king was displeased and experienced sorrow. He approached the royal sage; having approached, he said to the royal sage: ‘Sire, you should know that the celestial wheel treasure has disappeared.’

When this was said, |bhikkhus,::::| the royal sage said to the anointed noble king: ‘Do not be displeased, dear boy, at the disappearance of the celestial wheel treasure; do not experience sorrow. For the celestial wheel treasure is not an ancestral inheritance of yours. Come now, dear boy, fulfill the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch. For it is possible that, as you fulfill the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch, on the fifteenth day of the lunar fortnight of Uposatha, having bathed your head and observed the eight precepts, while standing on the upper terrace of your royal palace, the celestial wheel treasure with a thousand spokes and a nave will appear to you, complete in every aspect.’

##### 2.1. The Noble Duty of a Wheel-Turning Monarch

‘And what, sire, is the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch?’

‘In that case, dear boy, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, paying homage to the Dhamma, esteeming the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as your standard, the Dhamma as your banner, with the Dhamma as your authority, provide just protection, defense, and guardianship for the people of your household, for your armed forces, for the nobles, your vassals, for the brahmins and householders, for the town and country folk, for ascetics and brahmins, and for beasts and birds. And let no wrongdoing prevail in your realm, dear boy. And whoever in your realm is destitute, provide them with financial support.

And whatever ascetics and brahmins in your realm refrain from intoxication and negligence, are established in |patience::forbearance, endurance [khanti]| and |gentleness::kindness [soracca]|, each taming themselves, calming themselves, and |completely quenching::(of mental defilements) causing to be completely cooled [parinibbāpeti]| themselves: approach them from time to time, inquire of them, and consult them: “What, venerable sirs, is |wholesome::healthy, beneficial, useful [kusala]|? What is |unwholesome::unhealthy, unskillful, unbeneficial, or karmically unprofitable [akusala]|? What is |blameworthy::at fault, objectionable [sāvajja]|? What is blameless? What |should be practiced::should be associated with, should be undertaken [sevitabba]|? What should not be practiced? What action, if performed, will lead to my harm and suffering for a long time? Or what action, if performed, will lead to my welfare and happiness for a long time?” Having heard them, you should avoid what is unwholesome and undertake and practice what is wholesome.

This, dear boy, is the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch.’

##### 2.2. The Appearance of the Wheel Treasure

‘Yes, your majesty,’ the anointed noble king replied to the royal sage, and he lived fulfilling the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch.

As he was fulfilling the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch, on the fifteenth day of the lunar fortnight of Uposatha, having bathed his head and observed the eight precepts, while standing on the upper terrace of his royal palace, the celestial wheel treasure with a thousand spokes and a nave appeared to him, complete in every aspect. Seeing it, the anointed noble king thought: ‘I have heard: “When the celestial wheel treasure with a thousand spokes and a nave complete in every aspect appears to an anointed noble king who has bathed his head, observed the eight precepts on the fifteenth day of the lunar fortnight of Uposatha as he stands on the upper terrace of his royal palace, he becomes a wheel-turning monarch.” Am I then a wheel-turning monarch?’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king rose from his seat, arranged his outer robe over one shoulder, took a water vessel in his left hand, and with his right hand sprinkled water over the wheel treasure, saying: ‘Roll forth, auspicious wheel treasure! Conquer, auspicious wheel treasure!’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| that wheel treasure rolled forth toward the eastern quarter, and the wheel-turning king followed behind it with his fourfold army. In whatever region the wheel treasure came to a halt, there the wheel-turning king took up residence with his fourfold army. And the rival kings in the eastern quarter approached the wheel-turning king; having approached, they said to the wheel-turning king: ‘Come, great king! Welcome, great king! All this is yours, great king; instruct us, great king.’ The wheel-turning king said: ‘Living beings are not to be killed. What is not given is not to be taken. Sexual misconduct is not to be committed. False speech is not to be spoken. Intoxicants are not to be consumed. And enjoy your wealth as you did before.’ And the rival kings in the eastern quarter became vassals to the wheel-turning king.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| that wheel treasure plunged into the eastern ocean, emerged, and rolled forth toward the southern quarter ... having plunged into the southern ocean, emerged, and rolled forth toward the western quarter, and the wheel-turning king followed behind it with his fourfold army. In whatever region the wheel treasure came to a halt, there the wheel-turning king took up residence with his fourfold army. And the rival kings in the western quarter approached the wheel-turning king; having approached, they said to the wheel-turning king: ‘Come, great king! Welcome, great king! All this is yours, great king; instruct us, great king.’ The wheel-turning king said: ‘Living beings are not to be killed. What is not given is not to be taken. Sexual misconduct is not to be committed. False speech is not to be spoken. Intoxicants are not to be consumed. And enjoy your wealth as you did before.’ And the rival kings in the western quarter became vassals to the wheel-turning king.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| that wheel treasure plunged into the western ocean, emerged, and rolled forth toward the northern quarter, and the wheel-turning king followed behind it with his fourfold army. In whatever region the wheel treasure came to a halt, there the wheel-turning king took up residence with his fourfold army. And the rival kings in the northern quarter approached the wheel-turning king; having approached, they said to the wheel-turning king: ‘Come, great king! Welcome, great king! All this is yours, great king; instruct us, great king.’ The wheel-turning king said: ‘Living beings are not to be killed. What is not given is not to be taken. Sexual misconduct is not to be committed. False speech is not to be spoken. Intoxicants are not to be consumed. And enjoy your wealth as you did before.’ And the rival kings in the northern quarter became vassals to the wheel-turning king.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| that wheel treasure, having conquered the earth right up to its ocean boundaries, returned to that very royal capital and stood before the gate of the wheel-turning king’s inner palace at the entrance to the hall of justice, as if fixed to an axle, adorning the inner palace of the wheel-turning king.

#### 3. The Second and Subsequent Wheel-Turning Monarchs

A second wheel-turning king too|, bhikkhus,::::| ... a third ... a fourth ... a fifth ... a sixth ... and a seventh wheel-turning king, after many years, many centuries, many millennia had passed, addressed a certain man: ‘When, my good man, you see that the celestial wheel treasure has slipped down, shifted from its place, then report it to me.’ ‘Yes, your majesty,’ that man replied to the wheel-turning king. After many years, many centuries, many millennia had passed, that man saw that the celestial wheel treasure had slipped down, shifted from its place. Seeing this, he approached the wheel-turning king; having approached, he said to the wheel-turning king: ‘Sire, you should know that your celestial wheel treasure has slipped down, shifted from its place.’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the wheel-turning king summoned the eldest prince and said: ‘Dear prince, it is said that my celestial wheel treasure has slipped down, shifted from its place. And I have heard: “When the celestial wheel treasure of a wheel-turning king slips down, shifts from its place, that king does not have long to live.” Human pleasures have been enjoyed by me; now it is time for me to seek celestial pleasures. Come, dear prince, take charge of this earth bounded by the ocean. As for me, having shaved off my hair and beard and donned the ochre robes, I will go forth from the household life into homelessness.’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the wheel-turning king, having properly instructed the eldest prince in royal governance, shaved off his hair and beard, donned the ochre robes, and went forth from the household life into homelessness. And one week after the royal sage had gone forth, the celestial wheel treasure disappeared.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| a certain man approached the anointed noble king; having approached, he said to the anointed noble king: ‘Sire, you should know that the celestial wheel treasure has disappeared.’ At the disappearance of the celestial wheel treasure, the anointed noble king was displeased and experienced sorrow; but he did not approach the royal sage and ask about the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch. Instead, he governed the realm solely by his own ideas. As he governed the realm by his own ideas, the territories did not prosper in the previous manner, as they had under previous kings who fulfilled the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the ministers, courtiers, treasury officers, bodyguards, gatekeepers, and counselors gathered and said to the anointed noble king: ‘Sire, as you govern the realm by your own ideas, the territories do not prosper in the previous manner, as they had under previous kings who fulfilled the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch. There are in your realm, sire, ministers, courtiers, treasury officers, bodyguards, gatekeepers, and counselors—both ourselves and others—who preserve the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch. Come, sire, ask us about the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch! Being questioned, we will explain it to you.’

#### 4. The Decline of Lifespan, Beauty, and Morality

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king assembled the ministers, courtiers, treasury officers, bodyguards, gatekeepers, and counselors, and asked them about the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch. Questioned by him, they explained the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch. Having heard them, he provided just protection, defense, and guardianship, but he did not give financial support to the destitute. With no financial support being given to the destitute, poverty grew rampant.

When poverty had grown rampant, a certain man took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft. They seized him and brought him before the anointed noble king; having brought him, they said: ‘Sire, this man took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft.’

When this was said|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king said to that man: ‘Is it true, my good man, that you took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft?’

‘It is true, sire.’

‘For what reason?’

‘Because I have no livelihood, sire.’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king gave financial support to that man, saying: ‘With this financial support, my good man, maintain your own livelihood, support your mother and father, support your wife and children, engage in business, and establish an uplifting offering for ascetics and brahmins—one that is conducive to heaven, ripens in happiness, and leads to a heavenly world.’

‘Yes, your majesty,’ that man replied to the anointed noble king.

Then another man too|, bhikkhus,::::| took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft. They seized him and brought him before the anointed noble king; having brought him, they said: ‘Sire, this man took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft.’

When this was said|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king said to that man: ‘Is it true, my good man, that you took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft?’

‘It is true, sire.’

‘For what reason?’

‘Because I have no livelihood, sire.’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king gave financial support to that man, saying: ‘With this financial support, my good man, maintain your own livelihood, support your mother and father, support your wife and children, engage in business, and establish an uplifting offering for ascetics and brahmins—one that is conducive to heaven, ripens in happiness, and leads to a heavenly world.’

‘Yes, your majesty,’ that man replied to the anointed noble king.

People heard|, bhikkhus,::::|: ‘It is said that whoever takes what is not given from others through theft is given financial support by the king!’ Hearing this, they thought: ‘What if we too were to take what is not given from others through theft?’ Then|, bhikkhus,::::| another man took what was not given from others through theft.

They seized him and brought him before the anointed noble king; having brought him, they said: ‘Sire, this man took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft.’

When this was said|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king said to that man: ‘Is it true, my good man, that you took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft?’

‘It is true, sire.’

‘For what reason?’

‘Because I have no livelihood, sire.’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| it occurred to the anointed noble king: ‘If I give financial support to whoever takes what is not given from others through theft, theft will only increase. What if I make an absolute end of this man, cut him down at the root, and have him beheaded?’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king commanded his men: ‘In that case, men, bind this man’s arms tightly behind his back with a strong rope, shave his head bald, parade him to the harsh sound of a drum from street to street and crossroad to crossroad, take him out through the southern gate, and to the south of the city make an absolute end of him, cut him down at the root, and cut off his head!’

‘Yes, your majesty,’ those men replied to the anointed noble king. They bound the man’s arms tightly behind his back with a strong rope, shaved his head bald, paraded him to the harsh sound of a drum from street to street and crossroad to crossroad, took him out through the southern gate, and to the south of the city made an absolute end of him, cut him down at the root, and cut off his head.

People heard|, bhikkhus,::::|: ‘It is said that whoever takes what is not given from others through theft is made an absolute end of by the king, cut down at the root, and beheaded!’ Hearing this, they thought: ‘What if we have sharp swords made, and when we take what is not given from others through theft, we make an absolute end of them, cut them down at the root, and cut off their heads?’ So they had sharp swords made, and having done so, they began raiding villages, raiding towns, raiding cities, and committing highway robbery. And from whomever they took what was not given through theft, they made an absolute end of them, cut them down at the root, and cut off their heads.

Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| when financial support was not given to the destitute, poverty grew rampant; when poverty grew rampant, |stealing::taking what is not given [adinnādāna]| grew rampant; when stealing grew rampant, the use of weapons grew rampant; when the use of weapons grew rampant, |killing living beings::destruction of life [pāṇātipāta]| grew rampant. As killing living beings grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was eighty thousand years had a lifespan of only forty thousand years.

Among humans with a lifespan of forty thousand years|, bhikkhus,::::| a certain man took what was not given from others through theft. They seized him and brought him before the anointed noble king; having brought him, they said: ‘Sire, this man took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft.’

When this was said|, bhikkhus,::::| the anointed noble king said to that man: ‘Is it true, my good man, that you took what was not given from others, which was regarded as theft?’

‘No, sire!’ he |lied knowingly::intentionally lied [sampajānamusā]|.

Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| when financial support was not given to the destitute, poverty grew rampant; when poverty grew rampant, stealing grew rampant; when stealing grew rampant, the use of weapons grew rampant; when the use of weapons grew rampant, killing living beings grew rampant; when killing living beings grew rampant, |lying::false speech [musāvāda]| grew rampant. As lying grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was forty thousand years had a lifespan of only twenty thousand years.

Among humans with a lifespan of twenty thousand years|, bhikkhus,::::| a certain man took what was not given from others through theft. Another man reported him to the anointed noble king, saying: ‘Sire, the man named so-and-so took what was not given from others through theft’—thus engaging in |divisive speech::malicious gossip, slander [pisuṇā vācā]|.

Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| when financial support was not given to the destitute, poverty grew rampant; when poverty grew rampant, stealing grew rampant; when stealing grew rampant, the use of weapons grew rampant; when the use of weapons grew rampant, killing living beings grew rampant; when killing living beings grew rampant, lying grew rampant; when lying grew rampant, divisive speech grew rampant. As divisive speech grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was twenty thousand years had a lifespan of only ten thousand years.

Among humans with a lifespan of ten thousand years|, bhikkhus,::::| some beings were beautiful and some were ugly. There, the ugly beings, coveting the beautiful beings, committed misconduct with the wives of others.

Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| when financial support was not given to the destitute, poverty grew rampant ... |sexual misconduct::adultery, improper sexual conduct [kāmesumicchācāra]| grew rampant. As sexual misconduct grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was ten thousand years had a lifespan of only five thousand years.

Among humans with a lifespan of five thousand years|, bhikkhus,::::| two qualities grew rampant: |harsh speech::offensive or abusive speech [pharusā vācā]| and |frivolous chatter::idle gossip, meaningless talk [samphappalāpa]|. As these two qualities grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was five thousand years had a lifespan of only two and a half thousand years for some, and two thousand years for others.

Among humans with a lifespan of two and a half thousand years|, bhikkhus,::::| |craving::greediness, wanting, yearning [abhijjhā]| and  |ill will::hostile thought, malevolent intent, wishing ruin or downfall [byāpāda]| grew rampant. As craving and ill will grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was two and a half thousand years had a lifespan of only one thousand years.

Among humans with a lifespan of one thousand years, |bhikkhus,::::| |wrong view::a distorted perception, an untrue view, a false belief [micchādiṭṭhi]| grew rampant. As wrong view grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was one thousand years had a lifespan of only five hundred years.

Among humans with a lifespan of five hundred years, |bhikkhus,::::| three qualities grew rampant: |illicit desire::immoral passion [adhammarāga]|, |unbalanced greed::excessive greed [visamalobha]|, and |wrongdoing::improper conduct [micchādhamma]|. As these three qualities grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was five hundred years had a lifespan of only two and a half hundred years for some, and two hundred years for others.

Among humans with a lifespan of two and a half hundred years, |bhikkhus,::::| these qualities grew rampant: lack of filial respect for mothers, lack of filial respect for fathers, lack of respect for ascetics, lack of respect for brahmins, and lack of honoring the elders in the family.

Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| when financial support was not given to the destitute, poverty grew rampant; when poverty grew rampant, stealing grew rampant; when stealing grew rampant, the use of weapons grew rampant; when the use of weapons grew rampant, killing living beings grew rampant; when killing living beings grew rampant, lying grew rampant; when lying grew rampant, divisive speech grew rampant; when divisive speech grew rampant, sexual misconduct grew rampant; when sexual misconduct grew rampant, two qualities grew rampant: harsh speech and frivolous chatter; when these two qualities grew rampant, craving and ill will grew rampant; when craving and ill will grew rampant, wrong view grew rampant; when wrong view grew rampant, three qualities grew rampant: illicit desire, unbalanced greed, and wrongdoing; when these three qualities grew rampant, these qualities grew rampant: lack of filial respect for mothers, lack of filial respect for fathers, lack of respect for ascetics, lack of respect for brahmins, and lack of honoring the elders in the family. As these qualities grew rampant, the lifespan of those beings declined and their beauty declined. With the decline of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans whose lifespan was two and a half hundred years had a lifespan of only one hundred years.

#### 5. The Era of the Ten-Year Lifespan

There will come a time|, bhikkhus,::::| when the children of these humans will have a lifespan of only ten years. Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, girls of five years will be eligible for marriage. Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, these flavors will disappear, namely: ghee, fresh butter, oil, honey, molasses, and salt. Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, |coarse grain::wild grass seed, poor quality millet [kudrūsaka]| will be the finest food. Just as today fine rice and meat is considered the finest food, so too, |bhikkhus,::::| among humans with a ten-year lifespan, coarse grain will be the finest food.

Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, the ten wholesome courses of action will entirely disappear, and the ten unwholesome courses of action will blazingly prevail. Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, there will not even be the word ‘wholesome,’ let alone anyone who does what is wholesome. Those who lack filial respect for mother and father, lack respect for ascetics and brahmins, and do not honor the elders of the family will be revered and praised. Just as today those who respect mother and father, respect ascetics and brahmins, and honor the elders of the family are revered and praised; so too, among humans with a ten-year lifespan, those who lack filial respect for mother and father, lack respect for ascetics and brahmins, and do not honor the elders of the family will be revered and praised.

Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, there will be no recognition of ‘mother,’ ‘maternal aunt,’ ‘maternal uncle’s wife,’ ‘teacher’s wife,’ or ‘the wives of elders.’ The world will fall into confusion, promiscuity, like goats, sheep, chickens, pigs, dogs, and jackals.

Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, fierce hostility will arise toward one another, fierce ill will, fierce malice, and thoughts of murder. Mother toward child and child toward mother; father toward child and child toward father; brother toward sister and sister toward brother: fierce hostility will arise, fierce ill will, fierce malice, and thoughts of murder. Just as a hunter on seeing a deer feels fierce hostility, fierce ill will, fierce malice, and thoughts of murder, so too, among humans with a ten-year lifespan, fierce hostility will arise toward one another, fierce ill will, fierce malice, and thoughts of murder.

Among humans with a ten-year lifespan, there will be a seven-day |sword-interval period::a period of mutual slaughter where people perceive each other as wild animals and kill with weapons [satthantarakappa]|. They will perceive one another as wild beasts. Sharp swords will appear in their hands. With those sharp swords, crying ‘This is a beast! This is a beast!’ they will slaughter one another.

Then it will occur to some beings: ‘Let us not harm anyone, nor let anyone harm us! What if we enter thick grass, jungle thickets, tree hollows, impassable river ravines, or mountain chasms, and survive on forest roots and fruits?’ They will enter thick grass, jungle thickets, tree hollows, impassable river ravines, or mountain chasms, and survive for seven days on forest roots and fruits.

At the end of those seven days, emerging from the thick grass, jungle thickets, tree hollows, impassable river ravines, and mountain chasms, they will embrace one another, weeping together and comforting one another, crying: ‘How wonderful, good being, you are alive! How wonderful, good being, you are alive!’

#### 6. The Growth of Lifespan and Beauty

Then it will occur to those beings: ‘It is because of undertaking unwholesome qualities that we have suffered such extensive destruction of our kin. What if we do what is wholesome? What wholesome deed should we do? What if we refrain from killing living beings, and undertake and practice this wholesome quality?’ They will refrain from killing living beings, and undertake and practice this wholesome quality. Because of undertaking wholesome qualities, their lifespan will increase and their beauty will increase. With the increase of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans with a ten-year lifespan will live for twenty years.

Then it will occur to those beings: ‘Because of undertaking wholesome qualities, our lifespan is increasing and our beauty is increasing. What if we do wholesome deeds to an even greater degree? What wholesome deed should we do? What if we refrain from stealing ... refrain from sexual misconduct ... refrain from lying ... refrain from divisive speech ... refrain from harsh speech ... refrain from frivolous chatter ... give up covetousness ... give up ill will ... give up wrong view ... give up three qualities: illicit lust, perverse greed, and wrong doctrine ... What if we show filial respect for mothers, show filial respect for fathers, respect ascetics, respect brahmins, and honor the elders in the family, undertaking and practicing this wholesome quality?’ They will show filial respect for mothers and fathers, respect ascetics and brahmins, and honor the elders in the family, undertaking and practicing this wholesome quality.

Because of undertaking wholesome qualities, their lifespan will increase and their beauty will increase. With the increase of their lifespan and beauty, the children of humans with a twenty-year lifespan will live for forty years ... eighty years ... one hundred and sixty years ... three hundred and twenty years ... six hundred and forty years ... two thousand years ... four thousand years ... eight thousand years ... twenty thousand years ... forty thousand years ... and the children of humans with a forty-thousand-year lifespan will have a lifespan of eighty thousand years. Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, |bhikkhus,::::| girls of five hundred years will be eligible for marriage.

#### 7. The Appearance of King Saṅkha

Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, |bhikkhus,::::| there will be only three afflictions: desire, hunger, and old age. Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, this |Jambudīpa::a reference to the Indian subcontinent, lit. land of the black plum aka jambu trees [jambudīpa]| will be successful and prosperous, with villages, towns, and royal capitals so close that a cock could fly from one to the next. Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, this Jambudīpa will be crowded with people like |Avīci::name of one of the great hells; lit. without waves; without an interval; uninterrupted [avīci]|, just like a forest of reeds or a thicket of canes. Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, this city of Varanasi will be a royal capital named |Ketumati::name of a future capital city; lit. adorned with banners [ketumatī]|, successful, prosperous, populous, crowded with people, and well supplied with food. Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, there will be eighty-four thousand cities in this Jambudīpa, headed by the royal capital of Ketumati.

Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, |bhikkhus,::::| in the royal capital of Ketumati, a king named Saṅkha will arise, a wheel-turning monarch, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, a conqueror of the four quarters, who has established stability in the realm, endowed with the seven treasures. He will have these seven treasures, namely: the wheel treasure, the elephant treasure, the horse treasure, the jewel treasure, the woman treasure, the householder treasure, and the counselor treasure as the seventh. Moreover, he will have more than a thousand sons who are valiant, heroic, able to crush the armies of their enemies. Having conquered this earth bounded by the ocean without rod or sword, solely through the Dhamma, he will rule over it.

#### 8. The Arising of the Buddha Metteyya

Among humans with an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, |bhikkhus,::::| a Blessed One named Metteyya will arise in the world, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One; just as I now have arisen in the world, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One. He will declare this world with its deities, Māras, Brahmas, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its rulers and people, which he has personally realized with direct knowledge; just as I now declare this world with its deities, Māras, Brahmas, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its rulers and people, which I have personally realized with direct knowledge. He will teach the Dhamma that is beneficial in the beginning, beneficial in the middle, and beneficial in the end, meaningful and well-phrased, and reveal a spiritual life that is entirely perfect and pure; just as I now teach the Dhamma that is beneficial in the beginning, beneficial in the middle, and beneficial in the end, meaningful and well-phrased, and reveal a spiritual life that is entirely perfect and pure. He will guide a Saṅgha of many thousands of bhikkhus, just as I now guide a Saṅgha of many hundreds of bhikkhus.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| King Saṅkha will re-erect the palace pillar that was built by King Mahāpanāda, live in it, and then relinquish it and give it away; having given gifts to ascetics, brahmins, the destitute, wayfarers, mendicants, and beggars, he will shave off his hair and beard, don the ochre robes, and go forth from the household life into homelessness in the presence of the Blessed One Metteyya, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One. Having thus gone forth, living alone, secluded, diligent, resolute, and with continuous effort, not long after, he will realize with direct knowledge and attain in that very life the unsurpassed culmination of the spiritual life, for the sake of which sons of good families rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness.

Dwell |with yourselves as your own island::independent, self-reliant; lit. own island [attadīpa]|, |bhikkhus,::::| with yourselves as your own refuge, with no other refuge; dwell with the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| as your island, with the Dhamma as your refuge, with no other refuge. And how, |bhikkhus,::::| does a bhikkhu dwell as an island unto himself, with himself as his refuge, with no other refuge, with the Dhamma as his island, with the Dhamma as his refuge, with no other refuge? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application [ātāpī]|, clearly aware and mindful, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; he dwells observing |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself ... the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| in and of itself ... |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and mindful, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. #### 9. The Growth of a Bhikkhu’s Lifespan, Beauty, and Happiness

Wander in your own resort, |bhikkhus,::::| your own ancestral domain. Wandering in your own resort, your own ancestral domain, you will grow in lifespan, you will grow in beauty, you will grow in happiness, you will grow in wealth, and you will grow in strength.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is a bhikkhu’s lifespan? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu cultivates the |basis of psychic power::basis for spiritual power, paths to supernormal ability [iddhipāda]| that is endowed with |collectedness::stability of mind, stillness of mind, mental composure [samādhi]| arising from |aspiration::a goal, an interest, an objective, i.e. a wholesome motivation [chanda]| and accompanied by intentional effort. He cultivates the basis of psychic power that is endowed with collectedness arising from |energy::willpower, determination [vīriya]| and accompanied by intentional effort. He cultivates the basis of psychic power that is endowed with collectedness arising from |[purification of] mind::clearing the affective awareness of hindrances [citta]| and accompanied by intentional effort. He cultivates the basis of psychic power that is endowed with collectedness arising from |investigation::consideration, reflection, and examination [vīmaṃsā]| and accompanied by intentional effort. Because he has cultivated and frequently practiced these four bases of psychic power, if he wishes, he could live for an |aeon::lifespan of a world system, a vast cosmic time span [kappa]| or for the remainder of an aeon. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is a bhikkhu’s lifespan.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is a bhikkhu’s beauty? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu is |virtuous::ethical, moral [sīlavant]|, dwells restrained with the restraint of the |moral code of conduct::monastic code of discipline which promotes harmonious and pure conduct with others [pātimokkha]|, who knows how to behave and keep to suitable places, who sees danger even in the slightest fault, and undertakes the training by practicing the training rules. And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is a bhikkhu’s happiness? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu, entirely secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome mental states, enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is |accompanied by thought::with thinking, with reflection [savitakka]| and |examination::with investigation, evaluation [savicāra]|, |born of seclusion::secluded from the defilements [vivekaja]|, and imbued with |uplifting joy and pleasure::delight and ease; sometimes experienced as ecstasy, intense exhilaration or rapture [pītisukha]|; with the settling of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second jhāna ... the third jhāna ... the fourth jhāna. And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is a bhikkhu’s wealth? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| with a mind imbued with |loving-kindness::goodwill, friendliness, benevolence [metta]|, a bhikkhu dwells pervading one quarter, then a second, then a third, then a fourth. Thus, with a mind imbued with equanimity, he pervades the entire world—above, below, across, everywhere, encompassing all beings—with a vast, exalted, boundless mind, without hostility and free from affliction. And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is a bhikkhu’s power? The Blessed One said this. The bhikkhus were delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words.
