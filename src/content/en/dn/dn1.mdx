#### 1. Talk on Wanderers

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was traveling along the highway between |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]| and |Nālandā::name of a town near Rājagaha [nāḷandā]| together with a large |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of bhikkhus, about five hundred bhikkhus. And the wanderer Suppiya was also traveling along the highway between Rājagaha and Nālandā together with his resident pupil, the student Brahmadatta. There, the wanderer Suppiya spoke dispraise of the Buddha in many ways, dispraise of the Dhamma in many ways, dispraise of the Saṅgha in many ways; but his pupil, the student Brahmadatta, spoke praise of the Buddha in many ways, praise of the Dhamma in many ways, praise of the Saṅgha in many ways. Thus these two, teacher and pupil, holding directly opposing views, were following closely behind the Blessed One and the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.

Then the Blessed One took up residence for the night in the royal rest-house at |Ambalaṭṭhikā::name of a park between Rājagaha and Nālandā; lit. mango sapling [ambalaṭṭhikā]| together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. And the wanderer Suppiya also took up residence for the night in the royal rest-house at Ambalaṭṭhikā together with his pupil, the student Brahmadatta. There too, the wanderer Suppiya spoke dispraise of the Buddha in many ways, dispraise of the Dhamma in many ways, dispraise of the Saṅgha in many ways; but his pupil, the student Brahmadatta, spoke praise of the Buddha in many ways, praise of the Dhamma in many ways, praise of the Saṅgha in many ways. Thus these two, teacher and pupil, holding directly opposing views, dwelt there.

Then several bhikkhus rose at the crack of dawn and sat gathered together in the circular pavilion, and this discussion arose among them:

“It is wonderful, friends, it is marvelous, friends, how the various dispositions of beings have been so clearly understood by the Blessed One, who knows and sees, the |Arahant::worthy one, fully awakened being, free from all mental defilements [arahant]|, the perfectly Awakened One! For this wanderer Suppiya speaks dispraise of the Buddha in many ways, dispraise of the Dhamma in many ways, dispraise of the Saṅgha in many ways; but his pupil, the student Brahmadatta, speaks praise of the Buddha in many ways, praise of the Dhamma in many ways, praise of the Saṅgha in many ways. Thus these two, teacher and pupil, holding directly opposing views, were following closely behind the Blessed One and the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.”

Then the Blessed One, knowing the discussion among those bhikkhus, went to the circular pavilion. Having approached, he sat down on the prepared seat. Having sat down, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus, what were you sitting talking about just now? What conversation was left unfinished?”

When this was said, those bhikkhus said to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, while we had risen at the crack of dawn and were sitting gathered together in the circular pavilion, this discussion arose among us: ‘It is wonderful, friends, it is marvelous, friends, how the various dispositions of beings have been so clearly understood by the Blessed One, who knows and sees, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One! For this wanderer Suppiya speaks dispraise of the Buddha in many ways, dispraise of the Dhamma in many ways, dispraise of the Saṅgha in many ways; but his pupil, the student Brahmadatta, speaks praise of the Buddha in many ways, praise of the Dhamma in many ways, praise of the Saṅgha in many ways. Thus these two, teacher and pupil, holding directly opposing views, were following closely behind the Blessed One and the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.’ This was our unfinished discussion, venerable sir, when the Blessed One arrived.”

“If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak dispraise of me, or dispraise of the Dhamma, or dispraise of the Saṅgha, you should not on that account harbor |anger::annoyance, irritation, loathing; lit. collision [āghāta]|, |bitterness::indignation [appaccaya]|, or displeasure. If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak dispraise of me, or dispraise of the Dhamma, or dispraise of the Saṅgha, and if on that account you were angry or displeased, that would be an obstacle for you yourselves. If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak dispraise of me, or dispraise of the Dhamma, or dispraise of the Saṅgha, and if on that account you were angry or displeased, would you recognize what is well-spoken by others and what is poorly spoken by others?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak dispraise of me, or dispraise of the Dhamma, or dispraise of the Saṅgha, you should explain what is untrue as untrue: ‘For this is untrue, for this is false. This does not exist in us, and this is not found among us.’

If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak praise of me, or praise of the Dhamma, or praise of the Saṅgha, you should not on that account harbor joy, gladness, or |elation::excitement; lit. jumping up state [uppilāvitattā]|. If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak praise of me, or praise of the Dhamma, or praise of the Saṅgha, and if on that account you were joyful, glad, and elated, that would be an obstacle for you yourselves. If others|, bhikkhus,::::| were to speak praise of me, or praise of the Dhamma, or praise of the Saṅgha, you should acknowledge what is true as true: ‘For this is true, for this is real. This exists in us, and this is found among us.’”

##### 3.2.5. Nibbāna in the Present Life

There are|, bhikkhus,::::| some ascetics and brahmins who are theorists of |supreme Nibbāna in the present life::attainment of highest peace through sensual gratification or the jhānas [paramadiṭṭhadhammanibbāna]|, who assert supreme Nibbāna in the present life for an existing being on five grounds. But someone else says to them: ‘<em>That</em> self of which you speak does exist, I don’t deny it. But that’s not how <em>this</em> self attains supreme Nibbāna in the present life. Why is that? These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the forty-four grounds on which those ascetics and brahmins who speculate about the future and hold views about the future make various assertions concerning the future. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the principles—profound, hard to perceive, difficult to comprehend, tranquil, sublime, beyond the realm of conjecture, subtle, to be experienced by the wise—which the Tathāgata makes known having realized them for himself through direct knowledge, and about which those who would speak genuine praise of the Tathāgata would rightly speak.”

### 5. Transcending the Cycle

When|, bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu discerns as they truly are the arising, the passing away, the gratification, the drawback, and the escape regarding the |six fields of contact::the six internal and external sense bases [channaṁ phassāyatanānaṁ]|, he clearly knows what lies far beyond all these things.

Whatever ascetics and brahmins there are|, bhikkhus,::::| who speculate about the past, speculate about the future, or speculate about both the past and the future, holding views about the past and future, who make various assertions concerning the past and future—all of them are caught in the net of these sixty-two grounds. Enclosed in it, whenever they emerge they emerge within it; trapped in it, whenever they emerge they emerge within it.

Just as if|, bhikkhus,::::| a skilled fisherman or fisherman’s apprentice were to cast a fine-meshed net over a small pool of water. He would think: ‘Whatever sizable creatures there are in this pool of water, all of them are caught in this net. Enclosed in it, whenever they emerge they emerge within it; trapped in it, whenever they emerge they emerge within it.’ In the same way|, bhikkhus::::|, whatever ascetics and brahmins there are who speculate about the past, speculate about the future, or speculate about both the past and the future, holding views about the past and future, who make various assertions concerning the past and future—all of them are caught in the net of these sixty-two grounds. Enclosed in it, whenever they emerge they emerge within it; trapped in it, whenever they emerge they emerge within it.

The Tathāgata’s body remains|, bhikkhus,::::| but his conduit to existence has been cut off. As long as his body remains, gods and humans will see him. But with the breakup of the body after the exhaustion of life, gods and humans will see him no more.

Just as|, bhikkhus,::::| when a cluster of mangoes is cut off at the stalk, all the mangoes that were attached to the stalk follow along with it, so too|, bhikkhus::::|, the Tathāgata’s body remains, but his conduit to existence has been cut off. As long as his body remains, gods and humans will see him. But with the breakup of the body after the exhaustion of life, gods and humans will see him no more.”

When this was said, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “It is wonderful, venerable sir, it is marvelous, venerable sir! Venerable sir, what is the name of this Dhamma discourse?”

“As to that, Ānanda, you may remember this Dhamma discourse as ‘The Net of Meaning’; you may remember it as ‘The Net of the Dhamma’; you may remember it as ‘The Supreme Net’; you may remember it as ‘The Net of Views’; you may remember it as ‘The Unsurpassed Triumph in Battle.’”

The Blessed One said this. The bhikkhus were delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words. And while this exposition was being spoken, the ten-thousandfold world system shook.
