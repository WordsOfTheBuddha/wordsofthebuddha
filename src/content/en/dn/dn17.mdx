Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling near |Kusinārā::Kushinagar, a present-day town in Uttar Pradesh, India, revered as the place where the Buddha attained Final Nibbāna. [kusinārā]| in the Sal Grove of the |Mallas::name of the people of Malla, Mallans; lit. wrestlers [mallā]|, between the twin sāla trees, at the time of his |final Nibbāna::complete cooling, full quenching, total emancipation, dying one’s final death [parinibbāna]|.

Then the venerable Ānanda approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Ānanda addressed the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may the Blessed One not attain final Nibbāna in this small insignificant town, this little rough-country town, this small branch town. There are, venerable sir, other great cities, namely: Campā, Rājagaha, Sāvatthi, Sāketa, Kosambi, and Varanasi. Let the Blessed One attain final Nibbāna there. There are many wealthy aristocrats, wealthy brahmins, and wealthy householders there who have deep confidence in the Tathāgata. They will honor the Tathāgata’s body.”

“Do not speak thus, Ānanda. Do not say, Ānanda: ‘a small insignificant town, a little rough-country town, a small branch town.’

#### 1. The Royal Capital of Kusāvati

Formerly, Ānanda, there was a king named |Mahāsudassana::name of an ancient king; a previous birth of the Buddha [mahāsudassana]|, an anointed king of the noble class, a conqueror of the four quarters, who had attained stability in his realm. Ānanda, this Kusinārā was King Mahāsudassana’s royal capital, named |Kusāvati::name of the royal capital of king Mahāsudassana; lit. having lots of kusa grass [kusāvatī]|. It extended twelve |yojanas::yojana is a unit of distance used in ancient India, ranging from 3.5 to 15 km [yojana]| from east to west, and seven yojanas from north to south. Ānanda, the royal capital Kusāvati was successful, prosperous, populous, crowded with people, and well supplied with food. Just as, Ānanda, the royal capital of the gods named |Āḷakamandā::name of a heavenly capital city [āḷakamandā]| is successful, prosperous, populous, crowded with |spirits::nature spirits, powerful deities [yakkha]|, and well supplied with food—so too, Ānanda, the royal capital Kusāvati was successful, prosperous, populous, crowded with people, and well supplied with food.

Ānanda, the royal capital Kusāvati was never without ten sounds by day or by night, namely: the sound of elephants, the sound of horses, the sound of chariots, the sound of kettle drums, the sound of drums, the sound of lutes, the sound of singing, the sound of conches, the sound of cymbals, and the sound of clapping; and the tenth sound: ‘Eat, drink, chew!’

The royal capital Kusāvati, Ānanda, was encircled by seven encircling walls: one made of gold, one made of silver, one made of beryl, one made of crystal, one made of ruby, one made of emerald, and one made of all gems.

The royal capital Kusāvati, Ānanda, had gates of four colors: one gate was made of gold, one made of silver, one made of beryl, and one made of crystal. At each gate, seven posts were erected, three fathoms deep and twelve fathoms high: one pillar was made of gold, one made of silver, one made of beryl, one made of crystal, one made of ruby, one made of emerald, and one made of all gems.

The royal capital Kusāvati, Ānanda, was encircled by seven rows of palm trees: one row of palm trees was made of gold, one made of silver, one made of beryl, one made of crystal, one made of ruby, one made of emerald, and one made of all gems. The golden palm tree had a golden trunk, with silver leaves and fruits. The silver palm tree had a silver trunk, with golden leaves and fruits. The beryl palm tree had a beryl trunk, with crystal leaves and fruits. The crystal palm tree had a crystal trunk, with beryl leaves and fruits. The ruby palm tree had a ruby trunk, with emerald leaves and fruits. The emerald palm tree had an emerald trunk, with ruby leaves and fruits. The palm tree of all gems had a trunk of all gems, with leaves and fruits of all gems. And when those rows of palm trees were stirred by the wind, Ānanda, their sound was melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating. Just as, Ānanda, the sound of a five-piece musical ensemble, well tuned, skillfully played, and performed by master musicians, is melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating—even so, Ānanda, when those rows of palm trees were stirred by the wind, their sound was melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating. And whatever rogues, drunkards, and drinkers there were in the royal capital of Kusāvati at that time, Ānanda, they revelled to the sound of those rows of palm trees stirred by the wind.

#### 2. Endowed with the Seven Treasures

##### 2.1. The Wheel Treasure

King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with seven treasures and four powers. What seven?

Here, Ānanda, on an uposatha day of the fifteenth, when King Mahāsudassana had washed his head and observed the uposatha, having gone up to the top story of the royal palace, the divine wheel treasure appeared to him, with a thousand spokes, with rim and hub, complete in all aspects. Seeing it, King Mahāsudassana thought: ‘I have heard: “When the divine wheel treasure appears to an anointed king of the noble class on an uposatha day of the fifteenth, when he has washed his head and observed the uposatha, having gone up to the top story of the royal palace, complete in all aspects with a thousand spokes, rim, and hub, he becomes a |wheel-turning monarch::a just and virtuous ruler whose domain extends over the entire known world [rājā cakkavattī]|.” Might I be a wheel-turning monarch?’

Then, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana rose from his seat, draped his outer robe over one shoulder, took a golden water pitcher in his left hand, and with his right hand sprinkled the wheel treasure with water, saying: ‘Roll forth, precious wheel! Triumph, precious wheel!’

Then, Ānanda, that wheel treasure rolled forth toward the eastern quarter, and King Mahāsudassana followed behind it with his fourfold army. In whatever region, Ānanda, the wheel treasure came to a halt, there King Mahāsudassana took up residence with his fourfold army.

Whatever rival kings there were in the eastern quarter, Ānanda, approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘Come, Great King! Welcome, Great King! All this is yours, Great King; instruct us, Great King.’

King Mahāsudassana said: ‘Do not kill living beings. Do not take what is not given. Do not engage in sexual misconduct. Do not speak falsely. Do not consume intoxicants. And enjoy your wealth as you did before.’ Whatever rival kings there were in the eastern quarter, Ānanda, became vassals of King Mahāsudassana.

Then, Ānanda, that wheel treasure plunged into the eastern ocean, emerged, and rolled forth toward the southern quarter ...

... having plunged into the southern ocean, emerged, and rolled forth toward the western quarter ...

... having plunged into the western ocean, emerged, and rolled forth toward the northern quarter, and King Mahāsudassana followed behind it with his fourfold army. In whatever region, Ānanda, the wheel treasure came to a halt, there King Mahāsudassana took up residence with his fourfold army.

Whatever rival kings there were in the northern quarter, Ānanda, approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘Come, Great King! Welcome, Great King! All this is yours, Great King; instruct us, Great King.’

King Mahāsudassana said: ‘Do not kill living beings. Do not take what is not given. Do not engage in sexual misconduct. Do not speak falsely. Do not consume intoxicants. And enjoy your wealth as you did before.’ Whatever rival kings there were in the northern quarter, Ānanda, became vassals of King Mahāsudassana.

Then, Ānanda, that wheel treasure, having conquered the earth right up to its ocean boundaries, returned to the royal capital of Kusāvati and stood before the gate of King Mahāsudassana’s inner palace at the entrance to the hall of justice, as if fixed to an axle, adorning the inner palace of King Mahāsudassana. Such was the wheel treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

##### 2.2. The Elephant Treasure

Furthermore, Ānanda, the elephant treasure appeared to King Mahāsudassana: all white, seven-limbed, possessed of psychic power, traveling through the air, a royal elephant named Uposatha. Seeing him, King Mahāsudassana’s heart was gladdened: ‘A magnificent elephant mount indeed, if only he would submit to training!’ And that elephant treasure, Ānanda, submitted to training just like a noble thoroughbred elephant well-trained for a long time.

Formerly, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana, testing that very elephant treasure, mounted him early in the morning, rode across the earth right up to its ocean boundaries, and returned to the royal capital of Kusāvati in time for breakfast. Such was the elephant treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

##### 2.3. The Horse Treasure

Furthermore, Ānanda, the horse treasure appeared to King Mahāsudassana: all white with a black head, with mane like muñja grass, possessed of psychic power, traveling through the air, a royal steed named Valāhaka. Seeing him, King Mahāsudassana’s heart was gladdened: ‘A magnificent horse mount indeed, if only he would submit to training!’ And that horse treasure, Ānanda, submitted to training just like a noble thoroughbred horse well-trained for a long time.

Formerly, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana, testing that very horse treasure, mounted him early in the morning, rode across the earth right up to its ocean boundaries, and returned to the royal capital of Kusāvati in time for breakfast. Such was the horse treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

##### 2.4. The Jewel Treasure

Furthermore, Ānanda, the jewel treasure appeared to King Mahāsudassana. It was a beautiful beryl gem of purest water, eight-faceted, well cut, clear and transparent, luminous, and endowed with all good qualities. And the radiance of that jewel treasure, Ānanda, spread for a whole yojana all around.

Formerly, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana, testing that very jewel treasure, marshaled his fourfold army, mounted the jewel atop his banner, and set out in the pitch darkness of the night. And whatever villages there were all around, Ānanda, they set about their work by that light, believing it was daytime. Such was the jewel treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

##### 2.5. The Woman Treasure

Furthermore, Ānanda, the woman treasure appeared to King Mahāsudassana: lovely, pleasing to see, charming, endowed with supreme beauty of complexion; not too tall nor too short, not too thin nor too fat, not too dark nor too fair; surpassing human beauty without reaching divine radiance. And the bodily touch of that woman treasure, Ānanda, was like a tuft of cotton or a tuft of kapok. In the cold her limbs were warm, and in the heat they were cool. The scent of sandalwood drifted from her body, and the fragrance of blue lotuses from her mouth. That woman treasure, Ānanda, rose before King Mahāsudassana, retired after him, was eager to serve, pleasing in her conduct, and spoke affectionate words. That woman treasure, Ānanda, was never unfaithful to King Mahāsudassana even in thought, let alone in body. Such was the woman treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

##### 2.6. The Householder Treasure

Furthermore, Ānanda, the householder treasure appeared to King Mahāsudassana. Through the ripening of his past deeds, the divine eye appeared to him, with which he could see treasures, both with owners and without owners.

He approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘Do not be anxious, sire! I will manage all affairs involving wealth for you.’

Formerly, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana, testing that very householder treasure, boarded a boat, steered it into the middle of the river Ganges, and said to the householder treasure: ‘Householder, I have need of gold and treasure.’

‘Then, Great King, let the boat approach one of the banks.’

‘It is right here, householder, that I have need of gold and treasure.’

Then, Ānanda, that householder treasure dipped both hands into the water, pulled up a jar full of gold and treasure, and said to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Is this enough, Great King? Has enough been done, Great King? Have you been honored enough, Great King?’

King Mahāsudassana said: ‘This is enough, householder! Enough has been done, householder! I have been honored enough, householder!’

Such was the householder treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

##### 2.7. The Counselor Treasure

Furthermore, Ānanda, the counselor treasure appeared to King Mahāsudassana: wise, competent, intelligent, able to guide King Mahāsudassana in what should be approached, what should be avoided, and what should be maintained.

He approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘Do not be anxious, sire! I will counsel you.’

Such was the counselor treasure, Ānanda, that appeared to King Mahāsudassana.

King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with these seven treasures.

#### 3. Endowed with the Four Powers

King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with four powers. What four?

Here, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana was handsome, pleasing to look at, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, far surpassing other human beings. King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with this first power.

Furthermore, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana was long-lived, enduring for a great length of time, far surpassing other human beings. King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with this second power.

Furthermore, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana was without affliction and rarely ill, endowed with a balanced digestion that was neither too cold nor too hot, far surpassing other human beings. King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with this third power.

Furthermore, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana was dear and pleasing to the brahmins and householders. Just as, Ānanda, a father is dear and pleasing to his children, even so, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana was dear and pleasing to the brahmins and householders. And the brahmins and householders were also dear and pleasing to King Mahāsudassana. Just as, Ānanda, children are dear and pleasing to their father, even so, Ānanda, the brahmins and householders were dear and pleasing to King Mahāsudassana.

Formerly, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana went out to the pleasure garden with his fourfold army. Then, Ānanda, the brahmins and householders approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘Travel slowly, sire, so that we may gaze upon you for longer!’ And King Mahāsudassana addressed his charioteer: ‘Drive the chariot slowly, charioteer, so that I may gaze upon the brahmins and householders for longer!’ King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with this fourth power.

King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, was endowed with these four powers.

#### 4. The Palace of the Dhamma and the Lotus Pond

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Why don’t I have lotus ponds built between the rows of palm trees, at intervals of a hundred bow-lengths?’

And King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, had lotus ponds built between the rows of palm trees at intervals of a hundred bow-lengths. Those lotus ponds, Ānanda, were lined with tiles of four colors: one tile was made of gold, one made of silver, one made of beryl, and one made of crystal.

Moreover, Ānanda, at those lotus ponds, there were four flights of stairs made of four different materials: one flight of stairs was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal. The golden flight of stairs had posts made of gold, with banisters and crossbars made of silver. The silver flight of stairs had posts made of silver, with banisters and crossbars made of gold. The beryl flight of stairs had posts made of beryl, with banisters and crossbars made of crystal. The crystal flight of stairs had posts made of crystal, with banisters and crossbars made of beryl. Furthermore, those lotus ponds, Ānanda, were encircled by two balustrades: one made of gold and one made of silver. The golden balustrade had posts made of gold, with banisters and crossbars made of silver. The silver balustrade had posts made of silver, with banisters and crossbars made of gold.

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Why don’t I plant flowers in these lotus ponds—blue lotuses, pink lotuses, and white lotuses—blooming in all seasons, accessible to all people?’ And King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, planted flowers in those lotus ponds—blue lotuses, pink lotuses, and white lotuses—blooming in all seasons, accessible to all people.

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Why don’t I station bath attendants on the banks of these lotus ponds, who will bathe the people as they come?’ And King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, stationed bath attendants on the banks of those lotus ponds, who bathed the people as they came.

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Why don’t I establish charity on the banks of these lotus ponds—food for those who need food, drink for those who need drink, clothing for those who need clothing, vehicles for those who need vehicles, beds for those who need beds, women for those who need women, beasts of burden for those who need beasts of burden, silver for those who need silver, and gold for those who need gold?’ And King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, established charity on the banks of those lotus ponds—food for those who need food, drink for those who need drink, clothing for those who need clothing, vehicles for those who need vehicles, beds for those who need beds, women for those who need women, beasts of burden for those who need beasts of burden, silver for those who need silver, and gold for those who need gold.

Then, Ānanda, the brahmins and householders, taking abundant wealth, approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘Sire, this abundant wealth has been brought specifically for you; may Your Majesty accept it!’

‘Enough, good sirs! I already have abundant wealth raised through righteous taxes. Let this remain yours, and take even more from here!’

Having been declined by the king, they withdrew to one side and considered: ‘It would not be proper for us to take this wealth back to our own homes. Why don’t we build a dwelling for King Mahāsudassana?’

They approached King Mahāsudassana and said: ‘We will build a dwelling for you, sire.’ King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, consented in silence.

Then, Ānanda, Sakka, lord of the gods, knowing with his own mind the line of thought in King Mahāsudassana’s mind, addressed the divine artisan Vissakamma: ‘Come, dear Vissakamma, build a dwelling for King Mahāsudassana—a palace named the Dhamma.’

‘Yes, Your Majesty,’ the divine artisan Vissakamma replied to Sakka, lord of the gods. Then, just as easily as a strong man might extend his flexed arm or flex his extended arm, he disappeared from among the gods of the Thirty-Three and appeared before King Mahāsudassana.

Then, Ānanda, the divine artisan Vissakamma said to King Mahāsudassana: ‘I will build a dwelling for you, sire—a palace named the Dhamma.’ King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, consented in silence.

And the divine artisan Vissakamma, Ānanda, built for King Mahāsudassana a dwelling—the palace named the Dhamma.

The Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, was one yojana in length from east to west, and half a yojana in width from north to south. The foundation of the Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, was raised three fathoms high, lined with tiles made of four different materials: one tile was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal.

The Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, had eighty-four thousand posts made of four different materials: one post was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal. The Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, was covered with floor panels made of four different materials: one panel was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal.

The Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, had twenty-four flights of stairs made of four different materials: one flight of stairs was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal. The golden flight of stairs had posts made of gold, with banisters and crossbars made of silver. The silver flight of stairs had posts made of silver, with banisters and crossbars made of gold. The beryl flight of stairs had posts made of beryl, with banisters and crossbars made of crystal. The crystal flight of stairs had posts made of crystal, with banisters and crossbars made of beryl.

In the Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, there were eighty-four thousand gabled halls made of four different materials: one gabled hall was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal. In each golden gabled hall, a silver couch was prepared; in each silver gabled hall, a golden couch was prepared; in each beryl gabled hall, an ivory couch was prepared; in each crystal gabled hall, a hardwood couch was prepared. At the door of each golden gabled hall stood a silver palm tree with a silver trunk and golden leaves and fruits. At the door of each silver gabled hall stood a golden palm tree with a golden trunk and silver leaves and fruits. At the door of each beryl gabled hall stood a crystal palm tree with a crystal trunk and beryl leaves and fruits. At the door of each crystal gabled hall stood a beryl palm tree with a beryl trunk and crystal leaves and fruits.

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Why don’t I have an all-golden palm grove planted at the door of the Great Complex gabled hall, where I can sit for the midday rest?’ And King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, had an all-golden palm grove planted at the door of the Great Complex gabled hall, where he sat for the midday rest.

Furthermore, the Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, was encircled by two balustrades: one made of gold and one made of silver. The golden balustrade had posts made of gold, with banisters and crossbars made of silver. The silver balustrade had posts made of silver, with banisters and crossbars made of gold.

The Palace of the Dhamma, Ānanda, was encircled by two bell networks: one made of gold and one made of silver. The golden network had silver bells, and the silver network had golden bells. And when those bell networks were stirred by the wind, Ānanda, their sound was melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating. Just as, Ānanda, the sound of a five-piece musical ensemble, well tuned, skillfully played, and performed by master musicians, is melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating—even so, Ānanda, when those bell networks were stirred by the wind, their sound was melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating. And whatever rogues, drunkards, and drinkers there were in the royal capital of Kusāvati at that time, Ānanda, they revelled to the sound of those bell networks stirred by the wind. And when the Palace of the Dhamma was completed, Ānanda, it was dazzling to behold, overwhelming to the eyes. Just as, Ānanda, in the last month of the rainy season, during the autumn time, when the sky is clear and free from clouds, the sun—ascending into the sky—is dazzling to behold, overwhelming to the eyes; even so, Ānanda, the Palace of the Dhamma was dazzling to behold, overwhelming to the eyes.

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘Why don’t I have a lotus pond named the Dhamma built in front of the Palace of the Dhamma?’ And King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, had a lotus pond named the Dhamma built in front of the Palace of the Dhamma. The Dhamma lotus pond, Ānanda, was one yojana in length from east to west, and half a yojana in width from north to south. The Dhamma lotus pond, Ānanda, was lined with tiles made of four different materials: one tile was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal.

Moreover, Ānanda, at the Dhamma lotus pond, there were twenty-four flights of stairs made of four different materials: one flight of stairs was made of gold, one of silver, one of beryl, and one of crystal. The golden flight of stairs had posts made of gold, with banisters and crossbars made of silver. The silver flight of stairs had posts made of silver, with banisters and crossbars made of gold. The beryl flight of stairs had posts made of beryl, with banisters and crossbars made of crystal. The crystal flight of stairs had posts made of crystal, with banisters and crossbars made of beryl.

Furthermore, the Dhamma lotus pond, Ānanda, was encircled by two balustrades: one made of gold and one made of silver. The golden balustrade had posts made of gold, with banisters and crossbars made of silver. The silver balustrade had posts made of silver, with banisters and crossbars made of gold.

The Dhamma lotus pond, Ānanda, was encircled by seven rows of palm trees: one row of palm trees was made of gold, one made of silver, one made of beryl, one made of crystal, one made of ruby, one made of emerald, and one made of all gems. The golden palm tree had a golden trunk, with silver leaves and fruits. The silver palm tree had a silver trunk, with golden leaves and fruits. The beryl palm tree had a beryl trunk, with crystal leaves and fruits. The crystal palm tree had a crystal trunk, with beryl leaves and fruits. The ruby palm tree had a ruby trunk, with emerald leaves and fruits. The emerald palm tree had an emerald trunk, with ruby leaves and fruits. The palm tree of all gems had a trunk of all gems, with leaves and fruits of all gems. And when those rows of palm trees were stirred by the wind, Ānanda, their sound was melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating. Just as, Ānanda, the sound of a five-piece musical ensemble, well tuned, skillfully played, and performed by master musicians, is melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating—even so, Ānanda, when those rows of palm trees were stirred by the wind, their sound was melodious, delightful, captivating, and intoxicating. And whatever rogues, drunkards, and drinkers there were in the royal capital of Kusāvati at that time, Ānanda, they revelled to the sound of those rows of palm trees stirred by the wind.

And when the Palace of the Dhamma and the Dhamma lotus pond were completed, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana, having satisfied with all sensual pleasures whoever at that time were recognized as ascetics among ascetics or as brahmins among brahmins, ascended the Palace of the Dhamma.

The first recitation section is completed.

#### 5. The Attainment of the Jhānas

Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘This is the fruit of three deeds, the result of three deeds, by which I now have such great psychic power and such great might, namely: |giving::the act of giving or donating, with an intention to give [dāna]|, |taming::discipline [dama]|, and |self-control::holistic ethical discipline, comprehensive restraint [saṁyama]|.’

Then, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana approached the Great Complex gabled hall. Having approached, standing at the door of the Great Complex gabled hall, he uttered this inspired exclamation: ‘Halt, |thoughts of sensuality::thoughts related to enjoyment of sensual pleasures, sexual thoughts [kāmavitakka]|! #### 6. The Eighty-Four Thousand Cities and Possessions

King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, had eighty-four thousand cities, with the royal capital of Kusāvati the foremost; eighty-four thousand palaces, with the Palace of the Dhamma the foremost; eighty-four thousand gabled halls, with the Great Complex gabled hall the foremost; eighty-four thousand couches made of gold, silver, ivory, and hardwood, spread with fleeces, white woolen blankets, embroidered flower sheets, and spreads of fine antelope hide, with canopies above and red cushions at both ends; eighty-four thousand royal bull elephants with gold adornments and golden banners, draped with nets of gold, with the royal bull elephant named Uposatha the foremost; eighty-four thousand horses with gold adornments and golden banners, draped with nets of gold, with the royal steed named Valāhaka the foremost; eighty-four thousand chariots covered with the hides of lions, tigers, and leopards, and yellow blankets, with gold adornments and golden banners, draped with nets of gold, with the chariot named Vejayanta the foremost; eighty-four thousand jewels, with the jewel treasure the foremost; eighty-four thousand women, with Queen Subhaddā the foremost; eighty-four thousand householders, with the householder treasure the foremost; eighty-four thousand vassal aristocrats, with the counselor treasure the foremost; eighty-four thousand milk cows giving milk into bronze pails; eighty-four thousand crores of fine garments of linen, cotton, silk, and wool; and King Mahāsudassana, Ānanda, had eighty-four thousand meal servings brought to him as offerings morning and evening.

Now at that time, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana’s eighty-four thousand royal elephants came to attend on him morning and evening. Then, Ānanda, this thought occurred to King Mahāsudassana: ‘These eighty-four thousand elephants of mine come to attend on me morning and evening. What if forty-two thousand elephants took turns to attend on me once every hundred years?’ Then, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana addressed the counselor treasure: ‘Friend counselor, these eighty-four thousand elephants come to attend on me morning and evening. Therefore, friend counselor, let forty-two thousand elephants take turns to attend on me once every hundred years.’ ‘Yes, Your Majesty,’ the counselor treasure replied to King Mahāsudassana. And after that, Ānanda, forty-two thousand elephants came to attend on King Mahāsudassana once every hundred years.

#### 7. Queen Subhaddā’s Visit

Then, Ānanda, after the passage of many years, many hundred years, many thousand years, this thought occurred to Queen Subhaddā: ‘It has been a long time since I saw King Mahāsudassana. Why don’t I go to visit King Mahāsudassana?’

Then, Ānanda, Queen Subhaddā addressed the women of the harem: ‘Come, wash your heads and put on yellow garments. It has been a long time since we saw King Mahāsudassana; we will go to visit King Mahāsudassana.’

‘Yes, my lady,’ the women of the harem replied to Queen Subhaddā, washed their heads, put on yellow garments, and approached Queen Subhaddā.

Then, Ānanda, Queen Subhaddā addressed the counselor treasure: ‘Prepare the fourfold army, friend counselor. It has been a long time since we saw King Mahāsudassana; we will go to visit King Mahāsudassana.’

‘Yes, Your Majesty,’ the counselor treasure replied to Queen Subhaddā, and having prepared the fourfold army, he announced to Queen Subhaddā: ‘The fourfold army is prepared, Your Majesty. You may proceed whenever you think it is time.’

Then, Ānanda, Queen Subhaddā, together with the fourfold army and the women of the harem, went to the Palace of the Dhamma. Having arrived and ascended the Palace of the Dhamma, she approached the Great Complex gabled hall and stood leaning against the doorpost.

Then, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana, hearing a sound, thought: ‘What is that sound as of a great crowd of people?’ Coming out from the Great Complex gabled hall, he saw Queen Subhaddā standing leaning against the doorpost. Seeing her, he said to Queen Subhaddā: ‘Stay right there, Queen, do not enter.’

Then, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana addressed a certain man: ‘Come, good man, bring out the golden couch from the Great Complex gabled hall and prepare it in the all-golden palm grove.’

‘Yes, sire,’ that man replied to King Mahāsudassana, and bringing out the golden couch from the Great Complex gabled hall, he prepared it in the all-golden palm grove. #### 8. Rebirth in the Brahmā World

Then, Ānanda, not long after, King Mahāsudassana passed away. Just as a householder or a householder’s son feels drowsiness after eating a delicious meal, even so, Ānanda, was the dying feeling of King Mahāsudassana.

And having passed away, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana was reborn in a good destination, the Brahmā world. For eighty-four thousand years, Ānanda, King Mahāsudassana played childhood games; for eighty-four thousand years he ruled as viceroy; for eighty-four thousand years he ruled as king; for eighty-four thousand years, as a layman, he lived the spiritual life in the Palace of the Dhamma. Having developed the four divine abidings, upon the breaking up of the body, after death, he was reborn in the Brahmā world.

Now, Ānanda, it may occur to you: ‘Perhaps at that time King Mahāsudassana was someone else.’ But it should not be seen in that way, Ānanda. I was King Mahāsudassana at that time.

Mine were those eighty-four thousand cities headed by the royal capital of Kusāvati; mine were those eighty-four thousand palaces headed by the Palace of the Dhamma; mine were those eighty-four thousand gabled halls headed by the Great Complex gabled hall; mine were those eighty-four thousand couches made of gold, silver, ivory, and hardwood, spread with fleeces, white woolen blankets, embroidered flower sheets, and spreads of fine antelope hide, with canopies above and red cushions at both ends; mine were those eighty-four thousand royal elephants with gold adornments and golden banners, draped with nets of gold, headed by the royal elephant Uposatha; mine were those eighty-four thousand horses with gold adornments and golden banners, draped with nets of gold, headed by the royal steed Valāhaka; mine were those eighty-four thousand chariots covered with the hides of lions, tigers, and leopards, and yellow blankets, with gold adornments and golden banners, draped with nets of gold, headed by the Vejayanta chariot; mine were those eighty-four thousand jewels headed by the jewel treasure; mine were those eighty-four thousand women headed by Queen Subhaddā; mine were those eighty-four thousand householders headed by the householder treasure; mine were those eighty-four thousand vassal aristocrats headed by the counselor treasure; mine were those eighty-four thousand milk cows giving milk into bronze pails; mine were those eighty-four thousand crores of fine garments of linen, cotton, silk, and wool; mine were those eighty-four thousand meal servings brought as offerings morning and evening.

Yet of those eighty-four thousand cities, Ānanda, there was only one city that I resided in at that time: the royal capital of Kusāvati. Of those eighty-four thousand palaces, only one palace that I resided in: the Palace of the Dhamma. Of those eighty-four thousand gabled halls, only one gabled hall that I resided in: the Great Complex gabled hall. Of those eighty-four thousand couches, only one couch that I used: made of gold, silver, ivory, or hardwood. Of those eighty-four thousand elephants, only one elephant that I mounted: Uposatha the royal elephant. Of those eighty-four thousand horses, only one horse that I mounted: Valāhaka the royal steed. Of those eighty-four thousand chariots, only one chariot that I mounted: the Vejayanta chariot. Of those eighty-four thousand women, only one woman who attended upon me: an aristocratic lady or a merchant woman. Of those eighty-four thousand crores of fine garments, only one pair of garments that I wore: of fine linen, fine cotton, fine silk, or fine wool. Of those eighty-four thousand meal servings, only one dish from which I ate: at most a measure of rice and suitable curry.

See, Ānanda: all those conditioned phenomena are past, ceased, and changed. So impermanent are conditioned phenomena, Ānanda; so unreliable are conditioned phenomena, Ānanda; so precarious are conditioned phenomena, Ānanda. Therefore, Ānanda, it is enough |to become disenchanted::to become disillusioned [nibbindituṃ]|, |to become detached::to become dispassionate [virajjituṁ]| and |to become free from::to be liberated from [vimuccituṃ]| |all conditions::all formations, all activities, all fabrications [sabbasaṅkhāra]|.

Six times, Ānanda, I recall laying down this body in this place when I was a |wheel-turning monarch::a just and virtuous ruler whose domain extends over the entire known world [rājā cakkavattī]|, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, a conqueror of the four quarters, who had attained stability in his realm, endowed with the seven treasures.
