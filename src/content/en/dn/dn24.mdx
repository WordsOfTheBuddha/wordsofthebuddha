#### 1. The Story of Sunakkhatta

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling among the |Mallas::name of the people of Malla, Mallans; lit. wrestlers [mallā]|, in a market town of the Mallas named |Anupiya::name of a Mallan town [anupiya]|. Then the Blessed One, having dressed early in the morning, took his alms bowl and outer robe and entered Anupiya for alms. Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “It is still too early to walk for alms in Anupiya. Why don’t I go to the park of the wanderer of the Bhaggava clan, where the wanderer of the Bhaggava clan is?”

Then the Blessed One went to the park of the wanderer of the Bhaggava clan, where the wanderer of the Bhaggava clan was. The wanderer of the Bhaggava clan said to the Blessed One: “Let the Blessed One come, venerable sir. Welcome to the Blessed One, venerable sir. It has been a long time since the Blessed One has made the opportunity to come here. Let the Blessed One sit down; this seat is prepared.”

The Blessed One sat down on the prepared seat. The wanderer of the Bhaggava clan, taking another, lower seat, sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the wanderer of the Bhaggava clan said to the Blessed One: “In recent days, venerable sir, and days prior to that, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached me; having approached, he said to me: ‘I have now |renounced::repudiated, rejected [paccakkhāta]| the Blessed One, Bhaggava. I am no longer living dedicated to the Blessed One.’ Is it really so, venerable sir, just as Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, said?”

“It is just so, Bhaggava, as Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, said.

In recent days, Bhaggava, and days prior to that, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached me; having approached, he paid homage to me and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, said to me: ‘Venerable sir, I now renounce the Blessed One. I will no longer live dedicated to the Blessed One.’

When this was said, Bhaggava, I said to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘Sunakkhatta, did I ever say this to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live dedicated to me”?’

‘No, venerable sir.’

‘Or did you ever say to me: “Venerable sir, I will live dedicated to the Blessed One”?’

‘No, venerable sir.’

‘So then, Sunakkhatta, it is not the case that I have ever said to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live dedicated to me.” Nor is it the case that you have ever said to me: “Venerable sir, I will live dedicated to the Blessed One.” Since this is so, |misguided man::vain person [moghapurisa]|, who are you and whom are you rejecting? See, misguided man, how far you have gone wrong!’

‘But, venerable sir, the Blessed One does not perform any superhuman |miracle of psychic power::display of spiritual power [iddhipāṭihāriya]| for me.’

‘Sunakkhatta, did I ever say this to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live dedicated to me, and I will perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power for you”?’

‘No, venerable sir.’

‘Or did you ever say to me: “Venerable sir, I will live dedicated to the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power for me”?’

‘No, venerable sir.’

‘So then, Sunakkhatta, it is not the case that I have ever said to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live dedicated to me, and I will perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power for you.” Nor is it the case that you have ever said to me: “Venerable sir, I will live dedicated to the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power for me.” Since this is so, misguided man, who are you and whom are you rejecting? What do you think, Sunakkhatta: whether a superhuman miracle of psychic power is performed or whether a superhuman miracle of psychic power is not performed, does the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| taught by me for that purpose lead the one acting accordingly to the complete |wearing away of suffering::extinction of stress, termination of tension [dukkhakkhaya]|?’

‘Whether a superhuman miracle of psychic power is performed, venerable sir, or whether a superhuman miracle of psychic power is not performed, the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One for that purpose leads the one acting accordingly to the complete wearing away of suffering.’

‘So then, Sunakkhatta, whether a superhuman miracle of psychic power is performed or whether a superhuman miracle of psychic power is not performed, the Dhamma taught by me for that purpose leads the one acting accordingly to the complete wearing away of suffering. In that case, Sunakkhatta, what purpose would the performance of a superhuman miracle of psychic power serve? See, misguided man, how far you have gone wrong!’

‘But, venerable sir, the Blessed One does not declare the |origin of the world::primeval origin, beginning of things [aggañña]| to me.’

‘Sunakkhatta, did I ever say this to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live dedicated to me, and I will declare the origin of the world to you”?’

‘No, venerable sir.’

‘Or did you ever say to me: “Venerable sir, I will live dedicated to the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will declare the origin of the world to me”?’

‘No, venerable sir.’

‘So then, Sunakkhatta, it is not the case that I have ever said to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live dedicated to me, and I will declare the origin of the world to you.” Nor is it the case that you have ever said to me: “Venerable sir, I will live dedicated to the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will declare the origin of the world to me.” Since this is so, misguided man, who are you and whom are you rejecting? What do you think, Sunakkhatta: whether the origin of the world is declared or whether the origin of the world is not declared, does the Dhamma taught by me for that purpose lead the one acting accordingly to the complete wearing away of suffering?’

‘Whether the origin of the world is declared, venerable sir, or whether the origin of the world is not declared, the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One for that purpose leads the one acting accordingly to the complete wearing away of suffering.’

‘So then, Sunakkhatta, whether the origin of the world is declared or whether the origin of the world is not declared, the Dhamma taught by me for that purpose leads the one acting accordingly to the complete wearing away of suffering. In that case, Sunakkhatta, what purpose would declaring the origin of the world serve? See, misguided man, how far you have gone wrong!

In many ways, Sunakkhatta, you have spoken praise of me in the Vajjian village: “The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.” Thus, Sunakkhatta, in many ways you have spoken praise of me in the Vajjian village.

In many ways, Sunakkhatta, you have spoken praise of the Dhamma in the Vajjian village: “The Dhamma is well proclaimed by the Blessed One, directly visible, immediate, inviting verification, |onward leading::applicable, relevant, practical [opaneyyika]|, to be personally experienced by the wise.” Thus, Sunakkhatta, in many ways you have spoken praise of the Dhamma in the Vajjian village.

In many ways, Sunakkhatta, you have spoken praise of the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| in the Vajjian village: “The community of the Blessed One’s disciples is practicing the good way, practicing the upright way, practicing the true way, practicing the proper way; that is, the four pairs of persons, the eight types of individuals—this community of the Blessed One’s disciples is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.” Thus, Sunakkhatta, in many ways you have spoken praise of the Saṅgha in the Vajjian village.

I tell you, Sunakkhatta, I declare to you, Sunakkhatta: there will be those who say of you, Sunakkhatta: “Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, was unable to lead the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| under the ascetic Gotama; being unable to do so, he renounced the training and reverted back to the lay life.” Thus, Sunakkhatta, there will be those who say this of you.’

Even when spoken to by me in this way, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, still departed from this Dhamma and |Vinaya::code of monastic discipline rules, training [vinaya]|, just as one bound for a state of misery and hell.

#### 2. The Story of Korakkhattiya

On one occasion, Bhaggava, I was dwelling among the Thūlus, in a market town of the Thūlus named Uttaraka. Then, Bhaggava, early in the morning, having dressed and taken my alms bowl and outer robe, I entered Uttaraka for alms with Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, as my |attendant monk::monk who walks behind as attendant [pacchāsamaṇa]|. Now at that time, the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, who |practiced dog-duty asceticism::performing the ascetic practice of behaving like a dog; lit. with dog practice [kukkuravatika]|, was moving on all fours, eating and consuming food strewn on the ground with his mouth alone.

Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, Bhaggava, saw the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, who practiced dog-duty asceticism, moving on all fours, eating and consuming food strewn on the ground with his mouth alone. Having seen him, it occurred to him: ‘How admirable is this ascetic who moves on all fours, eating and consuming food strewn on the ground with his mouth alone!’

Then, Bhaggava, knowing with my mind the chain of thought in the mind of Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, I said to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘Do even you, misguided man, claim to be an ascetic who is a son of the Sakyans?’

‘Why, venerable sir, does the Blessed One say this to me: “Do even you, misguided man, claim to be an ascetic who is a son of the Sakyans?”’

‘Sunakkhatta, when you saw this naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, who practices dog-duty asceticism, moving on all fours, eating and consuming food strewn on the ground with his mouth alone, did this thought not occur to you: “How admirable is this ascetic who moves on all fours, eating and consuming food strewn on the ground with his mouth alone!”?’

‘Yes, venerable sir. Why, venerable sir, is the Blessed One |envious of::jealous of [maccharāyati]| Arahantship?’

‘I am not envious of Arahantship, misguided man. Rather, this harmful |view::speculative opinion, belief [diṭṭhigata]| has arisen in you yourself; abandon it, lest it be for your harm and suffering for a long time.

As for this naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, Sunakkhatta, of whom you think: “How admirable is this ascetic”—on the seventh day from now he will die of |acute indigestion::severe abdominal stasis or food poisoning [alasaka]|. And having died, he will be reborn among the |Kālakañjas::a low class of asuras (titan-spirits) [kālakañcikā]|, the lowest company of |asuras::titan-like spirits in conflict with the devas [asura]|. When he is dead, they will cast him out in the charnel ground among the clumps of |vetiver grass::fragrant coarse grass (Andropogon muricatus) [bīraṇa]|. If you wish, Sunakkhatta, you may approach the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya and ask: “Friend Korakkhattiya, do you know your own destination?” And it is possible, Sunakkhatta, that the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya will declare to you: “Friend Sunakkhatta, I know my own destination. I have been reborn among the Kālakañjas, the lowest company of asuras.”’

Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya; having approached, he said to the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya: ‘Friend Korakkhattiya, the ascetic Gotama has declared of you: “On the seventh day from now, the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya will die of acute indigestion. And having died, he will be reborn among the Kālakañjas, the lowest company of asuras. When he is dead, they will cast him out in the charnel ground among the clumps of vetiver grass.” Therefore, friend Korakkhattiya, you should eat food in strict moderation and drink water in strict moderation, so that the ascetic Gotama’s word may prove false.’

Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, counted off the seven days and nights one by one, not trusting the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|. And on the seventh day, Bhaggava, the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya died of acute indigestion. Having died, he was reborn among the Kālakañjas, the lowest company of asuras. And when he was dead, they cast him out in the charnel ground among the clumps of vetiver grass.

Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, Bhaggava, heard: ‘The naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, it seems, has died of acute indigestion and been cast out in the charnel ground among the clumps of vetiver grass.’ Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, went to the charnel ground among the clumps of vetiver grass, where the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya lay; having approached, he struck the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya three times with his hand: ‘Friend Korakkhattiya, do you know your own destination?’

Then, Bhaggava, rubbing his back with his hand, the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya rose up and said: ‘Friend Sunakkhatta, I know my own destination. I have been reborn among the Kālakañjas, the lowest company of asuras.’ Having spoken thus, he fell back flat on his back right there.

Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached me; having approached, he paid homage to me and sat down to one side. While he was seated to one side, Bhaggava, I said this to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘What do you think, Sunakkhatta: did the result turn out just as I declared to you concerning the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, or otherwise?’

‘The result turned out just as the Blessed One declared to me concerning the naked ascetic Korakkhattiya, venerable sir, not otherwise.’

‘What do you think, Sunakkhatta: this being the case, has a superhuman miracle of psychic power been performed, or has it not been performed?’

‘Surely, venerable sir, this being the case, a superhuman miracle of psychic power has been performed, not unperformed.’

‘Even when I perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power in this way, misguided man, you say of me: “But, venerable sir, the Blessed One does not perform any superhuman miracle of psychic power for me.” See, misguided man, how far you have gone wrong!’ Even when spoken to by me in this way, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, still departed from this Dhamma and Vinaya, just as one bound for a state of misery and hell.

#### 3. The Story of the Naked Ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka

On one occasion, Bhaggava, I was dwelling at |Vesāli::capital of the Licchavīs [vesālī]|, in the |Great Forest::name of a forest outside Vesāli; lit. great forest [mahāvana]|, in the hall with the peaked roof. Now at that time, the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka was residing at Vesāli, having reached the peak of gain and the peak of fame in the Vajjian village. He had undertaken and maintained seven vows: ‘For life may I be a naked ascetic and never put on garments; for life may I be celibate and never engage in sexual intercourse; for life may I subsist solely on liquor and meat, and never eat boiled rice or sour gruel; I will never go beyond the Udena Shrine to the east of Vesāli; I will never go beyond the Gotamaka Shrine to the south of Vesāli; I will never go beyond the Sattamba Shrine to the west of Vesāli; I will never go beyond the Bahuputta Shrine to the north of Vesāli.’ Because of undertaking these seven vows, he had reached the peak of gain and the peak of fame in the Vajjian village.

Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka; having approached, he asked the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka a question. When asked the question by him, the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka could not answer. Being unable to answer, he displayed anger, aversion, and bitterness. Then, Bhaggava, it occurred to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘We have offended an admirable arahant ascetic! May this not be for our harm and suffering for a long time!’

Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached me; having approached, he paid homage to me and sat down to one side. While he was seated to one side, Bhaggava, I said this to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘Do even you, misguided man, claim to be an ascetic who is a son of the Sakyans?’

‘Why, venerable sir, does the Blessed One say this to me: “Do even you, misguided man, claim to be an ascetic who is a son of the Sakyans?”’

‘Did you not, Sunakkhatta, approach the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka and ask him a question? When asked the question by you, the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka could not answer; being unable to answer, he displayed anger, aversion, and bitterness. Then this thought occurred to you: “We have offended an admirable arahant ascetic! May this not be for our harm and suffering for a long time!”’

‘Yes, venerable sir. Why, venerable sir, is the Blessed One envious of Arahantship?’

‘I am not envious of Arahantship, misguided man. Rather, this harmful view has arisen in you yourself; abandon it, lest it be for your harm and suffering for a long time.

As for this naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka, Sunakkhatta, of whom you think: “How admirable is this ascetic”—before long he will put on garments, wander about with a companion, eat boiled rice and sour gruel, cross beyond all the shrines of Vesāli, fall from his fame, and die.’

Then, Bhaggava, before long the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka put on garments, wandered about with a companion, ate boiled rice and sour gruel, crossed beyond all the shrines of Vesāli, fell from his fame, and died.

Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, Bhaggava, heard: ‘The naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka, it seems, put on garments, wandered about with a companion, ate boiled rice and sour gruel, crossed beyond all the shrines of Vesāli, fell from his fame, and died.’ Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached me; having approached, he paid homage to me and sat down to one side. While he was seated to one side, Bhaggava, I said this to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘What do you think, Sunakkhatta: did the result turn out just as I declared to you concerning the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka, or otherwise?’

‘The result turned out just as the Blessed One declared to me concerning the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka, venerable sir, not otherwise.’

‘What do you think, Sunakkhatta: this being the case, has a superhuman miracle of psychic power been performed, or has it not been performed?’

‘Surely, venerable sir, this being the case, a superhuman miracle of psychic power has been performed, not unperformed.’

‘Even when I perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power in this way, misguided man, you say of me: “But, venerable sir, the Blessed One does not perform any superhuman miracle of psychic power for me.” See, misguided man, how far you have gone wrong!’ Even when spoken to by me in this way, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, still departed from this Dhamma and Vinaya, just as one bound for a state of misery and hell.

#### 4. The Story of the Naked Ascetic Pāthikaputta

On one occasion, Bhaggava, I was dwelling right there at Vesāli, in the Great Forest, in the hall with the peaked roof. Now at that time, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta was residing at Vesāli, having reached the peak of gain and the peak of fame in the Vajjian village. He spoke thus before the Vesāli assembly: ‘The ascetic Gotama claims knowledge, and I too claim knowledge. One who claims knowledge ought to display a superhuman miracle of psychic power with one who claims knowledge. If the ascetic Gotama comes halfway, I too will go halfway. There we may both perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power. If the ascetic Gotama performs one superhuman miracle of psychic power, I will perform two. If the ascetic Gotama performs two superhuman miracles of psychic power, I will perform four. If the ascetic Gotama performs four superhuman miracles of psychic power, I will perform eight. Thus, whatever number of superhuman miracles of psychic power the ascetic Gotama performs, I will perform double that number!’

Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, approached me; having approached, he paid homage to me and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, said to me: ‘Venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is residing at Vesāli, having reached the peak of gain and the peak of fame in the Vajjian village. He speaks thus before the Vesāli assembly: “The ascetic Gotama claims knowledge, and I too claim knowledge. One who claims knowledge ought to display a superhuman miracle of psychic power with one who claims knowledge. If the ascetic Gotama comes halfway, I too will go halfway. There we may both perform a superhuman miracle of psychic power. If the ascetic Gotama performs one superhuman miracle of psychic power, I will perform two. If the ascetic Gotama performs two superhuman miracles of psychic power, I will perform four. If the ascetic Gotama performs four superhuman miracles of psychic power, I will perform eight. Thus, whatever number of superhuman miracles of psychic power the ascetic Gotama performs, I will perform double that number!”’

When this was said, Bhaggava, I said to Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis: ‘Sunakkhatta, unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into my presence. Even if he should think: “Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,” his head would split apart.’

‘Let the Blessed One watch those words, venerable sir! Let the Accomplished One watch those words!’

‘Why do you say this to me, Sunakkhatta: “Let the Blessed One watch those words, venerable sir! Let the Accomplished One watch those words!”?’

‘Because, venerable sir, the Blessed One has made this categorical statement: “Unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into my presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart.” Yet if the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta were to come into the Blessed One’s presence in |disguise::an altered or assumed form [virūparūpa]|, venerable sir, then the Blessed One’s word would prove false.’

‘Sunakkhatta, would the Tathāgata ever utter speech that is |ambiguous::open to two interpretations; lit. going two ways [dvayagāminī]|?’

‘Venerable sir, is it by having encompassed his mind with your own mind that the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is known to the Blessed One thus: “Unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into my presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart”? Or did deities report this matter to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into the Blessed One’s presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart”?’

‘Sunakkhatta, both by having encompassed his mind with my own mind is the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta known to me thus: “Unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into my presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart”; and deities also reported this matter to me: “Venerable sir, unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into the Blessed One’s presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart.” Furthermore, Ajita, the general of the Licchavis, who recently passed away and was reborn in the company of the |Thirty-Three::devas of Tāvatiṁsa heaven ruled by Sakka [tāvatiṁsakāya]|, also approached me and reported thus: “Venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is shameless; venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is a liar! Of me too, venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta declared in the Vajjian village: ‘Ajita, the general of the Licchavis, has been reborn in the Great Hell.’

Yet I, venerable sir, was not reborn in the Great Hell; I was reborn in the company of the Thirty-Three. Venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is shameless; venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is a liar! And unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, venerable sir, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into the Blessed One’s presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart.”

Thus, Sunakkhatta, both by having encompassed his mind with my own mind is the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta known to me: “Unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into my presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart”; and deities also reported this matter to me: “Venerable sir, unless he abandons that speech, unless he abandons that [state of] mind, and unless he relinquishes that view, the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta is incapable of coming into the Blessed One’s presence. Even if he should think: ‘Without abandoning that speech, without abandoning that [state of] mind, and without relinquishing that view, I will go into the presence of the ascetic Gotama,’ his head would split apart.”

Now, Sunakkhatta, after walking for alms in Vesāli and returning from my alms round after my meal, I shall go to the park of the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta |for the day’s abiding::meditation place for the day [divāvihāra]|. Tell whomever you wish, Sunakkhatta.’

#### 5. The Discussion on the Miracle of Psychic Power

Then, Bhaggava, early in the morning, having dressed and taken my alms bowl and outer robe, I entered Vesāli for alms. After going on alms round in Vesāli and returning from my alms round after my meal, I went to the park of the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta for the day’s abiding. Then, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta, the son of the Licchavis, hurriedly entered Vesāli and approached the most prominent Licchavis; having approached, he said to the most prominent Licchavis: ‘Friends, after walking for alms in Vesāli and returning from his alms round after his meal, the Blessed One has gone to the park of the naked ascetic Pāthikaputta for the day’s abiding. Come forth, venerable ones! Come forth, venerable ones! Having come to this state of existence, they go forth from the home life into homelessness. Having gone forth from the home life into homelessness, through ardor, striving, devotion, diligence, and right attention, they attain such a collectedness of mind that, when their mind is thus collected, they recollect that former abode, but no further than that.

They say: “Those worthy gods who are not corrupted in mind do not spend an excessive time gazing at one another. Not spending an excessive time gazing at one another, they do not corrupt their minds toward one another. With minds uncorrupted toward one another, they do not grow weary in body or weary in mind. Those gods do not pass away from that host; they are permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change, and will remain just the same for all eternity. But we who were corrupted in mind spent an excessive time gazing at one another. Spending an excessive time gazing at one another, we corrupted our minds toward one another. With minds corrupted toward one another, we grew weary in body and weary in mind. Thus we passed away from that host, and being impermanent, not lasting, short-lived, and liable to pass away, we have come to this state of existence.” Is it not in this way, venerable sirs, that you declare it as your traditional doctrine that the origin of the world is due to corruption of mind?’

They say: ‘It is just so, friend Gotama, that we have heard it, just as the venerable Gotama says.’

I know the origin of the world, Bhaggava ... directly knowing which, the Tathāgata does not fall into misfortune.

There are, Bhaggava, some ascetics and brahmins who declare it as their traditional doctrine that the origin of the world is |fortuitously originated::arisen by chance, without cause [adhiccasamuppanna]|. Having approached them, I say: ‘Is it true, venerable sirs, that you declare it as your traditional doctrine that the origin of the world is fortuitously originated?’ When asked by me in this way, they confirm: ‘Yes.’ Then I say to them: ‘But in what way, venerable sirs, do you declare it as your traditional doctrine that the origin of the world is fortuitously originated?’ Being asked by me, they cannot answer; unable to answer, they question me in return. Being asked, I explain to them:

‘There are, friends, gods named “|non-percipient beings::deities existing in a plane of unconscious materiality [asaññasattā]|.” When perception arises, those gods pass away from that host.

It is possible, friends, that a certain being passes away from that host and comes to this state of existence. Having come to this state of existence, they go forth from the home life into homelessness. Having gone forth from the home life into homelessness, through ardor, striving, devotion, diligence, and right attention, they attain such a collectedness of mind that, when their mind is thus collected, they recollect that arising of perception, but no further than that.

They say: “The self and the world arose fortuitously. Why is that? Because formerly I did not exist, whereas now, having not existed, I have transformed into the state of existing.” Is it not in this way, venerable sirs, that you declare it as your traditional doctrine that the origin of the world is fortuitously originated?’

They say: ‘It is just so, friend Gotama, that we have heard it, just as the venerable Gotama says.’

I know the origin of the world, Bhaggava. I know that, and I know what goes beyond that; yet knowing that, I do not obstinately grasp it, and for me, not grasping it, quenching is personally known—directly knowing which, the Tathāgata does not fall into misfortune.

When I speak thus, Bhaggava, and declare thus, some ascetics and brahmins falsely, baselessly, untruthfully, and wrongly accuse me, saying: ‘The ascetic Gotama and his bhikkhus are |misguided::perverted, inverted, mistaken [viparīta]|.

The ascetic Gotama says: “When one enters and dwells in the |liberation of the beautiful::meditative liberation based on pure, radiant kasina perception [subha + vimokkha]|, at that time one perceives everything as unattractive.”’

Yet I, Bhaggava, do not say: ‘When one enters and dwells in the liberation of the beautiful, at that time one perceives everything as unattractive.’ Rather, Bhaggava, I say this: ‘When one enters and dwells in the liberation of the beautiful, at that time one perceives it simply as beautiful.’”

“Venerable sir, it is those who regard the Blessed One and the bhikkhus as misguided who are themselves misguided! I have such confidence in the Blessed One, venerable sir: the Blessed One is capable of teaching me the Dhamma in such a way that I may enter and dwell in the liberation of the beautiful.”

“It is hard for you, Bhaggava—holding other views, having other inclinations, having other preferences, following another pursuit, under another teacher—to enter and dwell in the liberation of the beautiful. Come now, Bhaggava, carefully guard this very confidence that you have in me.”

“If, venerable sir, it is hard for me—holding other views, having other inclinations, having other preferences, following another pursuit, under another teacher—to enter and dwell in the liberation of the beautiful, then I shall carefully guard this very confidence that I have in the Blessed One.”

The Blessed One said this. The wanderer of the Bhaggava clan was delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words.
