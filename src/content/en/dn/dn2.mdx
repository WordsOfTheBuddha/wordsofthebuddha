### 1. The Discussion of the Royal Ministers

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]|, in the mango grove of |Jīvaka Komārabhacca::the famous royal physician and personal doctor to the Buddha [jīvaka komārabhacca]|, together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus.

On that occasion—the |Uposatha::observance day, the Buddhist sabbath on the full moon, new moon, and quarter moon days [uposatha]| day of the fifteenth, on the full moon night of the |Komudī::the full moon of the fourth lunar month (Kattika, Oct-Nov) when white water-lilies bloom [komudī]| full moon of the fourth month— |King Ajātasattu::name of the king of Magadha, son of Bimbisāra; lit. unborn enemy [ajātasattu]| of |Magadha::An ancient kingdom in northeastern India and the setting for much of the Buddha’s teachings after his full awakening [magadha]|, |son of Vedehi::epithet of King Ajātasattu; lit. son of Vedehi [vedehiputta]|, was seated on the upper terrace of his royal palace surrounded by his royal ministers.

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, uttered this inspired utterance on that Uposatha day: “Delightful indeed, good sirs, is this moonlit night! Beautiful indeed, good sirs, is this moonlit night! Charming indeed, good sirs, is this moonlit night! Inspiring indeed, good sirs, is this moonlit night! Auspicious indeed, good sirs, is this moonlit night! Which |ascetic::a person who labours, toils, or exerts themselves for some higher or religious purpose; renunciant; holy man; Skt. śramaṇa [samaṇa]| or |brahmin::a member of the priestly caste; holy person [brāhmaṇa]| might we attend upon today, attending upon whom our mind might be inspired with confidence?”

When this was said, a certain royal minister said to King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi: “Your Majesty, this Pūraṇa Kassapa is the |leader of an order::head of a congregation [saṅghin]|, the leader of a group, the teacher of a group, well-known, famous, the |founder of a sect::founder of a religious order; lit. ford maker [titthakara]|, regarded as holy by many people, of long standing, long gone forth, advanced in years, and arrived at the final stage of life. Let Your Majesty attend upon Pūraṇa Kassapa. Perhaps when attending upon Pūraṇa Kassapa, Your Majesty’s mind might be inspired with confidence.” When this was said, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, remained silent.

Another royal minister said to King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi: “Your Majesty, this Makkhali Gosāla is the leader of an order, the leader of a group, the teacher of a group, well-known, famous, the founder of a sect, regarded as holy by many people, of long standing, long gone forth, advanced in years, and arrived at the final stage of life. Let Your Majesty attend upon Makkhali Gosāla. Perhaps when attending upon Makkhali Gosāla, Your Majesty’s mind might be inspired with confidence.” When this was said, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, remained silent.

Perhaps when attending upon Ajita Kesakambala, Your Majesty’s mind might be inspired with confidence.” When this was said, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, remained silent.

Another royal minister said to King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi: “Your Majesty, this Pakudha Kaccāyana is the leader of an order, the leader of a group, the teacher of a group, well-known, famous, the founder of a sect, regarded as holy by many people, of long standing, long gone forth, advanced in years, and arrived at the final stage of life. ### 2. The Discussion of Jīvaka Komārabhacca

Now at that time, Jīvaka Komārabhacca was sitting silently not far from King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi. Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, said to Jīvaka Komārabhacca: “And you, my good Jīvaka, why are you silent?”

“Your Majesty, this Blessed One, an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, is dwelling in our mango grove together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus. And concerning that Blessed One, a good report has spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ Let Your Majesty attend upon the Blessed One. Perhaps when attending upon the Blessed One, Your Majesty’s mind might be inspired with confidence.”

“Then, my good Jīvaka, have elephant transports readied.”

“Yes, Your Majesty,” Jīvaka Komārabhacca replied to King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi. Having had about five hundred she-elephants readied along with the king’s state bull elephant, he announced to King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi: “Your Majesty, your elephant transports are ready; you may proceed whenever you think it is time.”

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, having mounted individual women attendants upon the five hundred she-elephants and having mounted his state bull elephant, departed from Rājagaha with full royal majesty by torchlight, and set out for the mango grove of Jīvaka Komārabhacca.

Then, not far from the mango grove, fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror arose in King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi. Frightened, agitated, and with his hair standing on end, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, said to Jīvaka Komārabhacca: “Are you not deceiving me, my good Jīvaka? Are you not playing a trick on me, my good Jīvaka? Are you not betraying me to my enemies, my good Jīvaka? For how could there be neither a sneeze, nor a cough, nor a sound from so large a Saṅgha of bhikkhus, twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus?”

“Do not fear, great king! Do not fear, great king! I am not deceiving you, Your Majesty; I am not playing a trick on you, Your Majesty; I am not betraying you to your enemies, Your Majesty. Advance, great king! Advance, great king! There are lamps burning in the circular pavilion.”

### 3. The Question on the Fruits of the Spiritual Life

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, rode on his elephant as far as the ground allowed, then dismounted from the elephant and approached the door of the circular pavilion on foot. Having approached, he said to Jīvaka Komārabhacca: “Where, my good Jīvaka, is the Blessed One?”

“That is the Blessed One, great king; that is the Blessed One, great king, seated against the central column, facing east, in front of the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.”

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, approached the Blessed One; having approached, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, surveying the silent Saṅgha of bhikkhus seated in absolute stillness, like a serene lake, uttered this inspired utterance: “May my son, Prince Udayabhadda, be endowed with such peace as this Saṅgha of bhikkhus is now endowed with!”

“Do your thoughts, great king, follow the call of your affection?”

“Beloved to me, venerable sir, is Prince Udayabhadda. May Prince Udayabhadda be endowed with such peace as this Saṅgha of bhikkhus is now endowed with!”

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, having paid homage to the Blessed One and having raised his hands in reverent salutation to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus, sat down to one side. Seated to one side, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I would ask the Blessed One about a certain matter, if the Blessed One would grant me the opportunity to receive an answer to my question.”

“Ask, great king, whatever you wish.”

“Just as, venerable sir, there are these various common crafts, namely: elephant riders, cavalrymen, charioteers, archers, standard-bearers, camp marshals, supply officers, fierce warriors, royal princes, skirmishers, great champions, brave soldiers, leather-armored warriors, sons of slave women, cooks, barbers, bath attendants, confectioners, garland-makers, dyers, weavers, basket-makers, potters, accountants, finger-mathematicians, and whatever other such common crafts there may be; in this very life they enjoy the visible fruit of their craft; with that they give happiness and pleasure to themselves, give happiness and pleasure to their parents, give happiness and pleasure to their wife and children, give happiness and pleasure to their friends and companions, and establish an uplifting spiritual gift for ascetics and brahmins leading to heaven, yielding happy results, conducive to celestial realms. Is it possible, venerable sir, in the very same way to point out a |fruit of the spiritual life::visible benefit or spiritual realization of recluseship [sāmaññaphala]| that is |apparent in the present life::visible here and now, directly experienced [sandiṭṭhika]|?”

“Do you recall, great king, having asked this question of other ascetics and brahmins?”

“I recall, venerable sir, having asked this question of other ascetics and brahmins.”

“How did they explain it to you, great king? If it is not troublesome for you, please speak.”

“It is not troublesome for me, venerable sir, wherever the Blessed One or someone like the Blessed One is seated.”

“Then speak, great king.”

#### 3.1. The Doctrine of Pūraṇa Kassapa

“At one time, venerable sir, I approached Pūraṇa Kassapa; having approached, I exchanged friendly greetings with Pūraṇa Kassapa. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, I sat down to one side. Seated to one side, venerable sir, I said to Pūraṇa Kassapa: ‘Just as, sir Kassapa, there are these various common crafts, namely: elephant riders, cavalrymen, charioteers, archers, standard-bearers, camp marshals, supply officers, fierce warriors, royal princes, skirmishers, great champions, brave soldiers, leather-armored warriors, sons of slave women, cooks, barbers, bath attendants, confectioners, garland-makers, dyers, weavers, basket-makers, potters, accountants, finger-mathematicians, and whatever other such common crafts there may be; in this very life they enjoy the visible fruit of their craft; with that they give happiness and pleasure to themselves, give happiness and pleasure to their parents, give happiness and pleasure to their wife and children, give happiness and pleasure to their friends and companions, and establish an uplifting spiritual gift for ascetics and brahmins leading to heaven, yielding happy results, conducive to celestial realms. Is it possible, sir Kassapa, in the very same way to point out a fruit of the spiritual life that is apparent in the present life?’

When this was said, venerable sir, Pūraṇa Kassapa said to me: ‘Great king, by one who acts or causes another to act, who cuts or causes another to cut, who burns or causes another to burn, who grieves or causes another to grieve, who tortures or causes another to torture, who inflicts distress or causes another to inflict distress, who kills living beings, who takes what is not given, who breaks into houses, who plunders, who robs isolated houses, who waits in ambush on highways, who commits adultery with another’s wife, who speaks falsehood—by one who acts thus, no evil is done. Even if with a razor-edged disc one were to reduce all the living beings on this earth to a single mass of flesh, a single heap of flesh, there would be no evil from that source, no outcome of evil. Even if one were to go along the south bank of the Ganges killing and slaughtering, cutting and causing to cut, burning and causing to burn, there would be no evil from that source, no outcome of evil. Even if one were to go along the north bank of the Ganges giving and causing to give, sacrificing and causing to sacrifice, there would be no merit from that source, no outcome of merit. Through generosity, self-control, restraint, and truthful speech there is no merit, no outcome of merit.’

Thus, venerable sir, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, Pūraṇa Kassapa explained |non-action::the doctrine that actions bear no karmic fruit [akiriya]|. Just as if, venerable sir, when asked about a mango, one were to answer with a breadfruit, or when asked about a breadfruit, one were to answer with a mango—even so, venerable sir, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, Pūraṇa Kassapa explained non-action. Then, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘How could one like me think of disparaging an ascetic or brahmin living in my realm?’ So, venerable sir, I neither approved nor rejected the statement of Pūraṇa Kassapa. Without approving or rejecting it, though dissatisfied, without uttering a word of dissatisfaction, without accepting or discarding that statement, I rose from my seat and departed.

#### 3.2. The Doctrine of Makkhali Gosāla

At one time, venerable sir, I approached Makkhali Gosāla; having approached, I exchanged friendly greetings with Makkhali Gosāla. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, I sat down to one side. Seated to one side, venerable sir, I said to Makkhali Gosāla: ‘Just as, sir Gosāla, there are these various common crafts ... Is it possible, sir Gosāla, in the very same way to point out a fruit of the spiritual life that is apparent in the present life?’

When this was said, venerable sir, Makkhali Gosāla said to me: ‘There is, great king, no cause and no condition for the defilement of beings; without cause and without condition beings are defiled. There is no cause and no condition for the purification of beings; without cause and without condition beings are purified. There is no self-agency, no other-agency, no human agency; there is no power, no energy, no human strength, no human exertion. All beings, all living creatures, all organisms, all life-forces are without control, without power, without energy; transformed by |destiny::fate, fixed cosmic order [niyati]|, circumstance, and nature, they experience pleasure and pain in the six classes of rebirth. There are fourteen hundred thousand principal modes of origin [for living beings] and six thousand [others] and six hundred [others]. There are five hundred kinds of kamma and five kinds of kamma and three kinds of kamma and full kamma and half-kamma. There are sixty-two pathways, sixty-two sub-aeons, six classes of men, eight stages in the life of man, forty-nine hundred modes of livelihood, forty-nine hundred kinds of wanderers, forty-nine hundred abodes of Nāgas, two thousand faculties, three thousand hells, thirty-six realms of dust, seven spheres of percipient beings, seven spheres of non-percipient beings, seven kinds of jointed plants, seven kinds of gods, seven kinds of human beings, seven kinds of demons, seven great lakes, seven major kinds of knots, seven hundred minor kinds of knots, seven major precipices, seven hundred minor precipices, seven major kinds of dreams, seven hundred minor kinds of dreams, eighty-four hundred thousand great aeons. The foolish and the wise, having roamed and wandered through these, will alike make an end to suffering. Therein, there is no such thing as: “By this ethical conduct, or by this observance, or by this austerity, or by this spiritual life, I will bring unripened kamma to maturity, or by repeatedly experiencing ripened kamma, I will eliminate it.” That cannot be. For pleasure and pain are measured out, as if with a bushel, and saṁsāra is bounded; there is no shortening or lengthening, no increasing or decreasing. Just as a ball of string, when thrown, unwinds until it runs out; even so fools and the wise alike, having transmigrated and wandered on, will make an end of suffering.’

Thus, venerable sir, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, Makkhali Gosāla explained |purification through wandering in saṁsāra::doctrine of salvation purely through the deterministic cycle of transmigration [saṁsārasuddhi]|. Just as if, venerable sir, when asked about a mango, one were to answer with a breadfruit, or when asked about a breadfruit, one were to answer with a mango—even so, venerable sir, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, Makkhali Gosāla explained purification through wandering in saṁsāra. Then, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘How could one like me think of disparaging an ascetic or brahmin living in my realm?’ So, venerable sir, I neither approved nor rejected the statement of Makkhali Gosāla. Without approving or rejecting it, though dissatisfied, without uttering a word of dissatisfaction, without accepting or discarding that statement, I rose from my seat and departed.

#### 3.3. The Doctrine of Ajita Kesakambala

At one time, venerable sir, I approached Ajita Kesakambala; having approached, I exchanged friendly greetings with Ajita Kesakambala. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, I sat down to one side. Seated to one side, venerable sir, I said to Ajita Kesakambala: ‘Just as, sir Ajita, there are these various common crafts ... Is it possible, sir Ajita, in the very same way to point out a fruit of the spiritual life that is apparent in the present life?’

When this was said, venerable sir, Ajita Kesakambala said to me: ‘There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; no fruit or result of good and bad actions; no this world, no other world; no mother, no father; no beings who are reborn spontaneously; no ascetics and brahmins in the world who are rightly practicing or have rightly practiced, and who, having realized for themselves by direct knowledge, declare this world and the other world. This person is composed of the four great elements. When one dies, earth returns to and merges with the earth-body; water returns to and merges with the water-body; fire returns to and merges with the fire-body; wind returns to and merges with the wind-body. The faculties pass into space. Four men, with the bier as the fifth, carry the corpse. The funeral orations last as far as the cremation ground; the bones turn dove grey; the fire offering ends in ashes. Giving is prescribed by fools. Fools and the wise are alike cut off and perish with the breakup of the body; after death they do not exist.’

#### 3.4. The Doctrine of Pakudha Kaccāyana

When this was said, venerable sir, Pakudha Kaccāyana said to me: ‘These seven bodies, great king, are |unmade::uncreated, uncrafted [akaṭa]|, uncaused, unconstructed, unproduced, barren, stable as a mountain peak, established like a pillar. They do not move, do not change, do not obstruct one another; nor are they capable of causing one another pleasure, or pain, or both pleasure and pain. What are the seven? The earth body, the water body, the fire body, the air body, pleasure, pain, and the |life-faculty::life-principle, soul [jīva]| as the seventh—these seven bodies are unmade, uncaused, unconstructed, unproduced, barren, stable as a mountain peak, established like a pillar. They do not move, do not change, do not obstruct one another; nor are they capable of causing one another pleasure, or pain, or both pleasure and pain. Therein, there is no killer or slaughterer, no hearer or speaker, no knower or explainer. #### 3.5. The Doctrine of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta

When this was said, venerable sir, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta said to me: ‘Here, great king, a Nigaṇṭha ascetic is restrained by the |fourfold restraint::the fourfold code of restraint practiced by Jain ascetics [cātuyāmasaṁvara]|. And how, great king, is a Nigaṇṭha restrained by the fourfold restraint? Here, great king, a Nigaṇṭha is warded off from all water; he is endowed with the avoidance of all evil; he is cleansed by the avoidance of all evil; he is suffused with the avoidance of all evil. Thus, great king, is a Nigaṇṭha restrained by the fourfold restraint. #### 3.6. The Doctrine of Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta

At one time, venerable sir, I approached Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta; having approached, I exchanged friendly greetings with Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, I sat down to one side. Seated to one side, venerable sir, I said to Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta: ‘Just as, sir Sañcaya, there are these various common crafts ... Is it possible, sir Sañcaya, in the very same way to point out a fruit of the spiritual life that is apparent in the present life?’

When this was said, venerable sir, Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta said to me: ‘If you ask me: “Is there another world?”—if I thought there was another world, I would declare to you: “There is another world.” But I do not say so; I do not say thus; I do not say otherwise; I do not say no; I do not deny it. “Is there no other world? ... Is there both another world and no other world? ... Is there neither another world nor no other world? ... Are there spontaneously arisen beings? ... Are there no spontaneously arisen beings? ... Are there both spontaneously arisen beings and no spontaneously arisen beings? ... Are there neither spontaneously arisen beings nor no spontaneously arisen beings? ... Is there fruit and result of good and bad deeds? ... Is there no fruit and result of good and bad deeds? ... Is there both fruit and result and no fruit and result of good and bad deeds? ... Is there neither fruit and result nor no fruit and result of good and bad deeds? ... Does a Tathāgata exist after death? ... Does a Tathāgata not exist after death? ... Does a Tathāgata both exist and not exist after death? ... Does a Tathāgata neither exist nor not exist after death?”—if you ask me: “Does a Tathāgata neither exist nor not exist after death?”—if I thought a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death, I would declare to you: “A Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death.” But I do not say so; I do not say thus; I do not say otherwise; I do not say no; I do not deny it.’

Thus, venerable sir, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta explained |evasion::verbal deflection, endless equivocation, eel-wriggling [vikkhepa]|. Just as if, venerable sir, when asked about a mango, one were to answer with a breadfruit, or when asked about a breadfruit, one were to answer with a mango—even so, venerable sir, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta explained evasion. Then, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘Of all these ascetics and brahmins, this Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta is the most foolish, the most confused. For how could one, when asked about a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life, explain evasion?’ Then, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘How could one like me think of disparaging an ascetic or brahmin living in my realm?’ So, venerable sir, I neither approved nor rejected the statement of Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta. Without approving or rejecting it, though dissatisfied, without uttering a word of dissatisfaction, without accepting or discarding that statement, I rose from my seat and departed.

### 4. The Fruits of the Spiritual Life

#### 4.1. The First Evident Fruit of the Spiritual Life

“And so, venerable sir, I ask the Blessed One as well: ‘Just as, venerable sir, there are these various common crafts, namely: elephant riders, cavalrymen, charioteers, archers, standard-bearers, camp marshals, supply officers, fierce warriors, royal princes, skirmishers, great champions, brave soldiers, leather-armored warriors, sons of slave women, cooks, barbers, bath attendants, confectioners, garland-makers, dyers, weavers, basket-makers, potters, accountants, finger-mathematicians, and whatever other such common crafts there may be; in this very life they enjoy the visible fruit of their craft; with that they give happiness and pleasure to themselves, give happiness and pleasure to their parents, give happiness and pleasure to their wife and children, give happiness and pleasure to their friends and companions, and establish an uplifting spiritual gift for ascetics and brahmins leading to heaven, yielding happy results, conducive to celestial realms. Is it possible, venerable sir, in the very same way to point out a fruit of the spiritual life that is apparent in the present life?’”

“It is possible, great king. Therefore, great king, I will question you on this matter in return. Answer as you see fit. What do you think, great king? Suppose you had a man who was a slave, a worker, who rises before you, sleeps after you, is willing to do whatever you command, is agreeable in conduct, speaks politely, and awaits your command. It might occur to him: ‘Astonishing indeed, good sirs! Marvelous indeed, good sirs, is the destination of merits, the fruit of merits! For this King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, is a human being; I too am a human being. Yet this King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, enjoys himself fully endowed and provided with the five cords of sensual pleasure, as if he were a deva. But I am his slave, a worker, who rises before him, sleeps after him, is willing to do whatever he commands, is agreeable in conduct, speaks politely, and awaits his command. Surely I too should perform merits. What if I were to shave off my hair and beard, don ochre robes, and go forth from the household life into homelessness?’

Then, after some time, having shaved off his hair and beard, and donned ochre robes, he goes forth from the household life into homelessness. Having thus gone forth, he dwells restrained in body, restrained in speech, restrained in mind, content with basic food and clothing, and delighting in solitude. If your men were to report this to you: ‘If it please Your Majesty, you should know that the man who was your slave, a worker, who rose before you, slept after you, was willing to do whatever you commanded, was agreeable in conduct, spoke politely, and awaited your command—he, sire, having shaved off his hair and beard, and donned ochre robes, has gone forth from the household life into homelessness. Having thus gone forth, he dwells restrained in body, restrained in speech, restrained in mind, content with basic food and clothing, and delighting in solitude.’ Would you say: ‘Let that man return to me, and let him again be a slave, a worker, rising before me, sleeping after me, willing to do whatever I command, agreeable in conduct, speaking politely, and awaiting my command’?”

“Certainly not, venerable sir. Rather, we ourselves would pay homage to him, rise up for him, invite him to a seat, and invite him to accept requisites of robes, almsfood, lodgings, and medicines for the sick; and we would arrange for him lawful protection, shelter, and security.”

“What do you think, great king? This being the case, is there a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life or not?”

“Surely, venerable sir, this being the case, there is a fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life.”

“This, great king, is the first fruit of the spiritual life apparent in the present life that has been declared by me.”

Just as a person, great king, who was imprisoned. Then later on he is released from prison, safe and secure, without any loss of his belongings. He would think: ‘Before, I was imprisoned, now I have been released from prison, safe and secure, without any loss of my belongings.’ On that account, he would attain joy and experience pleasure.

Just as a person, great king, who was a slave, not free, dependent on others, unable to go where he wants. Then later on he is freed from slavery, becomes independent, not dependent on others, free to go where he wants. He would think: ‘Before, I was a slave, not free, dependent on others, unable to go where I want. Now, I am freed from slavery, independent, not dependent on others, free to go where I want.’ On that account, he would attain joy and experience pleasure.

Just as a person, great king, with wealth and possessions would travel along a long desert path, scarce in food and fraught with danger. Then later on he crosses over that desert safely and reaches the safety of a village, secure and free from danger. He would think: ‘Before, with wealth and possessions, I traveled along a long desert path, scarce in food and fraught with danger. Now, I have crossed over that desert safely and reached the safety of a village, secure and free from danger.’ On that account, he would attain joy and experience pleasure.

In the very same way, great king, a bhikkhu sees these five hindrances in himself as if they were a debt, a disease, a prison, slavery, and a dangerous path.

Just as freedom from debt, good health, release from prison, freedom from slavery, and a place of safety; even so, great king, when these five hindrances are abandoned in himself, a bhikkhu sees himself as free from debt, healthy, released from prison, free from slavery, in a place of safety.

### 5. Ajātasattu Declares Himself a Lay Follower

When this was said, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, said to the Blessed One: “Excellent, venerable sir! Excellent, venerable sir! “Indeed, great king, a transgression overcame you, in that, being foolish, misguided, and unskillful, you deprived your father, a righteous man, a righteous king, of his life. When this was said, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, said to the Blessed One: “Well then, venerable sir, we must be going now; we have many duties and much to do.”

The Blessed One said this. The bhikkhus were delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words.
