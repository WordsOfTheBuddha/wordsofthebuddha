Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]|, on Vulture Peak. Now at that time King Ajātasattu of Magadha, |son of Vedehi::epithet of King Ajātasattu; lit. son of Vedehi [vedehiputta]|, wanted to invade the |Vajjis::name of the people of Vajji—a confederation of clans in ancient northern India governed by a republic; Vajjians [vajjī]|. He declared: “I shall wipe out these Vajjis, so mighty and powerful! I shall destroy them, and lay ruin and devastation upon them!”

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, addressed Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha: “Come, brahmin, go to the Blessed One. When you have approached him, bow with your head at the Blessed One’s feet on my behalf, and ask whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably, saying: ‘Venerable sir, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, bows with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and asks whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably.’ And say this: ‘Venerable sir, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, wants to invade the Vajjis. He says: “I shall wipe out these Vajjis, so mighty and powerful! I shall destroy them, and lay ruin and devastation upon them!”’ As the Blessed One answers you, remember it well and report it to me. For |Tathāgatas::those who have arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| do not speak what is untrue.”

#### 1. The Brahmin Vassakāra

“Yes, sir,” Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, replied to King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi. Having had the finest carriages harnessed, he mounted a fine carriage and, along with other fine carriages, set out from Rājagaha toward |Vulture Peak::name of a mountain peak around Rājagaha; lit. vulture’s peak [gijjhakūṭa]|. He went by carriage as far as the ground would allow, then dismounted from the carriage and approached the Blessed One on foot. Having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One.

After having engaged in courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, said to the Blessed One: “Sir Gotama, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, bows with his head at sir Gotama’s feet, and asks whether he is well and healthy, nimble, strong, and dwelling comfortably. Sir Gotama, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, wants to invade the Vajjis. He says: ‘I shall wipe out these Vajjis, so mighty and powerful! I shall destroy them, and lay ruin and devastation upon them!’”

#### 2. Principles That Prevent Decline

Now at that time the venerable Ānanda was standing behind the Blessed One fanning him. Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Have you heard, Ānanda, that the Vajjis assemble frequently and have many meetings?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘The Vajjis assemble frequently and have many meetings.’”

“As long as the Vajjis assemble frequently and have many meetings, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.

Have you heard, Ānanda, that the Vajjis assemble in harmony, adjourn in harmony, and carry out the affairs of the Vajjis in harmony?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘The Vajjis assemble in harmony, adjourn in harmony, and carry out the affairs of the Vajjis in harmony.’”

“As long as the Vajjis assemble in harmony, adjourn in harmony, and carry out the affairs of the Vajjis in harmony, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.

Have you heard, Ānanda, that the Vajjis do not authorize what has not been authorized, do not abolish what has been authorized, and proceed having undertaken the ancient Vajjian traditions as they have been authorized?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘The Vajjis do not authorize what has not been authorized, do not abolish what has been authorized, and proceed having undertaken the ancient Vajjian traditions as they have been authorized.’”

“As long as the Vajjis do not authorize what has not been authorized, do not abolish what has been authorized, and proceed having undertaken the ancient Vajjian traditions as they have been authorized, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.

Have you heard, Ānanda, that the Vajjis honor, respect, revere, and venerate the elders of the Vajjis, and consider them worth listening to?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘The Vajjis honor, respect, revere, and venerate the elders of the Vajjis, and consider them worth listening to.’”

“As long as the Vajjis honor, respect, revere, and venerate the elders of the Vajjis, and consider them worth listening to, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.

Have you heard, Ānanda, that the Vajjis do not forcibly abduct the women and maidens of the clans and make them live with them?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘The Vajjis do not forcibly abduct the women and maidens of the clans and make them live with them.’”

“As long as the Vajjis do not forcibly abduct the women and maidens of the clans and make them live with them, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.

Have you heard, Ānanda, that the Vajjis honor, respect, revere, and venerate the Vajjian |shrines::sacred monuments, sanctuaries, or places of veneration [cetiya]|, both domestic and remote, and do not waste the righteous offerings previously given and previously made?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘The Vajjis honor, respect, revere, and venerate the Vajjian shrines, both domestic and remote, and do not waste the righteous offerings previously given and previously made.’”

“As long as the Vajjis honor, respect, revere, and venerate the Vajjian shrines, both domestic and remote, and do not waste the righteous offerings previously given and previously made, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.

Have you heard, Ānanda, that lawful protection, shelter, and security are well provided for |Arahants::worthy ones, fully awakened beings, free from all mental defilements [arahant]| among the Vajjis, so that Arahants who have not yet arrived might come to the realm, and Arahants who have arrived might dwell comfortably in the realm?”

“I have heard so, venerable sir: ‘Lawful protection, shelter, and security are well provided for Arahants among the Vajjis, so that Arahants who have not yet arrived might come to the realm, and Arahants who have arrived might dwell comfortably in the realm.’”

“As long as lawful protection, shelter, and security are well provided for Arahants among the Vajjis, so that Arahants who have not yet arrived might come to the realm, and Arahants who have arrived might dwell comfortably in the realm, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, Ānanda, not decline.”

Then the Blessed One addressed Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha: “Brahmin, this one time I was dwelling near |Vesāli::name of the capital city of the Vajjian confederacy [vesālī]| at the Sārandada Shrine. There I taught the Vajjis these seven |principles that prevent decline::conditions and practices that ensure ongoing prosperity and prevent degeneration in communities [aparihāniyā dhammā]|. As long as these seven principles that prevent decline last among the Vajjis, and as long as the Vajjis are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the Vajjis, brahmin, not decline.”

When this was said, Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, said to the Blessed One: “Sir Gotama, for Vajjis endowed with even a single one of these principles that prevent decline, only growth is to be expected, not decline. How much more so all seven? The Vajjis are not to be fought in war by King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, except by persuasion or by sowing dissension. And now, sir Gotama, we must depart. We have many duties and much to do.”

“You may go, brahmin, at your own convenience.” Then Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, having delighted in and approved of the Blessed One’s words, rose from his seat and departed.

#### 3. Principles That Prevent Decline Among the Bhikkhus

Then, not long after Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, had left, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Go, Ānanda, as many bhikkhus as are dwelling in dependence on Rājagaha, have them all assemble in the assembly hall.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One. Having assembled as many bhikkhus as were dwelling in dependence on Rājagaha in the assembly hall, he approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and stood to one side. Standing to one side, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “The bhikkhu |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| is assembled, venerable sir. Now is the time for the Blessed One to do as he thinks fit.”

Then the Blessed One rose from his seat and approached the assembly hall. Having approached, he sat down on the prepared seat. Having sat down, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you seven principles that prevent decline. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus assemble frequently and have many meetings, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus assemble in harmony, adjourn in harmony, and carry out the affairs of the Saṅgha in harmony, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not authorize what has not been authorized, do not abolish what has been authorized, and proceed having undertaken the training rules as they have been authorized, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus honor, respect, revere, and venerate those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers and leaders of the Saṅgha, and consider them worth listening to, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not fall under the sway of arisen |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇhā]| that leads to |renewed existence::re-becoming, taking up a new birth [ponobbhavikā]|, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus are intent on forest lodgings, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus individually establish |mindfulness::having active presence of mind and lucid retention; remaining naturally anchored and attentive without losing one’s bearings [sati]|, so that |well-behaved::amiable, of lovely conduct [pesala]| companions in the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| who have not yet come might come, and well-behaved companions in the spiritual life who have come might dwell comfortably, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as these seven principles that prevent decline last among the bhikkhus, and as long as the bhikkhus are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you another seven principles that prevent decline. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.” “Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not delight in work, do not relish work, and are not habitually devoted to work, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not delight in talk, do not relish talk, and are not habitually devoted to talk, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not delight in sleep, do not relish sleep, and are not habitually devoted to sleep, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not delight in company, do not relish company, and are not habitually devoted to company, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not have |evil desires::yearnings that produce harm [pāpiccha]| and do not fall under the sway of evil desires, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not have bad friends, bad companions, and bad associates, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus do not stop halfway with a trifling attainment of distinction, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as these seven principles that prevent decline last among the bhikkhus, and as long as the bhikkhus are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you another seven principles that prevent decline... As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus are |faithful::confident, believing, devoted, trusting [saddhā]|... are |conscientious::honorable [hirimana]|... have |moral dread::fear of wrongdoing out of regard for others [ottappa]|... are |learned::well educated, knowledgeable [bahussuta]|... have |energy aroused::with initiative [āraddhavīriya]|... have mindfulness established... are |wise::discerning, percipient; lit. having the wisdom quality [paññavant]|, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline. As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as these seven principles that prevent decline last among the bhikkhus, and as long as the bhikkhus are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you another seven principles that prevent decline. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus cultivate the |awakening factor of mindfulness::the quality of mindfulness leading to full awakening, remembering as a factor of enlightenment; first of the seven awakening factors [satisambojjhaṅga]|... cultivate the |awakening factor of investigation of states::the quality of discriminative investigation into states, mental qualities and other phenomena; analyzing the teaching as a factor of enlightenment; second of the seven awakening factors [dhammavicayasambojjhaṅga]|... cultivate the |awakening factor of energy::the quality of unrelenting effort, the sustaining power of persistence as a factor of enlightenment; third of the seven awakening factors [vīriyasambojjhaṅga]|... cultivate the |awakening factor of joy::the quality of heartfelt gladness, spiritual rapture untainted by sensuality, as a factor of enlightenment; fourth of the seven awakening factors [pītisambojjhaṅga]|... cultivate the |awakening factor of tranquility::the quality of inner stillness, bodily and mental calm, as a factor of enlightenment; fifth of the seven awakening factors [passaddhisambojjhaṅga]|... cultivate the |awakening factor of collectedness::the quality of unification, stability, and deep stillness of the mind, as a factor of enlightenment; sixth of the seven awakening factors [samādhisambojjhaṅga]|... cultivate the |awakening factor of equanimity::the quality of balanced observation, non-reactivity, and mental equipoise as a factor of enlightenment; seventh of the seven awakening factors [upekkhāsambojjhaṅga]|, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as these seven principles that prevent decline last among the bhikkhus, and as long as the bhikkhus are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you another seven principles that prevent decline. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus cultivate the |perception of impermanence::perception of instability, inconsistency [aniccasaññā]|... cultivate the |perception of not-self::recognition of impersonality [anattasaññā]|... cultivate the perception of |unattractiveness::disagreeableness, recognizing the not aesthetically pleasing characteristics [asubha]|... cultivate the perception of |drawback::disadvantage, unsatisfactoriness, inadequacy, danger [ādīnava]|... cultivate the perception of |giving up::letting go, abandoning, removing [pahāna]|... cultivate the perception of |fading of desire::dispassion, detachment [virāga]|... cultivate the perception of |cessation::ending, termination [nirodha]|, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as these seven principles that prevent decline last among the bhikkhus, and as long as the bhikkhus are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you six principles that prevent decline. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus maintain bodily acts of |loving-kindness::goodwill towards, friendliness to, benevolence for [mettā]| toward their fellow spiritual companions, both openly and in private, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline. As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus maintain verbal acts of loving-kindness... maintain mental acts of loving-kindness toward their fellow spiritual companions, both openly and in private, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus, whatever righteous gains they have obtained in accordance with the Dhamma, even the mere contents of the alms bowl, do not keep such gains to themselves, but share them in common with their virtuous fellow spiritual companions, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus dwell both openly and in private possessing in common with their virtuous fellow spiritual companions those virtues that are unbroken, untorn, not spotty, unblemished, freeing, praised by the wise, irreproachable, and |leading to collectedness::leading to samadhi, conducive to mental composure [samādhisaṃvattanika]|, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as the bhikkhus dwell both openly and in private possessing in common with their virtuous fellow spiritual companions that view which is noble and |leading to liberation::redemptive, leading to deliverance [niyyānika]|, which leads one who practices it to the complete |wearing away of suffering::extinction of stress, termination of tension [dukkhakkhaya]|, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.

As long|, bhikkhus,::::| as these six principles that prevent decline last among the bhikkhus, and as long as the bhikkhus are seen following them, only growth is to be expected for the bhikkhus, not decline.”

And while dwelling at Rājagaha on Vulture Peak, the Blessed One often gave this Dhamma talk to the bhikkhus:

“Such is |virtue::moral conduct, ethical behavior [sīla]|, such is |collectedness::stability of mind, stillness of mind, mental composure [samādhi]|, such is |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]|. Then, having dwelt at Rājagaha as long as he wished, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Come, Ānanda, let us go to |Ambalaṭṭhikā::name of a park between Rājagaha and Nālanda; lit. mango sapling [ambalaṭṭhikā]|.”

There the Blessed One dwelled at Ambalaṭṭhikā in the royal rest-house. And while dwelling at Ambalaṭṭhikā in the royal rest-house, too, the Blessed One often gave this Dhamma talk to the bhikkhus:

“Such is virtue, such is collectedness, such is wisdom. Collectedness imbued with virtue is of great fruit and great benefit. Wisdom imbued with collectedness is of great fruit and great benefit. The mind imbued with wisdom is completely freed from the taints—namely, the taint of sensual desire, the taint of becoming, and the taint of ignorance.”

Then, having dwelt at Ambalaṭṭhikā as long as he wished, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Come, Ānanda, let us go to |Nālanda::name of a town near Rājagaha [nāḷandā]|.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One, together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, arrived at Nālanda. There the Blessed One dwelled at Nālanda in Pāvārika’s mango grove.

#### 4. Sāriputta’s Lion’s Roar

Then the venerable |Sāriputta::foremost disciple of the Buddha in great wisdom [sāriputta]| approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Sāriputta said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I have such |faith::confidence, inspiration; lit. settled forward [pasanna]| in the Blessed One. There was not, there will not be, nor is there now another ascetic or brahmin more knowledgeable than the Blessed One in |full awakening::enlightenment [sambodhi]|.”

“Truly remarkable, Sāriputta! You have spoken exalted speech, declared an absolute truth, and roared a lion’s roar: ‘Venerable sir, I have such faith in the Blessed One. There was not, there will not be, nor is there now another ascetic or brahmin more knowledgeable than the Blessed One in full awakening.’

What about those who were Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones in past times, Sāriputta? Did you know all those Blessed Ones by encompassing their minds with your mind: ‘Such was the virtue of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such was the character of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such was the wisdom of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such was the conduct of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such was the liberation of those Blessed Ones’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“What about those who will be Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones in future times, Sāriputta? Will you know all those Blessed Ones by encompassing their minds with your mind: ‘Such will be the virtue of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such will be the character of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such will be the wisdom of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such will be the conduct of those Blessed Ones,’ ‘such will be the liberation of those Blessed Ones’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“What about me, Sāriputta, now, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One? Am I known to you by having encompassed my mind with your mind: ‘Such is the Blessed One’s virtue,’ ‘such is the Blessed One’s character,’ ‘such is the Blessed One’s wisdom,’ ‘such is the Blessed One’s conduct,’ ‘such is the Blessed One’s liberation’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Since you do not have knowledge encompassing the minds of the Arahants, the perfectly Awakened Ones of the past, future, and present, Sāriputta, then what about the exalted speech that has been spoken, the absolute truth that has been declared, the lion’s roar that has been uttered: ‘Venerable sir, I have such faith in the Blessed One. There was not, there will not be, nor is there now another ascetic or brahmin more knowledgeable than the Blessed One in full awakening’?”

“Venerable sir, I do not have knowledge encompassing the minds of the Arahants, the perfectly Awakened Ones of the past, future, and present; but I have known this principle of the Dhamma. Suppose, venerable sir, a king’s frontier fortress had strong foundations, strong encircling walls and arches, and a single gate. There, the gatekeeper is wise, experienced, and discerning, keeping out strangers and admitting acquaintances. As he patrols the encircling path all around that fortress, he would not see any gap or opening in the encircling wall, not even one big enough for a cat to slip through. He would then think: ‘Whatever large creatures enter or leave this fortress, all of them enter or leave through this very gate.’

So too, venerable sir, I have known this principle of the Dhamma: ‘All those who were Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones in past times, those Blessed Ones awakened to the unsurpassed perfect awakening by abandoning the |five hindrances::the five mental obstacles—sensual desire, ill will, dullness and drowsiness, restlessness and worry, and doubt—that block clarity and stillness [pañca nīvaraṇā]|, mental impurities that weaken wisdom, with minds well established in the |four establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as the direct path of practice [cattāro satipaṭṭhānā]|, and having cultivated the |seven awakening factors::mindfulness, investigation of states, energy, joy, tranquility, collectedness, and equanimity [satta bojjhaṅgā]| as they truly are. All those who will be Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones in future times, those Blessed Ones will awaken to the unsurpassed perfect awakening by abandoning the five hindrances, mental impurities that weaken wisdom, with minds well established in the four establishments of mindfulness, and having cultivated the seven awakening factors as they truly are. And the Blessed One, venerable sir, the Arahant, the perfectly Awakened One at present, has awakened to the unsurpassed perfect awakening by abandoning the five hindrances, mental impurities that weaken wisdom, with his mind well established in the four establishments of mindfulness, and having cultivated the seven awakening factors as they truly are.’”

And while dwelling at Nālanda in Pāvārika’s mango grove, too, the Blessed One often gave this Dhamma talk to the bhikkhus:

“Such is virtue, such is collectedness, such is wisdom. Collectedness imbued with virtue is of great fruit and great benefit. Wisdom imbued with collectedness is of great fruit and great benefit. The mind imbued with wisdom is completely freed from the taints—namely, the taint of sensual desire, the taint of becoming, and the taint of ignorance.”

#### 5. The Dangers of Unprincipled Conduct

Then, having dwelt at Nālanda as long as he wished, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Come, Ānanda, let us go to |Pāṭaligāma::name of a village in Magadha [pāṭaligāma]|.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One, together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhus, arrived at Pāṭaligāma.

#### 7. Building a Fortification

Now at that time, Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, were building a |fortification::citadel, city, fortress [nagara]| at Pāṭaligāma to ward off the Vajjis. In the area where middle-ranking deities took possession of sites, the minds of middle-ranking kings and royal ministers were inclined to build residences there. In the area where lower-ranking deities took possession of sites, the minds of lower-ranking kings and royal ministers were inclined to build residences there.

The Blessed One, with the |divine eye::the faculty of clairvoyance, the ability to see beyond the ordinary human range [dibbacakkhu]|, purified and surpassing human vision, saw those thousands upon thousands of deities taking possession of sites in Pāṭaligāma. Then towards the time of dawn, having risen, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Ānanda, who is building a fortification at Pāṭaligāma?”

“Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, venerable sir, are building a fortification at Pāṭaligāma to ward off the Vajjis.”

“It is as if, Ānanda, they had consulted with the deities of the Thirty-Three; just so are Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, building a fortification at Pāṭaligāma to ward off the Vajjis. Here, Ānanda, I saw with the divine eye, purified and surpassing human vision, a great many deities, thousands upon thousands, taking possession of sites in Pāṭaligāma. In the area where prominent deities took possession of sites, the minds of prominent kings and royal ministers were inclined to build residences there. In the area where middle-ranking deities took possession of sites, the minds of middle-ranking kings and royal ministers were inclined to build residences there. In the area where lower-ranking deities took possession of sites, the minds of lower-ranking kings and royal ministers were inclined to build residences there. Then Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, they exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One, and after exchanging courteous and polite conversation, they stood to one side. Standing to one side, Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, said to the Blessed One: “May sir Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus accept a meal from us today.” The Blessed One consented in silence.

Then Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, having received the Blessed One’s consent, went to their own dwelling. Having had various kinds of excellent food prepared in their own dwelling, they announced the time to the Blessed One: “It is time, sir Gotama, the meal is ready.”

Then the Blessed One, having dressed early in the morning, taking his alms bowl and outer robe, went to the dwelling of Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. Having approached, he sat down on the prepared seat. Then Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, personally served and satisfied the Saṅgha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha with various kinds of excellent food. “In whatever place a wise person

the restrained, who lead the spiritual life—

being honored, they honor in return,

Then the Blessed One, having offered these verses of appreciation to Sunidha and Vassakāra, the chief ministers of Magadha, rose from his seat and departed.

Then the Blessed One approached the Ganges river.

making a bridge and leaving the |pond::lake, marsh [pallala]| behind;
while people are still tying together a raft,
the wise have already crossed over.”

#### 11. Ambapālī the Courtesan

How could it possibly be otherwise?”

Then the venerable Anuruddha addressed the bhikkhus: “Enough, friends, do not grieve, do not lament. Has it not been declared by the Blessed One beforehand, friends: ‘That we must be parted, separated, and severed from all who are dear and agreeable to us’? How could it possibly be obtained here, friends? That which is born, become, conditioned, of a nature to decay—that it should not decay: that is not possible. The deities, friends, are complaining.”

“But of what kind, venerable sir, are the deities that the venerable Anuruddha is attending to?”

“There are, friend Ānanda, deities in space who are percipient of the earth. With hair disheveled they wail, with arms outstretched they wail, they fall as if their feet were cut off, they roll back and forth: ‘Too soon the Blessed One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Accomplished One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Eye of the World has disappeared!’ There are, friend Ānanda, deities on the earth who are percipient of the earth. With hair disheveled they wail, with arms outstretched they wail, they fall as if their feet were cut off, they roll back and forth: ‘Too soon the Blessed One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Accomplished One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Eye of the World has disappeared!’ But those deities who are free of passion endure, mindful and clearly aware: ‘Conditioned phenomena are impermanent. How could it possibly be otherwise?’”

Then the venerable Anuruddha and the venerable Ānanda spent the remainder of that night in Dhamma talk.

Then the venerable Anuruddha addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Go, friend Ānanda, enter Kusinārā and inform the Mallas of Kusinārā: ‘Vāseṭṭhas, the Blessed One has attained final Nibbāna. Now is the time to do as you think fit.’”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Ānanda replied to the venerable Anuruddha. Having dressed in the morning, taking his bowl and outer robe, he entered Kusinārā with a companion.

Now at that time the Mallas of Kusinārā were assembled in the council hall on that same business. Then the venerable Ānanda approached the council hall of the Mallas of Kusinārā; having approached, he informed the Mallas of Kusinārā: “Vāseṭṭhas, the Blessed One has attained final Nibbāna. Now is the time to do as you think fit.”

When they heard the venerable Ānanda’s words, the Mallas, the sons of the Mallas, the daughters-in-law of the Mallas, and the wives of the Mallas became distressed, saddened, and overcome with grief. And some, with hair disheveled, wailed; with arms outstretched they wailed; they fell as if their feet were cut off; they rolled back and forth: “Too soon the Blessed One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Accomplished One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Eye of the World has disappeared!”

#### 37. Honoring the Blessed One’s Body

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā instructed their men: “Well then, sirs, gather together fragrances and garlands and all the musical instruments in Kusinārā.”

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā, taking fragrances and garlands, all the musical instruments, and five hundred pairs of cloths, approached the sāla grove of the Mallas at Upavattana, where the Blessed One’s body was; having approached, they spent one day honoring, respecting, revering, and venerating the Blessed One’s body with dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances, making cloth canopies and preparing pavilions.

Then it occurred to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “It is too late today to cremate the Blessed One’s body. Tomorrow we will cremate the Blessed One’s body.” Then the Mallas of Kusinārā, honoring, respecting, revering, and venerating the Blessed One’s body with dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances, making cloth canopies and preparing pavilions, spent a second day ... a third day ... a fourth day ... a fifth day ... a sixth day.

Then on the seventh day it occurred to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “Honoring, respecting, revering, and venerating the Blessed One’s body with dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances, we will carry it to the south of the town, and cremate the Blessed One’s body there to the south, outside the town.”

Now at that time eight leading Mallas, having washed their heads and dressed in unused clothes, said: “We will lift the Blessed One’s body.” But they were unable to lift it.

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā said to the venerable Anuruddha: “What, venerable Anuruddha, is the cause, what is the reason, that these eight leading Mallas, having washed their heads and dressed in unused clothes, saying, ‘We will lift the Blessed One’s body,’ are unable to lift it?”

“You have one intention, Vāseṭṭhas, and the deities have another intention.”

“But, venerable sir, what is the deities’ intention?”

“Your intention, Vāseṭṭhas, is: ‘Honoring, respecting, revering, and venerating the Blessed One’s body with dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances, we will carry it to the south of the town, and cremate the Blessed One’s body there to the south, outside the town.’ The deities’ intention, Vāseṭṭhas, is: ‘Honoring, respecting, revering, and venerating the Blessed One’s body with heavenly dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances, we will carry it to the north of the town, enter the town by the northern gate, carry it through the center of the town, leave by the eastern gate, and cremate the Blessed One’s body there to the east of the town, at the Mallas’ shrine named Makuṭabandhana.’”

“Let it be, venerable sir, as the deities intend.”

Now at that time Kusinārā, even including the dirty rubbish heaps at the junctions, was covered knee-deep with coral tree flowers. Then the deities and the Mallas of Kusinārā, honoring, respecting, revering, and venerating the Blessed One’s body with heavenly and human dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances, carried it to the north of the town, entered the town by the northern gate, carried it through the center of the town, left by the eastern gate, and set down the Blessed One’s body there to the east of the town, at the Mallas’ shrine named |Makuṭabandhana::name of a Mallan shrine; lit. crown-binding [makuṭabandhana]|.

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā said to the venerable Ānanda: “How, venerable Ānanda, should we conduct ourselves toward the Tathāgata’s body?”

“As they conduct themselves toward the body of a wheel-turning monarch, Vāseṭṭhas, so should they conduct themselves toward the Tathāgata’s body.”

“But, venerable Ānanda, how do they conduct themselves toward the body of a wheel-turning monarch?”

“Vāseṭṭhas, they wrap the body of a wheel-turning monarch in unused cloth. Having wrapped it in unused cloth, they wrap it in carded cotton. Having wrapped it in carded cotton, they wrap it in unused cloth. In this way they wrap the body of a wheel-turning monarch in five hundred pairs of cloths. Then they place it in an iron vat filled with oil and cover it with another iron vat. Then, having built a funeral pyre of all kinds of fragrances, they burn the body of the wheel-turning monarch. They build a |stupa::a mound-like reliquary monument, a shrine [thūpa]| for the wheel-turning monarch at a crossroads. That, Vāseṭṭhas, is how they conduct themselves toward the body of a wheel-turning monarch. As they conduct themselves toward the body of a wheel-turning monarch, so should they conduct themselves toward the Tathāgata’s body. A stupa for the Tathāgata is to be built at a crossroads. Those who there offer a garland, or fragrance, or powder, or pay homage, or establish confidence in their heart—that will be for their welfare and happiness for a long time.”

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā instructed their men: “Well then, sirs, gather together carded cotton for the Mallas.”

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā wrapped the Blessed One’s body in unused cloth. Having wrapped it in unused cloth, they wrapped it in carded cotton. Having wrapped it in carded cotton, they wrapped it in unused cloth. In this way they wrapped the Blessed One’s body in five hundred pairs of cloths. Then they placed it in an iron vat filled with oil and covered it with another iron vat. Then, having built a funeral pyre of all kinds of fragrances, they placed the Blessed One’s body on the pyre.

#### 38. The Account of the Elder Mahākassapa

Now at that time the venerable Mahākassapa was traveling on the highway from Pāvā to Kusinārā together with a large Saṅgha of five hundred bhikkhus. Then the venerable Mahākassapa left the road and sat down at the foot of a certain tree.

Now at that time a certain Ājīvaka, having taken a coral tree flower in Kusinārā, was traveling on the highway to Pāvā. The venerable Mahākassapa saw that Ājīvaka coming from afar. Having seen him, he said to that Ājīvaka: “Friend, do you know our Teacher?”

“Yes, friend, I know. It is seven days today since the ascetic Gotama attained final Nibbāna. That is why I took this coral tree flower.” Those bhikkhus there who were not free of passion—some of them, with arms outstretched, wailed; they fell as if their feet were cut off; they rolled back and forth: “Too soon the Blessed One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Accomplished One has attained final Nibbāna! Too soon the Eye of the World has disappeared!” But those bhikkhus who were free of passion endured, mindful and clearly aware: “Conditioned phenomena are impermanent. How could it possibly be otherwise?”

Now at that time a bhikkhu named Subhadda, who had gone forth in old age, was sitting in that assembly. Then Subhadda, gone forth in old age, said to those bhikkhus: “Enough, friends, do not grieve, do not lament. We are well rid of that Great Ascetic. We were troubled: ‘This is allowable for you; this is not allowable for you.’ Now we shall do what we wish, and we shall not do what we do not wish.”

Then the venerable Mahākassapa addressed the bhikkhus: “Enough, friends, do not grieve, do not lament. Has it not been declared by the Blessed One beforehand, friends: ‘That we must be parted, separated, and severed from all who are dear and agreeable to us’? How could it possibly be obtained here, friends? That which is born, become, conditioned, of a nature to decay—that the Tathāgata’s body too should not decay: that is not possible.”

Now at that time four leading Mallas, having washed their heads and dressed in unused clothes, said: “We will light the Blessed One’s funeral pyre.” But they were unable to light it.

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā said to the venerable Anuruddha: “What, venerable Anuruddha, is the cause, what is the reason, that these four leading Mallas, having washed their heads and dressed in unused clothes, saying, ‘We will light the Blessed One’s funeral pyre,’ are unable to light it?”

“The deities have a different intention, Vāseṭṭhas.”

“But, venerable sir, what is the deities’ intention?”

“The deities’ intention, Vāseṭṭhas, is this: ‘This venerable Mahākassapa is traveling on the highway from Pāvā to Kusinārā together with a large Saṅgha of five hundred bhikkhus. The Blessed One’s funeral pyre will not burn until the venerable Mahākassapa has bowed with his head at the Blessed One’s feet.’”

“Let it be, venerable sir, as the deities intend.”

Then the venerable Mahākassapa approached Kusinārā, the Mallas’ shrine named Makuṭabandhana, where the Blessed One’s funeral pyre was; having approached, he arranged his robe over one shoulder, raised his joined palms, circumambulated the pyre three times, keeping it on his right, and bowed with his head at the Blessed One’s feet. And those five hundred bhikkhus also arranged their robes over one shoulder, raised their joined palms, circumambulated the pyre three times, keeping it on their right, and bowed with their heads at the Blessed One’s feet. And when the venerable Mahākassapa and those five hundred bhikkhus had paid homage, the Blessed One’s funeral pyre burst into flame by itself.

And when the Blessed One’s body was burning, whatever there was of outer skin, inner skin, flesh, sinews, or synovial fluid—no ash or soot from that was found; only the relics remained. Just as when ghee or oil is burning, no ash or soot is found—so too, when the Blessed One’s body was burning, whatever there was of outer skin, inner skin, flesh, sinews, or synovial fluid—no ash or soot from that was found; only the relics remained. And of those five hundred pairs of cloths, only two cloths were not burned: the innermost of all and the outermost. And when the Blessed One’s body had been burned, a stream of water appeared in the sky and extinguished the Blessed One’s funeral pyre. Water also welled up from the sāla trees and extinguished the Blessed One’s funeral pyre. And the Mallas of Kusinārā extinguished the Blessed One’s funeral pyre with water of all fragrances.

Then the Mallas of Kusinārā placed the Blessed One’s relics in the council hall, made a cage of spears, surrounded it with a rampart of bows, and for seven days honored, respected, revered, and venerated them with dance, song, music, garlands, and fragrances.

#### 39. The Distribution of the Relics

King Ajātasattu of Magadha, |son of Vedehi::epithet of King Ajātasattu; lit. son of Vedehi [vedehiputta]|, heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and I too am an aristocrat. I too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. I too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

The Licchavis of Vesāli heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the Licchavis of Vesāli sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and we too are aristocrats. We too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. We too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

The Sakyans dwelling at Kapilavatthu heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the Sakyans dwelling at Kapilavatthu sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was our foremost kinsman. We too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. We too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

The Bulis of Allakappa heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the Bulis of Allakappa sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and we too are aristocrats. We too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. We too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

The Koliyas of Rāmagāma heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the Koliyas of Rāmagāma sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and we too are aristocrats. We too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. We too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

The brahmin of Veṭhadīpa heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the brahmin of Veṭhadīpa sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and I am a brahmin. I too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. I too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

The Mallas of Pāvā heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the Mallas of Pāvā sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and we too are aristocrats. We too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. We too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

When this was said, the Mallas of Kusinārā said to those groups and companies: “The Blessed One attained final Nibbāna in our village territory. We will not give a share of the Blessed One’s relics.”

When this was said, Doṇa the brahmin said to those groups and companies:

“Listen, sirs, to a single word from me.
Our Buddha was a speaker of peace.
It would not be good that there should be a quarrel
over a share of the relics of the supreme person.

Let all of you, sirs, united and harmonious,
rejoicing together, make eight portions.
Let stupas be widespread in the directions,
so that many people may have confidence in the One with Vision.”

“Well then, brahmin, you yourself divide the Blessed One’s relics into eight equal, well-distributed portions.”

“Yes, sirs,” Doṇa the brahmin replied to those groups and companies. Having divided the Blessed One’s relics into eight equal, well-distributed portions, he said to those groups and companies: “Sirs, give me this urn. I too will build a stupa for the urn and hold a festival.” They gave Doṇa the brahmin the urn.

The Moriyas of Pipphalivana heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, has attained final Nibbāna at Kusinārā.” Then the Moriyas of Pipphalivana sent a messenger to the Mallas of Kusinārā: “The Blessed One was an aristocrat, and we too are aristocrats. We too deserve a share of the Blessed One’s relics. We too will build a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics and hold a festival.”

“There is no share of the Blessed One’s relics; the Blessed One’s relics have been divided. Take the embers from here.” They took the embers from there.

#### 40. Honoring the Relic Stupas

Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Rājagaha and held a festival. The Licchavis of Vesāli built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Vesāli and held a festival. The Sakyans dwelling at Kapilavatthu built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Kapilavatthu and held a festival. The Bulis of Allakappa built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Allakappa and held a festival. The Koliyas of Rāmagāma built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Rāmagāma and held a festival. The brahmin of Veṭhadīpa built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Veṭhadīpa and held a festival. The Mallas of Pāvā built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Pāvā and held a festival. The Mallas of Kusinārā built a stupa for the Blessed One’s relics at Kusinārā and held a festival. Doṇa the brahmin built a stupa for the urn and held a festival. The Moriyas of Pipphalivana built a stupa for the embers at Pipphalivana and held a festival. Thus there were eight relic stupas, a ninth urn stupa, and a tenth ember stupa. That is how it was in former times.

There were eight portions of the relics of the One with Vision.
Seven portions are honored in Jambudīpa.
And one portion of the most excellent of men
is honored by a nāga king in Rāmagāma.

One tooth is venerated in the heaven of the Thirty-Three,
and one is honored in the city of Gandhāra;
another in the realm of the Kāliṅga king,
and one is honored by a nāga king.

Through his radiance, this rich earth
is adorned with the finest offerings.
Thus the relics of the One with Vision
are well honored by those who are honorable.

Honored by lords of gods, nāgas, and men,
and likewise honored by the finest of human lords—
honor it with joined palms when you get the chance,
for a Buddha is rare even in a hundred aeons.

Forty even teeth,
and the hair of the head and body, all of them—
the gods carried them off one by one
throughout the successive world-systems.
