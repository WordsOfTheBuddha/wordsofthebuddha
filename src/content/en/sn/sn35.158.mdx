---
title: Ajjhattaaniccanandikkhaya sutta - Impermanence of the Internal and Wearing Away of Delight
description: By radically attending to the impermanence of the six sense bases, a bhikkhu becomes disenchanted; with delight and passion worn away, the mind is well liberated.
qualities: recognition of impermanence, disenchantment, delight, passion, liberation, radical attention
theme: wisdom, training guideline
slug: sn35.158
priority: 1.1
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| radically attend to the |eye::faculty of seeing, vision [cakkhu]| and see the |impermanence::instability [aniccatā]| of the eye |as it truly is::as it has come to be, as it is in actuality [yathābhūta]|. A bhikkhu attending radically to the eye, and seeing the impermanence of the eye as it truly is, becomes |disenchanted with::disillusioned with [nibbindati]| the eye. With the wearing away of delight comes the |wearing away of passion::exhaustion of intense desire, ending of strong emotions, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāgakkhaya]|; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is said to be well liberated.

|Bhikkhus,::::| radically attend to the ear ... the nose ... radically attend to the tongue, and see the impermanence of the tongue as it truly is. A bhikkhu attending radically to the tongue, and seeing the impermanence of the tongue as it truly is, becomes disenchanted with the tongue. With the wearing away of delight comes the wearing away of passion; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is said to be well liberated. ... the body ... radically attend to the |mind::mental faculty, intellect [mana]|, and see the impermanence of the mind as it truly is. A bhikkhu attending radically to the mind, and seeing the impermanence of the mind as it truly is, becomes disenchanted with the mind. With the wearing away of delight comes the wearing away of passion; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is said to be well liberated.”
