At Sāvatthi.

Seated to one side, venerable Rādha addressed the Blessed One:

“‘Cessation of the conduit to existence, cessation of the conduit to existence,’ is said, venerable sir. What, venerable sir, is the conduit to existence, and what is the cessation of the conduit to existence?”

“Rādha, in |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]|, whatever |desire::intention, wish, impulse, interest [chanda]|, |passion::intense desire, strong emotion, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāga]|, |delight::pleasure, enjoyment, satisfaction [nandī]|, and |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]| there is, whatever |engaging and grasping::approaching and clinging, going near and grabbing hold [upayupādāna]|, whatever |determinations, attachments, and underlying tendencies::prejudices, adherences, and dormant proclivities [adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusaya]| of the mind—this is called the conduit to existence. The cessation of these is the cessation of the conduit to existence.

In |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]|<collapse>, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is, whatever engaging and grasping, whatever determinations, attachments, and underlying tendencies of the mind—this is called the conduit to existence. The cessation of these is the cessation of the conduit to existence. In</collapse> |perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [saññā]|<collapse>, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is, whatever engaging and grasping, whatever determinations, attachments, and underlying tendencies of the mind—this is called the conduit to existence. The cessation of these is the cessation of the conduit to existence. In</collapse> |intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]|<collapse>, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is, whatever engaging and grasping, whatever determinations, attachments, and underlying tendencies of the mind—this is called the conduit to existence. The cessation of these is the cessation of the conduit to existence.In</collapse> |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]|, whatever desire<collapse>, passion, delight, and craving there is, whatever engaging and grasping, whatever determinations, attachments, and</collapse> underlying tendencies of the mind—this is called the conduit to existence. The cessation of these is the cessation of the conduit to existence.”
