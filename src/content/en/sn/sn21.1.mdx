Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park. There, the venerable |Mahāmoggallāna::foremost disciple of the Buddha in psychic power; lit. Moggallāna the great [mahāmoggallāna]| addressed the bhikkhus: “Friends, bhikkhus.”

“Friend,” those bhikkhus replied to the venerable Mahāmoggallāna.

The venerable Mahāmoggallāna said this:

“Here, friends, while I was alone in |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, the following |reflection::contemplation, train of thought [parivitakka]| arose in my mind: ‘“Noble silence, noble silence,” it is said. Now what is noble silence?’

Then, friends, it occurred to me: ‘Here, with the |settling::calming, conciliation, subsiding [vūpasama]| of reflection and examination, a bhikkhu enters and dwells in the second jhāna, which is characterized by internal |tranquility::calming, settling, confidence [sampasādana]| and |unification::singleness, integration [ekodibhāva]| of mind, free from reflection and examination, |born of collectedness::born from a stable mind [samādhija]|, and imbued with |uplifting joy and pleasure::delight and ease; sometimes experienced as ecstasy, intense exhilaration or rapture [pītisukha]|. This is called noble silence.’

And so, friends, with the settling of reflection and examination, I entered and dwelled in the second jhāna, which is characterized by internal tranquility and unification of mind, free from reflection and examination, born of collectedness, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure.

Then, friends, while I was dwelling in that abiding, |conceptual engagements::attention driven by knowing-together [saññāmanasikārā]| accompanied by reflection |overwhelmed::assailed, frequented [samudācarati]| me.

Then, friends, the Blessed One approached me by |psychic power::supernormal ability, psychic potency, spiritual power [iddhi]| and said this: ‘Moggallāna, Moggallāna, do not |neglect::be heedless of [pamādi]| noble silence, brahmin. Steady the mind in noble silence; unify the mind in noble silence; |compose::collect [samādahati]| the mind in noble silence.’

And so, friends, on a later occasion, with the settling of reflection and examination, I entered and dwelled in the second jhāna, which is characterized by internal tranquility and unification of mind, free from reflection and examination, born of collectedness, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure.

For if, friends, one speaking rightly were to say: ‘A disciple |assisted::supported, helped, fostered [anuggahita]| by the Teacher has attained |profound direct knowledge::lit. great knowledge state [mahābhiññatā]|,’ it is of me that one speaking rightly would say: ‘A disciple assisted by the Teacher has attained profound direct knowledge.’”
