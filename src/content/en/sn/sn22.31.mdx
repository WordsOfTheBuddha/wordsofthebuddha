At Sāvatthi.


“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you about |angst::gloom, misery [agha]| and the root of angst. Listen to this.

What is angst? Form is angst, feeling is angst, perception is angst, intentional constructs are angst, consciousness is angst. This is called angst.

And what is the root of angst? Whatever |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]| [there is that] leads to rebirth, accompanied by |delight::pleasure, enjoyment, relish [nandi]| and |passion::intense desire, strong emotion, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāga]|, i.e. |seeking delight here and there::thoroughly enjoying this and that [tatratatrābhinandī]|—namely, craving for sensual pleasure, |craving for existence::craving for continued existence, desire for a stable identity, attachment to a becoming, a future self, attainment, or experience [bhavataṇhā]|, and |craving for non-becoming::A craving to end suffering through erasure of current experience or identity, e.g. “This self is unbearable; I want out.” [vibhavataṇhā]|. This is called the root of angst.”
