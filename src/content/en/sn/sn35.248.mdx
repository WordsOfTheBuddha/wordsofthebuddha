---
title: Yavakalāpi sutta - Sheaf of Barley
description: The Buddha compares the senses of an uninstructed person to a sheaf of barley thrashed by six flails at a crossroads—and a seventh flail if they seek future existence. Using the parable of the captive asura lord Vepacitti, whose bonds tightened or vanished based solely on his thoughts, the Buddha illustrates how conceiving “I am” is a subtle, subjective trap.
qualities: conceit, mental proliferation, perturbation, self-making, imperturbable
theme: wisdom, inquisitiveness
slug: sn35.248
simile: sheaf of barley
priority: 1.6
character:
  - Vepacitti, lord of the asuras
  - Sakka, lord of the devas
---

“Suppose, |bhikkhus,::::| a sheaf of barley were placed at a crossroads. Then six men would come with flails in hand. They would beat the sheaf of barley with the six flails. Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| that sheaf of barley would be thoroughly beaten, being beaten by six flails. Then a seventh man would come with a flail in hand. He would beat that sheaf of barley with a seventh flail. Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| that sheaf of barley would be even more thoroughly beaten, being beaten by a seventh flail.

In the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| an uninstructed ordinary person is beaten in the eye by agreeable and disagreeable forms ... is beaten in the tongue by agreeable and disagreeable flavors ... is beaten in the mind by agreeable and disagreeable mental objects. If|, bhikkhus,::::| that uninstructed ordinary person intends for a renewed existence in the future, then that |misguided person::vain person [moghapurisa]| is even more thoroughly beaten, just like that sheaf of barley being beaten by a seventh flail.

Once in the past, |bhikkhus,::::| a battle was fought between the devas and the asuras. Then, |bhikkhus,::::| |Vepacitti::name of a daemon lord [vepacitti]::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, addressed the asuras: ‘Good sirs, if in the ensuing battle between the devas and the asuras, the asuras win and the devas are defeated, bind Sakka, lord of the devas, with a fivefold bond at the neck and bring him to my presence in the city of the asuras.’

|Sakka::name of the king of the deities [sakka]|, lord of the devas, also addressed the devas of the Thirty-Three: ‘Good sirs, if in the ensuing battle between the devas and the asuras, the devas win and the asuras are defeated, bind |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, with a fivefold bond at the neck and bring him to my presence in the Sudhamma assembly hall.’

In that battle, |bhikkhus,::::| the devas won and the asuras were defeated. Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the devas of the Thirty-Three bound |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, with a fivefold bond at the neck and brought him into the presence of Sakka, lord of the devas, in the Sudhamma assembly hall.

There, |bhikkhus,::::| |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, was bound by the fivefold bond at the neck. Whenever|, bhikkhus,::::| it occurred to |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras: ‘The devas are righteous, the asuras are unrighteous; I will go to the city of the devas right now’—then he saw himself freed from the fivefold bond at the neck, and supplied and endowed with the five divine sensual pleasures, he amused himself.

But whenever|, bhikkhus,::::| it occurred to |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras: ‘The asuras are righteous, the devas are unrighteous; I will go right back there to the city of the asuras’—then he saw himself bound by the fivefold bond at the neck, and he was deprived of the five divine sensual pleasures.

So subtle|, bhikkhus,::::| is |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|’s bond. But subtler than that is |Māra::the ruler of the sensual realm, often depicted as a tempter who tries to obstruct beings from the path to liberation [māra]|’s bond. By |conceiving::imagining, thinking [maññamāna]|, |bhikkhus,::::| one is bound by Māra; by not conceiving, one is freed from the Evil One.

‘I am’ is a conceiving. ‘I am this’ is a conceiving. ‘I will be’ is a conceiving. ‘I will not be’ is a conceiving. ‘I will have form’ is a conceiving. ‘I will be formless’ is a conceiving. ‘I will be percipient’ is a conceiving. ‘I will be non-percipient’ is a conceiving. ‘I will be neither percipient nor non-percipient’ is a conceiving. Conceiving|, bhikkhus,::::| is a disease, conceiving is a tumor, conceiving is a dart. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will dwell with a mind free from conceiving.’

‘I am’ is a |perturbation::agitation, disturbance, movement, turmoil [iñjita]|. ‘I am this’ is a perturbation. ‘I will be’ is a perturbation. ‘I will not be’ is a perturbation. ‘I will have form’ is a perturbation. ‘I will be formless’ is a perturbation. ‘I will be percipient’ is a perturbation. ‘I will be non-percipient’ is a perturbation. ‘I will be neither percipient nor non-percipient’ is a perturbation. Perturbation|, bhikkhus,::::| is a disease, perturbation is a tumor, perturbation is a dart. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will dwell with an |imperturbable::unshaken, undisturbed [aniñjamāna]| mind.’

‘I am’ is an |agitation::pulsation, trembling [phandita]|. ‘I am this’ is an agitation. ‘I will be’ ... ‘I will not be’ ... ‘I will have form’ ... ‘I will be formless’ ... ‘I will be percipient’ ... ‘I will be non-percipient’ ... ‘I will be neither percipient nor non-percipient’ is an agitation. Agitation|, bhikkhus,::::| is a disease, agitation is a tumor, agitation is a dart. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will dwell with an unagitated mind.’

‘I am’ is a |proliferation::process of ever-expanding ideation; lit. expanded [papañcita]|. ‘I am this’ is a proliferation. ‘I will be’ ... ‘I will not be’ ... ‘I will have form’ ... ‘I will be formless’ ... ‘I will be percipient’ ... ‘I will be non-percipient’ ... ‘I will be neither percipient nor non-percipient’ is a proliferation. Proliferation|, bhikkhus,::::| is a disease, proliferation is a tumor, proliferation is a dart. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will dwell with a mind |free from proliferation::not forming various opinions; epithet of Nibbāna [nippapañca]|.’

‘I am’ is a |conceit::self-view expressed as comparison—seeing oneself as superior, inferior, or equal; the persistent “I am” conceit (asmimāna) that underlies identification [māna]|. ‘I am this’ is a conceit. ‘I will be’ is a conceit. ‘I will not be’ is a conceit. ‘I will have form’ is a conceit. ‘I will be formless’ is a conceit. ‘I will be percipient’ is a conceit. ‘I will be non-percipient’ is a conceit. ‘I will be neither percipient nor non-percipient’ is a conceit. Conceit|, bhikkhus,::::| is a disease, conceit is a tumor, conceit is a dart. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will dwell with a mind that has struck down conceit.’”
