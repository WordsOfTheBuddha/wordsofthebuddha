At Sāvatthi.

Seated to one side, King Pasenadi of Kosala said this to the Blessed One: “Where, venerable sir, should a |gift::offering, donation, charity [dāna]| be given?”

“Wherever, great king, the mind feels inspired.”

“Where, however, venerable sir, does what is given bear great fruit?”

“Where a gift should be given, great king, is one thing, but where what is given bears great fruit is another. What is given to a |virtuous::ethical, endowed with moral conduct [sīlavant]| person, great king, bears great fruit, not so to an |unprincipled::unethical, immoral [dussīla]| person. Therefore, great king, I will question you on this matter in return. Answer as you see fit.

What do you think, great king? Suppose a war was at hand and a battle was to be engaged in. Then an |aristocrat::noble warrior, ruler caste [khattiya]| youth who is untrained, unskilled, unpracticed, and inexperienced approaches. And he’s fearful, scared, frightened, quick to flee. Would you employ such a man? Would he be of any use to you?”

“No, venerable sir, I would not employ such a man, and he would be of no use to me.”

“Suppose a brahmin youth who is untrained ... a merchant youth who is untrained ... a worker youth who is untrained, unskilled, unpracticed, and inexperienced approaches. And he’s fearful, scared, frightened, quick to flee. Would you employ such a man? Would he be of any use to you?”

“No, venerable sir, I would not employ such a man, and he would be of no use to me.”

“What do you think, great king? Suppose a war was at hand and a battle was to be engaged in. Then an aristocrat youth who is trained, skilled, practiced, and experienced approaches. And he is brave, fearless, bold, not taking flight. Would you employ such a man? Would he be of any use to you?”

“I would employ such a man, venerable sir, and he would be of use to me.”

“Suppose a brahmin youth who is trained ... a merchant youth who is trained ... a worker youth who is trained, skilled, practiced, and experienced approaches. And he is brave, fearless, bold, not taking flight. Would you employ such a man? Would he be of any use to you?”

“I would employ such a man, venerable sir, and he would be of use to me.”

“Just so, great king, from whatever family one has gone forth from the household life into homelessness, if one has abandoned five factors and is endowed with five factors, what is given to them bears great fruit.

What five factors are abandoned? |Sensual desire::interest in sensual pleasure, sensual impulse [kāmacchanda]| is abandoned, |ill will::hostile thought, malevolent intent, wishing ruin or downfall [byāpāda]| is abandoned, |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness [thinamiddha]| is abandoned, |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]| is abandoned, and |doubt::uncertainty, indecisiveness [vicikicchā]| is abandoned. These are the five factors that are abandoned.

What five factors are they endowed with? They are endowed with an |adept’s::an arahant; one beyond training who has completed the spiritual path [asekkha]| aggregate of |virtue::moral conduct, ethical behavior [sīla]|, an adept’s aggregate of |collectedness::stability of mind, unshakeable mind, samādhi [samādhi]|, an adept’s aggregate of |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]|, an adept’s aggregate of |liberation::freedom [vimutti]|, and an adept’s aggregate of |knowledge and vision of liberation::experiential understanding and insight into freedom [vimuttiñāṇadassana]|. They are endowed with these five factors.

Thus, what is given to one who has abandoned five factors and is endowed with five factors bears great fruit.”

The Blessed One said this. Having spoken thus, the Accomplished One, the Teacher, further said this:

“A king seeking battle would support
whatever youth has skill in archery,
strength, and willpower;
he would not support a coward on account of birth.

Even so, one in whom the qualities
of |patience::forbearance, endurance [khanti]| and |gentleness::kindness [soracca]| are established—
one of noble conduct, discerning—
one should honor, even if of low birth.

One should build pleasant hermitages,
and settle the very learned therein;
one should make water stations in the wilderness,
and passages in rugged terrain.

One should give to the upright
food, drink, edibles,
clothing, and lodgings—
with a |settled::calm, tranquil [vippasanna]| mind.

Just as a roaring rain cloud,
wreathed in lightning and with a hundred knots,
pours rain upon the earth,
filling the high grounds and the low;

Even so, a wise person, faithful and learned,
having prepared a meal,
satisfies wayfarers
with food and drink.

Rejoicing, he distributes gifts,
saying: ‘Give! Give!’
For that is his roar,
like the sky pouring down rain.
That vast shower of merit
pours down upon one who gives.”
