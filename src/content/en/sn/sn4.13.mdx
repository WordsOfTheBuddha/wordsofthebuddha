Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]|, in the Maddakucchi Deer Park.

Now at that time, the Blessed One’s foot had been cut by a |stone splinter::rock shard, splinter, potsherd [sakalikā]|. Severe pains assailed the Blessed One—bodily feelings that were painful, piercing, sharp, |acute::bitter, severe, racking [kaṭuka]|, |unpleasant::disagreeable, uncomfortable [asāta]|, and |disagreeable::unappealing, displeasing [amanāpa]|. Yet the Blessed One, |mindful::having active presence of mind and lucid retention; remaining naturally anchored and attentive without losing one's bearings [sata]| and |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]|, endured them |untroubled::not annoyed, not upset [avihaññamāna]|. Then the Blessed One, having folded his outer robe in four and spread it out, lay down on his right side in the lion’s posture, placing one foot overlapping the other, mindful and clearly aware.

Then |Māra::the ruler of the sensual realm, often depicted as a tempter who tries to obstruct beings from the path to liberation [māra]| the Evil One approached the Blessed One; having approached, he recited this verse in the Blessed One’s presence:

“Do you lie down in a |stupor::dullness, sluggishness; lit. stupid state [mandī]|, or |drunk on poetry::intoxicated with poetic inspiration [kāveyyamatta]|?
Are your goals not |plentiful::abundant, numerous [sampacura]| enough?
Alone in a |secluded::isolated, solitary, quiet [vivitta]| lodging,
with a |sleepy face::dozy face [niddāmukha]|, why this sleeping?”

“I do not lie down in a stupor, nor drunk on poetry;
having attained the |goal::ultimate purpose, highest good [attha]|, I am |free from sorrow::rid of grief [apetasoka]|.
Alone in a secluded lodging,
I lie down |compassionate toward all beings::sympathetic and caring toward all living beings [sabbabhūtānukampī]|.

Even those with a |dart::any sharp pointed object which causes pain, arrow, spike, dagger, barb [salla]| pierced into the breast,
striking the heart moment by moment—
even they, still with the dart, find sleep here;
so why should I not sleep, with my dart removed?

Awake I do not |doubt::worry, hesitate, lie anxious [saṅkati]|, nor do I fear to sleep;
the nights and days do not afflict me.
I see no decline anywhere in the world;
therefore I sleep, compassionate toward all beings.”

Then Māra the Evil One, thinking, “The Blessed One knows me, the Accomplished One knows me,” dejected and disappointed, disappeared right there.
