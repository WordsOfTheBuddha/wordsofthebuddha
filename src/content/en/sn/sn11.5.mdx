At Sāvatthi.

“Once in the past, |bhikkhus,::::| a battle was fought between the devas and the |asura::titan [asura]|s.

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| |Vepacitti::name of a daemon lord [vepacitti]::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, said to Sakka, lord of the devas: ‘Lord of the devas, let there be victory by |well-spoken::articulate, eloquent [subhāsita]| speech!’

‘Vepacitti, let there be victory by well-spoken speech!’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| the devas and the asuras appointed a panel of judges, saying: ‘These will decide what is well spoken and poorly spoken by us.’

Then|, bhikkhus,::::| Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, said to Sakka, lord of the devas: ‘Lord of the devas, speak a verse!’

When this was said, |bhikkhus,::::| Sakka, lord of the devas, said to Vepacitti, lord of the asuras: ‘You are the earlier deity here, Vepacitti. Speak a verse, Vepacitti!’

When this was said, |bhikkhus,::::| Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, spoke this verse:

‘Fools would only become more aggressive
if there were no one to stop them.
Therefore, with a heavy stick,
the wise one should restrain the fool.’

When that verse had been spoken by Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, the asuras applauded, while the devas remained silent.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, said to Sakka, lord of the devas: ‘Lord of the devas, speak a verse!’ When this was said, |bhikkhus,::::| Sakka, lord of the devas, spoke this verse:

‘I think this is exactly the way
to put a stop to a fool:
knowing the other is enraged,
one |mindfully::having active presence of mind and lucid retention; remaining naturally anchored and attentive without losing one's bearings [sata]| |settles themselves::calms themselves [upasammati]|.’

When that verse had been spoken by Sakka, lord of the devas, the devas applauded, while the asuras remained silent.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| Sakka, lord of the devas, said to Vepacitti, lord of the asuras: ‘Speak a verse, Vepacitti!’ When this was said, |bhikkhus,::::| Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, spoke this verse:

‘I see this very fault
in enduring, |Vāsava::epithet of Sakka; lit. of the Vasu's [vāsava]|:
when the fool thinks,
“he endures me out of fear,”
the |undiscerning one::one lacking discernment, who has not cultivated wisdom, unwise [dummedha]| gets even more aggressive,
like a cow chasing someone running away.’

When that verse had been spoken by Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, the asuras applauded, while the devas remained silent.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, said to Sakka, lord of the devas: ‘Lord of the devas, speak a verse!’ When this was said, |bhikkhus,::::| Sakka, lord of the devas, spoke these verses:

‘Let him think it or not,
“he endures me out of fear.”
Among goals that culminate in one’s true good,
nothing is found to be better than patience.

When someone who is truly strong
endures a weakling,
they call that the highest |patience::acceptance, receptivity in emotional or interpersonal contexts [khanti]|;
for a weak person must always be patient.

They call that strength weakness,
when a fool’s strength is his power.
But for the strong person guarded by the Dhamma,
no challenger can be found.

Worse off is the one
who retaliates against an angry person;
Not retaliating against anger,
one wins a battle hard to win.

They act for the benefit of both,
their own and the other’s:
who, knowing the other is enraged,
|settles themselves::calms themselves [upasammati]| with mindfulness.

Those who heal both,
themselves and the other:
are thought to be fools
by those unskilled in the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|.’

When those verses had been spoken by Sakka, lord of the devas, the devas applauded, while the asuras remained silent.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the judges of both the devas and the asuras said this: ‘The verses spoken by Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, involve punishment and weapons; thus there is conflict, contention, and strife. But the verses spoken by Sakka, lord of the devas, involve neither punishment nor weapons; thus there is non-conflict, non-contention, and harmony.

Victory by good speech belongs to Sakka, lord of the devas!’

So it was, |bhikkhus,::::| that victory by good speech belonged to Sakka, lord of the devas.”
