At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in the land of Magadhans in Andhakavinda.

At that time, the Blessed One was sitting in the open air during the pitch black darkness of night, while a gentle rain drizzled down.

Then, when the night had advanced, |Brahmā Sahampati::name of the Brahma who inspired the Buddha to teach [sahampati]|, with a radiant appearance, illuminating the entirety of Andhakavinda, approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One, stood to one side, and recited these verses in the presence of the Blessed One:

“One should frequent secluded lodgings,
practice for release from the |fetters::chains, bonds [saṁyojana]|;
but if one does not find delight there,
self-guarded and mindful, one should live amidst the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]|.

Walking for alms from family to family,
with sense faculties guarded, |prudent::sensible and careful when making judgments and decisions, discerning [nipaka]| and mindful;
one should frequent secluded lodgings,
freed from fear, liberated in the fearless.

Where fearsome crawling creatures dwell,
where lightning flashes and the sky thunders,
in the pitch black darkness of night,
there a bhikkhu sits, |unperturbed by fear::without hair standing on end [vigatalomahaṁsa]|.

For this has actually been seen by me,
it is not merely hearsay:
in this one |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]|,
a thousand have gone beyond death.

More than five hundred are |trainees::learners, those in the seven stages of a noble person before full awakening [sekha]|,
and ten times a tenfold ten,
all have |reached the stream::A stream-enterer is an individual who has attained the first stage of awakening, having completely abandoned the three lower fetters: personal identity view, doubt, and attachment to rules and rituals. A stream-enterer is assured of full awakening within seven lifetimes at most [sotāpanna]|,
never returning to the animal realm.

And as for the other people who remain—
who, to my mind, partake in merit—
I cannot even number them,
from dread of speaking falsely.”
