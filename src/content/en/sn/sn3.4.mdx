At Sāvatthi.

Seated to one side, King Pasenadi of Kosala said this to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, while I was alone in seclusion, a reflection arose in my mind: ‘Who now treat themselves as |dear::beloved [piya]|, and who treat themselves as |a foe::an enemy, an opposition; lit. unloved [appiya]|?’

Then, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘Those who engage in |bodily misconduct::killing living beings, harming others, theft, or sexual misconduct [kāyaduccarita]|, |verbal misconduct::false speech, harsh speech, divisive speech, idle chatter [vacīduccarita]|, or |mental misconduct::yearning with strong eagerness and desire, having ill will or hatred, delighting in harm [manoduccarita]| treat themselves as a foe. Even though they might say, “We regard ourselves as dear,” still, they treat themselves as a foe. For what reason? Because however a foe might act toward a foe, they act toward themselves in the same way. Therefore, they treat themselves as a foe.

But those who engage in |good bodily conduct::good physical conduct, virtuous bodily behavior [kāyasucarita]|, |good verbal conduct::applying the five factors of well-spoken speech - 1) speaking at the proper time, 2) being truthful, 3) being gentle, 4) speaking beneficially, and 5) speaking with a mind of loving-kindness [vacīsucarita]|, and |good mental conduct::being with thoughts of renunciation, non-ill will, and non-harm [manosucarita]| treat themselves as dear. Even though they might say, ‘We regard ourselves as a foe,’ still, they treat themselves as dear. For what reason? Because however a dear person might act toward a dear person, they act toward themselves in the same way. Therefore, they treat themselves as dear.”

“So it is, great king! So it is, great king! Those who engage in bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, or mental misconduct treat themselves as a foe. Even though they might say, ‘We regard ourselves as dear,’ still, they treat themselves as a foe. For what reason? Because however a foe might act toward a foe, they act toward themselves in the same way. Therefore, they treat themselves as a foe.

But those who engage in good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, and good mental conduct treat themselves as dear. Even though they might say, ‘We regard ourselves as a foe,’ still, they treat themselves as dear. For what reason? Because however a dear person might act toward a dear person, they act toward themselves in the same way. Therefore, they treat themselves as dear.”

The Blessed One said this. Having spoken thus, the Accomplished One further said this:

“If one regards oneself as dear,
one should not yoke oneself to wrondoing;
for happiness is not easily gained
by one who does wrong.

For one |overpowered by death::assailed by death [antakenādhipanna]|,
leaving human existence behind,
what is truly their own?
What do they take when they depart?
What follows along behind them,
like a shadow that never departs?

Both the good deed and the misdeed,
whatever a mortal does here:
that is what is truly theirs,
that is what they take when they depart,
that is what follows along behind them,
like a shadow that never departs.

Therefore, one should do |good::what is beneficial, wholesome, skillful, meritorious [kalyāṇa]|
as a provision for the next life;
for deeds of merit are the support for beings
[when they arise] in the next world.”
