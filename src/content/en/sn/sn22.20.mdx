---
title: Sahetuanatta sutta - With A Not-self Cause
description: The causes, supporting conditions for the arising of the five aggregates are not-self, so then how could the five aggregates be self?
qualities: form, feeling, perception, intentional constructs, consciousness, disenchantment, recognition of not-self
theme: wisdom, principle
slug: sn22.20
priority: 1.1
---

At Sāvatthi.

“|Form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]||, bhikkhus,::::| is |not-self::not suitable to identify with, impersonality, non-subjectivity, essencelessness [anatta]|. Whatever cause, whatever supporting condition there is for the arising of form, that is also not-self. Arising from what is not-self, |bhikkhus,::::| how could form be |self::soul, spirit, essence, personal, related to oneself [atta]|?

|Feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| is not-self. Whatever cause, whatever supporting condition there is for the arising of feeling, that is also not-self. Arising from what is not-self, |bhikkhus,::::| how could feeling be self?

|Perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [sañña]| is not-self. Whatever cause, whatever supporting condition there is for the arising of perception, that is also not-self. Arising from what is not-self, |bhikkhus,::::| how could perception be self?

|Intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]| are not-self. Whatever cause, whatever supporting condition there is for the arising of intentional constructs, that is also not-self. Arising from what is not-self, |bhikkhus,::::| how could intentional constructs be self?

|Consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object; fifth of the five aggregates [viññāṇa]| is not-self. Whatever cause, whatever supporting condition there is for the arising of consciousness, that is also not-self. Arising from what is not-self, |bhikkhus,::::| how could consciousness be self?

Seeing thus, |bhikkhus,::::| the learned disciple of the Noble Ones becomes |disenchanted with::disillusioned with [nibbindati]| form, becomes disenchanted with feeling, becomes disenchanted with perception, becomes disenchanted with intentional constructs, becomes disenchanted with consciousness. Experiencing disenchantment, they become |dispassionate::detached [virajjati]|; through dispassion, there is release. When released, there arises the knowledge: ‘Released.’

He knows clearly: ‘Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.’”
