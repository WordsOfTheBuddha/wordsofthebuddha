At one time, the venerable Musīla, the venerable Paviṭṭha, the venerable Nārada, and the venerable Ānanda were dwelling at |Kosambi::name of a town; capital city of the Vatsas [kosambī]| in Ghosita’s Park. Then the venerable Paviṭṭha said to the venerable Musīla:

“Apart from |faith::confidence, conviction, trust [saddha]|, friend Musīla, apart from |inclination::liking, preference, choice, approval [ruci]|, apart from |oral tradition::what one has heard, hearsay [anussava]|, apart from |mode of reasoning::way of examination [ākāraparivitakka]|, apart from |approving a view after consideration::acceptance of a view after understanding it [diṭṭhinijjhānakkhanti]|, does the venerable Musīla have personal knowledge: ‘With birth as condition, aging and death come to be’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With birth as condition, aging and death come to be.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Musīla, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Musīla have personal knowledge: ‘With existence as condition, birth comes to be ... with clinging as condition, existence comes to be ... with craving as condition, clinging comes to be ... with felt experience as condition, craving comes to be ... with contact as condition, felt experience comes to be ... with the six sense bases as condition, contact comes to be ... with name and form as condition, the six sense bases come to be ... with consciousness as condition, name and form come to be ... with intentional constructs as condition, consciousness comes to be ... with ignorance as condition, intentional constructs come to be’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With ignorance as condition, intentional constructs come to be.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Musīla, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Musīla have personal knowledge: ‘With the cessation of birth, aging and death cease’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With the cessation of birth, aging and death cease.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Musīla, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Musīla have personal knowledge: ‘With the cessation of existence, birth ceases ... with the cessation of clinging, existence ceases ... with the cessation of craving, clinging ceases ... with the cessation of felt experience, craving ceases ... with the cessation of contact, felt experience ceases ... with the cessation of the six sense bases, contact ceases ... with the cessation of name and form, the six sense bases cease ... with the cessation of consciousness, name and form cease ... with the cessation of intentional constructs, consciousness ceases ... with the cessation of ignorance, intentional constructs cease’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With the cessation of ignorance, intentional constructs cease.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Musīla, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Musīla have personal knowledge: ‘The cessation of existence is |Nibbāna::complete cooling, letting go of everything, deathless, freedom from calamity, the non-disintegrating; lit. blowing away [nibbāna]|’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘The cessation of existence is Nibbāna.’”

“Then the venerable Musīla is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, |whose taints have worn away::who is awakened through the complete exhaustion of the taints of sensuality, becoming, and ignorance; an epithet for an Arahant [khīṇāsava]|?” When this was said, the venerable Musīla remained silent.

Then the venerable Nārada said to the venerable Paviṭṭha: “It would be good, friend Paviṭṭha, if I were to receive that question. Ask me that question, and I will answer it for you.”

“Let the venerable Nārada receive that question. I will ask the venerable Nārada that question, and may the venerable Nārada answer it for me.

Apart from faith, friend Nārada, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Nārada have personal knowledge: ‘With birth as condition, aging and death come to be’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With birth as condition, aging and death come to be.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Nārada, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Nārada have personal knowledge: ‘With existence as condition, birth comes to be ... with ignorance as condition, intentional constructs come to be’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With ignorance as condition, intentional constructs come to be.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Nārada, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Nārada have personal knowledge: ‘With the cessation of birth, aging and death cease’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With the cessation of birth, aging and death cease.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Nārada, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Nārada have personal knowledge: ‘With the cessation of existence, birth ceases ... with the cessation of ignorance, intentional constructs cease’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘With the cessation of ignorance, intentional constructs cease.’”

“Apart from faith, friend Nārada, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, does the venerable Nārada have personal knowledge: ‘The cessation of existence is Nibbāna’?”

“Apart from faith, friend Paviṭṭha, apart from inclination, apart from oral tradition, apart from mode of reasoning, apart from approving a view after consideration, I know this, I see this: ‘The cessation of existence is Nibbāna.’”

“Then the venerable Nārada is an Arahant, whose taints have worn away?”

“‘The cessation of existence is Nibbāna’ is properly seen by me as it truly is with right wisdom, yet I am not an Arahant whose taints have worn away. Suppose friend, there was a well on a desert road, and it had neither a rope nor a bucket. Then a person would come along, scorched and exhausted by the heat, weary, thirsty, and parched, and he would look down into that well. He would have the knowledge, ‘There is water,’ but he would not be able to make bodily contact with it.

In the same way, friend, that ‘The cessation of existence is Nibbāna’ is properly seen by me as it truly is with right wisdom, yet I am not an Arahant whose taints have worn away.”

When this was said, the venerable Ānanda said to the venerable Paviṭṭha: “When he speaks in this way, friend Paviṭṭha, what do you have to say to the venerable Nārada?”

“When the venerable Nārada speaks in this way, friend Ānanda, I have nothing to say to him except what is good, except what is wholesome.”
