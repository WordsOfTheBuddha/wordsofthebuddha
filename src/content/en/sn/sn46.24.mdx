---
title: Ayonisomanasikāra sutta - Attending Superficially
description: Attending superficially causes the five hindrances to expand and the awakening factors to cease. Attending radically leads to the abandoning of the five hindrances and the awakening factors to reach fulfillment.
qualities: superficial attention, radical attention, sensual desire, ill will, dullness, drowsiness, anxiety, doubt, mindfulness, equanimity
theme: wisdom, cultivating discernment
slug: sn46.24
priority: 1.2
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| for one |attending superficially::attending unwisely, attending peripherally, content-bound reflection; lit. attention not from the source [ayonisomanasikāra]|, unarisen |sensual desire::interest in sensual pleasure, sensual impulse [kāmacchanda]| arises, and arisen sensual desire increases and expands; unarisen |ill will::intentional act of mentally opposing or rejecting others; an intentional construct fueled by aversion, directed against kindness or compassion. It manifests as hostility of will, impeding goodwill and fostering internal or external conflict. [byāpāda]| arises, and arisen ill will increases and expands; unarisen |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness lit. stiffness (of mind/body due to tiredness) [thinamiddha]| arises, and arisen dullness and drowsiness increases and expands; unarisen |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]| arises, and arisen restlessness and worry increases and expands; unarisen |doubt::uncertainty, indecision, lack of conviction [vicikicchā]| arises, and arisen doubt increases and expands.

The unarisen |awakening factor of mindfulness::the quality of mindfulness leading to full awakening, remembering as a factor of enlightenment; first of the seven awakening factors [satisambojjhaṅga]| does not arise, and the arisen awakening factor of mindfulness ceases ... the unarisen |awakening factor of equanimity::the quality of balanced observation, non-reactivity, and mental equipoise as a factor of enlightenment; seventh of the seven awakening factors [upekkhāsambojjhaṅga]| does not arise, and the arisen awakening factor of equanimity ceases.

But|, bhikkhus,::::| for one |attending radically::wisely attending, attending to the structural source, contextually reflecting; lit. attending from the source [yoniso + manasi + karoti]|, unarisen sensual desire does not arise, and arisen sensual desire is abandoned; unarisen ill will does not arise, and arisen ill will is abandoned; unarisen dullness and drowsiness does not arise, and arisen dullness and drowsiness is abandoned; unarisen restlessness and worry does not arise, and arisen restlessness and worry is abandoned; unarisen doubt does not arise, and arisen doubt is abandoned.

The unarisen awakening factor of mindfulness arises, and the arisen awakening factor of mindfulness reaches fulfillment through cultivation ... the unarisen awakening factor of equanimity arises, and the arisen awakening factor of equanimity reaches fulfillment through cultivation.”
