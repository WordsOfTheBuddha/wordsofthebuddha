At Sāvatthi.

Then the venerable |Nanda::the Buddha’s half-brother and cousin, foremost disciple in guarding the doors of the sense faculties; read his story in [Ud 3.2 - Nanda sutta](/ud3.2) [nanda]|, the Blessed One’s |cousin on his mother’s side::son of Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, the Buddha’s maternal aunt and foster mother; lit. mother’s sister’s son [mātucchāputta]|, having put on robes that were |finely pressed and ironed::beaten and smoothed on both sides [ākoṭitapaccākoṭitāni]|, having applied eyeshadow, and carrying a glazed bowl, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. When the venerable Nanda was seated to one side, the Blessed One said to him:

“Nanda, it is not proper for you, a clansman who has gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness, that you should put on robes that are finely pressed and ironed, apply eyeshadow, and carry a glazed bowl. As a clansman who has gone forth out of faith from the household life into homelessness, Nanda, this is what is proper for you: that you should be a forest dweller, an |alms gatherer::one who subsists solely on alms food collected on alms round [piṇḍapātika]|, and a |wearer of rag robes::one who wears robes made from discarded cloth [paṁsukūlika]|, and that you should dwell |indifferent to::disinterested in, unconcerned about [anapekkha]| |sensual pleasures::sense desires, wishing, wanting, delighting in [kāma]|.”

The Blessed One said this. Having spoken thus, the Accomplished One, the Teacher, further said this:

“When shall I see Nanda,
a forest dweller, wearing rag robes,
|subsisting on gleanings from strangers::living on alms gathered from unacquainted households [aññātuñchena yāpentaṁ]|,
indifferent to sensual pleasures?”

Then, at a later time, the venerable Nanda became a forest dweller, an alms gatherer, and a wearer of rag robes, and he dwelled indifferent to sensual pleasures.
