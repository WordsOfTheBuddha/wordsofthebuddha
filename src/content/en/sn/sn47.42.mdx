---
title: Samudaya sutta - Arising
description: "The Buddha teaches the arising and passing away of the four establishments of mindfulness: the body with nutriment, felt experience with contact, the mind with name and form, and mental qualities with the steering of attention."
qualities: mindfulness, arising and passing away
theme: wisdom, principle
slug: sn47.42
related: sn47.40, sn22.56, an10.58
---

“I will teach you|, bhikkhus,::::| the |arising::appearance, origination [samudaya]| and the |passing away::disappearance, vanishing, settling down [atthaṅgama]| of the |four establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as the direct path of practice [cattāro + satipaṭṭhāna]|. Listen to this.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the arising of the body? With the arising of |sustenance::fuel, nourishment [āhāra]| comes the arising of the body; with the |cessation::ending, termination [nirodha]| of sustenance comes the passing away of the body.

With the arising of |contact::sense impingement, raw experience, touch [phassa]| comes the arising of |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]|; with the cessation of contact comes the passing away of felt experience.

With the arising of |name and form::mentality and materiality—the integrated structure of mental capacities (intention, attention, contact, feeling, perception) and physical form that together constitute and sustain an individual being [nāmarūpa]| comes the arising of the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]|; with the cessation of name and form comes the passing away of the mind.

With the arising of |attention::bringing-to-mind, focusing; lit. making in mind [manasikāra]| comes the arising of |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]|; with the cessation of attention comes the passing away of mental qualities.”
