At Sāvatthi.

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will explain to you the arising of |anxiety through clinging::agitation through grasping, turmoil by holding on, nervousness arising from attachment [upādāparitassana]| and the freedom from anxiety through non-clinging. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.

|Bhikkhus,::::| how does anxiety due to clinging arise? Here|, bhikkhus,::::,| an |uninstructed::uninitiated, untaught, untrained [assutavant]| ordinary person perceives |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]| as ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self.’ That form then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of form, there arises sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair. This person perceives |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| as ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self.’ That feeling then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of feeling, there arises sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair. This person perceives |perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [saññā]| as ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self.’ <collapse>That perception then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of perception, there arises sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair.</collapse> This person perceives |intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]| as ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self.’ <collapse>Those intentional constructs then change and become otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of intentional constructs, there arises sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair.</collapse> This person perceives |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object; fifth of the five aggregates [viññāṇa]| as ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self.’ That consciousness then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of consciousness, there arises sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair. This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| anxiety arises through clinging.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| is there freedom from anxiety through non-clinging? Here|, bhikkhus,::::,| a learned disciple of the Noble Ones perceives form as ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ That form then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of form, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair do not arise. They perceive feeling as ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ That feeling then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of feeling, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair do not arise. They perceive perception as ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ <collapse>That perception then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of perception, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair do not arise.</collapse> They perceive intentional constructs as ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ <collapse>Those intentional constructs then change and become otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of intentional constructs, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair do not arise.</collapse> They perceive consciousness as ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ That consciousness then changes and becomes otherwise. With the change and becoming otherwise of consciousness, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair do not arise. This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| there is freedom from anxiety through non-clinging.”
