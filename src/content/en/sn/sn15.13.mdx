At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]|, in the Bamboo Grove. Then, about thirty bhikkhus from Pāvā—all forest dwellers, all eaters of |alms food::::almsfood|, all wearers of rag-robes, all triple-robe wearers, all still with fetters—went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, they sat down to one side.

Then it occurred to the Blessed One: “These thirty bhikkhus from Pāvā are all forest dwellers, all eaters of |alms food::::almsfood|, all wearers of rag-robes, all triple-robe wearers, and all still with fetters. What if I were to teach them the Dhamma in such a way that while sitting on these very seats, their minds would be liberated from the taints through not clinging?”

Then the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus.”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“|Bhikkhus,::::| this |cyclic existence::wandering from one state of existence to another, the cycle of birth and death, moving on continuously [saṁsāra]| has an inconceivable beginning. A first point is not evident of beings who, obstructed by |ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]| and fettered by |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]|, run on and wander in this cycle of existence.

What do you think, |bhikkhus,::::| which is greater: the blood that has flowed and poured forth from you as your heads were severed while running on and wandering through this long course, or the water in the four great oceans?”

“As we understand the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One, venerable sir, this alone is greater: the blood that has flowed and poured forth from us as our heads were severed while running on and wandering through this long course, not the water in the four great oceans.”

“Good, good, bhikkhus, it is good that you understand the Dhamma taught by me in this way. This alone|, |bhikkhus,::::| is greater: the blood that has flowed and poured forth from you as your heads were severed while running on and wandering through this long course, not the water in the four great oceans. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have been cows, and as your heads were severed while being cows, the blood that flowed and poured forth from you was more than this, not the water in the four great oceans. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have been buffaloes, and as your heads were severed while being buffaloes, the blood that flowed and poured forth from you was more than this ... for a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have been sheep ... goats ... deer ... chickens ... pigs ... For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you were seized as bandits raiding villages, and as your heads were severed, the blood that flowed and poured forth was more than this. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you were seized as highway robbers, and as your heads were severed, the blood that flowed and poured forth was more than this. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you were seized as adulterers, and as your heads were severed, the blood that flowed and poured forth was more than this, not the water in the four great oceans.

For what reason? |Bhikkhus,::::| this cyclic existence has an inconceivable beginning. <collapse>A first point is not evident of beings who, obstructed by ignorance and fettered by craving, run on and wander in this cycle of existence. For such a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have experienced |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|, |agony::intense suffering, anguish [tibba]|, and |calamity::disaster [byasana]|, the |cemetery::charnel ground [kaṭasī]| has been filled up with your bones.</collapse> Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| it is enough |to become disenchanted::to become disillusioned [nibbindituṃ]|, |to become detached::to become dispassionate [virajjituṁ]| and |to become free from::to be liberated from [vimuccituṃ]| |all conditions::all formations, all activities, all fabrications [sabbasaṅkhāra]|.”

The Blessed One said this. The bhikkhus were delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words. As this discourse was being spoken, the minds of the thirty bhikkhus from Pāvā were liberated from the taints through not clinging.
