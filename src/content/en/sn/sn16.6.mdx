At |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]| in the |Bamboo Grove::Veluvana, a park near Rajagaha gifted to the Sangha by King Bimbisara [veḷuvana]|. Then the venerable |Mahākassapa::foremost disciple of the Buddha in ascetic practices [mahākassapa]| approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Blessed One said this to the venerable Mahākassapa:

“Admonish the bhikkhus, Kassapa; give the bhikkhus a talk on the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, Kassapa. Either I should admonish the bhikkhus, Kassapa, or you should; either I should give the bhikkhus a talk on the Dhamma, or you should.”

“Venerable sir, the bhikkhus at present are |difficult to admonish::hard to correct, obstinate [dubbaca]|, endowed with qualities that make them difficult to admonish, impatient, and do not receive instruction respectfully.

Here, venerable sir, I saw a bhikkhu named Bhaṇḍa, a co-resident of |Ānanda::foremost disciple of the Buddha in learning and personal attendant [ānanda]|, and a bhikkhu named Abhijika, a co-resident of |Anuruddha::foremost disciple of the Buddha in the divine eye [anuruddha]|, challenging each other in their learning: ‘Come, bhikkhu, who will speak more? Who will speak better? Who will speak longer?’”

Then the Blessed One addressed a certain bhikkhu: “Come, bhikkhu, in my name, call the bhikkhu Bhaṇḍa, the co-resident of Ānanda, and the bhikkhu Abhijika, the co-resident of Anuruddha, saying: ‘The Teacher calls the venerable ones.’”

“Yes, venerable sir,” that bhikkhu replied to the Blessed One. He approached where those bhikkhus were; having approached, he said to those bhikkhus: “The Teacher calls the venerable ones.”

“Yes, friend,” those bhikkhus replied to that bhikkhu. Having replied to that bhikkhu, they went to the Blessed One. Having approached the Blessed One, they paid homage and sat down to one side. When they were seated to one side, the Blessed One said this to those bhikkhus:

“Is it true, bhikkhus, that you challenge each other in your learning: ‘Come, bhikkhu, who will speak more? Who will speak better? Who will speak longer?’”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Bhikkhus, have you known me to teach the Dhamma in such a way: ‘Come, bhikkhus, challenge each other in your learning: “Come, bhikkhu, who will speak more? Who will speak better? Who will speak longer?”’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“If you have not known me to teach the Dhamma in such a way, bhikkhus, then knowing what and seeing what, |misguided men::vain persons [moghapurisā]|, having gone forth in such a well-proclaimed Dhamma and |Vinaya::code of monastic discipline rules, training [vinaya]|, do you challenge each other in your learning: ‘Come, bhikkhu, who will speak more? Who will speak better? Who will speak longer?’”

Then those bhikkhus bowed down with their heads at the Blessed One’s feet and said to the Blessed One: “A |transgression::fault, moral lapse [accaya]| overcame us, venerable sir, |foolish::immature, lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]|, |deluded::confused, disoriented [mūḷha]|, and unskillful as we were, in that, having gone forth in such a well-proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, we challenged each other in our learning: ‘Come, bhikkhu, who will speak more? Who will speak better? Who will speak longer?’ Venerable sir, may the Blessed One accept our transgression as a transgression, for the sake of |restraint in the future::future discipline and guarding [āyatiṁ saṁvara]|.”

“Indeed, bhikkhus, a transgression overcame you, foolish, deluded, and unskillful as you were, in that, having gone forth in such a well-proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, you challenged each other in your learning: ‘Come, bhikkhu, who will speak more? Who will speak better? Who will speak longer?’ But since you, bhikkhus, see your transgression as a transgression and make amends in accordance with the Dhamma, we accept it from you. For it is growth in the Vinaya of the Noble Ones, bhikkhus, when one sees one’s transgression as a transgression, makes amends in accordance with the Dhamma, and undertakes restraint in the future.”
