---
title: Karoto sutta - Acts
description: The view ‘There is no evil or outcome of evil, no merit or outcome of merit’ itself arises from clinging to the five aggregates. Recognizing any arising clinging to these and seeing the aggregates as impermanent, unsatisfactory, and subject to change leads to stream-entry.
qualities: speculation, wrong view, stream entry, recognition of impermanence, doubt, wisdom
theme: wisdom, cultivating discernment
slug: sn24.6
---

At Sāvatthi.

“When what exists, |bhikkhus,::::| by |clinging to::grasping (at), acquiring, appropriating, taking possession (of), identifying (with) [upādāya]| what, by |fixating::adhering, doubling down; lit. completely entering down [abhinivissa]| on what, does such a view arise: ‘By one who acts or causes another to act, who cuts or causes another to cut, who burns or causes another to burn, who grieves or causes another to grieve, who tortures or causes another to torture, who inflicts distress or causes another to inflict distress, who kills living beings, who takes what is not given, who breaks into houses, who plunders, who robs isolated houses, who waits in ambush on highways, who commits adultery with another’s wife, who speaks falsehood—by one who acts thus, no evil is done. Even if with a razor-edged disc one were to reduce all the living beings on this earth to a single mass of flesh, a single heap of flesh, there would be no evil from that source, no outcome of evil. Even if one were to go along the south bank of the Ganges killing and slaughtering, cutting and causing to cut, burning and causing to burn, there would be no evil from that source, no outcome of evil. Even if one were to go along the north bank of the Ganges giving and causing to give, sacrificing and causing to sacrifice, there would be no merit from that source, no outcome of merit. Through |giving::the act of giving or donating, with an intention to give [dāna]|, |taming::discipline [dama]|, |self-control::holistic ethical discipline, comprehensive restraint [saṁyama]|, and truthful speech, there is no merit, no outcome of merit’?”

“Venerable sir, our teachings are rooted in the Blessed One<collapse>, guided by the Blessed One, and take refuge in the Blessed One. It would be good if the Blessed One would explain the meaning of this statement. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will remember it.</collapse>”

“When |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]| exists, |bhikkhus,::::| by clinging to form, by fixating on form, such a view arises: ‘By one who acts or causes another to act... there is no merit, no outcome of merit.’ When |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling, second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| exists... When |perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [saññā]| exists... When |intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]| exist... When |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]| exists, by clinging to consciousness, by fixating on consciousness, such a view arises: ‘By one who acts or causes another to act... there is no merit, no outcome of merit.’”

“What do you think, |bhikkhus,::::| is form |permanent::stable, not in flux [nicca]| or |impermanent::not lasting, transient, unreliable [anicca]|?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But without clinging to that, could such a view arise: ‘By one who acts... there is no merit, no outcome of merit’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Is feeling permanent or impermanent?... Is perception permanent or impermanent?... Are intentional constructs permanent or impermanent?... Is consciousness permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But without clinging to that, could such a view arise: ‘By one who acts or causes another to act... there is no merit, no outcome of merit’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“That which is seen, heard, |sensed::smelled, tasted, felt, or thought [muta]|, cognized, attained, sought after, and pondered over by the mind—is that permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But without clinging to that, could such a view arise: ‘By one who acts or causes another to act... there is no merit, no outcome of merit’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“When|, bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones has abandoned |doubt::uncertainty, perplexity, hesitation—especially in relation to faith, truth, or decision-making on the path [kaṅkhā]| in these cases, and has abandoned doubt about |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|, has abandoned doubt about the arising of suffering, has abandoned doubt about the cessation of suffering, and has abandoned doubt about the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering—this disciple of the Noble Ones|, bhikkhus,::::| is called a |stream-enterer::A stream-enterer is an individual who has attained the first stage of awakening, having completely abandoned the three fetters: 1) personal existence view - identity view, belief in a self, 2) doubt or perplexity regarding suffering, its arising, its ending, and the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering, and 3) adherence to rules and observances as a means of liberation [sotāpanna]|, not liable to states of suffering, fixed in destiny, |with full awakening as his destination::culminating in enlightenment [sambodhiparāyaṇa]|.”
