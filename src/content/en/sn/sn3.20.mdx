Then King Pasenadi of Kosala approached the Blessed One in the middle of the day; having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. When King Pasenadi of Kosala was seated to one side, the Blessed One said this to him: “Now where are you coming from in the middle of the day, great king?”

“Here in Sāvatthi, venerable sir, a wealthy householder has passed away. Since he died childless, I have come after transferring his fortune to the royal treasury. There was ten million in gold alone, venerable sir, not to mention the silver. And yet, venerable sir, such was the food that this wealthy householder used: he ate meals of sour gruel with sour pickle. Such were the clothes he wore: he wore garments of hemp stitched together in three parts. Such was the vehicle he used: he traveled in a worn out carriage, shaded by a leaf parasol.”

“So it is, great king! So it is, great king! Once in the past, great king, that wealthy householder provided the Paccekabuddha named |Tagarasikhī::name of a privately awakened Buddha; lit. jasmine crest [tagarasikhī]| with |alms food::::almsfood|. Having said, ‘Give |alms food::::almsfood| to the ascetic,’ he rose from his seat and departed. But after giving, he |felt remorse:: lit. remembering back negatively [vippaṭisārī]|: ‘It would have been better if the bondservants or laborers had eaten this |alms food::::almsfood|.’ Furthermore, he deprived his brother’s only son of life for the sake of his fortune.

Because that wealthy householder, great king, provided the Paccekabuddha Tagarasikhī with |alms food::::almsfood|, as a result of that kamma he was reborn seven times in a good destination, in a heavenly world. Through the residue of that same kamma’s result, he held the position of financier seven times right here in Sāvatthi. Because that wealthy householder, after giving, felt remorse: ‘It would have been better if the bondservants or laborers had eaten this |alms food::::almsfood|,’ as a result of that kamma his mind did not incline toward the enjoyment of fine food, his mind did not incline toward the enjoyment of fine clothes, his mind did not incline toward the enjoyment of fine vehicles, his mind did not incline toward the enjoyment of the exquisite |five cords of sensual pleasure::the pleasant objects of the five physical senses [pañca kāmaguṇa]|. And because that wealthy householder deprived his brother’s only son of life for the sake of his fortune, as a result of that kamma he boiled in hell for many years, many hundreds of years, many thousands of years, many hundreds of thousands of years. Through the residue of that same kamma’s result, for this seventh time his childless fortune enters the royal treasury. Now, great king, the past merit of that wealthy householder has been exhausted, and new merit has not been accumulated. Today, great king, that wealthy householder boils in the |Great Roruva Hell::the Great Hell of Screams, a severe hell realm where beings wail from intense suffering [mahāroruva]|.”

“Venerable sir, has that wealthy householder truly been reborn in the Great Roruva Hell?”

“So it is, great king, that wealthy householder has been reborn in the Great Roruva Hell.”

The Blessed One said this. Having spoken thus, the Accomplished One further said this:

“Grain, wealth, silver, and gold,
or whatever other possessions there are;
bondservants, laborers, servants,
and those dependent on him for their livelihood:

All must be left behind when going,
all must be abandoned;
but whatever one does with body,
with speech, or with mind—

That is what is truly theirs,
that is what they take when they go;
that is what follows them,
like a shadow that never departs.

Therefore, one should do |good::what is beneficial, wholesome, skillful, meritorious [kalyāṇa]|
as a provision for the next life;
for deeds of merit are the support for beings
[when they arise] in the next world.”
