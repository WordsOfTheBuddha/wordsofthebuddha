---
title: Dutiya kusala sutta - Wholesome (Second)
description: The Buddha teaches that all wholesome qualities are rooted in radical attention. Supported by this radical attention, a practitioner is expected to naturally develop and cultivate the seven awakening factors, maturing in relinquishment.
qualities: radical attention, wholesome, cultivation, giving up
theme: wisdom, inspiration
slug: sn46.32
priority: 1.1
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| whatever qualities are wholesome, partake in the wholesome, and are on the side of the wholesome, all of them are rooted in |radical attention::wise steering of attention, root-level attention, attention to the structural source, contextual reflection; lit. attention from the source [yoniso + manasikāra]| and converge in radical attention; radical attention is declared the foremost of those qualities. For a bhikkhu accomplished in radical attention, |bhikkhus,::::| this is to be expected: they will cultivate the seven awakening factors, they will frequently practice the seven awakening factors.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does a bhikkhu accomplished in radical attention cultivate the seven awakening factors, frequently practice the seven awakening factors? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu cultivates the |awakening factor of mindfulness::the quality of mindfulness leading to full awakening, remembering as a factor of enlightenment; first of the seven awakening factors [satisambojjhaṅga]|, which is |supported by seclusion::dependent on detachment, by means of disengagement [vivekanissita]|<collapse>, supported by dispassion, supported by cessation, and culminating in complete relinquishment. He cultivates the |awakening factor of investigation of states::the quality of discriminative investigation into states, mental qualities and other phenomena; analyzing the teaching as a factor of enlightenment; second of the seven awakening factors [dhammavicayasambojjhaṅga]|, the |awakening factor of energy::the quality of unrelenting effort, the sustaining power of persistence as a factor of enlightenment; third of the seven awakening factors [vīriyasambojjhaṅga]|, the |awakening factor of joy::the quality of heartfelt gladness, spiritual rapture untainted by sensuality, as a factor of enlightenment; fourth of the seven awakening factors [pītisambojjhaṅga]|, the |awakening factor of tranquility::the quality of inner stillness, bodily and mental calm, as a factor of enlightenment; fifth of the seven awakening factors [passaddhisambojjhaṅga]|, the |awakening factor of collectedness::the quality of unification, stability, and deep stillness of the mind, as a factor of enlightenment; sixth of the seven awakening factors [samādhisambojjhaṅga]|,</collapse> and the |awakening factor of equanimity::the quality of balanced observation, non-reactivity, and mental equipoise as a factor of enlightenment; seventh of the seven awakening factors [upekkhāsambojjhaṅga]|, which is supported by seclusion, |supported by dispassion::based on fading of desire [virāganissita]|, |supported by cessation::based on ending [nirodhanissita]|, and |culminating in complete relinquishment::ripens in release, culminating in letting go [vossaggapariṇāmī]|. It is in this way|, bhikkhus,::::| that a bhikkhu accomplished in radical attention cultivates and frequently practices the seven factors of awakening.”
