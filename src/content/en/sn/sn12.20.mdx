At Sāvatthi.

“I will teach you|, bhikkhus,::::| about |dependent co-arising::arising together from a cause, chain of causation, dependent origination [paṭiccasamuppāda]| and dependently arisen phenomena. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

#### Dependent Co-arising

“And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is dependent co-arising? With birth as condition, |bhikkhus,::::| aging and death come to be. Whether or not there is the arising of Tathāgatas, this principle remains—this stability of the Dhamma, this invariance of the Dhamma, this |actuality of dependence::general law of conditionality [idappaccayatā]|. This, a Tathāgata |awakens to::realises, discovers [abhisambujjhati]| and |breaks through to::fully understands [abhisameti]|. Having fully awakened to and broken through to it, he points it out, teaches it, declares it, establishes it, clarifies it, analyzes it, and reveals it. And he says: ‘See, |bhikkhus,::::| with birth as condition, aging and death come to be.’

With existence as condition, there is birth ... with clinging as condition, there is existence ... with craving as condition, there is clinging ... with felt experience as condition, there is craving ... with contact as condition, there is felt experience ... with the six sense bases as condition, there is contact ... with name and form as condition, there are the six sense bases ... with consciousness as condition, there is name and form ... with intentional constructs as condition, there is consciousness ... with ignorance as condition, there are intentional constructs. Whether or not there is the arising of Tathāgatas, this principle remains—this stability of the Dhamma, this invariance of the Dhamma, this actuality of dependence. This, a Tathāgata awakens to and breaks through to. Having fully awakened to and broken through to it, he points it out, teaches it, declares it, establishes it, clarifies it, analyzes it, and reveals it. And he says: ‘See, |bhikkhus,::::| with ignorance as condition, there are intentional constructs.’ Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| the |actuality::reality, suchness [tathatā]| in this, the invariability, the |not-otherwiseness::absolute certainty [anaññathatā]|, this actuality of dependence—this is called dependent co-arising.

#### Dependently Arisen Phenomena

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| are the |dependently arisen::causally produced, arisen together from a cause [paṭiccasamuppanna]| phenomena? Aging and death|, bhikkhus,::::| is |impermanent::unstable, transient, unreliable [anicca]|, |conditioned::constructed, created, fabricated [saṅkhata]|, dependently arisen, subject to |wearing away::exhaustion, depletion, gradual destruction [khaya]|, |subject to going-away::of a nature to pass away [vayadhamma]|, subject to fading away and subject to |cessation::ending, termination [nirodha]|.

|Birth::rebirth, conception, coming into existence [jāti]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Existence::being, becoming; continued conditional existence, the karmically conditioned mode of being that leads to future rebirth [bhava]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Clinging::grasping, acquiring, appropriating, taking possession, identifying; fuel for fire; lit. taking near [upādāna]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling, second of the five aggregates [vedanā]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Contact::sense impingement, raw experience, touch [phassa]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

The |six sense bases::The six internal sense bases—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind—are the faculties that enable sensory contact and experience. They are not the physical organs themselves, but the functional conditions that enable consciousness to meet an object [saḷāyatana]||, bhikkhus,::::| are impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Name and form::The mutually dependent pair of mental designation and material perception: "Name" consists of feeling, perception, intention, contact, and attention; "Form" consists of the four great primaries (earth, water, fire, wind) and derived form, experienced as perception of form through sensory resistance [nāmarūpa]||, bhikkhus,::::| are impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Intentional constructs::intentions, decisions, choices, volitional activities, constructed experiences, production of kamma [saṅkhāra]||, bhikkhus,::::| are impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

|Ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]||, bhikkhus,::::| is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to going-away, subject to fading away and subject to cessation.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are called the dependently arisen phenomena.

when|, bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones has thoroughly seen |with right wisdom::with correct distinctive knowledge [sammappaññāya]| |as it truly is::as it has come to be, as it is in actuality [yathābhūta]| this dependent co-arising and these dependently arisen phenomena, it is impossible that he would run back to the past, thinking, ‘Was I in the past? Was I not in the past? What was I in the past? How was I in the past? Having been what, what was I in the past?’ or that he would run forward to the future, thinking, ‘Will I be in the future? Will I not be in the future? What will I be in the future? How will I be in the future? Having been what, what will I be in the future?’ or that he would now be inwardly |doubtful::perplexed [kathaṅkathī]| about the present time, thinking, ‘Am I? Am I not? What am I? How am I? Where has this being come from, and where will it go?’—that is impossible.

For what reason? Because|, bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones has thoroughly seen with right wisdom, as it truly is, ‘This is dependent co-arising, and these are the dependently arisen phenomena.’”
