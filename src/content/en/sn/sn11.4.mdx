At Sāvatthi.

“Once in the past, |bhikkhus,::::| a battle was fought between the devas and the |asura::titan [asura]|s.

Then, |bhikkhus,::::| |Vepacitti::name of a daemon lord [vepacitti]::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, addressed the asuras: ‘Good sirs, if in the ensuing battle between the devas and the asuras, the asuras win and the devas are defeated, bind Sakka, lord of the devas, with a fivefold bond at the neck and bring him to my presence in the city of the asuras.’

|Sakka::name of the king of the deities [sakka]|, lord of the devas, also addressed the devas of the Thirty-Three: ‘Good sirs, if in the ensuing battle between the devas and the asuras, the devas win and the asuras are defeated, bind |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, with a fivefold bond at the neck and bring him to my presence in the Sudhamma assembly hall.’

In that battle, |bhikkhus,::::| the devas won and the asuras were defeated. Then, |bhikkhus,::::| the devas of the Thirty-Three bound |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, with a fivefold bond at the neck and brought him into the presence of Sakka, lord of the devas, in the Sudhamma assembly hall.

There, |bhikkhus,::::| bound by the fivefold bond at the neck, |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|, lord of the asuras, abused and insulted Sakka, lord of the devas, with rude and harsh words as Sakka was entering and leaving the Sudhamma assembly hall. Then, |bhikkhus,::::| |Mātali::name of Sakka's charioteer [mātali]| the charioteer addressed Sakka, lord of the devas, in verse:

‘Is it out of fear or weakness, Maghavā,
that you |endure::withstand, bear, tolerate [titikkhati]|,
hearing the harsh words of |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|
right to your face, O Sakka?’

‘It is neither from fear nor from weakness
that I endure |Vepacitti::::Vepachitti|.
For how could a wise person like me
engage in an argument with a fool?’

‘Fools would only become more aggressive
if there were no one to stop them.
Therefore, with a heavy stick,
the wise one should restrain the fool.’

‘I think this is exactly the way
to put a stop to a fool:
knowing the other is enraged,
one |mindfully::having active presence of mind and lucid retention; remaining naturally anchored and attentive without losing one's bearings [sata]| |settle themselves::calm themselves [upasammati]|.’

‘I see this very fault in enduring,
|Vāsava::epithet of Sakka; lit. of the Vasu's [vāsava]|:
when the fool thinks,
“he endures me out of fear,”
the |undiscerning one::one lacking discernment, who has not cultivated wisdom, unwise [dummedha]| gets even more aggressive,
like a cow chasing someone running away.’

‘Let him think it or not,
“he endures me out of fear.”
Among goals that culminate in one’s own true good,
nothing is found to be better than patience.

When someone who is truly strong
endures a weakling,
they call that the highest |patience::acceptance, receptivity in emotional or interpersonal contexts [khanti]|;
for a weak person must always be patient.

They call that strength weakness,
when a fool’s strength is his power.
But for the strong person guarded by the Dhamma,
no challenger can be found.

Worse off is the one,
who retaliates against an angry person;
not retaliating against anger,
one wins a battle hard to win.

They act for the benefit of both,
their own and the other’s—
who, knowing the other is enraged,
mindfully |settle themselves::calm themselves [upasammati]|.

Those who heal both,
themselves and the other—
are thought to be fools,
by those unskilled in the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|.’

Indeed, |bhikkhus,::::| if Sakka, lord of the devas, living on the fruit of his own merit and exercising sovereign rule over the devas of the Thirty-Three, speaks in praise of patience and gentleness, how much more fitting would it be here for you, bhikkhus, who have gone forth in this well-declared Dhamma and |Vinaya::code of monastic discipline rules, training [vinaya]|, to be patient and gentle!”
