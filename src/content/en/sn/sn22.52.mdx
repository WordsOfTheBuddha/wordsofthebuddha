---
title: Dutiya nandikkhaya sutta - Wearing Away of Delight (Second)
description: By radically attending to the impermanence of the five aggregates, a bhikkhu becomes disenchanted; with delight and passion worn away, the mind is well liberated.
qualities: radical attention, recognition of impermanence, delight, ending, passion, disenchantment, liberation
theme: wisdom, training guideline
slug: sn22.52
priority: 1.1
---

At Sāvatthi.

“Bhikkhus, |radically attend to::wisely attend to, attends to the structural source of, contextually reflect on; lit. attends from the source [yoniso + manasi + karoti]| form, and see the |impermanence::instability [aniccatā]| of |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]| |as it truly is::as it has come to be, as it is in actuality [yathābhūta]|. A bhikkhu attending radically to form, and seeing the impermanence of form as it truly is, becomes |disenchanted with::disillusioned with [nibbindati]| form. With the wearing away of delight comes the |wearing away of passion::exhaustion of intense desire, ending of strong emotions, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāgakkhaya]|; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is |liberated::freed [vimutta]| and is said to be well liberated.

|Bhikkhus,::::| radically attend to |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]|, and see the impermanence of feeling as it truly is. A bhikkhu attending radically to feeling, and seeing the impermanence of feeling as it truly is, becomes disenchanted with feeling. With the wearing away of delight comes the wearing away of passion; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is liberated and is said to be well liberated.

|Bhikkhus,::::| radically attend to |perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [sañña]|, <collapse>and see the impermanence of perception as it truly is. A bhikkhu attending radically to perception, and seeing the impermanence of perception as it truly is, becomes disenchanted with perception. With the wearing away of delight comes the wearing away of passion; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is liberated and is said to be well liberated.</collapse>

|Bhikkhus,::::| radically attend to |intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]|, and see the impermanence of intentional constructs as it truly is. A bhikkhu attending radically to intentional constructs, and seeing the impermanence of intentional constructs as it truly is, becomes disenchanted with intentional constructs. With the wearing away of delight comes the wearing away of passion; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is liberated and is said to be well liberated.

|Bhikkhus,::::| radically attend to |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]|, and see the impermanence of consciousness as it truly is. A bhikkhu attending radically to consciousness, and seeing the impermanence of consciousness as it truly is, becomes disenchanted with consciousness. With the wearing away of delight comes the wearing away of passion; with the wearing away of passion comes the wearing away of delight. With the wearing away of delight and passion, the mind is liberated and is said to be well liberated.”
