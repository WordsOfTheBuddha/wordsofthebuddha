At Sāvatthi.

“|Bhikkhus,::::| I will teach you the |fragile::perishable [pabhaṅgu]| and the durable. Listen to this.

And what is fragile, and what is durable? |Form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]| is fragile. Its |cessation::finishing, ending, termination [nirodha]|, |settling::calming, conciliation, subsiding [vūpasama]|, and |passing away::disappearance, vanishing, settling down; lit. going home [atthaṅgama]| is durable.

|Feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| is fragile. Its cessation, settling, and passing away is durable. |Perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [sañña]| is fragile. |Intentional constructs::intentions, decisions, choices, volitional activities, constructed experiences, production of kamma [saṅkhārā]| are fragile. Their cessation, settling, and passing away is durable. |Consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]| is fragile. Its cessation, settling, and passing away is durable.”
