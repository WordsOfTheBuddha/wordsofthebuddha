At Sāvatthi.

Then the venerable Ānanda approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Ānanda addressed the Blessed One:

“‘Cessation, cessation,’ venerable sir, it is said.” The cessation of which things, venerable sir, is called ‘cessation’?”

“Form, Ānanda, is |impermanent::unstable, transient, unreliable [anicca]|, |conditioned::constructed, created, fabricated [saṅkhata]|, dependently arisen, subject to |wearing away::exhaustion, depletion, gradual destruction [khaya]|, subject to |disintegration::disappearance, dissolution, vanishing [vaya]|, subject to fading away and subject to |cessation::ending, termination [nirodha]|. Its cessation is called ‘cessation.’

Feeling is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to disintegration, subject to fading away and subject to cessation. Its cessation is called ‘cessation.’

Perception <collapse>is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to disintegration, subject to fading away and subject to cessation. Its cessation is called ‘cessation.’</collapse>

Intentional constructs are impermanent,conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to disintegration, subject to fading away and subject to cessation. Its cessation is called ‘cessation.’

Consciousness is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to wearing away, subject to disintegration, subject to fading away and subject to cessation. Its cessation is called ‘cessation.’

The cessation of these things, Ānanda, is called ‘cessation.’”
