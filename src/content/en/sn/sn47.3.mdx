At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Then a certain bhikkhu approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “It would be good if the Blessed One were to teach the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| in brief, so that, having heard the Dhamma from the Blessed One, I might dwell alone, secluded, |with diligence::quality of doing one’s work or duty well, with alertness, carefulness and care [appamatta]|, |continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, and |resoluteness::applying oneself [pahitatta]|.”

“It is in just this way that certain |misguided persons::vain persons [moghapurisā]| make requests of me, and when the Dhamma has been taught, they think only of following me around.”

“May the Blessed One teach me the Dhamma in brief, venerable sir; may the Accomplished One teach me the Dhamma in brief! Perhaps I may understand the meaning of the Blessed One’s words; perhaps I may become an |heir::successor [dāyāda]| to the Blessed One’s words!”

“Therefore, bhikkhu, purify the very starting point of wholesome qualities. And what is the starting point of wholesome qualities? |Virtue::moral conduct, ethical behavior [sīla]| that is well purified, and view that is straight. When, bhikkhu, your virtue is well purified and your view is straight, then, relying on virtue, being established in virtue, you should cultivate the |four establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as a direct way of practice [cattāro + satipaṭṭhāna]| in a threefold way.

What four?

1.) Here, bhikkhu, dwell observing the body in and of itself internally, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; or dwell observing the body in and of itself externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world; or dwell observing the body in and of itself both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world.

2.) Dwell observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself internally, <collapse>with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> or dwell observing the felt experience in and of itself externally, <collapse>with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> or dwell observing the felt experience in and of itself both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world.

3.) Dwell observing the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| in and of itself internally, <collapse>with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> or dwell observing the mind in and of itself externally, <collapse>with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> or dwell observing the mind in and of itself both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world.

4.) Dwell observing the |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves internally, <collapse>with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> or dwell observing the mental qualities in and of themselves externally, <collapse>with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> or dwell observing the mental qualities in and of themselves both internally and externally, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world.

When, bhikkhu, relying on virtue and being established in virtue, you cultivate these four establishments of mindfulness thus in a threefold way, then, bhikkhu, whether night or day comes, only growth in wholesome qualities is to be expected for you, not decline.”

Then that bhikkhu, having delighted and approved of what the Blessed One had said, rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right, he departed.

Thereafter, that bhikkhu, living in seclusion, with diligence, continuous effort, and resoluteness, not long after, realized with |direct knowledge::experiential realization [abhiññā]| and attained in that very life the unsurpassed culmination of the spiritual life for which sons of good families rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness.

He |directly knew::understood experientially; lit. completely knew [abbhaññāsi]|: “Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.” And that bhikkhu became one of the |Arahants::fully awakened ones, free from all mental defilements; worthy of offerings and veneration; also an epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|.
