---
title: Assu sutta - Tears
description: "Using the simile of tears, the Buddha illustrates how cyclic existence has no discoverable beginning: the tears shed across countless bereavements far surpass the water in the four great oceans."
qualities: disenchantment, dispassion, liberation
theme: urgency

At Sāvatthi.

“|Bhikkhus,::::| this |cyclic existence::wandering from one state of existence to another, the cycle of birth and death, moving on continuously [saṁsāra]| has an inconceivable beginning. A first point is not evident of beings who, obstructed by |ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]| and fettered by |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]|, run on and wander in this cycle of existence. What do you think, |bhikkhus,::::| which is greater: the tears that have flowed and poured forth from you as you ran on and wandered through this long course, weeping and wailing from association with the displeasing and separation from the pleasing, or the water in the four great oceans?”

“As we understand the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One, venerable sir, this alone is greater: the tears that have flowed and poured forth from us as we ran on and wandered through this long course, weeping and wailing from association with the displeasing and separation from the pleasing, not the water in the four great oceans.”

“Good, good, bhikkhus, it is good that you understand the Dhamma taught by me in this way. This alone|, |bhikkhus,::::| is greater: the tears that have flowed and poured forth from you as you ran on and wandered through this long course, weeping and wailing from association with the displeasing and separation from the pleasing, not the water in the four great oceans. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have experienced the death of a mother; as you experienced the death of a mother, weeping and wailing from association with the displeasing and separation from the pleasing, the tears that flowed and poured forth from you were more than this, not the water in the four great oceans. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have experienced the death of a father ... the death of a brother ... the death of a sister ... the death of a son ... the death of a daughter ... the loss of relatives ... the loss of wealth. For a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have experienced loss through illness; as you experienced loss through illness, weeping and wailing from association with the displeasing and separation from the pleasing, the tears that flowed and poured forth from you were more than this, not the water in the four great oceans.

For what reason? |Bhikkhus,::::| this cyclic existence has an inconceivable beginning. <collapse>A first point is not evident of beings who, obstructed by ignorance and fettered by craving, run on and wander in this cycle of existence. For such a long time, |bhikkhus,::::| you have experienced |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|, |agony::intense suffering, anguish [tibba]|, and |calamity::disaster [byasana]|, the |cemetery::charnel ground [kaṭasī]| has been filled up with your bones.</collapse> Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| it is enough |to become disenchanted::to become disillusioned [nibbindituṃ]|, |to become detached::to become dispassionate [virajjituṁ]| and |to become free from::to be liberated from [vimuccituṃ]| |all conditions::all formations, all activities, all fabrications [sabbasaṅkhāra]|.”
