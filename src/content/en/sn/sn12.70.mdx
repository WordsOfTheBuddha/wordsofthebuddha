Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]|, in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ feeding ground.

At that time, the Blessed One was honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed, and he received robes, alms food, lodging, and medicinal requisites for the sick. The bhikkhu |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| was also honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed, and received robes, alms food, lodging, and medicinal requisites for the sick. But the wanderers of other sects were not honored, respected, revered, venerated, or esteemed; they did not receive robes, alms food, lodging, or medicinal requisites for the sick.

#### Susima Ordains Stealthily

Now at that time, the wanderer Susima was residing at Rājagaha together with a large assembly of wanderers.

Then the assembly of the wanderer Susima said to him: “Come, friend Susima, lead the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| under the ascetic Gotama. Having learned the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, teach us to recite it. Having learned that Dhamma, we will speak it to the laypeople. In that way, we too will be honored, respected, revered, venerated, and esteemed, and we will receive robes, alms food, lodging, and medicinal requisites for the sick.”

“Yes, friends,” the wanderer Susima replied to his own assembly, and he approached the venerable Ānanda; having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with the venerable Ānanda. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the wanderer Susima said to the venerable Ānanda: “Friend Ānanda, I wish to lead the spiritual life in this Dhamma and Vinaya.”

Then the venerable Ānanda took the wanderer Susima and approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this wanderer Susima says thus: ‘Friend Ānanda, I wish to lead the spiritual life in this Dhamma and Vinaya.’”

“Well then, Ānanda, give Susima the going forth.” And the wanderer Susima received the going forth in the presence of the Blessed One, and he received the full ordination.

#### Liberated by Wisdom

Now at that time, several bhikkhus had declared |final knowledge::spiritual insight of the complete wearing away of the taints; full awakening [aññā]| in the presence of the Blessed One: “We know clearly: ‘Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any |state of existence::state of being [itthatta]|.’”

The venerable Susima heard: “It is said that several bhikkhus have declared final knowledge in the presence of the Blessed One: ‘We know clearly: “Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.”’” Then the venerable Susima approached those bhikkhus; having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with those bhikkhus. After exchanging courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Susima said to those bhikkhus: “Is it true that the venerables have declared final knowledge in the presence of the Blessed One: ‘We know clearly: “Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence”’?”

“Yes, friend.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, do you venerable sirs experience the various kinds of |psychic powers::supernormal abilities, psychic potency, spiritual power [iddhi]|—being one, you become many; having been many, you become one; you appear and disappear; you pass unhindered through walls, enclosures, and mountains as though through space; you dive in and out of the earth as though it were water; you walk on water without sinking as though on solid ground; you travel cross-legged through the sky like a winged bird; with your hand you touch and stroke the moon and the sun, so mighty and powerful; and you wield mastery with the body even as far as the |Brahmā::God, the first deity to be born at the beginning of a new cosmic cycle and whose lifespan lasts for the entire cycle [brahmā]| world?”

“No, friend.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, do you venerable sirs, with the |divine ear element::clairaudience, the divine auditory faculty [dibba + sotadhātu]|, which is purified and surpasses the human range, hear both kinds of sounds, divine and human, whether distant or near?”

“No, friend.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, do you venerable sirs understand the minds of other beings, other persons, having encompassed them with your own mind—do you know a mind with lust as ‘a mind with lust,’ and a mind free from lust as ‘a mind free from lust’; a mind with hatred as ‘a mind with hatred,’ and a mind free from hatred as ‘a mind free from hatred’; a mind with delusion as ‘a mind with delusion,’ and a mind free from delusion as ‘a mind free from delusion’; a contracted mind as ‘a contracted mind,’ and a scattered mind as ‘a scattered mind’; an exalted mind as ‘an exalted mind,’ and an unexalted mind as ‘an unexalted mind’; a surpassable mind as ‘a surpassable mind,’ and an unsurpassable mind as ‘an unsurpassable mind’; a collected mind as ‘a collected mind,’ and an uncollected mind as ‘an uncollected mind’; a liberated mind as ‘a liberated mind,’ and an unliberated mind as ‘an unliberated mind’?”

“No, friend.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, do you venerable sirs recollect your manifold past lives, that is—one birth, two births, three births, four births, five births, ten births, twenty births, thirty births, forty births, fifty births, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births; many |aeons::lifespan of a world system, a vast cosmic time span [kappa]| of cosmic contraction, many aeons of cosmic expansion, many aeons of cosmic contraction and expansion: ‘There I was so named, of such a clan, with such an appearance, such was my food, such was my experience of pleasure and pain, such was my life span; and passing away from there, I was reborn elsewhere; and there too I was so named, of such a clan, with such an appearance, such was my food, such was my experience of pleasure and pain, such was my life span; and passing away from there, I was reborn here’? Thus with their aspects and particulars, do you recollect your manifold past lives?”

“No, friend.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, do you venerable sirs, with the |divine eye::the faculty of clairvoyance, the ability to see beyond the ordinary human range [dibbacakkhu]|, which is purified and surpasses human vision, see beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in fortunate and unfortunate destinations—and understand how beings fare |according to their kamma::in line with their actions [yathākammūpaga]|: ‘These beings, who were endowed with bad conduct of body, speech, and mind, who reviled the Noble Ones, held |wrong view::a distorted perception, an untrue view, a false belief [micchādiṭṭhi]|, and undertook actions under the influence of wrong view—with the breaking up of the body, after death, have reappeared in a state of misery, a bad destination, a |realm of downfall::realm of misery [vinipāta]|, even in hell; but these beings, who were endowed with good conduct of body, speech, and mind, who did not revile the Noble Ones, held |right view::view that is in line with the Dhamma - teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [sammādiṭṭhi]|, and undertook actions under the influence of right view—with the breaking up of the body, after death, have reappeared in a good destination, a heavenly world’? Thus with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses human vision, do you see beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in fortunate and unfortunate destinations—and understand how beings fare according to their kamma?”

“No, friend.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, do you venerable sirs contact with the body and dwell in those liberations that are peaceful and formless, surpassing forms?”

“No, friend.”

“Here now, venerable sirs, there is this declaration and the non-attainment of these states; how can this be, friends?”

“We are |liberated by wisdom::emancipated by insight [paññāvimutta]|, friend Susima.”

“I do not understand in detail the meaning of this brief statement made by the venerables. It would be good if the venerables would speak to me in such a way that I might understand in detail the meaning of this brief statement made by the venerables.”

“Whether you understand, friend Susima, or whether you do not understand, we are nonetheless liberated by wisdom.”

#### Stability of Dhamma

Then the venerable Susima rose from his seat and approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Susima reported to the Blessed One the entire conversation he had had with those bhikkhus.

“First, Susima, comes the |knowledge of the stability of Dhamma::direct understanding of the conditional, actual nature of the Dhamma (i.e. dependent co-arising) [dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇa]|; the knowledge of |Nibbāna::complete cooling, letting go of everything, deathless, freedom from calamity, the non-disintegrating; lit. blowing away [nibbāna]| comes afterwards.”

“Venerable sir, I do not understand in detail the meaning of this brief statement made by the Blessed One. It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would speak to me in such a way that I might understand in detail the meaning of this brief statement made by the Blessed One.”

“Whether you understand, Susima, or whether you do not understand, the knowledge of the stability of Dhamma comes first; the knowledge of Nibbāna comes afterwards.

#### The Five Aggregates

What do you think, Susima—is |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]| |permanent::stable, not in flux [nicca]| or |impermanent::not lasting, transient, unreliable [anicca]|?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent—is it |unsatisfactory::uncomfortable, unpleasant [dukkha]| or |pleasant::comfortable, easy, good [sukha]|?”

“Unsatisfactory, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent, unsatisfactory, and |subject to change::of the nature of alteration, decay [vipariṇāmadhamma]|—is it fitting to regard that as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Is |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent—is it unsatisfactory or pleasant?”

“Unsatisfactory, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent, unsatisfactory, and subject to change—is it fitting to regard that as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Is |perception::mental act of recognizing and labeling based on past experiences [saññā]| permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.” …

“Are |intentional constructs::intentions, decisions, choices, volitional activities, constructed experiences, production of kamma [saṅkhāra]| permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent—is it unsatisfactory or pleasant?”

“Unsatisfactory, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent, unsatisfactory, and subject to change—is it fitting to regard that as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Is |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]| permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent—is it unsatisfactory or pleasant?”

“Unsatisfactory, venerable sir.”

“And that which is impermanent, unsatisfactory, and subject to change—is it fitting to regard that as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Therefore, Susima, any kind of form whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, deficient or refined, far or near—all form is to be seen |with right wisdom::with correct distinctive knowledge [sammappaññāya]| |as it truly is::as it has come to be, as it is in actuality [yathābhūta]|: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ Any kind of feeling whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, deficient or refined, far or near—all feeling is to be seen with right wisdom as it truly is: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ Any kind of perception … any kind of intentional constructs whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, deficient or refined, far or near—all intentional constructs are to be seen with right wisdom as it truly is: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ Any kind of consciousness whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, deficient or refined, far or near—all consciousness is to be seen with right wisdom as it truly is: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’

Seeing thus, Susima, the |learned::well-studied, instructed [sutavant]| disciple of the Noble Ones becomes |disenchanted with::disillusioned with [nibbindati]| form, becomes disenchanted with feeling, becomes disenchanted with perception, becomes disenchanted with intentional constructs, becomes disenchanted with consciousness. Experiencing disenchantment, he becomes |dispassionate::detached [virajjati]|; through dispassion, he is liberated. When liberated, there arises the knowledge: ‘Liberated.’

He knows clearly: ‘Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.’

#### Dependent Co-Arising

Do you see, Susima: ‘With |birth::rebirth, conception, coming into existence [jāti]| as condition, aging and death come to be’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With |existence::being, becoming; continued conditional existence, the karmically conditioned mode of being that leads to future rebirth [bhava]| as condition, birth comes to be’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With |clinging::grasping, acquiring, appropriating, taking possession, identifying; fuel for fire; lit. taking near [upādāna]| as condition, existence comes to be’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]| as condition, clinging comes to be’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With feeling as condition, craving comes to be … with |contact::sense impingement, raw experience, touch [phassa]| as condition, feeling comes to be … with the |six sense bases::The six internal sense bases—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind—are the faculties that enable sensory contact and experience. They are not the physical organs themselves, but the functional conditions that enable consciousness to meet an object [saḷāyatana]| as condition, contact comes to be … with |name and form::The mutually dependent pair of mental designation and material perception: "Name" consists of feeling, perception, intention, contact, and attention; "Form" consists of the four great primaries (earth, water, fire, wind) and derived form, experienced as perception of form through sensory resistance [nāmarūpa]| as condition, the six sense bases come to be … with consciousness as condition, name and form come to be … with intentional constructs as condition, consciousness comes to be … with |ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]| as condition, intentional constructs come to be’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With the |cessation::ending, termination [nirodha]| of birth, aging and death cease’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With the cessation of existence, birth ceases’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Do you see, Susima: ‘With the cessation of clinging, existence ceases … with the cessation of craving, clinging ceases … with the cessation of feeling, craving ceases … with the cessation of contact, feeling ceases … with the cessation of the six sense bases, contact ceases … with the cessation of name and form, the six sense bases cease … with the cessation of consciousness, name and form cease … with the cessation of intentional constructs, consciousness ceases … with the cessation of ignorance, intentional constructs cease’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

#### Nibbāna Beyond Attainments

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, Susima, do you experience the various kinds of psychic powers—being one, you become many; having been many, you become one; you appear and disappear; you pass unhindered through walls, enclosures, and mountains as though through space; you dive in and out of the earth as though it were water; you walk on water without sinking as though on solid ground; you travel cross-legged through the sky like a winged bird; with your hand you touch and stroke the moon and the sun, so mighty and powerful; and you wield mastery with the body even as far as the Brahmā world?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, Susima, do you, with the divine ear element, which is purified and surpasses the human range, hear both kinds of sounds, divine and human, whether distant or near?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, Susima, do you understand the minds of other beings, other persons, having encompassed them with your own mind—do you know a mind with lust as ‘a mind with lust’ … and a liberated mind as ‘a liberated mind’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, Susima, do you recollect your manifold past lives, that is—one birth … Thus with their aspects and particulars, do you recollect your manifold past lives?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, Susima, do you, with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses human vision, see beings passing away … and understand how beings fare according to their kamma?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Knowing thus and seeing thus, Susima, do you contact with the body and dwell in those liberations that are peaceful and formless, surpassing forms?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Here now, Susima, there is this declaration and the non-attainment of these states; how can this be, Susima?”

#### Susima’s Confession

Then the venerable Susima bowed down with his head at the Blessed One’s feet and said to the Blessed One: “A |transgression::fault, moral lapse [accaya]| overcame me, venerable sir, |immature::lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]|, |deluded::confused, disoriented [mūḷha]|, and unskillful as I was, in that I went forth as a thief of the Dhamma in such a well-expounded Dhamma and Vinaya. Venerable sir, may the Blessed One accept my transgression as a transgression, for the sake of |restraint for the future::future discipline and guarding [āyatiṁ saṁvara]|.”

“Indeed, Susima, a transgression overcame you, immature, deluded, and unskillful as you were, in that you went forth as a thief of the Dhamma in such a well-expounded Dhamma and Vinaya. Suppose, Susima, they were to seize a thief, a criminal, and bring him before the king, saying: ‘Sire, this is a thief, a criminal; inflict on him whatever punishment you wish.’ Then the king might say to them: ‘Go, men, bind this man’s arms tightly behind his back with a strong rope, shave his head bald, parade him to the harsh sound of a drum from street to street and crossroad to crossroad, take him out through the southern gate, and to the south of the city cut off his head.’ Then the king’s men would bind his arms tightly behind his back with a strong rope, shave his head bald, parade him to the harsh sound of a drum from street to street and crossroad to crossroad, take him out through the southern gate, and to the south of the city cut off his head. What do you think, Susima, would that man experience pain and |displeasure::a negative state of mind; disagreeable feeling or unease born of mental contact, mental pain [domanassa]| because of that?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Whatever pain and displeasure that man would experience because of that, Susima, going forth as a thief of the Dhamma in such a well-expounded Dhamma and Vinaya has a more painful result and a more bitter result than that, and moreover, it leads to a realm of downfall. But since you, Susima, see your transgression as a transgression and make amends in accordance with the Dhamma, we accept it from you. For it is growth in the Vinaya of the Noble Ones, Susima, when one sees one’s transgression as a transgression, makes amends in accordance with the Dhamma, and undertakes restraint in the future.”
