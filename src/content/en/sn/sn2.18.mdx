Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāketa in the Deer Park at the |Añjana Grove::black wood, dark forest [añjanavana]|.

Then, when the night had advanced, the young deity |Kakudha::name of a young deity [kakudha]|, with a radiant appearance, illuminating the entire Añjana Grove, approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One, stood to one side, and said to the Blessed One: “Do you |delight::rejoice, take pleasure [nandati]|, |ascetic::recluse, holy man, epithet of the Buddha [samaṇa]|?”

“What have I gained, friend?”

“Well then, ascetic, do you |sorrow::grieve [socati]|?”

“What have I lost, friend?”

“Well then, ascetic, do you neither delight nor sorrow?”

“Yes, friend.”

“I hope you are |without distress::free from misery, misfortune, pain [anagha]|, bhikkhu,
I hope that |delight::pleasure, enjoyment, relish [nandi]| is not found in you;
I hope that, as you sit alone,
|discontent::dislike, dissatisfaction, aversion, boredom [arati]| |does not overwhelm::does not drown, does not submerge [nābhikīrati]| you.”

[The Blessed One]: “I am indeed without distress, spirit,
and delight is not found in me;
and as I sit alone,
discontent does not overwhelm me.”

“How are you without distress, bhikkhu?
How is delight not found in you?
How is it that, as you sit alone,
discontent does not overwhelm you?”

[The Blessed One]: “For one in whom |distress::misery, misfortune, pain [agha]| has arisen, there is delight,
for one in whom delight has arisen, there is distress;
a bhikkhu without delight, without distress—
know this, friend.”

“After a long time, indeed, I see,
a |sage::Brāhmaṇa, a title used for an Arahant, an awakened being [brāhmaṇa]| who is |completely quenched::completely cooled of mental defilements [parinibbuta]|;
a bhikkhu without delight, without distress,
having crossed over |entanglement in::attachment to, ensnarement in [visattika]| the world.”
