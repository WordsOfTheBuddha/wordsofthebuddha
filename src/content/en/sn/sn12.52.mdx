---
title: Upādāna sutta - Clinging
description: Using the simile of a great bonfire, the Buddha explains how perceiving gratification in graspable objects fuels craving and clinging, leading to suffering, whereas perceiving their drawbacks leads to the cessation of craving and the cessation of suffering.
qualities: perceiving drawback, craving, attachment, perceiving gratification, ending, suffering
theme: principle, cultivating discernment, wisdom
slug: sn12.52
simile: bonfire
priority: 2
---

At Sāvatthi.

“|Bhikkhus,::::| when one dwells perceiving |gratification::satisfaction, pleasure, enjoyment, sweetness [assāda]| in objects that can be |grasped at::clung to, taken possession of [upādāniya]|, |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]| increases. Dependent on craving, |clinging::grasping, acquiring, appropriating, taking possession, identifying; fuel for fire; lit. taking near [upādāna]| arises; dependent on clinging, |existence::being, becoming; continued conditional existence, the karmically conditioned mode of being that leads to future rebirth [bhava]| arises; dependent on existence, |birth::rebirth, conception, coming into existence [jāti]| arises; dependent on birth, aging and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair arise. Thus is the arising of this entire heap of |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|.

Suppose, |bhikkhus,::::| a great bonfire were set ablaze on ten, twenty, thirty, or forty cartloads of wood, and from time to time a man were to throw dry grass, dry cow dung, and dry sticks into it. Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| sustained by that |nutriment::fuel, sustenance [āhāra]|, |fueled by that::supported by that fuel; upādāna means both fuel and clinging [tadupādāna]|, that great bonfire would blaze for a long time, enduring for a great length of time.

In the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| when one dwells perceiving gratification in objects that can be grasped at, craving increases. Dependent on craving, clinging arises; <collapse>dependent on clinging, existence arises; dependent on existence, birth arises; dependent on birth, aging and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair arise.</collapse> Thus is the arising of this entire heap of suffering.

|Bhikkhus,::::| when one dwells perceiving the |drawbacks::disadvantages, unsatisfactoriness, inadequacy, danger [ādīnava]| in objects that can be grasped at, craving ceases. From the cessation of craving, there is the cessation of clinging; from the cessation of clinging, there is the cessation of existence; from the cessation of existence, there is the cessation of birth; from the cessation of birth, aging and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair cease. Thus there is the cessation of this entire heap of suffering.

Suppose, |bhikkhus,::::| a great bonfire were set ablaze on ten, twenty, thirty, or forty cartloads of wood, and a man were not to throw dry grass, dry cow dung, and dry sticks into it from time to time. Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| with the |exhaustion::depletion, wearing away [pariyādāna]| of the original |fuel::sustenance; upādāna means both fuel and clinging [upādāna]| and with no other [fuel] being provided, that great bonfire, being without nutriment, would be |extinguished::quenched, cooled [nibbāyati]|.

In the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| when one dwells perceiving the drawbacks in objects that can be grasped at, craving ceases. From the cessation of craving, there is the cessation of clinging; <collapse>from the cessation of clinging, there is the cessation of existence; from the cessation of existence, there is the cessation of birth; from the cessation of birth, aging and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair cease.</collapse> Thus there is the cessation of this entire heap of suffering.”
