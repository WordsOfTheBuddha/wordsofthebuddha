At Sāvatthi.

Then |King Ajātasattu::name of the king of Magadha, son of Bimbisāra; lit. unborn enemy [ajātasattu]| of |Magadha::An ancient kingdom in northeastern India (present day Bihar) and the setting for much of the Buddha’s teachings after his full awakening [magadha]|, |son of Vedehi::epithet of King Ajātasattu; lit. son of Vedehi [vedehiputta]|, marshaled a |fourfold army::an army of four divisions: elephants, cavalry, chariots, and infantry [caturaṅginī senā]| and marched toward |Kāsi::an ancient kingdom and region in northern India centered around Vārāṇasī [kāsi]| against King Pasenadi of Kosala. King Pasenadi of Kosala heard: “It seems King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, has marshaled a fourfold army and marched toward Kāsi against me.” Then King Pasenadi of Kosala marshaled a fourfold army and marched out to Kāsi against King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi. Then King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, and King Pasenadi of Kosala engaged in battle. In that battle, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, defeated King Pasenadi of Kosala. Defeated, King Pasenadi of Kosala retreated to his own royal capital, Sāvatthi.

Then several bhikkhus, having dressed early in the morning and taking their alms bowls and outer robes, entered Sāvatthi for alms. Then, having wandered for alms in Sāvatthi, those bhikkhus, after their meal and having returned from their alms round, approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. As they were seated thus, they said to the Blessed One:

“Here, venerable sir, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, marshaled a fourfold army and marched toward Kāsi against King Pasenadi of Kosala. Then, venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala heard: ‘It seems King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, has marshaled a fourfold army and marched toward Kāsi against me.’ Then, venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala marshaled a fourfold army and marched out to Kāsi against King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi. Then, venerable sir, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, and King Pasenadi of Kosala engaged in battle. In that battle, venerable sir, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, defeated King Pasenadi of Kosala. Defeated, venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala retreated to his own royal capital, Sāvatthi.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of Vedehi, has bad friends, bad companions, and bad associates. But King Pasenadi of Kosala, |bhikkhus,::::| has |good friends::noble friends, virtuous spiritual companions [kalyāṇamitta]|, good companions, and good associates. Yet today, |bhikkhus,::::| having been defeated, King Pasenadi of Kosala sleeps with difficulty this night.”

The Blessed One said this. Having spoken thus, the Accomplished One further said this:

“Victory breeds hatred,
the defeated sleep with difficulty;
|the composed::calmed, tranquil [upasanta]| sleep with ease,
having abandoned |victory and defeat::conquest and loss [jayaparājaya]|.”
