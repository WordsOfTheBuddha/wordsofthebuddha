Then, the wanderer Vacchagotta approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One, and after a courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. As he was seated to one side, the wanderer Vacchagotta said to the Blessed One:

“How is it, sir Gotama, is there a |self::soul, spirit, essence, personal, related to oneself [attā]|?” When this was said, the Blessed One remained silent.

“How is it then, sir Gotama, is there no self?” For a second time, the Blessed One remained silent. Then the wanderer Vacchagotta rose from his seat and left.

Then, not long after the wanderer Vacchagotta had left, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One:

“Why is it, venerable sir, that the Blessed One, when asked a question by the wanderer Vacchagotta, did not |answer::explain, declare; lit. make distinct [byākaroti]|?”

“If, Ānanda, when asked by the wanderer Vacchagotta ‘Is there a self?’ I had answered ‘There is a self,’ that would have been siding with those ascetics and brahmins who are |eternalists::those who hold the doctrine of an everlasting self or world [sassatavādā]|. And if, Ānanda, when asked by the wanderer Vacchagotta ‘Is there no self?’ I had answered ‘There is no self,’ that would have been siding with those ascetics and brahmins who are |annihilationists::those who hold the doctrine of annihilation at death [ucchedavādā]|.

If, Ānanda, when asked by the wanderer Vacchagotta ‘Is there a self?’ I had answered ‘There is a self,’ would that have been in accordance with the arising of |knowledge::understanding, insight [ñāṇa]| in me: ‘All |phenomena::all things, whether conditioned or unconditioned [dhammā]| are |not-self::not suitable to identify with, impersonal, non-subjective, essenceless [anatta]|’?”

“Indeed not, venerable sir.”

“And if, Ānanda, when asked by the wanderer Vacchagotta ‘Is there no self?’ I had answered ‘There is no self,’ the wanderer Vacchagotta, already bewildered, would have fallen into still greater |bewilderment::confusion, disorientation by delusion [sammoha]|, thinking: ‘Surely I had a self before, but now it does not exist!’”
