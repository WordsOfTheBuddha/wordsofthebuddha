At one time, several elder bhikkhus were dwelling at Kosambī, in Ghosita’s Park. Now at that time, the venerable Khemaka was dwelling at |Jujube Tree Park::Badarikārāma, a monastery at Kosambī [badarikārāma]|, sick, experiencing pain, and gravely ill.

### Elders’ Inquiry

#### Health

Then, in the late afternoon, those elder bhikkhus, having emerged from |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, addressed the venerable Dāsaka: “Come, friend Dāsaka, go to the bhikkhu Khemaka and speak to him thus: ‘The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: “Is it bearable for you, friend? Is it manageable? Are your painful feelings decreasing, not increasing? Is the subsiding of them, not their intensifying, discernible?”’”

“Yes, friends,” the venerable Dāsaka replied to the elder bhikkhus, and he went to the venerable Khemaka. Having approached, he said this to the venerable Khemaka:

“The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: ‘Is it bearable for you, friend? Is it manageable? Are your painful feelings decreasing, not increasing? Is the subsiding of them, not their intensifying, discernible?’”

“Friend, it is not bearable for me, it is not manageable for me. My painful feelings are increasing, not decreasing; their intensifying, not their subsiding, is discernible.”

Then the venerable Dāsaka went to the elder bhikkhus and said this to them: “Friend Khemaka said this: ‘Friend, it is not bearable for me, it is not manageable for me. My painful feelings are increasing, not decreasing; their intensifying, not their subsiding, is discernible.’”

#### Five Aggregates And Self

“Come, friend Dāsaka, go to the bhikkhu Khemaka and speak to him thus: ‘The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: “There are these |five aggregates subject to clinging::the physical and mental heaps that are appropriated, grasped at, or taken as self; the fivefold collection of form, feeling, perception, intentional constructs, and consciousness bound up with attachment [pañca + upādānakkhandha]| spoken of by the Blessed One, namely: the |form aggregate subject to clinging::attaching to the physical body or external objects as part of one’s identity or as something that provides lasting satisfaction [rūpupādānakkhandha]|, the |feeling aggregate subject to clinging::becoming attached to feelings of pleasure, resisting or rejecting feelings of pain, and becoming indifferent or unaware of neutral feelings [vedanupādānakkhandha]|, the |perception aggregate subject to clinging::becoming attached to how one interprets and perceives things [saññupādānakkhandha]|, the |intentional constructs aggregate subject to clinging::attachment to, or identification with one’s intentions, emotions, and decisions as part of ‘who I am’ [saṅkhārupādānakkhandha]|, and the |consciousness aggregate subject to clinging::attachment to, or identification with, quality of awareness [viññāṇupādānakkhandha]|. Does the venerable Khemaka regard anything in these five aggregates subject to clinging as self or as belonging to self?”’”

“Yes, friends,” the venerable Dāsaka replied to the elder bhikkhus, and he went to the venerable Khemaka. Having approached, he said: “The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: ‘There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging, the feeling aggregate subject to clinging, the perception aggregate subject to clinging, the intentional constructs aggregate subject to clinging, and the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. Does the venerable Khemaka regard anything in these five aggregates subject to clinging as self or as belonging to self?’”

“There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, friend, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging, the feeling aggregate subject to clinging, the perception aggregate subject to clinging, the intentional constructs aggregate subject to clinging, and the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. In these five aggregates subject to clinging, friend, I do not regard anything as self or as belonging to self.”

Then the venerable Dāsaka went to the elder bhikkhus and said this to them: “Friend Khemaka said this: ‘There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, friend, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging, the feeling aggregate subject to clinging, the perception aggregate subject to clinging, the intentional constructs aggregate subject to clinging, and the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. In these five aggregates subject to clinging, friend, I do not regard anything as self or as belonging to self.’”

“Come, friend Dāsaka, go to the bhikkhu Khemaka and speak to him thus: ‘The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: “There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging ... the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. If the venerable Khemaka does not regard anything in these five aggregates subject to clinging as self or as belonging to self, then the venerable Khemaka is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being [arahant]|, one whose taints are exhausted.”’”

“Yes, friends,” the venerable Dāsaka replied to the elder bhikkhus, and he went to the venerable Khemaka. Having approached, he said: “The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: ‘There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging ... the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. If the venerable Khemaka does not regard anything in these five aggregates subject to clinging as self or as belonging to self, then the venerable Khemaka is an Arahant, one whose taints are exhausted.’”

“There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, friend, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging ... the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. In these five aggregates subject to clinging, friend, I do not regard anything as self or as belonging to self, nor am I an Arahant whose taints are exhausted; however, friend, regarding these five aggregates subject to clinging, ‘I am’ is found in me, yet I do not regard [any of them as] ‘This I am.’”

Then the venerable Dāsaka went to the elder bhikkhus and said this to them: “Friend Khemaka said this: ‘There are these five aggregates subject to clinging spoken of by the Blessed One, friend, namely: the form aggregate subject to clinging ... the consciousness aggregate subject to clinging. In these five aggregates subject to clinging, friend, I do not regard anything as self or as belonging to self, nor am I an Arahant whose taints are exhausted; however, friend, regarding these five aggregates subject to clinging, “I am” is found in me, yet I do not regard [any of them as] “This I am.”’”

“Come, friend Dāsaka, go to the bhikkhu Khemaka and speak to him thus: ‘The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: “When you say ‘I am,’ friend Khemaka, what is this that you say is ‘I am’? Do you say that form is ‘I am,’ or that something apart from form is ‘I am’? Do you say that feeling ... perception ... intentional constructs ... consciousness is ‘I am,’ or that something apart from consciousness is ‘I am’? When you say ‘I am,’ friend Khemaka, what is this that you say is ‘I am’?”’”

“Yes, friends,” the venerable Dāsaka replied to the elder bhikkhus, and he went to the venerable Khemaka. Having approached, he said this to the venerable Khemaka: “The elders, friend Khemaka, say this: ‘When you say “I am,” friend Khemaka, what is this that you say is “I am”? Do you say that form is “I am,” or that something apart from form is “I am”? Do you say that feeling ... perception ... intentional constructs ... consciousness is “I am,” or that something apart from consciousness is “I am”? When you say “I am,” friend Khemaka, what is this that you say is “I am”?’”

“Enough, friend Dāsaka, why this running back and forth? Bring a staff, friend; I myself will go to the elder bhikkhus.”

Then the venerable Khemaka, leaning on a staff, went to the elder bhikkhus. Having approached, he exchanged friendly greetings with the elder bhikkhus. After the exchange of a courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the elder bhikkhus said this to the venerable Khemaka:

“When you say ‘I am,’ friend Khemaka, what is this that you say is ‘I am’? Do you say that form is ‘I am,’ or that something apart from form is ‘I am’? Do you say that feeling ... perception ... intentional constructs ... consciousness is ‘I am,’ or that something apart from consciousness is ‘I am’? When you say ‘I am,’ friend Khemaka, what is this that you say is ‘I am’?”

“Friends, I do not say that form is ‘I am,’ nor do I say that something apart from form is ‘I am.’ I do not say that feeling ... perception ... intentional constructs ... consciousness is ‘I am,’ nor do I say that something apart from consciousness is ‘I am.’ However, friends, regarding these five aggregates subject to clinging, ‘I am’ is found in me, yet I do not regard [any of them as] ‘This I am.’

### Scent of a Lotus

Suppose, friends, there is the scent of a blue lotus, a red lotus, or a white lotus. If someone were to say, ‘It is the scent of the petals,’ or ‘It is the scent of the color,’ or ‘It is the scent of the stamens,’ would that person be speaking rightly?”

“No, friend.”

“Then how, friend, would one rightly answer?”

“‘It is the scent of the flower,’ friend—thus would one rightly answer.”

“In the same way, friends, I do not say that form is ‘I am,’ nor do I say that something apart from form is ‘I am.’ I do not say that feeling ... perception ... intentional constructs ... consciousness is ‘I am,’ nor do I say that something apart from consciousness is ‘I am.’ However, friends, regarding these five aggregates subject to clinging, ‘I am’ is found in me, yet I do not regard [any of them as] ‘This I am.’

Even though, friends, the five |lower fetters::the five mental bonds tying beings to the lower sensual realm: personal existence view, doubt, clinging to rules and observances, sensual desire, and ill will [orambhāgiyāni saṁyojanāni]| have been abandoned by a noble disciple, there still remains in him a residual |conceit::self-conceit, comparison [māna]| ‘I am,’ a desire ‘I am,’ and an |underlying tendency::latent disposition [anusaya]| ‘I am,’ not yet uprooted regarding these five aggregates subject to clinging. Later on, he dwells observing arising and passing away in the five aggregates subject to clinging: ‘Such is form, such is the arising of form, such is the passing away of form; such is feeling ... such is perception ... such are intentional constructs ... such is consciousness, such is the arising of consciousness, such is the passing away of consciousness.’ While he dwells thus observing arising and passing away in these five aggregates subject to clinging, that residual conceit ‘I am,’ desire ‘I am,’ and underlying tendency ‘I am,’ not yet uprooted regarding the five aggregates subject to clinging, comes to be uprooted as well.

### Residual Odor

Suppose, friends, a cloth is |defiled::tainted, tarnished [saṅkiliṭṭha]| and stained. Its owners hand it over to a laundryman. The laundryman, having rubbed it with cleaning salt, lye, or cow dung, rinses it in clear water. Even though that cloth becomes pure and bright, there still remains a residual odor of cleaning salt, lye, or cow dung, not yet removed. The laundryman returns it to the owners. The owners place it in a scented chest. Whatever residual odor of cleaning salt, lye, or cow dung was not yet removed comes to be eliminated as well.

In the same way, friends, even though the five lower fetters have been abandoned by a noble disciple, there still remains in him a residual conceit ‘I am,’ desire ‘I am,’ and underlying tendency ‘I am,’ not yet uprooted regarding these five aggregates subject to clinging. Later on, he dwells observing arising and passing away in the five aggregates subject to clinging: ‘Such is form, such is the arising of form, such is the passing away of form; such is feeling ... such is perception ... such are intentional constructs ... such is consciousness, such is the arising of consciousness, such is the passing away of consciousness.’ While he dwells thus observing arising and passing away in these five aggregates subject to clinging, that residual conceit ‘I am,’ desire ‘I am,’ and underlying tendency ‘I am,’ not yet uprooted regarding the five aggregates subject to clinging, comes to be uprooted as well.”

When this was said, the elder bhikkhus said this to the venerable Khemaka: “We did not question the venerable Khemaka intending to trouble him; rather, the venerable Khemaka is capable of explaining, teaching, describing, establishing, clarifying, analyzing, and expounding the Blessed One’s dispensation in detail. And this has been explained, taught, described, established, clarified, analyzed, and expounded in detail by the venerable Khemaka.”

The venerable Khemaka said this. Delighted, the elder bhikkhus rejoiced in the venerable Khemaka’s words. And while this exposition was being spoken, the minds of sixty elder bhikkhus were liberated from the |taints::outflows, discharges; (comm) mental defilements [āsavā]| through not clinging, as well as that of the venerable Khemaka.
