At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Rājagaha::name of a city; capital of Magadha; lit. king’s house [rājagaha]|, in the Cool Grove.

Now at that time, the |householder Anāthapiṇḍika::foremost lay disciple of the Buddha in generosity, whose name means 'giver of alms to the unprotected' [anāthapiṇḍika]| had arrived at Rājagaha on some business. The householder Anāthapiṇḍika heard: “A |Buddha::an awakened one [buddha]|, it is said, has arisen in the world.”

Right then he wished to go see the Blessed One. But then it occurred to the householder Anāthapiṇḍika: “Today is not the time to go see the Blessed One. Tomorrow in good time I will go to see the Blessed One.” And he lay down with mindfulness directed to the Buddha.

During the night, he woke up three times thinking it was dawn. Then the householder Anāthapiṇḍika went to the charnel ground gate. Non-human beings opened the gate for him.

Then, as the householder Anāthapiṇḍika was leaving the city, the light vanished and darkness appeared; fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror arose in him, and right there he wanted to turn back.

Then the |native spirit::a spirit that may be protective or malevolent, a daemon, or a supernatural being [yakkha]| Sivaka, remaining invisible, made his voice heard:

“A hundred elephants, a hundred horses,
a hundred chariots drawn by she-mules,
a thousand maidens
adorned with jeweled earrings—
are not worth a sixteenth part
of a single step taken forward.

Advance, householder!
Advance, householder!
Advancing is better for you,
not turning back!”

Then the darkness vanished for the householder Anāthapiṇḍika and light appeared; the fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror that had arisen subsided.

A second time, the light vanished for the householder Anāthapiṇḍika and darkness appeared; fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror arose [again], and right there he wanted to turn back.

A second time, the native spirit Sivaka, remaining invisible, made his voice heard:

“A hundred elephants, a hundred horses,
a hundred chariots drawn by she-mules,
a thousand maidens
adorned with jeweled earrings—
are not worth a sixteenth part
of a single step taken forward.

Advance, householder!
Advance, householder!
Advancing is better for you,
not turning back!”

Then the darkness vanished for the householder Anāthapiṇḍika and light appeared; the fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror that had arisen subsided.

A third time, the light vanished for the householder Anāthapiṇḍika and darkness appeared; fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror arose [once again], and right there he wanted to turn back.

A third time, the native spirit Sivaka, remaining invisible, made his voice heard:

“A hundred elephants, a hundred horses,
a hundred chariots drawn by she-mules,
a thousand maidens
adorned with jeweled earrings—
are not worth a sixteenth part
of a single step taken forward.

Advance, householder!
Advance, householder!
Advancing is better for you,
not turning back!”

Then the darkness vanished for the householder Anāthapiṇḍika and light appeared; the fear, consternation, and hair-raising terror that had arisen subsided. Then the householder Anāthapiṇḍika went to the Cool Grove, to where the Blessed One was.

At that time, in the hour before dawn, the Blessed One had risen and was walking back and forth in the open air. The Blessed One saw the householder Anāthapiṇḍika coming in the distance. Seeing him, he stepped down from the walkway and sat down on the prepared seat. Having sat down, the Blessed One said to the householder Anāthapiṇḍika: “Come, Sudatta.”

Then the householder Anāthapiṇḍika, thinking: “The Blessed One addresses me by name!”, joyful and elated, prostrated himself right there with his head at the Blessed One’s feet and said to the Blessed One: “I hope, venerable sir, that the Blessed One slept comfortably?”

“He ever sleeps with ease,
the |brahmin::one who has attained the highest spiritual goal; an arahant; a true sage [brāhmaṇa]| who has attained |Nibbāna::complete cooling, letting go of everything, deathless, freedom from calamity, the non-disintegrating; lit. blowing away [nibbāna]|;
who does not stick to |sensual pleasures::desire for sense gratification [kāma]|,
having |become cool::calmed, liberated [sītibhūta]| and |free from attachment::free from grasping, not taking as mine, not appropriating [nirūpadhi]|.

Having cut off every dependence,
having removed |anguish::fear, dread [dara]| from the |heart::core, kernel [hadaya]|;
|the peaceful::calmed, tranquil, composed [upasanta]| one sleeps with ease,
having attained peace of mind.”
