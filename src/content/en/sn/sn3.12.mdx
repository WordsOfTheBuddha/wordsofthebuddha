At Sāvatthi.

Now at that time, among five kings headed by Pasenadi—who were enjoying themselves, fully provided and endowed with the |five cords of sensual pleasure::the pleasant objects of the five physical senses [pañca kāmaguṇā]|—this conversation arose: “What now is the |foremost::best, chief, highest [agga]| of sensual pleasures?”

In this regard, some said: “|Forms::visible objects [rūpā]| are the foremost of sensual pleasures.”

Some said: “|Sounds::auditory objects [saddā]| are the foremost of sensual pleasures.”

Some said: “|Odors::olfactory objects, smells [gandhā]| are the foremost of sensual pleasures.”

Some said: “|Tastes::gustatory objects, flavors [rasā]| are the foremost of sensual pleasures.”

Some said: “|Tangible objects::tactile sensations, touches [phoṭṭhabbā]| are the foremost of sensual pleasures.”

Since those kings were unable to convince one another, King Pasenadi of Kosala said to those kings: “Come, good sirs, let us go to the Blessed One; having approached, let us ask the Blessed One about this matter. In whatever way the Blessed One explains it, that is how we should remember it.”

“Yes, good sir,” those kings replied to King Pasenadi of Kosala.

Then those five kings headed by Pasenadi approached the Blessed One; having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, among us five kings—who were enjoying ourselves, fully provided and endowed with the five cords of sensual pleasure—this conversation arose: ‘What now is the foremost of sensual pleasures?’ Some said: ‘Forms are the foremost of sensual pleasures.’ Some said: ‘Sounds are the foremost of sensual pleasures.’ Some said: ‘Odors are the foremost of sensual pleasures.’ Some said: ‘Tastes are the foremost of sensual pleasures.’ Some said: ‘Tangible objects are the foremost of sensual pleasures.’ What now, venerable sir, is the foremost of sensual pleasures?”

“Great king, I say that what is foremost among the five cords of sensual pleasure is determined by what is |pleasing::agreeable, likeable [manāpa]|. Those very forms, great king, that are pleasing to one person are |disagreeable::unpleasant, unappealing [amanāpa]| to another. When one is |delighted::pleased [attamana]| with certain forms and one’s intention is fulfilled, one does not wish for any other form more excellent or more sublime than those forms. For him, those forms are supreme; for him, those forms are |unsurpassed::highest, incomparable [anuttara]|.

Those very sounds, great king, that are pleasing to one person are disagreeable to another. When one is delighted with certain sounds and one’s intention is fulfilled, one does not wish for any other sound more excellent or more sublime than those sounds. For him, those sounds are supreme; for him, those sounds are unsurpassed. Those very odors, great king, that are pleasing to one person are disagreeable to another. When one is delighted with certain odors and one’s intention is fulfilled, one does not wish for any other odor more excellent or more sublime than those odors. For him, those odors are supreme; for him, those odors are unsurpassed. Those very tastes, great king, that are pleasing to one person are disagreeable to another. When one is delighted with certain tastes and one’s intention is fulfilled, one does not wish for any other taste more excellent or more sublime than those tastes. For him, those tastes are supreme; for him, those tastes are unsurpassed. Those very tangible objects, great king, that are pleasing to one person are disagreeable to another. When one is delighted with certain tangible objects and one’s intention is fulfilled, one does not wish for any other tangible object more excellent or more sublime than those tangible objects. For him, those tangible objects are supreme; for him, those tangible objects are unsurpassed.”

Now at that time, the lay follower Candanaṅgalika was sitting in that assembly. Then the lay follower Candanaṅgalika rose from his seat, draped his upper robe over one shoulder, raised his joined palms toward the Blessed One, and said to the Blessed One: “An inspiration has come to me, Blessed One! An inspiration has come to me, Accomplished One!”

“Then express your inspiration, Candanaṅgalika,” said the Blessed One.

Then the lay follower Candanaṅgalika, in the presence of the Blessed One, extolled him with a suitable verse:

“Just like a fragrant red lotus blooming
in the morning, its fragrance |undiminished::unfaded, not gone away [avītagandha]|;
behold |Aṅgīrasa::the Radiant One, an epithet of the Buddha [aṅgīrasa]| shining forth,
like the sun blazing in the sky.”

Then those five kings presented the lay follower Candanaṅgalika with five upper robes. And the lay follower Candanaṅgalika presented the Blessed One with those five upper robes.
