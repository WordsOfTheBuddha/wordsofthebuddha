Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was residing among the Sakyans in |Kapilavatthu::name of the capital city of the Sakyans, the birthplace of the Buddha [kapilavatthu]|, in the Banyan Park. Then |Mahānāma::name of a Sakyan layman; lit. great name [mahānāma]| the Sakyan approached the Blessed One. Having approached and paid homage to the Blessed One, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Mahānāma the Sakyan said to the Blessed One:

“Venerable sir, this Kapilavatthu is prosperous and flourishing, populous, crowded with people, with congested alleyways. When I have attended upon the Blessed One or |esteemed::worthy of respect, mind-developed [manobhāvanīya]| bhikkhus, and am entering Kapilavatthu in the evening, I encounter |runaway::straying, roaming out of control [bhanta]| elephants, runaway horses, runaway chariots, runaway carts, and reckless people. At that time, venerable sir, my mindfulness regarding the Buddha |lapses::is lost, forgotten [mussati]|, my mindfulness regarding the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| lapses, and my mindfulness regarding the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| lapses. Then, venerable sir, this thought occurs to me: ‘If I were to die at this time, what would be my destination? What would be my future state?’”

“Do not fear, Mahānāma! Do not fear, Mahānāma! Your death will not be bad; your passing will not be bad. Mahānāma, a disciple of the Noble Ones endowed with four qualities |slants toward Nibbāna::leans toward unbinding [nibbānaninna]|, |slopes toward Nibbāna::tilts toward unbinding [nibbānapoṇa]|, and |inclines toward Nibbāna::bends toward unbinding [nibbānapabbhāra]|. What four? Here, Mahānāma, a disciple of the Noble Ones is endowed with |perfect clarity::unshakable confidence that arises through direct experience, by personally seeing the benefits in one’s own practice [aveccappasāda]| in the Buddha thus: ‘The Blessed One is <collapse>an Arahant, perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons,</collapse> teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ He is endowed with perfect clarity in the Dhamma... He is endowed with perfect clarity in the Saṅgha... He is endowed with virtues dear to the Noble Ones<collapse>—unbroken, untorn, not spotty, unblemished, freeing, praised by the wise, irreproachable,</collapse> and |leading to collectedness::leading to samadhi, conducive to mental composure [samādhisaṃvattanika]|.

Suppose, Mahānāma, a tree slants toward the east, slopes toward the east, and inclines toward the east. If it were |severed at the root::cut at the base [mūlacchinna]|, which way would it fall?”

“In whichever direction it slants, slopes, and inclines, venerable sir.”

“Even so, Mahānāma, a disciple of the Noble Ones endowed with these four qualities slants toward Nibbāna, slopes toward Nibbāna, and inclines toward Nibbāna.”
