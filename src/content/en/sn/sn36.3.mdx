---
title: Pahāna sutta - Abandoning
description: Not discerning feeling leads to underlying tendencies to desire, aversion, and ignorance.
qualities: felt experience, sensual desire, ignorance, aversion
theme: wisdom, cultivating discernment
slug: sn36.3
priority: 1.1
---

“There are these three |felt experiences::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]|. What three?

Pleasant felt experience, painful felt experience, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant felt experience.

In regard to pleasant felt experience, the |underlying tendency to desire::latent disposition towards sensuality [rāgānusaya]| should be abandoned. In regard to painful felt experience, the |underlying tendency to aversion::inherent inclination towards resistance [paṭighānusaya]| should be abandoned. In regard to neither-painful-nor-pleasant felt experience, the |underlying tendency to ignorance::inherent inclination towards not understanding [avijjānusaya]| should be abandoned.

When|, bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has |abandoned::given up, dispelled, eliminated, left behind [pahīna]| the underlying tendency to desire in regard to pleasant felt experience, has abandoned the underlying tendency to aversion in regard to painful felt experience, and has abandoned the underlying tendency to ignorance in regard to neither-painful-nor-pleasant felt experience—he is called|, bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu |without underlying tendencies::without dormant dispositions; no sleeping alongside [niranusaya]|, one who sees rightly, who has cut off |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]|, uprooted the |fetters::chains, bonds, attachments, thing which binds [saṁyojanā]|, and through the complete comprehension of |conceit::self-view expressed as comparison—seeing oneself as superior, inferior, or equal; the persistent “I am” conceit (asmimāna) that underlies identification [māna]|, has made an end of |suffering::from intense and painful suffering to the mildest of discontentedness, stress, unsatisfactoriness, dis-ease [dukkha]|.

When experiencing pleasure,
if one does not |discern::distinguish, understand, know clearly [pajānāti]| feeling,
the underlying tendency to desire is present
for one who does not see the |escape::way out, remedy [nissaraṇa]|.

When experiencing pain,
if one does not discern feeling,
the underlying tendency to aversion is present
for one who does not see the escape.

The |peaceful::tranquil; lit. calmed [santa]| neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling,
taught by the |one of vast wisdom::one of extensive distinctive knowledge, one having the quality of discernment [bhūripañña]|;
if one seeks delight even in that,
one is still not freed from suffering.

But when a bhikkhu exerts |continuous effort::ardency, zealously, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|,
and does not neglect |clear awareness::attentiveness, clear and full comprehension [sampajañña]|;
then that |wise one::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled [paṇḍita]|
|completely comprehends::fully understands [parijānāti]| all feelings.

Having completely comprehended feelings,
|taintless::undefiled, free from effluents, free from mental defilements [anāsava]| in this very life,
with the breakup of the body, firmly established in the |Dhamma::the ultimate truth that the Buddha’s teachings point to [dhamma]|,
a |master of knowledge::one who has complete understanding [vedagū]| cannot be designated.”
