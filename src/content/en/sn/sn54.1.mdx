---
title: Ekadhamma sutta - One Thing
description: Cultivating mindfulness while breathing in and out yields great fruit and benefit. This sixteen-step practice guides a meditator from foundational breath awareness to calming bodily and mental constructs, ultimately culminating in the profound observation of impermanence and complete relinquishment.
qualities: breathing, recognition of impermanence
theme: wisdom
slug: sn54.1
---

At Sāvatthi.

There, the Blessed One said this:

“One thing, |bhikkhus,::::| when cultivated and frequently practiced, is of great fruit and great benefit. What is that one thing? Mindfulness while breathing in and out.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| is |mindfulness while breathing in and out::mindfulness during inhalation and exhalation [ānāpānassati]| cultivated and frequently practiced so that it is of great fruit and great benefit?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu, having gone to the forest or to the foot of a tree or to an empty hut, sits down; having folded his legs crosswise, aligns his body upright, and establishes mindfulness |as his primary focal point::as first priority, directed towards the meditation object; lit. around the front [parimukhaṁ]|.

Just mindful, he breathes in; just mindful, he breathes out.

#### Mindfulness of Body

1.) Breathing in long, he |discerns::distinguishes, understands, knows clearly [pajānāti]| the long in-breath; breathing out long, he discerns the long out-breath.
2.) Breathing in short, he discerns the short in-breath; breathing out short, he discerns the short out-breath.
3.) He trains to |experience the whole body::be conscious of the whole body, be sensitive to the whole process [sabbakāyapaṭisaṃvedī]| while breathing in; he trains to experience the whole body while breathing out.
4.) He trains to |settle::calm, still [passambhayanta]| the |bodily constructs::bodily processes associated with breathing, specifically the in-and-out breath. It encompasses the physical movements and sensations that arise from the act of breathing. [kāyasaṅkhārā]| while breathing in; he trains to settle the bodily constructs while breathing out.

#### Mindfulness of Felt Experience

1.) He trains to |experience uplifting joy::heartfelt or intense joy, feeling of love, rapture lit. lovely feeling [pītipaṭisaṃvedī]| while breathing in; he trains to experience uplifting joy while breathing out.
2.) He trains to |experience ease::contentment, happiness, pleasant abiding [sukhapaṭisaṃvedī]| while breathing in; he trains to experience ease while breathing out.
3.) He trains to |experience mental constructs::be sensitive to the mental processes; (comm) conscious of feeling and perception [cittasaṅkhārapaṭisaṃvedī]| while breathing in; he trains to experience mental constructs while breathing out.
4.) He trains to settle the mental constructs while breathing in; he trains to settle the mental constructs while breathing out.

#### Mindfulness of Mind

1.) He trains to |experience the mind::be conscious of the psyche, be sensitive to the mind [cittapaṭisaṃvedī]| while breathing in; he trains to experience the mind while breathing out.
2.) He trains to gladden the mind while breathing in; he trains to gladden the mind while breathing out.
3.) He trains to |collect::compose, stabilize [samādaha]| the mind while breathing in; he trains to collect the mind while breathing out.
4.) He trains to |disengage::detach, release; lit. making free [vimocayanta]| the mind while breathing in; he trains to disengage the mind while breathing out.

#### Mindfulness of Mental Qualities

1.) He trains to |observe impermanence::watching instability [aniccānupassī]| while breathing in; he trains to observe impermanence while breathing out.
2.) He trains to |observe fading of desire::watch dispassion [virāgānupassī]| while breathing in; he trains to observe fading of desire while breathing out.
3.) He trains to |observe cessation::watch the natural ending of phenomena [nirodhānupassī]| while breathing in; he trains to observe cessation while breathing out.
4.) He trains to |observe relinquishment::watch letting go of craving, attachment, identification with processes [paṭinissaggānupassī]| while breathing in; he trains to observe relinquishment while breathing out.

Mindfulness while breathing in and out, |bhikkhus,::::| when cultivated and frequently practiced in this way, is of great fruit and great benefit.”
