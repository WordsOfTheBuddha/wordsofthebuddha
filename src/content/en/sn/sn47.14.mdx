At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling among the Vajjis at |Ukkacelā::name of a Vajjian town; lit. cloth torch [ukkacelā]| on the banks of the river |Ganges::one of the five great rivers of ancient India [gaṅgā]| together with a large |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]| of bhikkhus, not long after Sāriputta and Moggallāna had attained |final Nibbāna::complete cooling, full quenching, total emancipation, dying one’s final death [parinibbuta]|. Now, at that time, the Blessed One was sitting in the open air surrounded by the bhikkhu Saṅgha.

Then, surveying the silent Saṅgha of bhikkhus, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus:

“This assembly|, bhikkhus,::::| seems empty to me now that |Sāriputta::foremost disciple of the Buddha in great wisdom [sāriputta]| and |Moggallāna::foremost disciple of the Buddha in psychic power; lit. Moggallāna the great [moggallāna]| have attained final Nibbāna. The assembly was not empty for me, |bhikkhus,::::| and I had no concern for that direction in which Sāriputta and Moggallāna were dwelling. |Bhikkhus,::::| those Blessed Ones who were Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones in the past, also had a pair of disciples who were the foremost, just as Sāriputta and Moggallāna were for me. |Bhikkhus,::::| those Blessed Ones who will be Arahants, perfectly Awakened Ones in the future, will also have a pair of disciples who will be the foremost, just as Sāriputta and Moggallāna were for me.

It is wonderful|, bhikkhus,::::| of the disciples; it is marvelous|, bhikkhus,::::| of the disciples: that they carry out the Teacher’s instructions and act according to his advise, and they are dear and agreeable to the four assemblies, respected and esteemed.

It is wonderful|, bhikkhus,::::| of the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|; it is marvelous|, bhikkhus,::::| of the Tathāgata: that even when such a pair of disciples has attained final Nibbāna, there is no sorrow or lamentation in the Tathāgata. How|, bhikkhus,::::| could it possibly be obtained here: ‘May what is born, come to be, conditioned, and |subject to disintegration::liable to fall apart [palokadhamma]| not disintegrate!’? This is impossible.

Just as|, bhikkhus,::::| a great tree standing sound and possessing heartwood might have its largest branches break off, so too, |bhikkhus,::::| in the great Saṅgha of bhikkhus standing sound and possessing heartwood, Sāriputta and Moggallāna have attained final Nibbāna.

How|, bhikkhus,::::| could it possibly be obtained here: ‘May what is born, come to be, conditioned, and subject to disintegration not disintegrate!’? This is impossible.

Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| dwell |with yourselves as your own island::self-reliant, independent [attadīpa]|, with yourselves as your own refuge, with no other refuge; dwell with the Dhamma as your island, with the Dhamma as your refuge, with no other refuge.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does a bhikkhu dwell as an island unto himself, with himself as his refuge, not dependent on another as a refuge, with the Dhamma as his island, with the Dhamma as his refuge, not dependent on another as a refuge? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world. Similarly, he dwells observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself, the mind in and of itself, and |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world.

This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu dwells as an island unto himself, with himself as his refuge, not dependent on another as a refuge, with the Dhamma as his island, with the Dhamma as his refuge, not dependent on another as a refuge.

Whoever|, bhikkhus,::::| now or after my passing, dwells as an island unto themselves, with themselves as their own refuge, not dependent on another as a refuge; with the Dhamma as their island, with the Dhamma as their refuge, not dependent on another as a refuge; they, bhikkhus, will be the foremost of those who are keen on the training.”
