description: The venerable Bhadda asks the venerable Ānanda why the good Dhamma lasts long or does not last long after the Tathāgata’s final Nibbāna. Ānanda answers that it depends on whether the four establishments of mindfulness are cultivated and frequently practiced.

The same setting. Seated to one side, the venerable Bhadda said this to the venerable Ānanda:

“Friend Ānanda, what is the cause, what is the condition, whereby after the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| has attained |final Nibbāna::complete cooling, full quenching, total emancipation, dying one’s final death [parinibbāna]| the |good Dhamma::good teaching, true doctrine [saddhamma]| does not last long? And what, friend Ānanda, is the cause, what is the condition, whereby after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma lasts long?”

“Good, good, friend Bhadda! Your approach is good, your intuition is good, and your inquiry is good. For thus you ask, friend Bhadda: ‘Friend Ānanda, what is the cause, what is the condition, whereby after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma does not last long? And what, friend Ānanda, is the cause, what is the condition, whereby after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma lasts long?’”

“Yes, friend.”

“Friend, it is because the four |establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as the direct path of practice [cattāro + satipaṭṭhāna]| are not cultivated and not frequently practiced that after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma does not last long. And it is because the four establishments of mindfulness are cultivated and frequently practiced that after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma lasts long.

What four? 1.) Here, friend, a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; 2.) he dwells observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| <collapse>in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> 3.) he dwells observing the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| <collapse>in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> 4.) he dwells observing the |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. Friend, it is because these four establishments of mindfulness are not cultivated and not frequently practiced that after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma does not last long. And it is because these four establishments of mindfulness are cultivated and frequently practiced that after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma lasts long.”
