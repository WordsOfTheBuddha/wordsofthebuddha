At Sāvatthi.

Then the venerable |Sāriputta::foremost disciple of the Buddha in great wisdom [sāriputta]| approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Sāriputta said to the Blessed One:

“Venerable sir, it is said ‘a |great man::an eminent person, a person of stature [mahāpurisa]|, a great man.’ In what way, venerable sir, is one a great man?”

“With a |liberated mind::a mind that is freed, emancipated [vimuttacitta]|, Sāriputta, I say one is a ‘great man.’ Without a liberated mind, I say one is ‘not a great man.’

And how, Sāriputta, does one have a liberated mind? Here, Sāriputta, a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world. As he dwells observing the body in and of itself, his mind |becomes dispassionate::is detached [virajjati]| and is liberated from the |taints::outflows, discharges; (comm) mental defilements [āsavā]| by not clinging. He dwells observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself ... dwells observing the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| in and of itself ... dwells observing the |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. As he dwells observing the mental qualities in and of themselves, his mind becomes dispassionate and is liberated from the taints by not clinging. This is how, Sāriputta, one has a liberated mind.

With a liberated mind, Sāriputta, I say one is a ‘great man.’ Without a liberated mind, I say one is ‘not a great man.’”
