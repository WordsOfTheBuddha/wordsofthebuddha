---
title: Ariya sutta - Noble
description: The Buddha teaches that the four establishments of mindfulness, when cultivated and frequently practiced, are noble and leading to liberation.

“|Bhikkhus,::::| these four |establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as the direct path of practice [cattāro + satipaṭṭhāna]|, when cultivated and frequently practiced, are noble and |leading to liberation::redemptive, leading to deliverance [niyyānika]|; they lead one who acts accordingly to the complete |wearing away of suffering::extinction of stress, termination of tension [dukkhakkhaya]|. What four?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; he dwells observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself ... dwells observing the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| in and of itself ... dwells observing the |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world.

These four establishments of mindfulness, |bhikkhus,::::| when cultivated and frequently practiced, are noble and leading to liberation; they lead one who acts accordingly to the complete |wearing away of suffering::extinction of stress, termination of tension [dukkhakkhaya]|.”
