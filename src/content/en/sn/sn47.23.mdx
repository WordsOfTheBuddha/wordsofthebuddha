At one time, the venerable Ānanda and the venerable Bhadda were dwelling at |Pāṭaliputta::capital of Magadha, modern Patna [pāṭaliputta]|, at the |Kukkuṭa’s park::a monastery in Pāṭaliputta donated by the merchant Kukkuṭa [kukkuṭārāma]|. Then, in the late afternoon, the venerable Bhadda, having emerged from |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, approached the venerable Ānanda. Having approached, he exchanged greetings with the venerable Ānanda and, after engaging in a courteous and polite conversation, sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Bhadda said this to the venerable Ānanda:

“Friend Ānanda, what is the cause, what is the condition, whereby there is |decline::deterioration, falling away, regression [parihāna]| of the |good Dhamma::good teaching, true doctrine [saddhamma]|? And what, friend Ānanda, is the cause, what is the condition, whereby there is |non-decline::non-regressing [aparihāna]| of the good Dhamma?”

“Good, good, friend Bhadda! Your approach is good, your intuition is good, and your inquiry is good. For thus you ask, friend Bhadda: ‘Friend Ānanda, what is the cause, what is the condition, whereby there is decline of the good Dhamma? And what, friend Ānanda, is the cause, what is the condition, whereby there is non-decline of the good Dhamma?’”

“Yes, friend.”

“Friend, it is because the four |establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as the direct path of practice [cattāro + satipaṭṭhāna]| are not cultivated and not frequently practiced that there is decline of the good Dhamma. And it is because the four establishments of mindfulness are cultivated and frequently practiced that there is non-decline of the good Dhamma.

What four? 1.) Here, friend, a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; 2.) |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| <collapse>in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> 3.) |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| <collapse>in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> 4.) |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. Friend, it is because these four establishments of mindfulness are not cultivated and not frequently practiced that there is decline of the good Dhamma. And it is because these four establishments of mindfulness are cultivated and frequently practiced that there is non-decline of the good Dhamma.”
