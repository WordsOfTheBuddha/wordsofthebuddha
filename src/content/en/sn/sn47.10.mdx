Then, early in the morning, the venerable Ānanda dressed, took his alms bowl and outer robe, and went to a certain |bhikkhunīs’ quarters::nuns’ lodging [bhikkhunupassaya]|. Having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat. Then several bhikkhunīs approached the venerable Ānanda; having approached, they paid homage to the venerable Ānanda and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, those bhikkhunīs said to the venerable Ānanda:

“Here, venerable Ānanda, several bhikkhunīs dwelling with their minds well established in the four establishments of mindfulness perceive successive stages of noble distinction.”

“So it is, sisters! So it is, sisters! For whoever, sisters, whether a bhikkhu or a bhikkhunī, dwells with their mind well established in the four establishments of mindfulness, it is to be expected of them: ‘They will perceive successive stages of noble distinction.’”

Then the venerable Ānanda, having instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened those bhikkhunīs with a talk on the Dhamma, rose from his seat and departed. Then, having walked for alms in Sāvatthi, after his meal and upon returning from alms, the venerable Ānanda went to the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One:

“Here, venerable sir, early in the morning, having dressed and taken my alms bowl and outer robe, I went to a certain bhikkhunīs’ quarters. Having arrived, I sat down on the prepared seat. Then, venerable sir, several bhikkhunīs approached me; having approached, they paid homage to me and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, venerable sir, those bhikkhunīs said to me: ‘Here, venerable Ānanda, several bhikkhunīs dwelling with their minds well established in the four establishments of mindfulness perceive successive stages of noble distinction.’ When this was said, venerable sir, I said to those bhikkhunīs: ‘So it is, sisters! So it is, sisters! For whoever, sisters, whether a bhikkhu or a bhikkhunī, dwells with their mind well established in the four establishments of mindfulness, it is to be expected of them: “They will perceive successive stages of noble distinction.”’”

“So it is, Ānanda! So it is, Ānanda! For whoever, Ānanda, whether a bhikkhu or a bhikkhunī, dwells with their mind well established in the four establishments of mindfulness, it is to be expected of them: ‘They will perceive successive stages of noble distinction.’

What four? Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, |with continuous effort::ardent, zealous, with energy, with application; lit. burning [ātāpī]|, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world. While he is dwelling observing the body in and of itself, based on the body there arises a |fever::mental torment, distress, strong desire, discomfort [pariḷāha]| in the body, or |sluggishness::stickiness, inertia [līnatta]| of mind, or the mind is |scattered::distracted, (comm) because of restlessness and worry; lit. throws apart [vikkhipati]| externally. That bhikkhu should then direct his mind toward some |inspiring theme::uplifting meditation subject, an image that is worthy of faith or that which evokes a spiritual experience [pasādanīya + nimitta]|. When he directs the mind to some inspiring theme, |joy::cheerfulness, gladness; a fresh and mild happiness arising from a sense of spiritual well-being and a clear conscience [pāmojja]| arises. When one is joyful, |uplifting joy::mental exhilaration; it ranges from a gentle delight to overwhelming rapture [pīti]| arises. In one whose mind is filled with uplifting joy, the body |becomes tranquil::calms down, relaxes [passambhati]|. One tranquil in body dwells in |contentment::ease, happiness, pleasant abiding [sukha]|. The mind of one who dwells in contentment |becomes collected::is calmed, becomes composed, becomes stable [samādhiyati]|. He |reflects::considers, discerns [paṭisañcikkhati]| thus: ‘The purpose for which I directed my mind has been accomplished. Let me now withdraw it.’ He withdraws it and does not reflect or examine. He discerns: ‘|Free from reflection::free of thought, a second jhāna or above [avitakka]| and examination, internally |mindful::endowed with active presence of mind; possessing lucid retention, and recollection [satimant]|, there is contentment.’

Furthermore, Ānanda, a bhikkhu dwells observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| in and of itself ... dwells observing the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| in and of itself ... dwells observing the |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. While he is dwelling observing mental qualities in and of themselves, based on mental qualities there arises a fever in the body, or sluggishness of mind, or the mind is scattered externally. That bhikkhu should then direct his mind toward some inspiring theme. When he directs the mind to some inspiring theme, joy arises. When one is joyful, uplifting joy arises. In one whose mind is filled with uplifting joy, the body becomes tranquil. One tranquil in body dwells in contentment. The mind of one who dwells in contentment becomes collected. He reflects thus: ‘The purpose for which I directed my mind has been accomplished. Let me now withdraw it.’ He withdraws it and does not reflect or examine. He discerns: ‘Free from reflection and examination, internally mindful, there is contentment.’ In this way, Ānanda, there is |directed::intentional [paṇidhāya]| |cultivation::development, meditation [bhāvanā]|.

And how, Ānanda, is there |undirected cultivation::meditation without directing the mind to an inspiring theme [appaṇidhāya + bhāvanā]|? Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘|Continuously::presently, lit. after and before [pacchāpure]|, it is |uncontracted::unconstricted [asaṅkhitta]|, |liberated::freed [vimutta]|, and |undirected::unguided, without intent [appaṇihita]|.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the body in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the felt experience in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the mind in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ Not directing the mind externally, Ānanda, a bhikkhu discerns: ‘The mind is not directed externally.’ And he discerns: ‘Continuously, it is uncontracted, liberated, and undirected.’ And furthermore, he discerns: ‘When dwelling observing the mental qualities in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware, mindful, there is contentment.’ In this way, Ānanda, there is undirected cultivation.

Thus, Ānanda, I have taught directed cultivation, and I have taught undirected cultivation. Whatever should be done by a teacher seeking the welfare of his disciples, out of compassion, that I have done for you, Ānanda. Here are these roots of trees, here are these empty huts. Meditate, Ānanda, do not be negligent; do not be |regretful::remorseful; lit. remembering back negatively [vippaṭisārī]| later. This is our instruction to you.”

The Blessed One said this. The venerable Ānanda was delighted and pleased with the Blessed One’s words.
