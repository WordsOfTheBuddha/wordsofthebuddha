“Reborn in the |Aviha heaven::the world of the "not falling" deities [aviha]|,
seven bhikkhus have been freed.
With the exhaustion of |lust and aversion::desire and hatred, passion and blame [rāgadosa]|,
they have crossed over |entanglement in::attachment to, ensnarement in [visattika]| the world.”

“And who are those who crossed the mire,
the realm of death, so hard to cross?
Who, having left behind the human body,
have overcome the celestial yoke?”

“Upaka and Palagaṇḍa,
and Pukkusāti, these three;
Bhaddiya and Khaṇḍadeva,
and Bāhuraggi and Siṅgiya;
they, having left behind the human body,
have overcome the celestial yoke.”

“You speak well of them,
who have abandoned the snare of Māra.
Having comprehended whose |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|
did they sever the bond of existence?”

“None other than the Blessed One!
None other than your instruction!
It was your Dhamma that they understood,
and severed the bond of existence.

Where |name and form::The mutually dependent pair of mental designation and material perception: "Name" consists of feeling, perception, intention, contact, and attention; "Form" consists of the four great primaries (earth, water, fire, wind) and derived form, experienced as perception of form through sensory resistance [nāmarūpa]|
completely cease without remainder—
having comprehended that Dhamma of yours here,
they severed the bond of existence.”

“You speak words that are profound,
hard to understand, extremely hard to comprehend.
Having understood whose Dhamma
do you speak such words?”

“In the past I was a potter
in Vekaḷiṅga named Ghaṭīkāra.
I supported my mother and father,
a lay follower of the Buddha Kassapa.

Abstaining from sexual intercourse,
living the |spiritual life::celibate life [brahmacārī]| free from worldly ties;
I was your fellow villager,
in the past I was your friend.

And so I know these
seven bhikkhus who are freed;
with passion and aversion completely eradicated,
they have crossed over entanglement in the world.”

“So it was then,
just as you say, Bhaggava!
In the past you were a potter
in Vekaḷiṅga named Ghaṭīkāra.
You supported your mother and father,
a lay follower of the Buddha Kassapa.

Abstaining from sexual intercourse,
living the spiritual life free from worldly ties;
you were my fellow villager,
in the past you were my friend.”

“Thus was the reunion
of companions of old times,
of the two Awakened Ones,
bearing their final body.”
