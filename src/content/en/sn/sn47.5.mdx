At Sāvatthi.

There the Blessed One said this:

“|Bhikkhus,::::| if one speaking rightly, were to speak of ‘|a heap of the unwholesome::a pile of the unbeneficial [akusalarāsi]|,’ it is the |five hindrances::the five mental obstacles—sensual desire, ill will, dullness and drowsiness, restlessness and worry, and doubt—that block clarity and stillness [pañca nīvaraṇā]| that one, speaking rightly, would call ‘a heap of the unwholesome.’ For this|, bhikkhus,::::| altogether is a heap of the unwholesome, that is, the five hindrances. What five? 1.) The hindrance of |sensual desire::interest in sensual pleasure, sensual impulse [kāmacchanda]|, 2.) the hindrance of |ill will::intentional act of mentally opposing or rejecting others; an intentional construct fueled by aversion, directed against kindness or compassion. It manifests as hostility of will, impeding goodwill and fostering internal or external conflict. [byāpāda]|, 3.) the hindrance of |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness lit. stiffness (of mind/body due to tiredness) [thinamiddha]|, 4.) the hindrance of |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]|, and 5.) the hindrance of |doubt::uncertainty, indecisiveness, lack of confidence in the Buddha’s teachings [vicikicchā]|. |Bhikkhus,::::| if one speaking rightly, were to speak of ‘a heap of the unwholesome,’ it is these five hindrances that one, speaking rightly, would call ‘a heap of the unwholesome.’ For this|, bhikkhus,::::| altogether is a heap of the unwholesome, that is, the five hindrances.

And if one speaking rightly, were to speak of ‘|a heap of the wholesome::a pile of the beneficial [kusalarāsi]|,’ it is the |four establishments of mindfulness::four foundations or objective domains of mindfulness that one gradually establishes and cultivates as a direct way of practice. [Read more in MN 10 - Satipaṭṭhānasutta - Establishments of Mindfulness](/mn10) [cattāro + satipaṭṭhāna]| that one, speaking rightly, would call ‘a heap of the wholesome.’ For this|, bhikkhus,::::| altogether is a heap of the wholesome, that is, the four establishments of mindfulness. What four? 1.) Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu dwells observing the body in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| with regard to the world; 2.) he dwells observing the |felt experience::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, feeling felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| <collapse>in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> 3.) he dwells observing the |mind::mindset, disposition; the affective state of awareness experiencing the present moment [citta]| <collapse>in and of itself, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world;</collapse> 4.) he dwells observing the |mental qualities::characteristics, traits, and tendencies of the mind, shaped by repeated actions and sustained attention, guided by particular ways of understanding; they may be wholesome or unwholesome, bright or dark [dhammā]| in and of themselves, with continuous effort, clearly aware and with excellent recollection, having removed craving and distress with regard to the world. If one speaking rightly, were to speak of ‘a heap of the wholesome,’ it is these four establishments of mindfulness that one, speaking rightly, would call ‘a heap of the wholesome.’ For this|, bhikkhus,::::| altogether is a heap of the wholesome, that is, the four establishments of mindfulness.”
