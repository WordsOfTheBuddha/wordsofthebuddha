At Sāvatthi.

Seated to one side, venerable Rādha addressed the Blessed One:

“‘Being, being,’ is said, venerable sir. In what way, venerable sir, is one called a being?”

“Rādha, in |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]|, whatever |desire::intention, wish, impulse, interest [chanda]|, |passion::intense desire, strong emotion, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāga]|, |delight::pleasure, enjoyment, satisfaction [nandī]|, and |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]| there is—because one is attached there, caught fast there, therefore one is called a ‘being.’ In |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]|<collapse>, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is—because one is attached there, caught fast there, therefore one is called a ‘being.’ In</collapse> |perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [saññā]|<collapse>, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is—because one is attached there, caught fast there, therefore one is called a ‘being.’ In</collapse> |intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]|<collapse>, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is—because one is attached there, caught fast there, therefore one is called a ‘being.’ In</collapse> |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]|, whatever desire, passion, delight, and craving there is—because one is attached there, caught fast there, therefore one is called a ‘being.’

Just as, Rādha, young boys or girls play with |sandcastles::little houses of sand or dirt [paṁsvāgāraka]|: as long as they are not free from passion, desire, affection, thirst, fever, and craving for those sandcastles, they are attached to them, cherish them, treasure them, and take them as their own. But when, Rādha, the young boys or girls are free from passion, desire, affection, thirst, fever, and craving for those sandcastles, then with their hands and feet they scatter them, demolish them, destroy them, and make them unplayable.

In just this way, Rādha, you too should scatter, demolish, and destroy form, and make it unplayable; you should practice for the |wearing away of craving::depletion of desire, extinction of longing [taṇhākkhaya]|. You should scatter, demolish, and destroy feeling, and make it unplayable; you should practice for the wearing away of craving. You should scatter, demolish, and destroy perception<collapse>, and make it unplayable; you should practice for the wearing away of craving. You should scatter, demolish, and destroy</collapse> intentional constructs, and make them unplayable; you should practice for the wearing away of craving. You should scatter, demolish, and destroy consciousness, and make it unplayable; you should practice for the wearing away of craving. For, Rādha, the wearing away of craving is Nibbāna.”
