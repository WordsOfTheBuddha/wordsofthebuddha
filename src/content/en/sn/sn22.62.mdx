At Sāvatthi.

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these three |pathways of language::modes of linguistic expression [niruttipatha]|, |pathways of designation::modes of naming or identifying [adhivacanapatha]|, and |pathways of description::modes of conceptual formulation [paññattipatha]| that are |unconfounded::unmingled, unmixed, unconfused; lit. not sprinkled together [asaṅkiṇṇa]|, never before confounded, which are not being confounded, nor will they be confounded, that are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins. What three? Whatever |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]||, bhikkhus,::::| is past, ceased, and changed: the designation, label, and description of ‘it was’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’

Whatever |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, the experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| is past, ceased, and changed: the designation, label, and description of ‘it was’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’

Whatever |perception::The mental process of recognizing and giving meaning to experience. It marks sensory information by signs, labels, or associations drawn from memory and the field of contact. Perception shapes how one experiences the world; third of the five aggregates [saññā]| <collapse>is past, ceased, and changed: the designation, label, and description of ‘it was’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’</collapse>

Whatever |intentional constructs::the constructive activity that shapes each moment of experience, expressed as bodily, verbal, and mental formations; the accumulated conditioning — patterns, tendencies, and habits — produced by prior action [saṅkhārā]| are past, ceased, and changed: the designation, label, and description of ‘they were’ applies to them, not the designation ‘they are,’ nor the designation ‘they will be.’

Whatever |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object [viññāṇa]| is past, ceased, and changed: the designation, label, and description of ‘it was’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’

Whatever form|, bhikkhus,::::| has not been born, has not become manifest: the designation, label, and description of ‘it will be’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it was.’

Whatever feeling has not been born, has not become manifest: the designation, label, and description of ‘it will be’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it was.’

Whatever perception <collapse>has not been born, has not become manifest: the designation, label, and description of ‘it will be’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it was.’</collapse>

Whatever intentional constructs have not been born, have not become manifest: the designation, label, and description of ‘they will be’ applies to them, not the designation ‘they are,’ nor the designation ‘they were.’

Whatever consciousness has not been born, has not become manifest: the designation, label, and description of ‘it will be’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it is,’ nor the designation ‘it was.’

Whatever form|, bhikkhus,::::| has come into being and manifested: the designation, label, and description of ‘it is’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it was,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’

Whatever feeling has come into being and manifested: the designation, label, and description of ‘it is’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it was,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’

Whatever perception <collapse>has come into being and manifested: the designation, label, and description of ‘it is’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it was,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’</collapse>

Whatever intentional constructs have come into being and manifested: the designation, label, and description of ‘they are’ applies to them, not the designation ‘they were,’ nor the designation ‘they will be.’

Whatever consciousness has come into being and manifested: the designation, label, and description of ‘it is’ applies to it, not the designation ‘it was,’ nor the designation ‘it will be.’

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the three pathways of language, pathways of designation, and pathways of description that are unconfounded, never before confounded, which are not being confounded, nor will they be confounded, that are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins.

|Bhikkhus,::::| even those teachers from |Okkala::inhabitants of the country Ukkala (modern day Orissa) [ukkalā]|, Vassa and Bhañña, |who held the doctrine of non-causality::who adhered to the theory that nothing has a reason [ahetukavāda]|, |the doctrine of non-action::who adhered to the theory of the inefficacy of action [akiriyavāda]|, and |the doctrine of ethical nihilism::who adhered to the theory of ethical inefficacy; lit. there is nothing doctrine [natthikavāda]|, would not think that these three pathways of language, pathways of designation, and pathways of description should be criticized and rejected. Why is that? For fear of blame, attack, censure, and reproach.”
