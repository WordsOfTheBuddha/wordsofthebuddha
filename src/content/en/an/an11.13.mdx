At one time, the Blessed One was residing among the Sakyans in |Kapilavatthu::name of the capital city of the Sakyans, the birthplace of the Buddha [kapilavatthu]|, in the Banyan Park.

Now at that time, the Blessed One wished to enter upon the rains residence at Sāvatthi.

Nandiya the Sakyan heard: “The Blessed One, it seems, wishes to enter upon the rains residence at Sāvatthi.” Then it occurred to Nandiya the Sakyan: “What if I too were to enter upon the rains residence at Sāvatthi? There I will attend to my work, and from time to time I will get to see the Blessed One.”

Then the Blessed One entered upon the rains residence at Sāvatthi. Nandiya the Sakyan also entered upon the rains residence at Sāvatthi. There he attended to his work, and from time to time he got to see the Blessed One.

At that time, several bhikkhus were making a robe for the Blessed One, thinking: “When the robe is finished, at the end of the three months, the Blessed One will set out wandering.”

Nandiya the Sakyan heard: “Several bhikkhus, it seems, are making a robe for the Blessed One, thinking: ‘When the robe is finished, at the end of the three months, the Blessed One will set out wandering.’” Then Nandiya the Sakyan approached the Blessed One. Having approached and paid homage to the Blessed One, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Nandiya the Sakyan said to the Blessed One:

“I have heard this, venerable sir: ‘Several bhikkhus, it seems, are making a robe for the Blessed One, thinking: “When the robe is finished, at the end of the three months, the Blessed One will set out wandering.”’ For us, venerable sir, who live in various ways, by what dwelling should we dwell?”

“Good, good, Nandiya! It is fitting, Nandiya, for you |clansmen::gentlemen, of good family [kulaputta]| to approach the Tathāgata and ask: ‘For us, venerable sir, who live in various ways, by what dwelling should we dwell?’ One who has |faith::confidence, conviction, trust [saddha]| is successful, Nandiya, not one who lacks faith. One who is |virtuous::ethical, moral [sīlavant]| is successful, Nandiya, not one who is |unprincipled::without regard for ethical conduct [dussīla]|. One |with energy aroused::with initiative [āraddhavīriya]| is successful, Nandiya, not one who is |lazy::procrastinating, inactive person, indolent [kusīta]|. One with established mindfulness is successful, Nandiya, not one who is |muddle-minded::forgetful, not mindful [muṭṭhassatī]|. One who is |collected::composed, settled [samāhita]| is successful, Nandiya, not one who is |distracted::with scattered attention, not collected, not well-composed [asamāhita]|. One who has wisdom is successful, Nandiya, not one who lacks wisdom. Established in these six qualities, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally regarding five qualities.

Here, Nandiya, you should recollect the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| thus: ‘The Blessed One is an |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ In this way, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally regarding the Tathāgata.

Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect the Dhamma thus: ‘The Dhamma is well proclaimed by the Blessed One, directly visible, immediate, inviting verification, |onward leading::applicable, relevant, practical [opaneyyika]|, and to be personally experienced by the wise.’ In this way, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally regarding the Dhamma.

Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect good friends thus: ‘It is a gain for me, it is a great blessing for me, that I have |good friends::noble friends who support one on the spiritual path [kalyāṇamittā]| who are compassionate, who desire my welfare, who advise me, and who instruct me.’ In this way, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally regarding good friends.

Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect your own |generosity::sharing, relinquishment [cāga]| thus: ‘It is a gain for me, it is a great blessing for me, that among people |obsessed by the stain of stinginess::possessed by the tarnish of selfishness [maccheramalapariyuṭṭhita]|, I dwell at home with a mind free from the stain of stinginess, freely generous, open-handed, delighting in relinquishment, responsive to requests, delighting in sharing gifts.’ In this way, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally regarding generosity.

Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect the deities thus: ‘Those deities who, having bypassed the company of deities that |consume edible food::feed on material sustenance [kabaḷīkārāhārabhakkha]|, have been reborn in a certain |mind-made::mentally produced [manomaya]| body, do not see anything further to be done for themselves, nor any addition to what has been done.’ Just as, Nandiya, a bhikkhu who has attained |abiding liberation::permanent emancipation; lasting release; abiding liberation; lit. not occasional liberation [asamayavimokkha]| does not see anything further to be done for himself, nor any addition to what has been done; in the same way, Nandiya, those deities who, having bypassed the company of deities that consume edible food, have been reborn in a certain mind-made body, do not see anything further to be done for themselves, nor any addition to what has been done. In this way, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally regarding the deities.

A disciple of the Noble Ones endowed with these eleven qualities, Nandiya, abandons harmful, unwholesome states and does not cling to them. Just as, Nandiya, an overturned pot pours out water and does not take back what was poured out; or just as, Nandiya, a fire let loose in dry grassland only goes on burning and does not turn back over what was burned; in the same way, Nandiya, a disciple of the Noble Ones endowed with these eleven qualities abandons harmful, unwholesome states and does not cling to them.”
