---
title: Ayoniso sutta - Superficially
description: "The Buddha contrasts an immature person and a wise person by three qualities: whether they ask and answer questions superficially or wisely, and whether they approve of another’s well-rounded, coherent reply."
qualities: immaturity, wisdom, superficial attention, radical attention, inquisitiveness
theme: training guideline, cultivating discernment, inquisitiveness
slug: an3.5
priority: 1.1
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| an |immature person::lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]| should be recognized by three qualities. What are these three? They ask a question |superficially::carelessly, unwisely; lit. not according to the source [ayoniso]|, they answer a question superficially, and when another person answers a question |wisely::radically, prudently, thoroughly, carefully; lit. according to the source [yoniso]|—with |well-rounded::complete, well-formed [parimaṇḍala]|, |coherent::connected, consistent, fitting together [siliṭṭha]|, and |relevant::pertinent, appropriate, to the point [upagata]| words and phrases—they do not |approve of::rejoice in, agree with, appreciate [abbhanumodati]| it.

Indeed, |bhikkhus,::::| an immature person should be recognized by these three qualities.

|Bhikkhus,::::| a |wise person::astute, learned, discerning, sensible person [paṇḍita]| should be recognized by three qualities. What are these three? They ask a question wisely, they answer a question wisely, and when another person answers a question wisely—with well-rounded, coherent, and relevant words and phrases—they approve of it.

Indeed, |bhikkhus,::::| a wise person should be recognized by these three qualities. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| <collapse>you should train thus: ‘Avoiding the three qualities by which an immature person is recognized, and adopting the three qualities by which a wise person is recognized, we will conduct ourselves accordingly.’ Thus, bhikkhus, you should train.</collapse>”
