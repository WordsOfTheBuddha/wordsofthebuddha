Then the householder Anāthapiṇḍika approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near and paid homage to the Blessed One, he sat down to one side. When Anāthapiṇḍika was seated to one side, the Blessed One addressed him thus:

“Householder, there are these four things that are wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world. What four? ‘May |wealth::possessions, property, riches [bhoga]| arise for me legitimately’—this is the first thing that is wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world.

‘Having gained wealth legitimately, may fame come to me together with my relatives and |teachers::preceptors, mentors [upajjhāya]|’—this is the second thing that is wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world.

‘Having gained wealth legitimately, and having gained fame together with my relatives and teachers, may I live long, preserving a long life span’—this is the third thing that is wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world.

‘Having gained wealth legitimately, having gained fame together with my relatives and teachers, and having lived long, preserving a long life span, with the breaking up of the body, after death, may I be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world’—this is the fourth thing that is wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world. These, householder, are the four things that are wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world.

There are four things, householder, that lead to the acquisition of these four things that are wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world. Which four? Accomplishment of faith, accomplishment of ethical conduct, accomplishment of generosity, and accomplishment of wisdom.

1.) And what, householder, is the |accomplishment of faith::success in confidence [saddhāsampadā]|? Here, householder, a disciple of the Noble Ones has confidence, is convinced in the awakening of the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, acknowledging: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly Awakened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, who has reached the destination, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide of trainable persons, a teacher of gods and humans, Buddha, Fortunate One.’ This is called, householder, the accomplishment of faith.

2.) And what, householder, is the |accomplishment of ethical conduct::attainment of virtue [sīlasampadā]|? Here, householder, a disciple of the Noble Ones abstains from |killing living beings::taking life, destruction of life [pāṇātipāta]|, abstains from |taking what is not given::theft, stealing, taking what is not offered [adinnādāna]|, abstains from |sexual misconduct::wrong behaviour in sexual matters [kāmesumicchācāra]|, abstains from |false speech::lying, deliberately speaking falsehood [musāvāda]|, and abstains from |consuming intoxicants::consuming wine, spirits, or drugs and getting intoxicated [surāmerayamajja]| that are the basis for |negligence::heedlessness, carelessness [pamāda]|. This is called, householder, the accomplishment of ethical conduct.

3.) And what, householder, is the |accomplishment of generosity::success in charitableness [cāgasampadā]|? Here, householder, a disciple of the Noble Ones dwells at home with a mind free from the stain of stinginess, freely generous, open-handed, delighting in relinquishment, responsive to requests, delighting in sharing gifts. This is called, householder, the accomplishment of generosity.

4.) And what, householder, is the |accomplishment of wisdom::success in understanding [paññāsampadā]|? Dwelling with a mind overcome by |craving::greediness, wanting, yearning [abhijjhā]| and |unbalanced greed::excessive greed [visamalobha]|, householder, one does what should not be done and neglects what should be done. Doing what should not be done and neglecting what should be done, one falls away from fame and happiness. Dwelling with a mind overcome by |ill will::hostile thought, malevolent intent, wishing ruin or downfall [byāpāda]|, householder, one does what should not be done and neglects what should be done. Doing what should not be done and neglecting what should be done, one falls away from fame and happiness. Dwelling with a mind overcome by |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness lit. stiffness (of mind/body due to tiredness) [thinamiddha]|, householder, one does what should not be done and neglects what should be done. Doing what should not be done and neglecting what should be done, one falls away from fame and happiness. Dwelling with a mind overcome by |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]|, householder, one does what should not be done and neglects what should be done. Doing what should not be done and neglecting what should be done, one falls away from fame and happiness. Dwelling with a mind overcome by |doubt::uncertainty, indecisiveness wrt suffering, its arising, its ending, and the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering [vicikiccha]|, householder, one does what should not be done and neglects what should be done. Doing what should not be done and neglecting what should be done, one falls away from fame and happiness.

That disciple of the Noble Ones, householder, having understood, ‘Craving and unbalanced greed are impurities that defile the mind,’ abandons craving and unbalanced greed, the impurity that defiles the mind. Having understood, ‘Ill will is an impurity that defiles the mind,’ he abandons ill will, the impurity that defiles the mind. Having understood, ‘Dullness and drowsiness are an impurity that defiles the mind,’ he abandons dullness and drowsiness, the impurity that defiles the mind. Having understood, ‘Restlessness and worry are an impurity that defiles the mind,’ he abandons restlessness and worry, the impurity that defiles the mind. Having understood, ‘Doubt is an impurity that defiles the mind,’ he abandons doubt, the impurity that defiles the mind.

When, householder, a disciple of the Noble Ones, having understood, ‘Craving and unbalanced greed are impurities that defile the mind,’ has abandoned craving and unbalanced greed, the impurity that defiles the mind; having understood, ‘Ill will is an impurity that defiles the mind,’ has abandoned ill will, the impurity that defiles the mind; having understood, ‘Dullness and drowsiness are an impurity that defiles the mind,’ has abandoned dullness and drowsiness, the impurity that defiles the mind; having understood, ‘Restlessness and worry are an impurity that defiles the mind,’ has abandoned restlessness and worry, the impurity that defiles the mind; having understood, ‘Doubt is an impurity that defiles the mind,’ has abandoned doubt, the impurity that defiles the mind—this disciple of the Noble Ones, householder, is called one of great wisdom, of widespread wisdom, who sees what is beneficial, accomplished in wisdom. This is called, householder, the accomplishment of wisdom. These four things, householder, lead to the acquisition of these four things that are wished for, desirable, agreeable, and rarely gained in the world.

That disciple of the Noble Ones, householder, with wealth acquired through initiative and effort, built up by hard work, earned by sweat, lawfully and legitimately obtained, performs four suitable actions. What four?

1.) Here, householder, a disciple of the Noble Ones, with wealth acquired through initiative and effort, built up by hard work, earned by sweat, lawfully and legitimately obtained, makes himself happy and pleased, and properly maintains himself in happiness. He makes his mother and father happy and pleased, and properly maintains them in happiness. He makes his children and wife, his bondservants, workers, and servants happy and pleased, and properly maintains them in happiness. He makes his friends and companions happy and pleased, and properly maintains them in happiness. This is his first case of wealth that has gone to a proper place, reached a fitting cause, and been utilized appropriately.

2.) Furthermore, householder, with wealth acquired through initiative and effort, built up by hard work, earned by sweat, lawfully and legitimately obtained, whatever misfortunes arise—whether from fire, water, kings, thieves, or displeasing heirs—he defends against such misfortunes and keeps himself safe. This is his second case of wealth that has gone to a proper place, reached a fitting cause, and been utilized appropriately.

3.) Furthermore, householder, with wealth acquired through initiative and effort, built up by hard work, earned by sweat, lawfully and legitimately obtained, a disciple of the Noble Ones makes five offerings: offerings to relatives, offerings to guests, offerings to the departed, offerings to the king, and offerings to the deities. This is his third case of wealth that has gone to a proper place, reached a fitting cause, and been utilized appropriately.

4.) Furthermore, householder, with wealth acquired through initiative and effort, built up by hard work, earned by sweat, lawfully and legitimately obtained, for those ascetics and brahmins who refrain from intoxication and negligence, who are settled in patience and gentleness, who tame themselves, calm themselves, and bring themselves to complete peace—he establishes an uplifting offering for such ascetics and brahmins, one that is conducive to heaven, ripens in happiness, and leads to a heavenly world. This is his fourth case of wealth that has gone to a proper place, reached a fitting cause, and been utilized appropriately.

That disciple of the Noble Ones, householder, with wealth acquired through initiative and effort, built up by hard work, earned by sweat, lawfully and legitimately obtained, performs these four suitable actions.

Householder, if anyone’s wealth is exhausted apart from these four suitable actions, that wealth is said to have gone to waste, failed to reach a fitting cause, and been utilized inappropriately. But householder, if anyone’s wealth is exhausted through these four suitable actions, that wealth is said to have gone to a proper place, reached a fitting cause, and been utilized appropriately.”

#### Verse

Wealth has been enjoyed, dependents supported,
misfortunes have been overcome by me;
an uplifting offering has been given,
and the five offerings have been made;
the virtuous have been served,
those restrained, living the spiritual life.

For whatever purpose a wise person
dwelling at home would wish for wealth,
that purpose has been attained by me;
what has been done brings no regret.

A mortal recollecting this,
a person standing firm in the noble Dhamma;
is praised right here in this life,
and after death, rejoices in heaven.”
