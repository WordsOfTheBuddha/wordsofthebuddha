title: Tikicchaka sutta - Physicians
description: The Buddha contrasts doctors’ medicinal purgatives, which sometimes succeed and sometimes fail, with the noble purgative of the tenfold path, which purges wrong view through right view and wrong liberation through right liberation without fail, freeing beings from birth, aging, and death.
qualities: right view, right intention, right speech, right livelihood, right effort, mindfulness, collectedness, wisdom, liberation, suffering
theme: training guideline, wisdom

“Physicians|, bhikkhus,::::| give a |purgative::laxative, purging treatment [virecana]| for dispelling afflictions originating from bile, for dispelling afflictions originating from phlegm, and for dispelling afflictions originating from wind. There is this purgative|, bhikkhus::::|—I do not deny it. But this purgative|, bhikkhus,::::| sometimes succeeds and sometimes fails.

And I|, bhikkhus,::::| will teach a noble purgative that only succeeds and never fails—relying upon which beings subject to birth are freed from birth; beings subject to aging are freed from aging; beings subject to death are freed from death; beings subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair are freed from sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair. Listen to this and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is that noble purgative that only succeeds and never fails—relying upon which beings subject to birth are freed from birth; beings subject to aging are freed from aging; beings subject to death are freed from death; beings subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair are freed from sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair?

For one with |right view::view that is in line with the Dhamma - teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [sammādiṭṭhi]|, |bhikkhus,::::| |wrong view::a distorted perception, an untrue view, a false belief [micchādiṭṭhi]| is purged; and the multitude of |harmful::injurious, bad, or evil. Encompasses the deceptively alluring that is ultimately detrimental or ruinous [pāpaka]|, unwholesome qualities that arise due to wrong view, they too are purged for him. And due to right view, many wholesome qualities reach |full development by cultivation::maturity by development, fulfillment by meditation [bhāvanāpāripūri]|.

For one with right intention, |bhikkhus,::::| |wrong intention::wrong notion, wrong idea [micchāsaṅkappa]| is purged ... For one with right speech, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong speech is purged ... For one with right action, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong action is purged ... For one with right livelihood, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong livelihood is purged ... For one with right effort, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong effort is purged ... For one with right mindfulness, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong mindfulness is purged ... For one with |right collectedness::stability of mind, stillness of mind, mental composure [samādhi]|, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong collectedness is purged ... For one with |right knowledge::distinctive knowledge, insight [sammāñāṇa]|, |bhikkhus,::::| wrong knowledge is purged ...

For one with |right liberation::right release, right freedom [sammāvimuttī]|, |bhikkhus,::::| false liberation is purged; and the multitude of harmful, unwholesome qualities that arise due to false liberation, they too are purged for him. And due to right liberation, many wholesome qualities reach full development by cultivation. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is that noble purgative that only succeeds and never fails—relying upon which beings subject to birth are freed from birth ... are freed from sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair.”
