Then, a certain bhikkhu approached his own |preceptor::mentor, teacher [upajjhāya]|; having approached, he said to his own preceptor: “Venerable sir, at present my body feels as if |intoxicated::doped, drowsy; lit. drunk type [madhurakajāta]|, the directions seem unclear to me, the Dhamma does not spring to mind, |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness lit. stiffness (of mind/body due to tiredness) [thinamiddha]| |occupy::obsess, control [pariyādāya]| my mind and persist, I lead the spiritual life without delight, and I have doubts about the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|.”

Then that bhikkhu, taking the co-resident bhikkhu with him; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this bhikkhu says: ‘At present, my body feels as if intoxicated, the directions seem unclear to me, the Dhamma does not spring to mind, dullness and drowsiness occupy my mind and persist, I lead the spiritual life without delight, and I have doubts about the Dhamma.’”

“So it is, bhikkhu! This is what happens to one who dwells 1.) unguarded in the sense faculties, 2.) |lacking moderation::lit. not knowing the measure [amattaññū]| in eating, 3.) not devoted to |wakefulness::lucidity, being awake, being alert [jāgariya]|, 4.) without insight into |wholesome qualities::skillful actions, good habits [kusaladhammā]|, and 5.) not dedicated to the cultivation of the |qualities that lead to awakening::more specifically, the 37 qualities that lead to awakening [bodhipakkhiyā dhammā]| during the first and last watch of the night. As a result, the body feels as if intoxicated, the directions seem unclear, the Dhamma does not spring to mind, dullness and drowsiness occupy the mind and persist, one leads the spiritual life without delight, and doubts about the Dhamma arise.

Therefore, |bhikkhu,::::| you should train yourself thus: ‘I will be guarded in the sense faculties, apply moderation in eating, be dedicated to wakefulness, have clear insight about wholesome qualities, and engage in the cultivation of the qualities that lead to awakening during the first and last watch of the night.’ Bhikkhu, this is how you should train yourself.”

Then, that bhikkhu, having been instructed by the Blessed One with this advise, rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right, he departed.

Thereafter, that bhikkhu, living in seclusion, with diligence, continuous effort, and resoluteness, not long after, realized with |direct knowledge::experiential realization [abhiññā]| and attained in that very life the unsurpassed culmination of the spiritual life for which sons of good families rightly go forth from the household life into homelessness.

He |directly knew::understood experientially; lit. completely knew [abbhaññāsi]|: “Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.” And that bhikkhu became one of the |Arahants::fully awakened ones, free from all mental defilements; worthy of offerings and veneration; also an epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|.

Then that bhikkhu, having attained arahantship, approached his own preceptor; having approached, he said to his own preceptor: “Venerable sir, at present my body no longer feels as if intoxicated, the directions are clear to me, the Dhamma springs to mind, dullness and drowsiness no longer occupies my mind, I lead the spiritual life with delight, and I have no doubts about the Dhamma.”

Then that bhikkhu, taking that co-resident bhikkhu with him, approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this bhikkhu says: ‘At present, my body no longer feels as if intoxicated, the directions are clear to me, the Dhamma springs to mind, dullness and drowsiness no longer occupy my mind and persist, I lead the spiritual life with delight, and I have no doubts about the Dhamma.’”

“So it is, bhikkhu! This is what happens to one who dwells 1.) guarded in the sense faculties, 2.) applying moderation in eating, 3.) devoted to wakefulness, 4.) with clear insight into wholesome qualities, and 5.) dedicated to the cultivation of the qualities that lead to awakening during the first and last watch of the night. As a result, the body does not feel as if intoxicated, the directions are clear, the Dhamma springs to mind, dullness and drowsiness does not occupy the mind and persist, one leads the spiritual life with delight, and there are no doubts about the Dhamma.

Therefore, bhikkhus, you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will be guarded in the sense faculties, apply moderation in eating, be devoted to wakefulness, have clear insight into wholesome qualities, and dedicate ourselves to the cultivation of the qualities that lead to awakening during the first and last watch of the night.’ Bhikkhus, this is how you should train yourselves.”
