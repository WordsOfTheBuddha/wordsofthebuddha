---
title: Paññā sutta - Wisdom
description: Claiming the end of rebirth requires far more than a passing state of calm. The Buddha presents nine profound qualities of a mind so thoroughly cultivated with wisdom.
qualities: passion, aversion, delusion
theme: wisdom, principle
slug: an9.25
priority: 1.2
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| when the mind of a bhikkhu is thoroughly cultivated with wisdom, it is proper for that bhikkhu to declare: ‘Birth is exhausted, the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.’

And how, |bhikkhus,::::| is the mind of a bhikkhu thoroughly cultivated with wisdom?

‘My mind is |free from passion::without lust [vītarāga]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is |free from aversion::without hatred [vītadosa]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is |free from delusion::with radical attention, without confusion [vītamoha]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is |not liable to passion::lit. not with desire nature [asarāgadhamma]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is |not liable to aversion::lit. not with hatred nature [asadosadhamma]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is |not liable to delusion::completely free of attending superficially [asamohadhamma]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is not liable to return to |sensual existence::existence in the realm of sense desire [kāmabhavāya]|,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is not liable to return to form existence,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated;
‘My mind is not liable to return to formless existence,’ thus with wisdom the mind is thoroughly cultivated.

When|, bhikkhus,::::| the mind of a bhikkhu is thoroughly cultivated with wisdom, it is proper for that bhikkhu to declare: ‘Birth is exhausted, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of existence.’”
