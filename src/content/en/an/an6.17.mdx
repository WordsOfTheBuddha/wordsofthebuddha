At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Then, in the late afternoon, having emerged from |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]|, the Blessed One approached the assembly hall; having approached, he sat down on the prepared seat. The venerable |Sāriputta::foremost disciple of the Buddha in great wisdom [sāriputta]| also, having emerged from seclusion in the late afternoon, approached the assembly hall; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. The venerable |Mahāmoggallāna::foremost disciple of the Buddha in psychic power; lit. Moggallāna the great [mahāmoggallāna]| also … the venerable |Mahākassapa::foremost disciple of the Buddha in preaching ascetic practices; lit. Kassapa the great [mahākassapa]| also … the venerable |Mahākaccāna::foremost disciple of the Buddha in explaining a brief instruction in detail; lit. great descendant of Kati [mahākaccāna]| also … the venerable |Mahākoṭṭhika::foremost disciple of the Buddha in analytical understanding [mahākoṭṭhika]| also … the venerable Mahācunda also … the venerable |Mahākappina::foremost disciple of the Buddha in instructing the monks; lit. great Kappina [mahākappina]| also … the venerable |Anuruddha::foremost disciple of the Buddha in divine eye; also a cousin of the Buddha [anuruddha]| also … the venerable |Revata::foremost disciple of the Buddha in forest dwelling [revata]| also … the venerable Ānanda also, having emerged from seclusion in the late afternoon, approached the assembly hall; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Then the Blessed One, having spent much of the night seated [in meditation], rose from his seat and entered his dwelling. And soon after the Blessed One had left, those venerable ones also rose from their seats and went to their respective dwellings.

But those bhikkhus there who were new, recently ordained, and who had just come to this |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| and |Vinaya::code of monastic discipline rules, training [vinaya]|, slept and |snored::grunted in sleep [kākacchamāna]| until sunrise. The Blessed One, with the |divine eye::the faculty of clairvoyance, the ability to see beyond the ordinary human range [dibbacakkhu]|, purified and surpassing human vision, saw those bhikkhus sleeping and snoring until sunrise. Having seen this, he approached the assembly hall; having approached, he sat down on the prepared seat. Having sat down, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus:

“Where, bhikkhus, is Sāriputta? Where is Mahāmoggallāna? Where is Mahākassapa? Where is Mahākaccāna? Where is Mahākoṭṭhika? Where is Mahācunda? Where is Mahākappina? Where is Anuruddha? Where is Revata? Where is Ānanda? Where|, bhikkhus,::::| have those elder disciples gone?”

“Venerable sir, soon after the Blessed One had left, those venerable ones also rose from their seats and went to their respective dwellings.”

“So then|, bhikkhus::::|, thinking that the elder bhikkhus have left, you sleep and snore until sunrise?

What do you think|, bhikkhus::::|? Have you ever seen or heard of an anointed aristocratic king who lives devoted to the |pleasure of beds::pleasure of lying down [seyyasukha]|, the pleasure of reclining, and the pleasure of dozing as much as he likes, yet rules the kingdom for his whole life or is dear and pleasing to the country?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Good|, bhikkhus::::|. I too, |bhikkhus,::::| have neither seen nor heard of an anointed aristocratic king who lives devoted to the pleasure of beds, the pleasure of reclining, and the pleasure of dozing as much as he likes, yet rules the kingdom for his whole life or is dear and pleasing to the country.

What do you think|, bhikkhus::::|? Have you ever seen or heard of a |provincial governor::district chief, regional ruler [raṭṭhika]| … a |hereditary official::minister who inherited his father's office or estate [pettaṇika]| … a |general::leader of the army [senāpatika]| … a |village headman::village chief [gāmagāmaṇika]| … or a |guild leader::head of a guild or association [pūgagāmaṇika]| who lives devoted to the pleasure of beds, the pleasure of reclining, and the pleasure of dozing as much as he likes, yet presides over the guild for his whole life or is dear and pleasing to the guild?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Good|, bhikkhus::::|. I too, |bhikkhus,::::| have neither seen nor heard of a guild leader who lives devoted to the pleasure of beds, the pleasure of reclining, and the pleasure of dozing as much as he likes, yet presides over the guild for his whole life or is dear and pleasing to the guild.

What do you think|, bhikkhus::::|? Have you ever seen or heard of an ascetic or brahmin who lives devoted to the pleasure of beds, the pleasure of reclining, and the pleasure of dozing as much as he likes—unguarded in the sense faculties, |lacking moderation::lit. not knowing the measure [amattaññū]| in eating, not devoted to |wakefulness::lucidity, being awake, being alert [jāgariya]|, without insight into |wholesome qualities::skillful actions, good habits [kusaladhammā]|, and not dedicated to the cultivation of the |qualities that lead to awakening::more specifically, the 37 qualities that lead to awakening [bodhipakkhiyā dhammā]| during the first and last watch of the night—who yet, with the |wearing away of the taints::gradual exhaustion and elimination of the mental defilements or deep-seated afflictions such as sensual desire, craving for existence, views, and ignorance [āsava + khaya]|, realizes for himself with |direct knowledge::experiential realization [abhiññā]| in this very life the taintless |liberation of mind::emancipated by mind/heart, samādhi obtained from fruition [cetovimutti]| and |liberation by wisdom::emancipation by insight [paññāvimutti]|, and having entered upon it, dwells in it?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Good|, bhikkhus::::|. I too, |bhikkhus,::::| have neither seen nor heard of an ascetic or brahmin who lives devoted to the pleasure of beds, the pleasure of reclining, and the pleasure of dozing as much as he likes—unguarded in the sense faculties, lacking moderation in eating, not devoted to wakefulness, without insight into wholesome qualities, and not dedicated to the cultivation of the qualities that lead to awakening during the first and last watch of the night—who yet, with the wearing away of the taints, realizes for himself with direct knowledge in this very life the taintless liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom, and having entered upon it, dwells in it.

Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will be guarded in the sense faculties, apply moderation in eating, be devoted to wakefulness, have clear insight into wholesome qualities, and dwell dedicated to the cultivation of the qualities that lead to awakening during the first and last watch of the night.’ This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| you should train yourselves.”
