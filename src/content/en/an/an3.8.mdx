---
title: Sabyābajjha sutta - Afflictive
description: The Buddha contrasts an immature person, recognized by afflictive bodily, verbal, and mental action, with a wise person, recognized by non-afflictive bodily, verbal, and mental action, and urges training to avoid the former and undertake the latter.
qualities: immaturity, wisdom, harm, non-harm
theme: training guideline, cultivating discernment, principle
slug: an3.8
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| an |immature person::lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]| should be recognized by three qualities. What are these three? |Afflictive::hurtful, harmful, bringing distress [sabyābajjha]| bodily action, afflictive verbal action, and afflictive mental action. <collapse>Indeed, bhikkhus, an immature person should be recognized by these three qualities. Bhikkhus, a wise person should be recognized by three qualities. What are these three?</collapse> |Non-afflictive::not harmful, free from distress, without suffering [abyābajjha]| bodily action, non-afflictive verbal action, and non-afflictive mental action. Indeed, |bhikkhus,::::| a |wise person::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled person [paṇḍita]| should be recognized by these three qualities.

Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train thus: ‘Avoiding the three qualities by which an immature person is recognized, and adopting the three qualities by which a wise person is known, we will behave accordingly.’ Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train.”
