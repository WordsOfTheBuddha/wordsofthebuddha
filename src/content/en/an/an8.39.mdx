---
title: Abhisanda sutta - Stream
description: "The Buddha describes eight streams of merit that lead to profound happiness and heavenly rebirth: taking refuge in the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Saṅgha, alongside observing the five precepts. He frames these precepts as ancient “great gifts” because abstaining from harmful actions like killing and intoxication grants limitless beings freedom from fear, hostility, and affliction."
qualities: merit, ethical conduct, faith, giving, wholesome, recollection of the Buddha, recollection of the Dhamma, recollection of the Sangha, non-harm, without fear, not taking what is not given, right speech, slaughtering, stealing, sexual misconduct, wrong speech, consuming intoxicants, negligence
theme: wisdom, cultivating discernment, inspiration

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these eight streams of merit, streams of the wholesome, nutriments of happiness—heavenly, ripening in happiness, leading to heaven—which lead to what is wished for, desired, agreeable, to welfare and happiness. What eight?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones has gone for refuge to the Buddha. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the first stream of |merit::good deeds, spiritual wealth [puñña]|, stream of the |wholesome::healthy, beneficial, useful [kusala]|, nutriment of happiness—heavenly, ripening in happiness, leading to heaven—which leads to what is wished for, desired, agreeable, to welfare and happiness.

Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones has gone for refuge to the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the second stream of merit, <collapse>stream of the wholesome, nutriment of happiness—heavenly, ripening in happiness, leading to heaven—which leads to what is wished for, desired, agreeable,</collapse> to welfare and happiness.

Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones has gone for refuge to the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]|. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the third stream of merit, stream of the wholesome, nutriment of happiness—heavenly, ripening in happiness, leading to heaven—which leads to what is wished for, desired, agreeable, to welfare and happiness.

|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these five gifts, great gifts—foremost, of long standing, traditional, ancient, |unadulterated::unmixed; lit. not sprinkled together [asaṅkiṇṇa]| and never before adulterated, which are not being adulterated and will not be adulterated, not despised by wise ascetics and brahmins. What five?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones, having abandoned the |killing of living beings::destruction of life [pāṇātipāta]|, abstains from killing living beings. Abstaining from killing living beings, |bhikkhus,::::| a disciple of the Noble Ones gives to a limitless number of beings freedom from fear, freedom from hostility, and freedom from affliction. Having given to a limitless number of beings freedom from fear, freedom from hostility, and freedom from affliction, he becomes a partaker of limitless freedom from fear, freedom from hostility, and freedom from affliction. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the first gift, a great gift—foremost, of long standing, traditional, ancient, unadulterated and never before adulterated. It is not being adulterated now nor will it be adulterated in the future. Wise ascetics and brahmins do not despise it. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the fourth stream of merit, stream of the wholesome, nutriment of happiness—heavenly, ripening in happiness, leading to heaven—which leads to what is wished for, desired, agreeable, to welfare and happiness.
