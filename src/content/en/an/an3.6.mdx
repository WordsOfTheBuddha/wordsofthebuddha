---
title: Akusala sutta - Unwholesome
description: The Buddha contrasts an immature person, recognized by unwholesome bodily, verbal, and mental action, with a wise person, recognized by wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental action.
qualities: immaturity, wisdom, unwholesome, wholesome
theme: training guideline, cultivating discernment, principle
slug: an3.6
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| an |immature person::lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]| should be recognized by three qualities. What are these three? |Unwholesome::unhealthy, unskillful, unbeneficial, or karmically unprofitable [akusala]| bodily action, unwholesome verbal action, and unwholesome mental action.

Indeed, |bhikkhus,::::| an immature person should be recognized by these three qualities.

|Bhikkhus,::::| a |wise::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled [paṇḍita]| person should be recognized by three qualities. What are these three? |Wholesome::healthy, skillful, beneficial, or karmically profitable [kusala]| bodily action, wholesome verbal action, and wholesome mental action.

Indeed, |bhikkhus,::::| a wise person should be recognized by these three qualities. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| <collapse>you should train thus: ‘Avoiding the three qualities by which an immature person is recognized, and adopting the three qualities by which a wise person is known, we will behave accordingly.’ This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| you should train.</collapse>”
