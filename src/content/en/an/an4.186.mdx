description: A bhikkhu asks the Buddha four fundamental questions—what leads the world, what makes one learned and a bearer of the Dhamma, what constitutes penetrative wisdom, and what defines great wisdom—and learns that the mind leads the world while great wisdom intends no harm and seeks the welfare of all.

Then a certain bhikkhu approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One:

“Venerable sir, by what is the world led? By what is the world dragged about? And under the control of what that has arisen does it come?”

“Good, good, bhikkhu. Your approach is good, your intuition is good, and your inquiry is good. For thus you ask, bhikkhu: ‘Venerable sir, by what is the world led? By what is the world dragged about? And under the control of what that has arisen does it come?’”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Bhikkhu, the world is led by the |mind::thought, intention, idea [citta]|, dragged about by the mind, and it comes under the control of the mind that has arisen.”

“Excellent, venerable sir,” the bhikkhu, pleased and appreciative of what the Blessed One had said, asked the Blessed One a further question:

“Venerable sir, it is said ‘one with |much learning::well educated, knowledgeable [bahussuta]| who is a |bearer of the Dhamma::one who knows the Dhamma by heart [dhammadhara]|, one with much learning who is a bearer of the Dhamma.’ To what extent, venerable sir, is one with much learning who is a bearer of the Dhamma?”

“Good, good, bhikkhu. Your approach is good, your intuition is good, and your inquiry is good. For thus you ask, bhikkhu: ‘Venerable sir, it is said “one with much learning who is a bearer of the Dhamma, one with much learning who is a bearer of the Dhamma.” To what extent, venerable sir, is one with much learning who is a bearer of the Dhamma?’” “Yes, venerable sir.” “Many teachings have been taught by me, bhikkhu—discourses, mixed prose and verse, expositions, verses, inspired utterances, sayings, birth stories, marvelous accounts, and questions-and-answers. Even with a four-line verse, bhikkhu, if one, understanding its meaning, understanding the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, |practices according to the Dhamma::accordant following of the Dhamma [dhammānudhammappaṭipanna]|, one is fit to be called ‘one with much learning who is a bearer of the Dhamma.’”

“Excellent, venerable sir,” the bhikkhu, pleased and appreciative of what the Blessed One had said, asked the Blessed One a further question:

“Venerable sir, it is said ‘one |who has heard::learned, instructed in the teachings [sutavant]|, |of penetrative wisdom::with wisdom that pierces through [nibbedhikapañña]|, one who has heard, of penetrative wisdom.’ To what extent, venerable sir, is one who has heard, of penetrative wisdom?”

“Good, good, bhikkhu. Your approach is good, your intuition is good, and your inquiry is good. For thus you ask, bhikkhu: ‘Venerable sir, it is said “one who has heard, of penetrative wisdom, one who has heard, of penetrative wisdom.” To what extent, venerable sir, is one who has heard, of penetrative wisdom?’” “Yes, venerable sir.” “Here, bhikkhu, a bhikkhu has heard, ‘This is |suffering::discomfort, unpleasantness, discontentment, dissatisfaction, stress, pain, disease, i.e. mild or intense suffering [dukkha]|,’ and with |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]| he penetrates and sees its meaning; he has heard, ‘This is the |arising of suffering::source of stress, appearance of discomfort [dukkhasamudaya]|,’ and with wisdom he penetrates and sees its meaning; he has heard, ‘This is the |cessation of suffering::end of suffering, cessation of distress [dukkhanirodha]|,’ and with wisdom he penetrates and sees its meaning; he has heard, ‘This is the |way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering::i.e. the Noble Eightfold Path consisting of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right collectedness [dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā]|,’ and with wisdom he penetrates and sees its meaning. In this way, bhikkhu, one is a person who has heard, of penetrative wisdom.”

“Excellent, venerable sir,” the bhikkhu, pleased and appreciative of what the Blessed One had said, asked the Blessed One a further question:

“Venerable sir, it is said ‘|wise::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled [paṇḍita]|, |of great wisdom::very wise, extremely intelligent [mahāpañña]|, wise, of great wisdom.’ To what extent, venerable sir, is one wise, of great wisdom?”

“Good, good, bhikkhu. Your approach is good, your intuition is good, and your inquiry is good. For thus you ask, bhikkhu: ‘Venerable sir, it is said “wise, of great wisdom, wise, of great wisdom.” To what extent, venerable sir, is one wise, of great wisdom?’” “Yes, venerable sir.” “Here, bhikkhu, one who is wise, of great wisdom, does not intend for his own affliction, does not intend for the affliction of others, and does not intend for the affliction of both; rather, when reflecting, he thinks solely of his own welfare, the welfare of others, the welfare of both, and the welfare of the whole world. In this way, bhikkhu, one is wise, of great wisdom.”
