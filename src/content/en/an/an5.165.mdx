There, the venerable |Sāriputta::foremost disciple of the Buddha in great wisdom [sāriputta]| addressed the bhikkhus: ... “Friends, whoever asks a question of another, they all do so on one or another of five |grounds::reasons, bases, occasions [ṭhāna]|. What five?

One asks a question of another out of |confusion::slowness of mind, stupidity, lack of intelligence [mandatta]| and |bewilderment::confusion, folly, infatuation [momūhatta]|; one with |evil desires::yearnings that produce harm [pāpiccha]|, |afflicted with desire::impelled by ambition [icchāpakata]|, asks a question of another; one asks a question of another |out of contempt::disparagingly, to belittle or humiliate [paribhavaṁ]|; one asks a question of another |wishing to understand::desiring to know, seeking comprehension [aññātukāma]|; or else one asks a question of another with this thought:

‘If, when he is asked my question, he answers |correctly::thoroughly, in accordance with the Dhamma [sammadeva]|, that is good; but if he does not answer correctly, then I will answer it correctly for him.’

Friends, whoever asks a question of another, they all do so on one or another of these five grounds.

As for me, friends, I ask a question of another with this thought:

‘If, when he is asked my question, he answers correctly, that is good; but if he does not answer correctly, then I will answer it correctly for him.’”
