#### 2.11 <!-- toc: Two Powers -->

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these two powers. What two? The |power of reflection::strength of deliberate contemplation, power of wise consideration [paṭisaṅkhānabala]| and the |power of cultivation::strength of meditation [bhāvanābala]|.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of reflection? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| someone reflects: ‘Bodily misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; verbal misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; mental misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life.’ Having reflected in this way, he abandons bodily misconduct and cultivates good bodily conduct; he abandons verbal misconduct and cultivates good verbal conduct; he abandons mental misconduct and cultivates good mental conduct; he keeps himself pure. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of reflection.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of cultivation? In this regard, |bhikkhus,::::| this power of cultivation is the power of the |trainee::disciples in higher training who have attained at least stream-entry but not yet arahantship [sekha]|. By relying on the power of a trainee, |bhikkhus,::::| one abandons |passion::intense desire, strong emotion, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāga]|, abandons |aversion::hatred, hostility, resentment [dosa]|, and abandons |delusion::fundamental distortion of reality, confusion, clouding of discernment [moha]|. Having abandoned passion, abandoned aversion, and abandoned delusion, one does not do what is unwholesome, one does not engage in what is harmful. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of cultivation. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two powers.”

#### 2.13 <!-- toc: Two Powers - III -->

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these two powers. What two? The power of reflection and the power of cultivation.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of reflection? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| someone reflects: ‘Bodily misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; verbal misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; mental misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life.’ Having reflected in this way, he abandons bodily misconduct and cultivates good bodily conduct; he abandons verbal misconduct and cultivates good verbal conduct; he abandons mental misconduct and cultivates good mental conduct; he keeps himself pure. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of reflection.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of cultivation? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| 1.) a bhikkhu, entirely secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome mental states, enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is |accompanied by thought::with thinking, with reflection [savitakka]| and |examination::with investigation, evaluation [savicāra]|, |born of seclusion::secluded from the defilements [vivekaja]|, and imbued with |uplifting joy and pleasure::delight and ease; sometimes experienced as ecstasy, intense exhilaration or rapture [pītisukha]|; 2.) with the |settling::calming, conciliation, subsiding [vūpasama]| of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second jhāna, which is characterized by internal |tranquility::calming, settling, confidence [sampasādana]| and |unification::singleness, integration [ekodibhāva]| of mind, is free from thought and examination, |born of collectedness::born from a stable mind [samādhija]|, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure; 3.) with the |fading of desire for::dispassion toward, detachment from [virāga]| uplifting joy, he dwells |equanimous::mentally poised, mentally balanced, non-reactive, disregarding [upekkhaka]|, |mindful and clearly aware::attentive and completely comprehending [sata + sampajāna]|, experiencing |pleasure::comfort, contentedness, happiness, ease [sukha]| with the body; he enters and dwells in the third jhāna, which the noble ones describe as, ‘one who dwells equanimous, mindful, and at ease’; 4.) with the abandoning of [bodily] pleasure and |pain::discomfort, unpleasantness. In this context, this is referring to bodily pain or sharp sensations. [dukkha]|, and with the prior settling down of |mental pleasure and displeasure::the duality of positive and negative states of mind; mental happiness and mental pain [somanassadomanassa]|, he enters and dwells in the fourth jhāna, which is characterized by purification of mindfulness through |equanimity::mental poise, mental balance, equipoise, non-reactivity, composure [upekkhā]|, experiencing a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of cultivation. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two powers.”

#### 2.15 <!-- toc: Disciplinary Issue -->

“|Bhikkhus,::::| in a disciplinary issue where neither the offending bhikkhu nor the accusing bhikkhu carefully examines himself, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will lead to acrimony and animosity for a long time, and the bhikkhus will not dwell at ease.’ But in a disciplinary issue where both the offending bhikkhu and the accusing bhikkhu carefully examine themselves, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will not lead to acrimony and animosity for a long time, and the bhikkhus will dwell at ease.’

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does an offending bhikkhu carefully examine himself? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| an offending bhikkhu reflects: ‘I have committed a certain unwholesome offense with the body. That bhikkhu saw me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. If I had not committed that unwholesome offense with the body, that bhikkhu would not have seen me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. But because I committed that unwholesome offense with the body, that bhikkhu saw me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. And seeing me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body, that bhikkhu was displeased. Being displeased, that bhikkhu spoke words of displeasure to me. Being spoken to with words of displeasure by that bhikkhu, I was displeased. Being displeased, I reported it to others. Thus, the offense here has overtaken me alone, like a payer of customs duty with regard to his goods.’ In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| an offending bhikkhu carefully examines himself.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does an accusing bhikkhu carefully examine himself? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| an accusing bhikkhu reflects: ‘This bhikkhu has committed a certain unwholesome offense with the body. I saw this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. If this bhikkhu had not committed that unwholesome offense with the body, I would not have seen this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. But because this bhikkhu committed that unwholesome offense with the body, I saw this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. And seeing this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body, I was displeased. Being displeased, I spoke words of displeasure to this bhikkhu. Being spoken to with words of displeasure by me, this bhikkhu was displeased. Being displeased, he reported it to others. Thus, the offense here has overtaken me alone, like a payer of customs duty with regard to his goods.’ In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| an accusing bhikkhu carefully examines himself.

In a disciplinary issue, |bhikkhus,::::| where neither the offending bhikkhu nor the accusing bhikkhu carefully examines himself, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will lead to acrimony and animosity for a long time, and the bhikkhus will not dwell at ease.’ But in a disciplinary issue where both the offending bhikkhu and the accusing bhikkhu carefully examine themselves, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will not lead to acrimony and animosity for a long time, and the bhikkhus will dwell at ease.’”

#### 2.16 <!-- toc: After Death -->

Then a certain brahmin approached the Blessed One, and having approached, exchanged greetings with him. After the exchange of a courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side and said to the Blessed One:

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a |state of loss::state of misery [apāya]|, in a bad destination, in a |realm of downfall::realm of misery [vinipāta]|, in hell?”

“Brahmin, it is because of |unethical conduct::conduct not in line with the Dhamma [adhammacariyā]| and |unwholesome behavior::immoral conduct, disharmonious behavior; lit. uneven behavior [visamacariyā]| that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell.”

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world?”

“Brahmin, it is because of |ethical conduct:: condcut in line with the Dhamma [dhammacariyā]| and |wholesome behavior::harmonious behavior, good conduct; lit. even conduct [samacariyā]| that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! Just as if one might set upright what had been overturned, reveal what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way, sir Gotama, the Dhamma has been explained by you in many ways. I go for refuge to venerable Gotama, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who, from this day forward, has gone for refuge from today onward for life.”

#### 2.17 <!-- toc: After Death - II -->

Then the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi approached the Blessed One; having drawn near, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. After the exchange of a courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi said this to the Blessed One:

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell?”

“Brahmin, it is both because of what has been done and what has not been done that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell.”

“And what is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world?”

“Brahmin, it is both because of what has been done and what has not been done that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“I do not understand in detail, sir Gotama, the meaning of this statement, which was spoken in brief, without being analyzed in detail. It would be good, sir Gotama, if you would teach the Dhamma in such a way that I might understand the detailed meaning of this statement which was spoken in brief, without being analyzed in detail.”

“In that case, brahmin, listen carefully and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, sir,” the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Here, brahmin, a certain person has engaged in bodily misconduct but has not engaged in good bodily conduct; has engaged in verbal misconduct but has not engaged in good verbal conduct; has engaged in mental misconduct but has not engaged in good mental conduct. Thus, brahmin, because of what has been done and what has not been done, some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell. Here, brahmin, a certain person has engaged in good bodily conduct but has not engaged in bodily misconduct; has engaged in good verbal conduct but has not engaged in verbal misconduct; has engaged in good mental conduct but has not engaged in mental misconduct. Thus, brahmin, because of what has been done and what has not been done, some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“Excellent, sir Gotama! ... Please consider me as a lay disciple who has gone to you for refuge from this day forth for life.”

#### 2.18 <!-- toc: Misconduct and its results -->

Then the Venerable Ānanda approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. As Venerable Ānanda was seated to one side, the Blessed One said to him: “Ānanda, I say |unequivocally::categorically, with absolute assurance [ekaṁsena]| that one should not engage in bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, and mental misconduct; they are not to be done.”

“Venerable sir, when that which the Blessed One has unequivocally declared should not be done—bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct—is done, what drawback is to be expected?”

“Ānanda, when that which I have unequivocally declared should not be done—bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct—is done, this drawback is to be expected: One blames oneself; after scrutiny, the wise criticize one; a bad reputation spreads; one dies confused; and with the breakup of the body, after death, one is reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell. When that which I have unequivocally declared should not be done—bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct—is done, this drawback is to be expected.

Ānanda, I speak unequivocally in favor of good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, and good mental conduct; they are to be done.”

“Venerable sir, when that which the Blessed One has unequivocally declared should be done—good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct—is done, what benefit is to be expected?”

“Ānanda, when that which I have unequivocally declared should be done—good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct—is done, this benefit is to be expected: One does not blame oneself; after scrutiny, the wise praise one; a good reputation spreads; one dies unconfused; and with the breakup of the body, after death, one is reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly realm. When that which I have unequivocally declared should be done—good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct—is done, this benefit is to be expected.”

#### 2.19 <!-- toc: Abandon the Unwholesome -->

“|Bhikkhus,::::| abandon the |unwholesome::unskillful, detrimental, harmful [akusala]|. It is possible|, bhikkhus,::::| to abandon the unwholesome. If it were not possible to abandon the unwholesome, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| abandon the unwholesome.’ But because it is possible to abandon the unwholesome, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| abandon the unwholesome.’ And if this unwholesome, when abandoned, were to lead to harm and suffering, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| abandon the unwholesome.’ But because the unwholesome, when abandoned, leads to welfare and happiness, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| abandon the unwholesome.’”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| cultivate the |wholesome::skillful, beneficial, healthy [kusala]|. It is possible|, bhikkhus,::::| to cultivate the wholesome. If it were not possible to cultivate the wholesome, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| cultivate the wholesome.’ But because it is possible to cultivate the wholesome, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| cultivate the wholesome.’ And if this wholesome, when cultivated, were to lead to harm and suffering, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| cultivate the wholesome.’ But because the wholesome, when cultivated, leads to welfare and happiness, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus,::::| cultivate the wholesome.’”

#### 2.20 <!-- toc: Non-disappearance of Dhamma -->

“|Bhikkhus,::::| these two things lead to the |lapse::forgetting, decline [sammosa]| and |disappearance::fading away [antaradhāna]| of the |good Dhamma::good teaching, true nature of things [saddhamma]|. What two? Misplaced words and phrases, and incorrectly interpreted meaning. When the words and phrases are misplaced, the meaning is also hard to infer or interpret. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two things that lead to the lapse and disappearance of the good Dhamma.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| these two things lead to the continuity, maintenance, and non-disappearance of the good Dhamma. What two? Well-placed words and phrases, and correctly interpreted meaning. When the words and phrases are well placed, the meaning is also easily inferred. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two things that lead to the continuity, maintenance, and non-disappearance of the good Dhamma.”
