#### 2.11 {Two Powers}

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these two powers. What two? The |power of reflection::strength of deliberate contemplation, power of wise consideration [paṭisaṅkhānabala]| and the |power of cultivation::strength of meditation [bhāvanābala]|.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of reflection? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| someone reflects: ‘Bodily misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; verbal misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; mental misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life.’ Having reflected in this way, he abandons bodily misconduct and cultivates good bodily conduct; he abandons verbal misconduct and cultivates good verbal conduct; he abandons mental misconduct and cultivates good mental conduct; he keeps himself pure. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of reflection.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of cultivation? In this context, |bhikkhus,::::| this power of cultivation is the power of the |trainees::disciples in higher training who have attained at least stream-entry but not yet arahantship [sekha]|. For relying on the power of a trainee, |bhikkhus,::::| one abandons |passion::intense desire, strong emotion, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāga]|, abandons |aversion::hatred, hostility, resentment [dosa]|, and abandons |delusion::fundamental distortion of reality, confusion, clouding of discernment [moha]|. Having abandoned passion, abandoned aversion, and abandoned delusion, one does not do what is unwholesome, one does not engage in what is harmful. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of cultivation. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two powers.”

#### 2.12 {Two Powers - II}

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these two powers. What two? The power of reflection and the power of cultivation.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of reflection? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| someone reflects: ‘Bodily misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; verbal misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; mental misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life.’ Having reflected in this way, he abandons bodily misconduct and cultivates good bodily conduct; he abandons verbal misconduct and cultivates good verbal conduct; he abandons mental misconduct and cultivates good mental conduct; he keeps himself pure. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of reflection.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of cultivation? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu cultivates the |mindfulness awakening factor::constituent of awakening that is mindfulness [satisambojjhaṅga]| supported by seclusion, supported by dispassion, supported by cessation, maturing in release. He cultivates the |investigation-of-phenomena awakening factor::constituent of awakening that investigates the nature of reality [dhammavicayasambojjhaṅga]| … the |energy awakening factor::constituent of awakening that is vigor [vīriyasambojjhaṅga]| … the |rapture awakening factor::constituent of awakening that is spiritual joy [pītisambojjhaṅga]| … the |tranquility awakening factor::constituent of awakening that is bodily and mental calming [passaddhisambojjhaṅga]| … the |collectedness awakening factor::constituent of awakening that is mental stillness [samādhisambojjhaṅga]| … the |equanimity awakening factor::constituent of awakening that is mental balance [upekkhāsambojjhaṅga]| supported by seclusion, supported by dispassion, supported by cessation, maturing in release. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of cultivation. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two powers.”

#### 2.13 {Two Powers - III}

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these two powers. What two? The power of reflection and the power of cultivation.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of reflection? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| someone reflects: ‘Bodily misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; verbal misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life; mental misconduct has a painful result both in this life and in the next life.’ Having reflected in this way, he abandons bodily misconduct and cultivates good bodily conduct; he abandons verbal misconduct and cultivates good verbal conduct; he abandons mental misconduct and cultivates good mental conduct; he keeps himself pure. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of reflection.

And what|, bhikkhus,::::| is the power of cultivation? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, a bhikkhu enters and abides in the first |jhāna::deep mental absorption [jhāna]|, which has applied thought and sustained examination, with rapture and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of applied thought and sustained examination, he enters and abides in the second jhāna, which has internal confidence and unification of mind, without applied thought and sustained examination, with rapture and happiness born of collectedness. With the fading away of rapture, he abides equanimous, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body, which the noble ones declare: ‘Equanimous and mindful, one abides in happiness’—thus he enters and abides in the third jhāna. With the abandoning of pleasure and pain, and with the previous disappearance of joy and grief, he enters and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure and purity of mindfulness through equanimity. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is called the power of cultivation. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two powers.”

#### 2.15 {Disciplinary Issue}

“|Bhikkhus,::::| in a disciplinary issue where neither the offending bhikkhu nor the accusing bhikkhu carefully examines himself, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will lead to lengthiness, harshness, and hostility, and the bhikkhus will not live comfortably.’ But in a disciplinary issue where both the offending bhikkhu and the accusing bhikkhu carefully examine themselves, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will not lead to lengthiness, harshness, and hostility, and the bhikkhus will live comfortably.’

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does an offending bhikkhu carefully examine himself? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| an offending bhikkhu reflects: ‘I have committed a certain unwholesome offense with the body. That bhikkhu saw me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. If I had not committed that unwholesome offense with the body, that bhikkhu would not have seen me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. But because I committed that unwholesome offense with the body, that bhikkhu saw me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. And seeing me committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body, that bhikkhu was displeased. Being displeased, that bhikkhu spoke words of displeasure to me. Being spoken to with words of displeasure by that bhikkhu, I was displeased. Being displeased, I reported it to others. Thus, the offense here has overtaken me alone, like a payer of customs duty with regard to his goods.’ In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| an offending bhikkhu carefully examines himself.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does an accusing bhikkhu carefully examine himself? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| an accusing bhikkhu reflects: ‘This bhikkhu has committed a certain unwholesome offense with the body. I saw this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. If this bhikkhu had not committed that unwholesome offense with the body, I would not have seen this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. But because this bhikkhu committed that unwholesome offense with the body, I saw this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body. And seeing this bhikkhu committing a certain unwholesome offense with the body, I was displeased. Being displeased, I spoke words of displeasure to this bhikkhu. Being spoken to with words of displeasure by me, this bhikkhu was displeased. Being displeased, he reported it to others. Thus, the offense here has overtaken me alone, like a payer of customs duty with regard to his goods.’ In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| an accusing bhikkhu carefully examines himself.

In a disciplinary issue, |bhikkhus,::::| where neither the offending bhikkhu nor the accusing bhikkhu carefully examines himself, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will lead to lengthiness, harshness, and hostility, and the bhikkhus will not live comfortably.’ But in a disciplinary issue where both the offending bhikkhu and the accusing bhikkhu carefully examine themselves, in that disciplinary issue this is to be expected: ‘It will not lead to lengthiness, harshness, and hostility, and the bhikkhus will live comfortably.’”

#### 2.16 {After Death}

Then a certain brahmin approached the Blessed One, and having approached, exchanged greetings with him. After the exchange of a courteous and politeconversation, he sat down to one side and said to the Blessed One:

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a |state of loss::state of misery [apāya]|, in a bad destination, in a |realm of downfall::realm of misery [vinipāta]|, in hell?”

“Brahmin, it is because of unrighteous conduct, unharmonious conduct that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell.”

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world?”

“Brahmin, it is because of righteous conduct, harmonious conduct that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“Excellent, sir Gotama! Excellent, sir Gotama! Just as if one might set upright what had been overturned, reveal what had been concealed, point out the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way, sir Gotama, the Dhamma has been explained by you in many ways. I go for refuge to venerable Gotama, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus. May sir Gotama remember me as a lay follower who, from this day forward, has gone to refuge for life.”

#### 2.17 {After Death - II}

Then the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi approached the Blessed One; having drawn near, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. After the exchange of a courteous and politeconversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi said this to the Blessed One:

“What is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell?”

“Brahmin, it is both because of what has been done and what has not been done that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell.”

“And what is the cause, sir Gotama, what is the reason why some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world?”

“Brahmin, it is both because of what has been done and what has not been done that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“I do not understand in detail, sir Gotama, the meaning of this statement, which was spoken in brief, without being analyzed in detail. It would be good, sir Gotama, if you would teach the Dhamma in such a way that I might understand the detailed meaning of this statement which was spoken in brief, without being analyzed in detail.”

“In that case, brahmin, listen carefully and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, sir,” the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Here, brahmin, a certain person has engaged in bodily misconduct but has not engaged in good bodily conduct; has engaged in verbal misconduct but has not engaged in good verbal conduct; has engaged in mental misconduct but has not engaged in good mental conduct. Thus, brahmin, because of what has been done and what has not been done, some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell. Here, brahmin, a certain person has engaged in good bodily conduct but has not engaged in bodily misconduct; has engaged in good verbal conduct but has not engaged in verbal misconduct; has engaged in good mental conduct but has not engaged in mental misconduct. Thus, brahmin, because of what has been done and what has not been done, some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.”

“Excellent, sir Gotama! ... Please consider me as a lay disciple who has gone to you for refuge from this day forth for life.”

#### 2.18 {Misconduct and its results}

Then the Venerable Ānanda approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. As Venerable Ānanda was seated to one side, the Blessed One said to him: “Ānanda, I say |with absolute assurance::with certainty [ekaṁsena]| that one should not engage in bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, or mental misconduct.”

“Venerable sir, regarding this which the Blessed One has declared with absolute assurance should not be done, when it is done, what drawback is to be expected?”

“Ānanda, regarding this which I have declared with absolute assurance should not be done, when it is done, this drawback is to be expected: oneself blames oneself; upon examination, the wise criticize one; a bad reputation spreads; one dies confused; and with the breakup of the body, after death, one is reborn in a state of loss, in a bad destination, in a realm of downfall, in hell. Regarding this which I have declared with absolute assurance should not be done, when it is done, this drawback is to be expected.

Ānanda, I say with absolute assurance that one should engage in good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, and good mental conduct.”

“Venerable sir, regarding this which the Blessed One has declared with absolute assurance should be done, when it is done, what benefit is to be expected?”

“Ānanda, regarding this which I have declared with absolute assurance should be done, when it is done, this benefit is to be expected: oneself does not blame oneself; upon examination, the wise praise one; a good reputation spreads; one dies unconfused; and with the breakup of the body, after death, one is reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly realm. Regarding this which I have declared with absolute assurance should be done, when it is done, this benefit is to be expected.”

#### 2.19 {Abandon the Unwholesome}

“|Bhikkhus,::::| abandon the |unwholesome::unskillful, detrimental, harmful [akusala]|. It is possible|, bhikkhus,::::| to abandon the unwholesome. If it were not possible to abandon the unwholesome, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|abandon the unwholesome.’ But because it is possible to abandon the unwholesome, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|abandon the unwholesome.’ And if this unwholesome, when abandoned, were to lead to harm and suffering, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|abandon the unwholesome.’ But because the unwholesome, when abandoned, leads to welfare and happiness, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|abandon the unwholesome.’”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| cultivate the |wholesome::skillful, beneficial, healthy [kusala]|. It is possible|, bhikkhus,::::| to cultivate the wholesome. If it were not possible to cultivate the wholesome, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|cultivate the wholesome.’ But because it is possible to cultivate the wholesome, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|cultivate the wholesome.’ And if this wholesome, when cultivated, were to lead to harm and suffering, I would not say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|cultivate the wholesome.’ But because the wholesome, when cultivated, leads to welfare and happiness, I say to you: ‘|Bhikkhus, ::::|cultivate the wholesome.’”

#### 2.20 {Non-disappearance of Dhamma}

“|Bhikkhus,::::| these two things lead to the |lapse::forgetting, decay [sammosa]| and |disappearance::fading away [antaradhāna]| of the |good Dhamma::good teaching, true nature of things [saddhamma]|. What two? Misplaced words and phrases, and incorrectly interpreted meaning. When the words and phrases are misplaced, the meaning is also hard to infer or interpret. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two things that lead to the lapse and disappearance of the good Dhamma.”

“|Bhikkhus,::::| these two things lead to the continuity, maintenance, and non-disappearance of the good Dhamma. What two? Well-placed words and phrases, and correctly interpreted meaning. When the words and phrases are well placed, the meaning is also plainly obvious. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the two things that lead to the continuity, maintenance, and non-disappearance of the good Dhamma.”
