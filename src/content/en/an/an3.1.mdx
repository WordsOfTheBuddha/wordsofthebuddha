Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthi, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park. There, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus.”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“|Bhikkhus,::::| whatever |dangers::fears, perils [bhaya]| arise, all of them arise from the |immature person::lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]|, not from the |wise::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled [paṇḍita]|. Whatever |misfortunes::calamities, distresses, afflictions [upaddava]| arise, all of them arise from the immature person, not from the wise. Whatever |troubles::afflictions, adversities, menaces [upasagga]| arise, all of them arise from the immature person, not from the wise. Just as|, bhikkhus,::::| a fire that has spread from a reed hut or a grass hut burns even gabled mansions that are plastered within and without, sheltered from the wind, with |well-fitted doors::secured bolts, fastened door-bars [phusitaggaḷa]| and shuttered windows; in the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| whatever dangers arise, all of them arise from the immature person, not from the wise. Whatever misfortunes arise, all of them arise from the immature person, not from the wise. Whatever troubles arise, all of them arise from the immature person, not from the wise.

Thus, |bhikkhus,::::| the immature person is a source of danger, the wise person is free from danger. The immature person is a source of misfortune, the wise person is free from misfortune. The immature person is a source of trouble, the wise person is free from trouble. There is|, bhikkhus,::::| no danger from the wise, no misfortune from the wise, no trouble from the wise.

Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| you should train thus: ‘Avoiding the three qualities by which an immature person is recognized, and adopting the three qualities by which a wise person is known, we will behave accordingly.’ This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| you should train.”
