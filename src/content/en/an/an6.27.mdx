---
title: Paṭhama samaya sutta - Proper Time (First)
description: "The Buddha explains the six appropriate times to seek guidance from an esteemed monk: whenever the mind is overcome by any of the five hindrances, or when one lacks the sign for the immediate wearing away of the taints."
qualities: doubt, sensual desire, ill will, anxiety, dullness, drowsiness, ending
theme: wisdom
slug: an6.27
---

Then a certain bhikkhu approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Sitting to one side, that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One:

“How many times, venerable sir, are there to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him?”

“|Bhikkhu,::::| there are these six times to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. What six?

1.) Here, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed and overcome by |sensual desire::passion or lust for sensual pleasures [kāmarāga]|, and he does not truly understand the escape from the arisen sensual desire, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I dwell with a mind obsessed and overcome by sensual desire, and I do not truly understand the escape from the arisen sensual desire. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| for the abandoning of sensual desire.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the abandoning of sensual desire. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the first time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

2.) Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed and overcome by |ill will::intentional act of mentally opposing or rejecting others; an intentional construct fueled by aversion, directed against kindness or compassion. It manifests as hostility of will, impeding goodwill and fostering internal or external conflict. [byāpāda]|, and he does not truly understand the escape from the arisen ill will, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I dwell with a mind obsessed and overcome by ill will, and I do not truly understand the escape from the arisen ill will. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the abandoning of ill will.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the abandoning of ill will. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the second time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

3.) Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed and overcome by |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness lit. stiffness (of mind/body due to tiredness) [thinamiddha]|, and he does not truly understand the escape from the arisen dullness and drowsiness, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I dwell with a mind obsessed and overcome by dullness and drowsiness, and I do not truly understand the escape from the arisen dullness and drowsiness. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the abandoning of dullness and drowsiness.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the abandoning of dullness and drowsiness. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the third time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

4.) Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed and overcome by |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]|, and he does not truly understand the escape from the arisen restlessness and worry, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I dwell with a mind obsessed and overcome by restlessness and worry, and I do not truly understand the escape from the arisen restlessness and worry. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the abandoning of restlessness and worry.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the abandoning of restlessness and worry. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the fourth time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

5.) Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed and overcome by |doubt::uncertainty, indecision, lack of conviction [vicikicchā]|, and he does not truly understand the escape from the arisen doubt, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I dwell with a mind obsessed and overcome by doubt, and I do not truly understand the escape from the arisen doubt. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the abandoning of doubt.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the abandoning of doubt. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the fifth time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

6.) Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu does not know the sign by relying on which and attending to which the immediate |wearing away of the taints::gradual exhaustion and elimination of the mental defilements or deep-seated afflictions such as sensual desire, craving for existence, views, and ignorance [āsava + khaya]| occurs, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I do not know the sign by relying on which and attending to which the immediate wearing away of the taints occurs. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the wearing away of the taints.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the wearing away of the taints. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the sixth time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

These|, bhikkhu,::::| are the six times to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.”
