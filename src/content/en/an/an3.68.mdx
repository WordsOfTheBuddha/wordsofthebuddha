description: Framing the distinction between passion, aversion, and delusion, the Buddha explains their differing degrees of blameworthiness and persistence. He then teaches how to abandon each of these unwholesome roots through radical attention to specific meditative antidotes.

“If|, bhikkhus,::::| wanderers of other sects should ask you thus: ‘Friends, there are these three things. What three? |Passion::intense desire, strong emotion, infatuation, obsession, lust [rāga]|, |aversion::hatred, hostility, mental attitude of rejection, fault-finding, resentful disapproval [dosa]|, |delusion::illusion, misperception, erroneous belief, false idea, misapprehension; a fundamental distortion of reality that sustains confusion, clouds discernment, and fuels further doubt [moha]|—these, friends, are the three things. What, friends, is the distinction, what is the disparity, what is the difference between these three things?’ Being asked thus, |bhikkhus,::::| how would you answer those wanderers of other sects?”

“Venerable sir, our teachings are rooted in the Blessed One, guided by the Blessed One, and have the Blessed One as their refuge. It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One himself would clarify the meaning of this statement. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will remember it.”

“Then listen to this|, bhikkhus,::::| and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the bhikkhus replied. The Blessed One said this:

“If|, bhikkhus,::::| wanderers of other sects should ask you thus: ‘Friends, there are these three things. What three? Passion, aversion, delusion—these, friends, are the three things. What, friends, is the distinction, what is the disparity, what is the difference between these three things?’ Being asked thus, |bhikkhus,::::| you should answer those wanderers of other sects like this: ‘Friends, passion is slightly blameworthy but slow to fade away; aversion is highly blameworthy but quick to fade away; delusion is highly blameworthy and slow to fade away.’

#### Superficial Attention

‘But what, friends, is the cause, what is the reason why unarisen passion arises, and arisen passion increases and expands?’ ‘“|The sign of the beautiful::a beautiful mental image, an attractive object that is the basis for lust [subhanimitta]|,” you should answer. For one |attending superficially::attending unwisely, attending peripherally, content-bound reflection; lit. attention not from the source [ayonisomanasikāra]| to the sign of the beautiful, unarisen passion arises, and arisen passion increases and expands. This, friends, is the cause, this is the reason why unarisen passion arises, and arisen passion increases and expands.’

‘But what, friends, is the cause, what is the reason why unarisen aversion arises, and arisen aversion increases and expands?’ ‘“An |aversive mental image::sign of resistance, mental image which agitates or disturbs one [paṭighanimitta]|,” you should answer. For one attending superficially to an aversive mental image, unarisen aversion arises, and arisen aversion increases and expands. This, friends, is the cause, this is the reason why unarisen aversion arises, and arisen aversion increases and expands.’

‘But what, friends, is the cause, what is the reason why unarisen delusion arises, and arisen delusion increases and expands?’ ‘“Superficial attention,” you should answer. For one attending superficially, unarisen delusion arises, and arisen delusion increases and expands. This, friends, is the cause, this is the reason why unarisen delusion arises, and arisen delusion increases and expands.’

#### Radical Attention

‘But what, friends, is the cause, what is the reason why unarisen passion does not arise, and arisen passion is abandoned?’ ‘“An |unattractive mental image::sign of the unattractive, repulsive mental image, unattractive appearance [asubhanimitta]|,” you should answer. For one |attending radically::wisely attending, attending to the structural source, contextually reflecting; lit. attending from the source [yoniso + manasi + karoti]| to an unattractive mental image, unarisen passion does not arise, and arisen passion is abandoned. This, friends, is the cause, this is the reason why unarisen passion does not arise, and arisen passion is abandoned.’

‘But what, friends, is the cause, what is the reason why unarisen aversion does not arise, and arisen aversion is abandoned?’ ‘“The |liberation of mind::emancipated by mind/heart, samādhi obtained from fruition [cetovimutti]| through |loving-kindness::goodwill, friendliness, benevolence [metta]|,” you should answer. For one attending radically to the liberation of mind through loving-kindness, unarisen aversion does not arise, and arisen aversion is abandoned. This, friends, is the cause, this is the reason why unarisen aversion does not arise, and arisen aversion is abandoned.’

‘But what, friends, is the cause, what is the reason why unarisen delusion does not arise, and arisen delusion is abandoned?’ ‘“Radical attention,” you should answer. For one attending radically, unarisen delusion does not arise, and arisen delusion is abandoned. This, friends, is the cause, this is the reason why unarisen delusion does not arise, and arisen delusion is abandoned.’”
