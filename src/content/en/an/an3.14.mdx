---
title: Cakkavatti sutta - The Wheel-Turning Monarch
description: Just as a righteous wheel-turning monarch relies on the Dhamma to provide protection for his people, so too the Buddha relies on the Dhamma to guide bodily, verbal, and mental actions, setting in motion the unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma.
qualities: safety, wisdom, ethical conduct, recollection of the Dhamma
theme: story, recollection of the Buddha
slug: an3.14
priority: 1.1
---

“Even a |wheel-turning monarch::a just and virtuous ruler whose domain extends over the entire known world [rājā cakkavattī]|, |bhikkhus,::::| a righteous king ruling by the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, does not roll forth the wheel without a king over him.” When this was said, a certain bhikkhu addressed the Blessed One:

“But who, venerable sir, is the king over the wheel-turning monarch, the righteous king ruling by the Dhamma?”

“The Dhamma|, bhikkhu::::|,” the Blessed One said.

“Here, |bhikkhu,::::| a wheel-turning monarch, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as his standard, the Dhamma as his banner, with the Dhamma as his authority, provides just protection, defense, and guardianship for the people of his household.

Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| a wheel-turning monarch, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as his standard, the Dhamma as his banner, with the Dhamma as his authority, provides just protection, defense, and guardianship for the nobles, for his vassals, for his armed forces, for the brahmins and householders, for the town and country folk, for ascetics and brahmins, and for beasts and birds. That wheel-turning monarch, bhikkhu, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as his standard, the Dhamma as his banner, with the Dhamma as his authority, having provided just protection, defense, and guardianship for the people of his household, and having provided just protection, defense, and guardianship for the nobles, <collapse>and having provided just protection, defense, and guardianship for</collapse> his vassals, his armed forces, the brahmins and householders, the town and country folk, ascetics and brahmins, and for beasts and birds, rolls forth the wheel solely through the Dhamma. And that wheel cannot be rolled back by any human adversary.

In the same way, |bhikkhu,::::| the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]|, the |Arahant::a worthy one, a fully awakened being, epithet of the Buddha [arahant]|, the |Perfectly Awakened One::fully awakened being, fully enlightened being [sammāsambuddha]|, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as his standard, the Dhamma as his banner, with the Dhamma as his authority, provides just protection, defense, and guardianship regarding bodily action: ‘Such bodily action is to be practiced; such bodily action is not to be practiced.’

Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| the Tathāgata, the Arahant, the Perfectly Awakened One, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as his standard, the Dhamma as his banner, with the Dhamma as his authority, provides just protection, defense, and guardianship regarding verbal action: ‘Such verbal action is to be practiced; such verbal action is not to be practiced’... and regarding mental action: ‘Such mental action is to be practiced; such mental action is not to be practiced.’

That Tathāgata, bhikkhu, the Arahant, the Perfectly Awakened One, a righteous king ruling by the Dhamma, relying on the Dhamma alone, honoring the Dhamma, respecting the Dhamma, revering the Dhamma, with the Dhamma as his standard, the Dhamma as his banner, with the Dhamma as his authority, having provided just protection, defense, and guardianship regarding bodily action, having provided just protection, defense, and guardianship regarding verbal action, and having provided just protection, defense, and guardianship regarding mental action, sets in motion the unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma solely through the Dhamma. And that wheel cannot be rolled back by any ascetic or brahmin, deity, |Māra::embodiment of all forces that keep beings trapped in the cycle of rebirth [māra]|, |Brahmā::God, the first deity to be born at the beginning of a new cosmic cycle and whose lifespan lasts for the entire cycle [brahmā]|, or anyone in the world.”
