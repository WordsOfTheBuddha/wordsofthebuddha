### Grounds for Laziness

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these eight grounds of laziness. What eight?

1.) Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has work to be done. He thinks: ‘I will have work to do, and while I am doing work my body will get tired. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the first ground of laziness.

2.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has completed some work. He thinks: ‘I have done work, and while I was doing work my body got tired. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the second ground of laziness.

3.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has a path to travel. He thinks: ‘I will have a path to travel, and while I am traveling the path my body will get tired. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the third ground of laziness.

4.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has traveled a path. He thinks: ‘I have traveled the path, and while I was traveling the path my body got tired. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the fourth ground of laziness.

5.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| while going for alms in a village or town, a bhikkhu does not get his fill of coarse or excellent food. He thinks: ‘While going for alms in a village or town, I did not get my fill of coarse or excellent food. My body is tired and unfit for work. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the fifth ground of laziness.

6.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| while going for alms in a village or town, a bhikkhu gets his fill of coarse or excellent food. He thinks: ‘While going for alms in a village or town, I got my fill of coarse or excellent food. My body is heavy and unfit for work, |like a heap of soaked beans::feeling weighed down and sluggish; lit. like a pile of wet mung beans, it seems [māsācitaṁ maññe]|. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the sixth ground of laziness.

7.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a slight illness has arisen in a bhikkhu. He thinks: ‘This slight illness has arisen in me. It is suitable to lie down. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the seventh ground of laziness.

8.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has recovered from an illness, not long after recovering from sickness. He thinks: ‘I have recovered from an illness, not long after recovering from sickness. My body is weak and unfit for work. Let me lie down.’ So he lies down and does not arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the eighth ground of laziness. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the eight grounds of laziness.

### Grounds for Arousing Energy

|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these eight grounds for arousing energy. What eight?

1.) Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has work to be done. He thinks: ‘I will have work to do, and while doing work it will not be easy to keep the teaching of the Buddhas in mind. Let me arouse energy in advance for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the first ground for arousing energy.

2.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has completed some work. He thinks: ‘I have done work, and while I was doing work I was unable to keep the teaching of the Buddhas in mind. Let me arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the second ground for arousing energy.

3.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has a path to travel. He thinks: ‘I will have a path to travel, and while traveling the path it will not be easy to keep the teaching of the Buddhas in mind. Let me arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the third ground for arousing energy.

4.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has traveled a path. He thinks: ‘I have traveled the path, and while I was traveling the path I was unable to keep the teaching of the Buddhas in mind. Let me arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the fourth ground for arousing energy.

5.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| while going for alms in a village or town, a bhikkhu does not get his fill of coarse or excellent food. He thinks: ‘While going for alms in a village or town, I did not get my fill of coarse or excellent food. My body is light and fit for work. Let me arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the fifth ground for arousing energy.

6.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| while going for alms in a village or town, a bhikkhu gets his fill of coarse or excellent food. He thinks: ‘While going for alms in a village or town, I got my fill of coarse or excellent food. My body is strong and fit for work. Let me arouse energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the sixth ground for arousing energy.

7.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a slight illness has arisen in a bhikkhu. He thinks: ‘This slight illness has arisen in me, and there is a possibility that my illness might worsen. Let me arouse energy in advance for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the seventh ground for arousing energy.

8.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu has recovered from an illness, not long after recovering from sickness. He thinks: ‘I have recovered from an illness, not long after recovering from sickness, and there is a possibility that my illness might relapse. Let me arouse energy in advance for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized.’ So he arouses energy for the attainment of what has not yet been attained, for the achievement of what has not yet been achieved, and for the realization of what has not yet been realized. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the eighth ground for arousing energy.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the eight grounds for arousing energy.”
