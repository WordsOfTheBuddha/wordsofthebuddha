Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Udāyī: “How many subjects of recollection are there, Udāyī?”

When this was said, the venerable Udāyī was silent.

For a second time, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Udāyī: “How many subjects of recollection are there, Udāyī?” For a second time, the venerable Udāyī was silent. For a third time, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Udāyī: “How many subjects of recollection are there, Udāyī?”

For a third time, the venerable Udāyī was silent.

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the venerable Udāyī: “The Teacher is addressing you, friend Udāyī.”

“I am listening to the Blessed One, friend Ānanda.

Here, venerable sir, a bhikkhu recollects his manifold past lives, that is, one birth, two births, <collapse>three births, four, five, ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many |aeon::lifespan of a world system, a vast cosmic time span [kappa]|s of cosmic contraction, many aeons of cosmic expansion, many aeons of cosmic contraction and expansion: ‘There I was so named, of such a clan, with such an appearance, such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my life-span; passing away from there, I was reborn elsewhere; there too I was so named, of such a clan, with such an appearance, such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my life-span; passing away from there, I was reborn here.’</collapse> Thus with their aspects and particulars, he recollects his manifold past lives. This, venerable sir, is a subject of recollection.”

Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “I knew, Ānanda: ‘This |misguided person::vain person [moghapurisa]| Udāyī does not dwell devoted to the |higher mind::meditation, mental development, higher consciousness [adhicitta]|.’ How many subjects of recollection are there, Ānanda?”

“There are five subjects of recollection, venerable sir. What five?

1.) Here, venerable sir, a bhikkhu, entirely secluded from sensual pleasures <collapse>and unwholesome mental states, enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by reflection and examination, born of seclusion, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure. Furthermore, with the settling of reflection and examination, a bhikkhu enters and dwells in the second jhāna, which is characterized by internal tranquility and unification of mind, free from reflection and examination, born of collectedness, and imbued with uplifting joy and pleasure. Furthermore, with the fading of desire for uplifting joy, the bhikkhu dwells equanimous, mindful and clearly aware, experiencing pleasure with the body. He</collapse> enters and dwells in the third jhāna. This subject of recollection, venerable sir, thus developed and thus cultivated, leads to a |pleasant abiding in the here and now::comfortable existence in this very life [diṭṭhadhammasukhavihāra]|.

2.) Furthermore, venerable sir, a bhikkhu attends to the |perception of brightness::perception of light [ālokasaññā]| and |resolves::determines; lit. stands firmly [adhiṭṭhāti]| on the |perception of daylight::concept of day [divāsaññā]|: as by day, so by night; as by night, so by day. Thus, with an open and unencumbered heart, he cultivates a mind full of radiance. This subject of recollection, venerable sir, thus developed and thus cultivated, leads to the attainment of |knowledge and vision::understanding and insight; (comm) gaining knowledge and vision via divine eye [ñāṇadassana]|.

3.) Furthermore, venerable sir, a bhikkhu |reviews::considers, reflects, regards [paccavekkhati]| this very body from the soles of the feet upwards and from the top of the hair downwards, bounded by skin and full of various impurities thus: ‘In this body, there are hairs of the head, hairs of the body, nails, teeth, skin; flesh, |sinews::tendons and ligaments connecting muscle to bone [nhāru]|, bones, |bone marrow::the soft tissue inside the bones [aṭṭhimiñjaṁ]|, kidneys; heart, liver, |inner membrane::the pleura or fascia wrapping the organs [kilomaka]|, |spleen::the blood-filtering organ under the left ribs [pihaka]|, lungs; intestines, |mesentery::the tissue binding the intestines; lit. intestine string [antaguṇa]|, |stomach contents::undigested food; lit. related to the belly [udariya]|, |feces::excrement, digested waste [karīsaṁ]|, [brain]; |bile::digestive fluid, usually visualized in the gall bladder [pittaṁ]|, |phlegm::mucus of the respiratory tract [semha]|, |pus::fluid resulting from infection or decay [pubba]|, blood, |sweat::perspiration exuded from pore spaces [seda]|, |fat::thick, adipose tissue [meda]|, tears, |grease::liquid fat or skin oils (distinct from solid fat) [vasā]|, saliva, |nasal mucus::snot, fluid from the nose [siṅghāṇikā]|, |synovial fluid::lubricating fluid inside the joints [lasikā]|, and |urine::liquid waste stored in the bladder [mutta]|.’ This subject of recollection, venerable sir, thus developed and thus cultivated, leads to the abandoning of sensual desire.

4.) Furthermore, venerable sir, a bhikkhu, just as if he saw a corpse thrown in a charnel ground one day dead, two days dead, or three days dead, bloated, discolored, and decomposing, he compares this very body to it thus: ‘This body too is of the same nature; it will become like that; it is not exempt from that fate.’

Or as if he were to see a corpse cast away in a charnel ground, being devoured by crows, hawks, vultures, dogs, jackals, or various kinds of worms. He compares this very body to it thus: ‘This body too is of the same nature; it will become like that; it is not exempt from that fate.’

Or just as if he saw a corpse thrown in a charnel ground, a skeleton with flesh and blood, held together by sinews ... or a skeleton without flesh but smeared with blood, held together by sinews ... or a skeleton devoid of flesh and blood, held together by sinews. Bones disconnected and scattered in all directions—here a hand bone, there a foot bone, here a shin bone, there a thigh bone, here a pelvis bone, there a rib, here a spine, there a shoulder blade, here a neck bone, there a jawbone, here a tooth, and there a skull; bones bleached white like the color of a conch shell; bones heaped in a pile, over a year old; or bones that are rotten and reduced to dust. He compares this very body to it thus: ‘This body too is of the same nature; it will become like that; it is not exempt from that fate.’ This subject of recollection, venerable sir, thus developed and thus cultivated, leads to the uprooting of the |conceit ‘I am’::egotism, self-conceit, self comparison [asmimāna]|.

5.) Furthermore, venerable sir, a bhikkhu, with the abandoning of pleasure ... enters and dwells in the fourth jhāna. This subject of recollection, venerable sir, thus developed and thus cultivated, leads to the |penetration of various elements::comprehension of numerous elements [anekadhātupaṭivedha]|. These, venerable sir, are the five subjects of recollection.”

“Good, good, Ānanda. Well then, Ānanda, you should also remember this sixth subject of recollection. 6.) Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu goes forward mindfully and returns mindfully; he stands mindfully, sits mindfully, lies down in sleeping posture mindfully, and resolves on his tasks mindfully. This subject of recollection, Ānanda, thus developed and thus cultivated, leads to |radical comprehension::complete comprehension of the arising, persisting, and fading away of phenomena, structural comprehension, discerning mindfulness [satisampajañña]|.”
