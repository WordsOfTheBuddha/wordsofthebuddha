---
title: Tatiya hita sutta - Benefit (Third)
description: The Buddha describes a person who neither develops virtue, collectedness, and wisdom personally nor inspires others to do so, practicing neither for their own welfare nor others’.
qualities: ethical conduct, collectedness, wisdom, liberation, vision
theme: wisdom, inspiration
slug: an5.19
priority: 1.1
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu endowed with five qualities is practicing neither for his own benefit nor for the benefit of others. What five?

1.) Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu is neither personally |accomplished in virtue::of excellent morality; accomplished in ethical conduct [sīlasampanna]|, nor does he |encourage::arouses, inspires, urges; lit. causes to take [samādapeti]| others in accomplishment in virtue;

2.) he is neither personally |accomplished in collectedness::accomplished in stability of mind, skilled in mental stillness [samādhisampanna]|, nor does he encourage others in accomplishment in collectedness;

3.) he is neither personally accomplished in |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]|, nor does he encourage others in accomplishment in wisdom;

4.) he is neither personally accomplished in |liberation::release, deliverance, freedom, emancipation [vimutti]|, nor does he encourage others in accomplishment in liberation;

5.) he is neither personally accomplished in the |knowledge and vision of liberation::understanding and realization of liberation, total understanding of emancipation [vimuttiñāṇadassana]|, nor does he encourage others in accomplishment in the knowledge and vision of liberation.

Endowed with these five qualities, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu is practicing neither for his own benefit nor for the benefit of others.”
