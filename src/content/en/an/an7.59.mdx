---
title: Kimila sutta - With Kimila
description: The Buddha identifies seven qualities of respect and reverence that ensure the long-lasting endurance of the good Dhamma.
qualities: respect, diligence, collectedness, ethical conduct
theme: principle
slug: an7.59
character: Venerable Kimila
priority: 1.1
similar: an5.201, an6.40
---

Thus have I heard—At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at |Kimilā::name of a city [kimilā]| in the Nicula Grove. Then the venerable Kimila went to the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the venerable Kimila said to the Blessed One:

“Venerable sir, what is the cause, what is the condition, whereby after the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| has attained |final Nibbāna::complete cooling, full quenching, total emancipation, dying one’s final death [parinibbāna]| the |good Dhamma::good teaching, true doctrine [saddhamma]| does not last long?”

“Here, Kimila, after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna, the bhikkhus, bhikkhunīs, male lay disciples, and female lay disciples dwell without respect and reverence for the |Teacher::The Buddha [satthā]|; they dwell without respect and reverence for the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|; they dwell without respect and reverence for the |Saṅgha::The community of monks and nuns practicing in line with the Buddha’s teachings. In the broader sense, this is the community of disciples who have realized the noble path and fruition through the Buddha’s teachings [saṅgha]|; they dwell without respect and reverence for the training; they dwell without respect and reverence for |collectedness::stability of mind, stillness of mind, mental composure [samādhi]|; they dwell without respect and reverence for |diligence::quality of wishing to do one’s work or duty well, with alertness, carefulness and care [appamāda]|; they dwell without respect and reverence for |courteousness::hospitality, friendly reception [paṭisanthāra]|. This, Kimila, is the cause, this is the condition, whereby after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma does not last long.”

“And what, venerable sir, is the cause, what is the condition, whereby after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma lasts long?”

“Here, Kimila, after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna, the bhikkhus, bhikkhunīs, male lay disciples, and female lay disciples dwell with respect and reverence for the Teacher; they dwell with respect and reverence for the Dhamma; they dwell with respect and reverence for the Saṅgha; they dwell with respect and reverence for the training; they dwell with respect and reverence for collectedness; they dwell with respect and reverence for diligence; they dwell with respect and reverence for courteousness. This, Kimila, is the cause, this is the condition, whereby after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna the good Dhamma lasts long.”
