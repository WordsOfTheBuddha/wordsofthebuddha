---
title: Sappurisa sutta - A Person of Integrity
description: The Buddha likens a person of integrity to a nourishing rain cloud. By practicing generosity and honoring family, friends, and spiritual practitioners, they dispel stinginess and attain an auspicious rebirth.
qualities: person of integrity, giving, faith, respect, stinginess
theme: wisdom
slug: an8.38
simile: cloud that rains everywhere
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| a |person of integrity::virtuous person [sappurisa]| born into a family is for the good, welfare, and happiness of many people—they are for the good, welfare, and happiness of their mother and father; for the good, welfare, and happiness of their spouse and children; for the good, welfare, and happiness of their slaves, workers, and servants; for the good, welfare, and happiness of their friends and companions; for the good, welfare, and happiness of their departed ancestors; for the good, welfare, and happiness of the king; for the good, welfare, and happiness of the deities; and for the good, welfare, and happiness of ascetics and brahmins.

Just as|, bhikkhus,::::| a great rain cloud nourishing all crops to fruition is for the good, welfare, and happiness of many people; in the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| a person of integrity born into a family is for the good, welfare, and happiness of many people—for the good, welfare, and happiness of their mother and father, of their spouse and children, of their slaves, workers, and servants, of their friends and companions, of their departed ancestors, of the king, of the deities, and of ascetics and brahmins.

Truly for the good of many,
the wise person dwells at home.
Day and night diligent toward
his mother, father, and ancestors,
he venerates them in accordance with the Dhamma,
recollecting what they did [for him] in the past.

Firm in faith, the pious man,
having known their good qualities,
venerates the homeless renunciants,
the mendicants who lead the spiritual life.

Beneficial to the king and the deities,
beneficial to relatives and friends.

Indeed, beneficial to all,
well-established in the good Dhamma,
he has removed the |stain of stinginess::tarnish of selfishness [maccheramala]|,
and fares on to an |auspicious::blessed, safe [siva]| world.”
