Then, the wanderer Vacchagotta approached the Blessed One. Having drawn near, he exchanged friendly greetings with the Blessed One, and after a courteous and polite conversation, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the wanderer Vacchagotta said to the Blessed One:

“Sir Gotama, I have heard this: ‘The ascetic Gotama says this: “A |gift::offering, donation, charity [dāna]| should only be given to me, not to others; a gift should only be given to my disciples, not to the disciples of others; only what is given to me bears great fruit, not what is given to others; only what is given to my disciples bears great fruit, not what is given to the disciples of others.”’ There are those who say this, sir Gotama: ‘The ascetic Gotama says this: “A gift should only be given to me, not to others. A gift should only be given to my disciples, not to the disciples of others. Only what is given to me bears great fruit, not what is given to others. Only what is given to my disciples bears great fruit, not what is given to the disciples of others.”’

“Do those who speak thus state what has been said by sir Gotama, and not misrepresent sir Gotama with what is untrue? Do they explain in accordance with the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]|, so that no reasonable consequence of their statement affords grounds for criticism? For we do not wish to misrepresent sir Gotama.”

“Vaccha, those who say this: ‘The ascetic Gotama says this: “A gift should only be given to me … only what is given to my disciples bears great fruit, not what is given to the disciples of others,”’ do not state what has been said by me, and they misrepresent me with what is untrue.

“Whoever, Vaccha, prevents another from giving a gift creates an obstruction for three, poses a roadblock for three. What three? He creates an obstruction for the merit making of the donor, he creates an obstruction for the gain of the recipient, and even beforehand, he has already injured and impaired himself. Whoever, Vaccha, prevents another from giving a gift creates an obstruction for these three, poses a roadblock for these three.

“I, however, Vaccha, say this: even if someone throws dish-washing water or bowl-rinsing water into a cesspool or a sewer, thinking: ‘May whatever living beings are there sustain themselves on this!’—I say that merit is acquired even from that. How much more, then, when given to a human being!

“However, Vaccha, I say that what is given to a |virtuous::ethical, endowed with moral conduct [sīlavant]| person bears great fruit, not so what is given to an |unprincipled::unethical, immoral [dussīla]| person. And [the most worthy recipient] is one who has abandoned five factors and is endowed with five factors.

What five factors are abandoned? |Sensual desire::interest in sensual pleasure, sensual impulse [kāmacchanda]| is abandoned, |ill will::hostile thought, malevolent intent, wishing ruin or downfall [byāpāda]| is abandoned, |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness [thinamiddha]| is abandoned, |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]| is abandoned, and |doubt::uncertainty, indecisiveness [vicikicchā]| is abandoned. These are the five factors that are abandoned.

What five factors are they endowed with? They are endowed with an |adept’s::an arahant; one beyond training who has completed the spiritual path [asekkha]| aggregate of |virtue::moral conduct, ethical behavior [sīla]|, an adept’s aggregate of |collectedness::stability of mind, unshakeable mind, samādhi [samādhi]|, an adept’s aggregate of |wisdom::distinctive knowledge, discernment [paññā]|, an adept’s aggregate of |liberation::freedom [vimutti]|, and an adept’s aggregate of |knowledge and vision of liberation::experiential understanding and insight into freedom [vimuttiñāṇadassana]|. They are endowed with these five factors.

Thus, I say that what is given to one who has abandoned five factors and is endowed with five factors bears great fruit.

Among cattle of any sort,
whether black, white, red, or golden,
mottled, uniform,
or pigeon-colored:

the tamed bull is born,
the one that can bear the load,
a draft ox endowed with strength,
with fine pace and stride—
they yoke the burden just to him;
not minding his color.

Even so among human beings,
in whatever birth one is born—
whether among |aristocrats::noble warriors, ruler caste [khattiya]|, brahmins, merchants,
workers, outcastes, or scavengers:

whichever among them
is born tamed, of good conduct,
firm in Dhamma, accomplished in virtue,
truthful, conscientious;

who has left behind birth and death,
consummate in the spiritual life,
with burden laid down, |disentangled::disengaged, unfettered [visaṁyutta]|,
done what had to be done, |taintless::undefiled, free from effluents, free from mental defilements [anāsava]|;

gone beyond |all states of mind::all mental phenomena, all things [sabbadhamma]|,
|quenched::liberated from mental defilements [nibbuta]| through |not grasping::not holding (onto), detaching (from), not taking possession (of) [anupādāya]|:
an offering yields abundant fruit,
when planted in that pure field.

Immature persons who do not comprehend,
|undiscerning::one lacking discernment, who has not cultivated wisdom, unwise [dummedha]| and unlearned;
give gifts to those outside,
not attending on the peaceful.

Those who attend on the peaceful,
the wise, esteemed as steadfast,
and whose faith in the Accomplished One
has taken root and is firmly established:

they go to the world of gods,
or are born here in a good family;
and advancing gradually,
those skilled ones attain |Nibbāna::complete cooling, letting go of everything, deathless, freedom from calamity, the non-disintegrating; lit. blowing away [nibbāna]|.”
