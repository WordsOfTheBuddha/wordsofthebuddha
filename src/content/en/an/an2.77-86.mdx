---
title: Sanimitta vagga - With a Basis
description: Harmful, unwholesome qualities do not arise out of nowhere. The Buddha shows they always have a basis, and that by abandoning that very basis, they do not come to be.
qualities: unwholesome, harm, perception, felt experience, consciousness
theme: cultivating discernment, inquisitiveness
slug: an2.77-86
priority: 1.2
---

#### 2.77

“|Bhikkhus,::::| |harmful::injurious, bad, or evil. Encompasses the deceptively alluring that is ultimately detrimental or ruinous [pāpaka]|, |unwholesome::unhealthy, unskillful, unbeneficial, or karmically unprofitable [akusala]| qualities |arise::appear, occur; lit. go up [uppajjati]| |with a basis::with a cause; lit. with sign [sanimitta]|, not without a basis. By abandoning that very basis, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.78

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise from a |source::foundation; lit. tying down [nidāna]|, not without a source. By abandoning that very source, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.79

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise from a |cause::ground, reason [hetu]|, not without a cause. By abandoning that very cause, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.80

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise |from a prompt::intentionally [sasaṅkhāra]|, not without a prompt. By abandoning that very prompt, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.81

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise from a |supporting condition::proximate cause, requisite [paccaya]|, not without a condition. By abandoning that very supporting condition, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.82

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise with |form::materiality, material existence, experience of the material world, i.e. encompassing both one’s body and external objects, whether near or far, gross or subtle, deficient or refined; first of the five aggregates [rūpa]|, not without form. By abandoning that very form, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.83

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise with |feeling::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensation, experience felt on contact through eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]|, not without feeling. By abandoning that very feeling, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.84

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise with |perception::the mental process of recognizing, conceptualizing, and interpreting sensory experience; third of the five aggregates [saññā]|, not without perception. By abandoning that very perception, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.85

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise with |consciousness::quality of awareness — distinctive knowing that arises in dependence on the meeting of eye and form, ear and sound, nose and odor, tongue and taste, body and tangible object, mind and mind object; fifth of the five aggregates [viññāṇa]|, not without consciousness. By abandoning that very consciousness, harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”

#### 2.86

“|Bhikkhus,::::| harmful, unwholesome qualities arise |based on the conditioned::with a conditioned object, founded on the constructed [saṅkhatārammaṇa]|, not based on the |unconditioned::not created, unconstructed, unformed, epithet of Nibbāna [asaṅkhata]|. By abandoning that very |conditioned::constructed, created, fabricated [saṅkhata]| [thing], harmful, unwholesome qualities do not come to be.”
