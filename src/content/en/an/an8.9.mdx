“|Bhikkhus,::::| speaking rightly of |Nanda::the Buddha’s half-brother and cousin, foremost disciple in guarding the doors of the sense faculties; read his story in [Ud 3.2 - Nanda sutta](/ud3.2) [nanda]|, one would call him a ‘|clansman::young gentleman, son of a good family [kulaputta]|.’ Speaking rightly of Nanda|, bhikkhus,::::| one would call him ‘strong.’ Speaking rightly of Nanda|, bhikkhus,::::| one would call him ‘graceful.’ Speaking rightly of Nanda|, bhikkhus,::::| one would call him ‘strongly prone to lust.’ How else|, bhikkhus,::::| could Nanda live the complete and perfectly pure |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]|, unless he |guards the doors::protects the mind with respect to; lit. guarded doors [guttadvāra]| of the sense faculties, observes moderation in eating, is devoted to |wakefulness::lucidity, being awake, being alert [jāgariya]|, and is endowed with |radical comprehension::complete comprehension of the arising, persisting, and fading away of phenomena, structural comprehension, discerning mindfulness [satisampajañña]|?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| this is how Nanda guards the doors of the sense faculties. If|, bhikkhus,::::| Nanda needs to look toward the eastern direction, having brought his whole mind to bear, Nanda looks toward the eastern direction, thinking: ‘While I am looking toward the eastern direction thus, harmful, |unwholesome::unhealthy, unskillful, unbeneficial, or karmically unprofitable [akusala]| states of |craving and distress::greediness and dissatisfaction, wanting and unhappiness, craving and aversion [abhijjhā + domanassa]| might not |stream in::flood in, overwhelm me [anvāssavati]|.’ Thus he is |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| in that regard.

If|, bhikkhus,::::| Nanda needs to look toward the western direction ... the northern direction ... the southern direction ... if he needs to look up ... to look down ... if he needs to survey the intermediate directions, having brought his whole mind to bear, Nanda surveys the intermediate direction, thinking: ‘While I am surveying the intermediate direction thus, harmful, unwholesome states of craving and distress might not stream in.’ Thus he is clearly aware in that regard. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is how Nanda guards the doors of the sense faculties.

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| this is how Nanda observes moderation in eating. Here, |bhikkhus,::::| Nanda consumes food after careful reflection: ‘[This food is] not for pleasure, not for indulgence, not for beautifying oneself, and not for the sake of physical appearance; but only for the support and maintenance of this body, to prevent harm, and as a support for the spiritual life. Thus I shall put an end to the old feeling [of hunger] and not give rise to a new feeling [from an excess of eating], and I shall sustain life blamelessly and dwell at ease.’ This|, bhikkhus,::::| is how Nanda observes moderation in eating.

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| this is how Nanda is devoted to wakefulness. Here, |bhikkhus,::::| during the day, Nanda practices walking meditation ^[1]^ and sitting [meditation], purifying the mind of |obstructive::hindering, impeding [āvaraṇīya]| states. During the first |watch::one eighth of the day; about three hours [yāma]| of the night, he practices walking meditation and sitting meditation, purifying the mind of obstructive states. During the middle watch of the night, he lies down on his right side in the lion’s posture, placing one foot on top of the other, mindful and clearly aware, having attended to the idea of rising up. During the last watch of the night, having risen up, he practices walking meditation and sitting meditation, purifying the mind of obstructive states. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is how Nanda is devoted to wakefulness.

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| this is how Nanda is endowed with radical comprehension. Here, |bhikkhus,::::| |felt experiences::pleasant, neutral, or painful sensations, feelings felt on contact; second of the five aggregates [vedanā]| arise known to Nanda, remain present known, and pass away known; perceptions arise known ... thoughts arise known, remain present known, and pass away known. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is how Nanda is endowed with radical comprehension.

How else|, bhikkhus,::::| could Nanda live the complete and perfectly pure spiritual life, unless he guards the doors of the sense faculties, observes moderation in eating, is devoted to wakefulness, and is endowed with radical comprehension?”
