“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these three causes for the arising of |kamma::action, deed, doing, volitional act [kamma]|. What three? 1.) |Greed::a grasping mental quality of craving, possessiveness, or lustful wanting that clings to objects or experiences; it fuels attachment and obstructs renunciation and contentment [lobha]| is a cause for the arising of kamma, 2.) |aversion::hatred, hostility, mental attitude of rejection, fault-finding, resentful disapproval [dosa]| is a cause for the arising of kamma, 3.) |delusion::illusion, misperception, erroneous belief, false idea, misapprehension; a fundamental distortion of reality that sustains confusion, clouds discernment, and fuels further doubt [moha]| is a cause for the arising of kamma.

|Bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from greed, arisen from greed, originating from greed, with greed as its source—wherever one’s |individual existence::personal state of being, physical and mental individuality [attabhāva]| comes into being, there that kamma ripens. Wherever that kamma ripens, there one experiences the |result::fruition, consequence, ripening of kamma [vipāka]| of that kamma, whether in this very life, or upon rebirth [in the next life], or on some subsequent occasion.

|Bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from aversion, arisen from aversion, originating from aversion, with aversion as its source—wherever one’s individual existence comes into being, there that kamma ripens. Wherever that kamma ripens, there one experiences the result of that kamma, whether in this very life, or upon rebirth [in the next life], or on some subsequent occasion.

|Bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from delusion, arisen from delusion, originating from delusion, with delusion as its source—wherever one’s individual existence comes into being, there that kamma ripens. Wherever that kamma ripens, there one experiences the result of that kamma, whether in this very life, or upon rebirth [in the next life], or on some subsequent occasion.

Suppose|, bhikkhus,::::| there are seeds that are unbroken, unspoiled, undamaged by wind and sun, fertile, and well-stored, sown in a good field in well-prepared soil, and the rain falls in due season. Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| those seeds would come to growth, increase, and maturity.

In the exact same way|, bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from greed, arisen from greed, originating from greed, with greed as its source—wherever one’s individual existence comes into being, there that kamma ripens. Wherever that kamma ripens, there one experiences the result of that kamma, whether in this very life, or upon rebirth [in the next life], or on some subsequent occasion.

Any kamma that is produced from aversion<collapse>, arisen from aversion, originating from aversion, with aversion as its source—wherever one’s individual existence comes into being, there that kamma ripens. Wherever that kamma ripens, there one experiences the result of that kamma, whether in this very life, or upon rebirth [in the next life], or on some subsequent occasion.</collapse>

Any kamma that is produced from delusion, arisen from delusion, originating from delusion, with delusion as its source—wherever one’s individual existence comes into being, there that kamma ripens. Wherever that kamma ripens, there one experiences the result of that kamma, whether in this very life, or upon rebirth [in the next life], or on some subsequent occasion. These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the three causes for the arising of kamma.

|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these three causes for the arising of kamma. What three? 1.) |Non-greed::the absence of greed, lack of craving or lustful wanting; contentment, renunciation [alobha]| is a cause for the arising of kamma, 2.) |non-aversion::the absence of aversion, ill will, or hostility; loving-kindness, goodwill, friendliness [adosa]| is a cause for the arising of kamma, 3.) |non-delusion::the absence of illusion, misapprehension, or distorted view; clear comprehension, understanding things as they really are [amoha]| is a cause for the arising of kamma.

|Bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from non-greed, arisen from non-greed, originating from non-greed, with non-greed as its source—once greed has vanished, that kamma is abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and no longer subject to future arising.

|Bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from non-aversion, arisen from non-aversion, originating from non-aversion, with non-aversion as its source—once aversion has vanished, that kamma is abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and no longer subject to future arising.

|Bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from non-delusion, arisen from non-delusion, originating from non-delusion, with non-delusion as its source—once delusion has vanished, that kamma is abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and no longer subject to future arising.

Suppose|, bhikkhus,::::| there are seeds that are unbroken, unspoiled, undamaged by wind and sun, fertile, and well-stored. Then a person burns them with fire; having burned them with fire, reduces them to ashes; and having reduced them to ashes, winnows them in a strong wind or lets them be carried away by a swift-flowing river. Thus|, bhikkhus,::::| those seeds would be cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and no longer subject to future arising.

In the exact same way|, bhikkhus,::::| any kamma that is produced from non-greed, arisen from non-greed, originating from non-greed, with non-greed as its source—once greed has vanished, that kamma is abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and no longer subject to future arising.

Any kamma that is produced from non-aversion<collapse>, arisen from non-aversion, originating from non-aversion, with non-aversion as its source—once aversion has vanished, that kamma is abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and no longer subject to future arising.</collapse> Any kamma that is produced from non-delusion, arisen from non-delusion, originating from non-delusion, with non-delusion as its source—once delusion has vanished, that kamma is abandoned<collapse>, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated,</collapse> and no longer subject to future arising.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the three causes for the arising of kamma.”

What an ignorant person produces—
born of greed, aversion, or delusion,
whether that kamma be little or much—
is to be experienced right here;
no other ground exists for it.

Therefore, one who knows [what is born of]
greed, aversion, and delusion as well—
a bhikkhu giving rise to |true knowledge::direct liberating understanding, clear knowing [vijjā]|—
should abandon all |bad destinations::unfortunate realms of rebirth, states of misery [duggati]|.
