---
title: Accaya sutta - Transgressions
description: "The Buddha contrasts an immature person and a wise person by three qualities: whether they recognize a transgression as a transgression, make amends in accordance with the Dhamma when they see it, and properly accept another’s confession."
qualities: immaturity, wisdom, sincerity, ethical conduct
theme: training guideline, cultivating discernment, principle
slug: an3.4
priority: 1.2
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| an |immature person::lacking in discernment or good sense, child-like in understanding [bāla]| should be recognized by three qualities. What three? They do not see a |transgression::fault, offense, moral lapse [accaya]| as a transgression; when they do see a transgression as a transgression, they do not |make amends::rectify it, make up for it [paṭikaroti]| |in accordance with the Dhamma::in accordance with the teaching, in accordance with the rule [yathādhamma]|; and when another person |confesses::acknowledges, points out, declares [deseti]| a transgression, they do not accept it in accordance with the Dhamma.

|Bhikkhus,::::| an immature person should be recognized by these three qualities.

|Bhikkhus,::::| a |wise::astute, intelligent, learned, skilled [paṇḍita]| person should be recognized by three qualities. What three? They see a transgression as a transgression; when they see a transgression as a transgression, they make amends in accordance with the Dhamma; and when another person confesses a transgression, they accept it in accordance with the Dhamma.

|Bhikkhus,::::| a wise person should be recognized by these three qualities. Therefore, |bhikkhus,::::| <collapse>you should train thus: ‘Avoiding the three qualities by which an immature person is recognized, and adopting the three qualities by which a wise person is known, we will behave accordingly.’ This is how|, bhikkhus,::::| you should train.</collapse>”
