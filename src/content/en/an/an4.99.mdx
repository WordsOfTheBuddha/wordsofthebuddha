---
title: Sikkhāpada sutta - Training Rules
description: The Buddha explains the four kinds of persons found existing in the world according to whether they observe the training rules themselves and encourage others to do so.
qualities: ethical conduct, spiritual life, slaughtering, stealing, sexual misconduct, wrong speech, consuming intoxicants
theme: training guideline, inspiration
slug: an4.99
priority: 1.1
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these four kinds of persons found existing in the world. What four?

1.) One who is practicing for one’s own benefit, not for the benefit of others,
2.) One who is practicing for the benefit of others, not for one’s own benefit,
3.) One who is practicing neither for one’s own benefit nor for the benefit of others,
4.) One who is practicing both for one’s own benefit and for the benefit of others.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does a person practice for one’s own benefit, not for the benefit of others? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person personally abstains from |killing living beings::taking life, destruction of life [pāṇātipāta]|, but does not |encourage::arouses, inspires, urges; lit. causes to take [samādapeti]| others to abstain from killing living beings; he personally abstains from |taking what is not given::theft, stealing, taking what is not offered [adinnādāna]|, but does not encourage others to abstain from taking what is not given; he personally abstains from |sexual misconduct::wrong behaviour in sexual matters [kāmesumicchācāra]|, but does not encourage others to abstain from sexual misconduct; he personally abstains from |false speech::lying, deliberately speaking falsehood [musāvāda]|, but does not encourage others to abstain from false speech; he personally abstains from |consuming intoxicants::consuming wine, spirits, or drugs and getting intoxicated [surāmerayamajja]| that are the basis for |negligence::heedlessness, carelessness [pamāda]|, but does not encourage others to abstain from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence. In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| a person is practicing for one’s own benefit, not for the benefit of others.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does a person practice for the benefit of others, not for one’s own benefit? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person does not personally abstain from killing living beings, but encourages others to abstain from killing living beings; he does not personally abstain from taking what is not given, but encourages others to abstain from taking what is not given; he does not personally abstain from sexual misconduct, but encourages others to abstain from sexual misconduct; he does not personally abstain from false speech, but encourages others to abstain from false speech; he does not personally abstain from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence, but encourages others to abstain from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence. In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| a person is practicing for the benefit of others, not for one’s own benefit.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does a person practice neither for one’s own benefit nor for the benefit of others? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person does not personally abstain from killing living beings, nor does he encourage others to abstain from killing living beings; he does not personally abstain from taking what is not given, nor does he encourage others to abstain from taking what is not given; he does not personally abstain from sexual misconduct, nor does he encourage others to abstain from sexual misconduct; he does not personally abstain from false speech, nor does he encourage others to abstain from false speech; he does not personally abstain from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence, nor does he encourage others to abstain from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence. In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| a person is practicing neither for one’s own benefit nor for the benefit of others.

And how|, bhikkhus,::::| does a person practice both for one’s own benefit and for the benefit of others? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person personally abstains from killing living beings, and he encourages others to abstain from killing living beings; he personally abstains from taking what is not given, and he encourages others to abstain from taking what is not given; he personally abstains from sexual misconduct, and he encourages others to abstain from sexual misconduct; he personally abstains from false speech, and he encourages others to abstain from false speech; he personally abstains from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence, and he encourages others to abstain from consuming intoxicants that are the basis for negligence. In this way, |bhikkhus,::::| a person is practicing both for one’s own benefit and for the benefit of others.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the four kinds of persons found existing in the world.”
