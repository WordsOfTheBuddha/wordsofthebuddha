---
title: Gilāna sutta - Patients
description: Just as nursing care is prescribed for the patient who recovers only with suitable food, medicine, and an attendant, the Dhamma is taught for the sake of those who awaken through hearing it.
qualities: wholesome, compassion, giving, faith
theme: wisdom, cultivating discernment
slug: an3.22
simile: patient
priority: 1.2
---

“There are these three kinds of |patients::sick persons, ill people [gilānā]| found existing in the world|, bhikkhus.::::.| What three?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain patient, whether he receives suitable food or does not receive suitable food, whether he receives suitable medicines or does not receive suitable medicines, whether he receives a competent |attendant::nurse, caregiver, one who looks after [upaṭṭhāka]| or does not receive a competent attendant, does not recover from that |illness::sickness, affliction, disease [ābādha]|.

But here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain patient, whether he receives suitable food or does not receive suitable food, whether he receives suitable medicines or does not receive suitable medicines, whether he receives a competent attendant or does not receive a competent attendant, recovers from that illness.

And here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain patient recovers from that illness only when he receives suitable food, not when he does not receive it; only when he receives suitable medicines, not when he does not receive them; only when he receives a competent attendant, not when he does not receive one.

There, |bhikkhus,::::| it is on account of this patient—who recovers from that illness only when he receives suitable food, not when he does not receive it; only when he receives suitable medicines, not when he does not receive them; only when he receives a competent attendant, not when he does not receive one—that food for the sick has been prescribed, medicine for the sick has been prescribed, and an attendant for the sick has been prescribed. And it is on account of this patient, |bhikkhus,::::| that other patients too should be attended to.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the three kinds of patients found existing in the world.

In the same way, |bhikkhus,::::| there are these three kinds of persons resembling patients found existing in the world. What three? Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person, whether they get to see the |Tathāgata::one who has arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgata]| or do not get to see the Tathāgata, whether they get to hear the |Dhamma::teachings of the Buddha that point to the nature of reality, the ultimate truth [dhamma]| and |Vinaya::code of monastic discipline rules, training [vinaya]| proclaimed by the Tathāgata or do not get to hear the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, does not enter upon the |fixed course of rightness::the irreversible path of stream-entry and the noble eightfold path leading to awakening [sammattaniyāma]| in |wholesome qualities::skillful actions, good habits [kusaladhammā]|.

But here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person, whether they get to see the Tathāgata or do not get to see the Tathāgata, whether they get to hear the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata or do not get to hear the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, enters upon the fixed course of rightness in wholesome qualities.

And here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person enters upon the fixed course of rightness in wholesome qualities only when they get to see the Tathāgata, not when they do not get to see him; only when they get to hear the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, not when they do not get to hear it.

There, |bhikkhus,::::| it is on account of this person—who enters upon the fixed course of rightness in wholesome qualities only when they get to see the Tathāgata, not when they do not get to see him; only when they get to hear the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, not when they do not get to hear it—that the teaching of the Dhamma has been prescribed. And it is on account of this person, |bhikkhus,::::| that the Dhamma should be taught to others as well.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the three kinds of persons resembling patients found existing in the world.”
