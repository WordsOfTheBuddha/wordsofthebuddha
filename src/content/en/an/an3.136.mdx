description: "Whether or not Tathāgatas arise, this principle remains—this stability and invariance of the Dhamma: all conditioned phenomena are impermanent, all conditioned phenomena are unsatisfactory, and all phenomena are not-self."
qualities: recognition of impermanence, recognition of unsatisfactoriness, recognition of not-self, wisdom, direct knowledge
theme: principle, wisdom, cultivating discernment

“|Bhikkhus,::::| whether or not there is the arising of |Tathāgatas::ones who have arrived at the truth, an epithet of a perfectly Awakened One [tathāgatā]|, this |principle::natural condition, inherent nature, fundamental property [dhātu]| remains—this |stability of the Dhamma::regularity of natural principles [dhammaṭṭhitatā]|, this |invariance of the Dhamma::orderliness of natural principles, certainty of the law of nature [dhammaniyāmatā]|: all |conditioned phenomena::fabrications, concoctions, intentional constructs, karmic activity [saṅkhārā]| are |impermanent::unstable, transient, unreliable [anicca]|. This, a Tathāgata |awakens to::realizes, discovers [abhisambujjhati]| and |breaks through to::fully understands [abhisameti]|. Having fully awakened to and broken through to it, he points it out, teaches it, declares it, establishes it, clarifies it, analyzes it, and reveals it: ‘All conditioned phenomena are impermanent.’

|Bhikkhus,::::| whether or not there is the arising of Tathāgatas, this principle remains—this stability of the Dhamma, this invariance of the Dhamma: all conditioned phenomena are |unsatisfactory::uncomfortable, unpleasant, bring discomfort, unease, trouble, stress, suffering [dukkha]|. This, a Tathāgata awakens to and breaks through to. Having fully awakened to and broken through to it, he points it out, teaches it, declares it, establishes it, clarifies it, analyzes it, and reveals it: ‘All conditioned phenomena are unsatisfactory.’

|Bhikkhus,::::| whether or not there is the arising of Tathāgatas, this principle remains—this stability of the Dhamma, this invariance of the Dhamma: all |phenomena::all things, whether conditioned or unconditioned; characteristics, experiences, mental states and qualities [dhammā]| are |not-self::not suitable to identify with, impersonal, non-subjective, without an essence [anatta]|. This, a Tathāgata awakens to and breaks through to. Having fully awakened to and broken through to it, he points it out, teaches it, declares it, establishes it, clarifies it, analyzes it, and reveals it: ‘All phenomena are not-self.’”
