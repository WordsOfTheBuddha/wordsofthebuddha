“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these four noble lineages—foremost, of long standing, traditional, and ancient—that are |unconfounded::unmingled, unmixed, unconfused; lit. not sprinkled together [asaṅkiṇṇa]|, never before confounded, which are not being confounded, nor will they be confounded, that are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins. What four?

Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu is content with whatever robe he gets, and praises contentment with whatever robe he gets. He does not resort to improper or unseemly search for the sake of a robe; if he does not obtain a robe, he is not agitated; and when he has obtained a robe, he uses it without being tied to it, without being infatuated, not blindly absorbed in it, seeing the |drawback::disadvantage, unsatisfactoriness, inadequacy, danger [ādīnava]| in it, and discerning the |escape::way out, remedy [nissaraṇa]|. Yet, on account of that contentment with whatever robe he gets, he neither exalts himself nor disparages others. A bhikkhu who is skilled in this, tireless, |clearly aware::with attentiveness, with clear and full comprehension, intentional, deliberate, purposeful [sampajāna]| and |mindful::remembering to be present with continuous effort, observing the body in and of itself, felt experience in and of itself, mind in and of itself, mental qualities in and of themselves [patissata]| is said to be standing in the ancient, foremost noble lineage.

Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu is content with whatever |alms food::::almsfood| he gets, and praises contentment with whatever |alms food::::almsfood| he gets. He does not resort to improper or unseemly search for the sake of |alms food::::almsfood|; if he does not obtain |alms food::::almsfood|, he is not agitated; and when he has obtained |alms food::::almsfood|, he eats it without being tied to it, without being infatuated, not blindly absorbed in it, seeing the drawback in it, and discerning the escape. Yet, on account of that contentment with whatever |alms food::::almsfood| he gets, he neither exalts himself nor disparages others. A bhikkhu who is skilled in this, tireless, clearly aware and mindful is said to be standing in the ancient, foremost noble lineage.

Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu is content with whatever lodging he gets, and praises contentment with whatever lodging he gets. He does not resort to improper or unseemly search for the sake of lodging; if he does not obtain lodging, he is not agitated; and when he has obtained lodging, he uses it without being tied to it, without being infatuated, not blindly absorbed in it, seeing the drawback in it, and discerning the escape. Yet, on account of that contentment with whatever lodging he gets, he neither exalts himself nor disparages others. A bhikkhu who is skilled in this, tireless, clearly aware and mindful is said to be standing in the ancient, foremost noble lineage.

Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu delights in |cultivation::development, meditation [bhāvanā]| and is devoted to cultivation; he delights in |abandoning::letting go, giving up, removing [pahāna]| and is devoted to abandoning. Yet, on account of that delight in cultivation and devotion to cultivation, and that delight in abandoning and devotion to abandoning, he neither exalts himself nor disparages others. A bhikkhu who is skilled in this, tireless, clearly aware and mindful is said to be standing in the ancient, foremost noble lineage.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the four noble lineages—foremost, of long standing, traditional, and ancient—that are unconfounded, never before confounded, which are not being confounded, nor will they be confounded, that are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins.

Moreover, |bhikkhus,::::| when a bhikkhu is endowed with these four noble lineages, even if he dwells in the eastern direction, he overcomes |discontent::dislike, dissatisfaction, aversion, boredom [arati]| and discontent does not overcome him; even if he dwells in the western direction, he overcomes discontent and discontent does not overcome him; even if he dwells in the northern direction, he overcomes discontent and discontent does not overcome him; even if he dwells in the southern direction, he overcomes discontent and discontent does not overcome him. For what reason? Because the wise one|, bhikkhus,::::| is a conqueror of |discontent and delight::aversion and craving [aratirati]|.

Discontent does not overcome the |steadfast one::intelligent one, stable, wise [dhīra]|,
discontent does not subdue the steadfast;
the steadfast one overcomes discontent,
for the steadfast one is a |conqueror of discontent::overcomer of boredom [aratissaha]|.

|One who has cast off all deeds::completely abandoned all intentional activity [sabbakammavihāyī]|,
who can hold him back?
Akin to a golden ornament,
who is worthy to criticize him?
Even the gods praise him,
and by |Brahmā::God, the first deity to be born at the beginning of a new cosmic cycle and whose lifespan lasts for the entire cycle [brahmā]| too, he is praised.”
