---
title: Aniccānupassī sutta - Observing Impermanence
description: Seven types of noble persons are worthy of offerings. By constantly observing impermanence in all conditions, they attain liberation, ranging from Arahants who exhaust the taints in this life to different types of Non-Returners.
qualities: liberation, recognition of impermanence
theme: cultivating discernment, wisdom
slug: an7.16
priority: 1.1
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| these seven persons are worthy of offerings, worthy of hospitality, worthy of gifts, worthy of reverential salutation, and are the unsurpassed field of merit for the world. What seven?

1.) Here, |bhikkhus,::::| a certain person dwells |observing impermanence::watching instability [aniccānupassī]| in |all conditions::all activities, all processes, all fabrications, all formations [sabbasaṅkhāra]|, perceiving impermanence, experiencing impermanence, constantly, continuously, and uninterruptedly |resolving::determining [adhimuccamāna]| on it with the mind, and fathoming it with wisdom. Through the wearing away of the |taints::outflows, discharges; (comm) mental defilements [āsavā]|, he realizes with direct knowledge <collapse>and attains in this very life, the taintless liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom, and having attained it,</collapse> he dwells in it. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the first person worthy of offerings, worthy of hospitality, worthy of gifts, worthy of reverential salutation, and is the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.

2.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| here a certain person dwells observing impermanence in all conditions, perceiving impermanence, experiencing impermanence, constantly, continuously, and uninterruptedly resolving on it with the mind, and fathoming it with wisdom. For him, the exhaustion of the taints and the exhaustion of life occur simultaneously. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the second person worthy of offerings, <collapse>worthy of hospitality, worthy of gifts, worthy of reverential salutation,</collapse> and is the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.

3.) Furthermore, |bhikkhus,::::| here a certain person dwells observing impermanence in all conditions, perceiving impermanence, experiencing impermanence, constantly, continuously, and uninterruptedly resolving on it with the mind, and fathoming it with wisdom. With the total ending of the five lower fetters, he becomes one who attains |final Nibbāna::complete cooling, full quenching, total emancipation, dying one’s final death [parinibbāyi]| in the interval ...

4.) ... he becomes one who attains final Nibbāna upon landing ...

5.) ... he becomes one who attains final Nibbāna without exertion ...

6.) ... he becomes one who attains final Nibbāna with exertion ...

7.) ... he becomes one bound upstream, |heading towards the Akaniṭṭha realm::headed to the highest deva realm [akaniṭṭhagāmī]|. This|, bhikkhus,::::| is the seventh person worthy of offerings, worthy of hospitality, worthy of gifts, worthy of reverential salutation, and is the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.

These seven persons|, bhikkhus,::::| are worthy of offerings, worthy of hospitality, worthy of gifts, worthy of reverential salutation, and are the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.”
