title: Chavālāta sutta - The Charred Log
description: "The Buddha ranks four kinds of persons: those practicing neither for their own benefit nor for others’, those practicing only for others, those practicing only for themselves, and those practicing for both. He likens the first to a charred log and the last to the cream of ghee."
qualities: spiritual life, compassion
theme: inspiration, wisdom
simile: charred log, cream of ghee
slug: an4.95

“|Bhikkhus,::::| there are these four kinds of persons found existing in the world. What four?

1.) One who is practicing neither for one’s own benefit nor for the benefit of others,

2.) One who is practicing for the benefit of others, not for one’s own benefit,

3.) One who is practicing for one’s own benefit, not for the benefit of others,

4.) One who is practicing both for one’s own benefit and for the benefit of others.

Just as|, bhikkhus,::::| a |charred log::firewood log used for burning a funeral pyre [chavālāta]|, burnt at both ends and smeared with filth in the middle, is of no use as wood either in the village or in the forest; so too, |bhikkhus,::::| I say this person is like that: the one who is practicing neither for one’s own benefit nor for the benefit of others.

There, |bhikkhus,::::| the person who is practicing for the benefit of others, not for one’s own benefit, is |more excellent::more wonderful [abhikkantatara]| and |more sublime::more refined [paṇītatara]| than these two persons. There, |bhikkhus,::::| the person who is practicing for one’s own benefit, not for the benefit of others, is more excellent and more sublime than these three persons. There, |bhikkhus,::::| the person who is practicing both for one’s own benefit and for the benefit of others is the foremost, the best, the preeminent, the highest, and the most excellent of these four persons.

Just as|, bhikkhus,::::| from a cow comes milk, from milk comes curd, from curd comes butter, from butter comes ghee, and from ghee comes the |cream of ghee::the finest skim of clarified butter [sappimaṇḍa]|, which is regarded as the best; so too, |bhikkhus,::::| the person who is practicing both for one’s own benefit and for the benefit of others is the foremost, the best, the preeminent, the highest, and the most excellent of these four persons.

These|, bhikkhus,::::| are the four kinds of persons found existing in the world.”
