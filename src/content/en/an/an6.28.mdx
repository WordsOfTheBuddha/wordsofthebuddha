title: Dutiya samaya sutta - Proper Time (Second)
description: "Elder bhikkhus debate the best time of day to approach an esteemed bhikkhu. Mahākaccāna resolves the discussion by quoting the Buddha: the proper time is not based on the clock, but rather whenever one is internally overwhelmed by the five hindrances or lacks the sign for the wearing away of the taints."
qualities: doubt, sensual desire, ill will, anxiety, dullness, drowsiness, ending
theme: wisdom
slug: an6.28
character: Venerable Mahākaccāna
priority: 1.1
---

At one time, many elder bhikkhus were dwelling in the Deer Park at Isipatana near |Varanasi::name of a city; modern day Varanasi [bārāṇasī]|. Then, having returned from their alms round after the meal, as those elder bhikkhus were seated and assembled together in the round pavilion, this conversation arose among them:

“When, friends, is the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him?”

When this was said, a certain bhikkhu said to the elder bhikkhus:

“Friends, when an esteemed bhikkhu, having returned from his alms round after the meal, has washed his feet and is seated; having folded his legs crosswise, aligned his body upright, and established mindfulness as his primary focal point—that is the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.”

When this was said, another bhikkhu said to that bhikkhu:

“That is not the proper time, friends, to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. When an esteemed bhikkhu, having returned from his alms round after the meal, has washed his feet and is seated; having folded his legs crosswise, aligned his body upright, and established mindfulness as his primary focal point, at that time his fatigue from walking has not yet subsided, and his fatigue from the meal has not yet subsided. Therefore, that is not the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. Friends, when an esteemed bhikkhu, in the late afternoon, has emerged from |seclusion::solitude, privacy [paṭisallāna]| and is seated in the shade of the dwelling; having folded his legs crosswise, aligned his body upright, and established mindfulness as his primary focal point—that is the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.”

When this was said, another bhikkhu said to that bhikkhu:

“That is not the proper time, friends, to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. When an esteemed bhikkhu, in the late afternoon, has emerged from seclusion and is seated in the shade of the dwelling; having folded his legs crosswise, aligned his body upright, and established mindfulness as his primary focal point, whatever |object of collectedness::theme of mental stillness [samādhinimitta]| he attended to during the day still occupies him at that time. Therefore, that is not the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. Friends, when an esteemed bhikkhu rises at dawn and is seated; having folded his legs crosswise, aligned his body upright, and established mindfulness as his primary focal point—that is the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.”

When this was said, another bhikkhu said to that bhikkhu:

“That is not the proper time, friends, to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. When an esteemed bhikkhu rises at dawn and is seated; having folded his legs crosswise, aligned his body upright, and established mindfulness as his primary focal point, at that time his body is full of vitality, and it is comfortable for him to attend to the teaching of the Buddhas. Therefore, that is not the proper time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.”

When this was said, the venerable Mahākaccāna said to the elder bhikkhus:

“Friends, I have heard this directly from the Blessed One, learned this in his presence: ‘|Bhikkhu,::::| there are these six times to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him. What six?

1.) Here, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed and overcome by |sensual desire::passion or lust for sensual pleasures [kāmarāga]|, and he does not truly understand the escape from the arisen sensual desire, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: “Friend, I dwell with a mind obsessed and overcome by sensual desire, and I do not truly understand the escape from the arisen sensual desire. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the abandoning of sensual desire.” The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the abandoning of sensual desire. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the first time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.

‘Furthermore, |bhikkhu,::::| when a bhikkhu dwells with a mind obsessed by |ill will::intentional act of mentally opposing or rejecting others; an intentional construct fueled by aversion, directed against kindness or compassion. It manifests as hostility of will, impeding goodwill and fostering internal or external conflict. [byāpāda]| ... dwells with a mind obsessed by |dullness and drowsiness::lack of mental clarity or alertness, mental sluggishness, lethargy, sleepiness lit. stiffness (of mind/body due to tiredness) [thinamiddha]| ... dwells with a mind obsessed by |restlessness and worry::agitation and edginess, distraction, fidgeting, fiddling, uneasiness [uddhaccakukkucca]| ... dwells with a mind obsessed by |doubt::uncertainty, indecision, lack of conviction [vicikicchā]| ...

6.) When a bhikkhu does not know the sign by relying on which and attending to which the immediate |wearing away of the taints::gradual exhaustion and elimination of the mental defilements or deep-seated afflictions such as sensual desire, craving for existence, views, and ignorance [āsava + khaya]| occurs, at that time he should approach an esteemed bhikkhu and say to him: ‘Friend, I do not know the sign by relying on which and attending to which the immediate wearing away of the taints occurs. It would be good, venerable sir, if you would teach me the Dhamma for the wearing away of the taints.’ The esteemed bhikkhu then teaches him the Dhamma for the wearing away of the taints. This|, bhikkhu,::::| is the sixth time to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.’

Friends, I have heard this directly from the Blessed One, learned this in his presence: ‘These|, bhikkhu,::::| are the six times to approach an esteemed bhikkhu to see him.’”
