Then the Blessed One, having dressed early in the morning, taking his alms bowl and outer robe, went to the residence of the householder Anāthapiṇḍika. Having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat.

Now on that occasion, people in the householder Anāthapiṇḍika’s residence were making a loud noise and great commotion. Then the householder Anāthapiṇḍika approached the Blessed One, paid homage to the Blessed One, and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Blessed One said to the householder Anāthapiṇḍika: “Why are the people in your residence making a loud noise and great commotion, householder? One would think that it was fishermen making a haul of fish.”

“Venerable sir, this daughter-in-law of mine, Sujātā, has been brought from a wealthy family. She does not obey her mother-in-law, she does not obey her father-in-law, she does not obey her husband, and she does not honor, respect, revere, or venerate the Blessed One.”

Then the Blessed One addressed the daughter-in-law Sujātā: “Come, Sujātā.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the daughter-in-law Sujātā replied to the Blessed One. She approached the Blessed One; having approached, she paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Blessed One said to the daughter-in-law Sujātā:

“Sujātā, there are these seven kinds of wives for a man. What seven? One like a slayer, one like a thief, one like a master, one like a mother, one like a sister, one like a friend, and one like a handmaid. These, Sujātā, are the seven kinds of wives for a man. Which of these are you?”

“Venerable sir, I do not understand in detail the meaning of this brief statement made by the Blessed One. It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would teach me the Dhamma in such a way that I might understand in detail the meaning of this brief statement.”

“In that case, Sujātā, listen and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the daughter-in-law Sujātā replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“With a |corrupted mind::corrupted state of mind, such as having anger, being upset, or being resentful [paduṭṭhacitta]|, lacking sympathy,
enamoured with others, she despises her husband;
bent on slaying him who bought her with wealth:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a slayer.’

Whatever wealth a woman’s husband acquires,
applying himself to a craft, trade, or farming,
she desires to pilfer even if [he earns] but little:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a thief.’

Disliking work, lazy, gluttonous,
harsh, |aggressive::fierce, savage, wrathful [caṇḍī]|, and |speaking cruel words::impolite, speaking rudely [duruttavādī]|,
she dominates her industrious husband:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a master.’

She who is always caring and kind,
protecting her husband as a mother does her son,
and guarding the wealth he has accumulated:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a mother.’

Just as a younger sister respects her elder brother,
so she is respectful toward her own husband;
conscientious, deferential to her husband’s wishes:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a sister.’

She who rejoices upon seeing her husband,
as one rejoices on seeing a friend after a long time,
of noble birth, virtuous, devoted to her husband:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a friend.’

Without anger when threatened with rod,
with a pure mind, she endures her husband;
without anger, obedient to her husband’s wishes:
whatever wife of a man is of such a kind,
she is called ‘a wife like a handmaid.’

Those wives here who are called ‘slayers,’
and those who are called ‘thieves’ and ‘masters’:
unprincipled, harsh, and disrespectful,
with the breakup of the body, they go to hell.

But those wives here who are called
‘mothers,’ ‘sisters,’ ‘friends,’ and ‘handmaids’:
established in virtue, restrained for a long time,
with the breakup of the body, they go to a good destination.

These, Sujātā, are the seven kinds of wives for a man. Which of these are you?”

“From this day forth, venerable sir, may the Blessed One remember me as a wife to my husband who is like a handmaid.”
