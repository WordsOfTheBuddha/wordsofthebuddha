---
title: Pahāna sutta - Abandoning
description: The spiritual life is lived for the abandoning and complete uprooting of the seven fetters—attraction, aversion, views, doubt, conceit, passion for existence, and ignorance—and one who has cut them off at the root has made an end of suffering.
qualities: spiritual life, aversion, wrong view, doubt, conceit, passion, ignorance, craving, suffering
theme: wisdom, principle
slug: an7.9
---

“|Bhikkhus,::::| the |spiritual life::a life of celibacy, contemplation, and ethical discipline lived for the sake of liberation; oriented toward inner development rather than sensual pleasures [brahmacariya]| is lived for the abandoning and |complete uprooting::cutting off, total eradication [samuccheda]| of the seven |fetters::chains, bonds, attachments, things which bind [saṁyojanā]|. What seven?

1.) The spiritual life is lived for the abandoning and complete uprooting of the |fetter of attraction::bond of affection, compliant desire, fondness [anunayasaṁyojana]|, ... 2.) the |fetter of aversion::fetter of mental resistance, irritation, conflict [paṭighasaṁyojana]|, ... 3.) the |fetter of views::bond of opinions, conceptual beliefs [diṭṭhisaṁyojana]|, ... 4.) the fetter of |doubt::uncertainty, indecisiveness wrt suffering, its arising, its ending, and the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering [vicikiccha]|, ... 5.) the fetter of |conceit::self-view expressed as comparison—seeing oneself as superior, inferior, or equal; the persistent “I am” conceit (asmimāna) that underlies identification [māna]|, ... 6.) the fetter of passion for |existence::being, becoming; continued conditional existence, the karmically conditioned mode of being that leads to future rebirth [bhava]|, and 7.) the spiritual life is lived for the abandoning and complete uprooting of the fetter of |ignorance::fundamental unawareness or misunderstanding of the true nature of reality, not experientially understanding the four noble truths [avijjā]|.

|Bhikkhus,::::| the spiritual life is lived for the abandoning and complete uprooting of these seven fetters.

|Bhikkhus,::::| when a bhikkhu has abandoned the fetter of attraction—|cut off at the root::eradicated at the source [ucchinnamūla]|, uprooted like a palm stump, utterly obliterated, and rendered incapable of arising in the future—and likewise the fetter of aversion, ... the fetter of views, ... the fetter of doubt, ... the fetter of conceit, ... the fetter of passion for existence, ... and the fetter of ignorance has been abandoned—cut off at the root, uprooted like a palm stump, utterly obliterated, and rendered incapable of arising in the future—he is called|, bhikkhus,::::| a bhikkhu who has cut off |craving::wanting, yearning, longing, attachment, lit. thirst [taṇha]|, uprooted the fetters, and through the complete comprehension of conceit, has made an end of |suffering::from intense and painful suffering to the mildest of discontentedness, stress, unsatisfactoriness, dis-ease [dukkha]|.”
